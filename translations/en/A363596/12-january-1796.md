The year 1796.
On 1 January the farmer Grunewald, with his wife and 4 children, attended the New Year meeting, which we held under the trees. On the 2nd Brother Kühnel was at Daniel Dohi's to buy a load of flour, which he promised to deliver in 8 days. Many of our Hottentots have been at the harvest both with him and with his brother, and both testified that they had never had better people; they wished they could have such every year. On the 4th Mrs Teunis visited us with 2 other ladies from the Cape, who were very friendly. On the 9th we had Holy Communion, which Maria partook of for the first time. On the 11th we had rainy weather, so the brick-moulding had to be stopped. Until now we have had the weather we wished for our building, but what troubles us now is that we still have no carpenter. Towards evening Daniel Dohi brought the load of flour we had ordered. It was high time, for we were eating our last bread and had no more flour. A great deal is used up with us now, since every day we have to provide over 20 workers with bread and meat. Fortunately the brick-making will soon be finished.

<!-- lines 3117-3137 -->
On the 12th 2 Hottentot captains came here and said that they meant to go to the Cape with part of their men, to speak with the General about the farmers of the Unterland [1], who are very rebellious again and are making threats against us and the Hottentots too. Several Hottentots who wanted to leave them are said to have had the sinews under their calves cut through by them, so that they cannot walk. Since our carpenter has still not come, but our building has advanced so far that the carpentry must be begun, we sent our cart to the Cape on the 13th, partly to bring him here and partly to fetch the necessary ironwork.

<!-- lines 3137-3177 -->
On the 17th young Teunis visited us with 2 other gentlemen. One of them, named van Baling, formerly a captain but now, like all the others, out of service, has often been in Zeist, and here too he liked everything very much. On the 18th Brother Kühnel rode into the bush to see how far the felling of the building timber had got, and also to hire a wagon from a farmer to bring the timber in. He got a wagon for a couple of loads. It gives us a great deal of trouble to get wagons, for they suffer much in the mountains, and since iron is so dear at present, a wagon now costs 300 rixdollars. If a farmer lends us his wagon for one load, or two at most, that is a lot; and we must have more than 10 loads. The oxen we got from the Hottentots. On the 19th our cart came back from the Cape, and our carpenter with it. He began his work on the 20th. We sent 20 Hottentot women to a farm 1½ hours from here to thresh corn, so that we can get straw for our church roof. We get the straw from the farmer free of charge, but the threshing will cost us a good deal, since we have to feed the people. To hasten this work, which will last at least 3 weeks, we then decided that one of us should always be there, taking turns, however hard it was to stand the whole day in great heat under the open sky. We now had over 30 people at work.

## Notes

1. *Unterländiſche Bauren*: literally "farmers of the lower country". The context shows the rebellious burghers of the districts east of the mission, towards Swellendam, who are also called "the Nationals" in entries 06 to 09. Entry 13 reports that the threatened commando turned out to include farmers from near the mission.
