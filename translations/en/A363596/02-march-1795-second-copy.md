Diary of the three Brethren Marsveld, Schwinn and Kühnel at the Cape, from 1 to 31 March 1795 [1].

<!-- lines 414-422 -->
On 1 March we were visited by the schoolmaster Lohr [2], a German who lives 4 hours from here, together with another man named Rudolph. He too is a German and well known in Neudietendorf. The schoolmaster Lohr also spent 14 days in Dietendorf, but went away from there and became a schoolmaster. He has been here for 10 years. Among other things he said he wondered, when he thought about it, that he had not stayed there.

<!-- lines 422-449 -->
Afterwards Juffrouw Teunis came, with children and her sister. We were much surprised at this visit, since she is at times very hostile towards us. They all attended the meeting, in which Brother Marsveld spoke on Matthew 15:21–28. Afterwards Juffrouw Teunis went to our Sister Martha and began a violent quarrel with her. The cause of it was this. Martha was lately with her at the post, and one of the Juffrouw's slaves said to Martha that in the evenings things were terribly bad and coarse in the house [about us]. Martha told us this again, and Juffrouw Teunis came to hear of it. She became very angry about it and said to our Martha that she should have told this not to us but to her herself [3]; then she would have thrashed the slave woman for it. Juffrouw Teunis denied everything they had said about us [4]. It is, besides, a peculiarity of this woman that what she asserts today she will stubbornly deny tomorrow. As she said, she had come to visit us for this reason, to rage it out properly for once. As soon as she had finished this work, she drove back to her place and did not stay to eat with us. In the afternoon the schoolmaster Lohr mentioned above also drove away.

<!-- lines 450-474 -->
Towards evening, however, he came back with his companion and a boy whom they keep with them. They told us that it had rained heavily in the mountains in these days, so that the Sonderend River had risen so high that the oxen had to swim across. They had taken a Hottentot with them, who was to swim beside the oxen and guide them. Because, unfortunately, they went over a tree that had been laid across the river, to call another Hottentot, the oxen got so very deep into the river with the wagon that they stuck [5]. So the oxen had to be cut loose from the wagon, that they should not drown. As soon as this was done, the oxen swam to land; the wagon had to be left stuck there overnight, in order to fetch more people to help. The travellers stayed the night with us and did not leave until the next day, when they heard that the water had gone down.

<!-- lines 474-510 -->
That same evening Juffrouw Teunis sent to ask that one of us might come to her early next morning; she had stayed the night at her place. Brother Kühnel rode to her early on the 2nd. Her complaint was that during the past night 15 head of cattle from the Hottentots' kraal had got into her garden and quite ruined it; she had had them driven into the kraal. When she asked what she should do about it, Brother Kühnel answered that we could not help it; she must do what she thought best. While she was talking with Brother Kühnel in this way, 300 head of cattle belonging to the Hottentots came towards the garden. "There, you see," she said, "what am I to do? There is nobody with the cattle. I will have the whole lot driven to the post, and there they can wait until my husband comes back from the Cape." Brother Kühnel parried: she should do everything as she wished. She had her slaves drive the cattle together and take them to the post. Brother Kühnel [told] her that [we had] often [admonished] the Hottentots to take better care of their cattle, but that it was almost impossible to bring it into order [6]. She said: "I cannot imagine that the Hottentots [would not] do as they are told [7]. I have often seen and heard it myself: when they are told to do this or that, they answer, 'Oh no, not we, for the Herrnhuters [8], our teachers, have said it is not good and we should not do it any more'; or, 'We will first ask our teacher whether that is good too.' Should they not, then, out of obedience to you, [obey] when you tell them that they must take better care of their cattle?"

<!-- lines 510-525 -->
On the 3rd Brother Schwinn spoke on John 12:32; Brother Kühnel held the singing meeting in front of the house. On the 4th at midday, as we were sitting down to eat, a Hottentot came and said that a man who was making a ditch round his garden had fainted. Brother Marsveld went there at once, but it turned out that he was dead. While digging he had said several times, "I don't know what is happening to me, I feel so strange," and was going to leave off; but when he had gone a few steps he fell down and gave no further sign of life. In the evening Brother Kühnel read the 2nd chapter of the Acts of the Apostles. On the 5th the man who had died the day before was buried; in the evening Brother Kühnel spoke on John 13:20.

<!-- lines 525-551 -->
On the 6th our Sister Susanna came home again. She had been to the Landdrost at Stellenbosch and complained about a farmer named Stoffel Grunewald, in whose service her son is and whom she wants back. But the farmer will not let him go; he demands that he serve him a year longer, because he let the oxen he was herding get into the farmer's [?] and corn [9]. His time of service had already ended last year; but because he had let the oxen he was herding get into the farmer's corn, and great damage was done to him by it, he forced him to stay in service another year. This term has now run out as well; but since one of the farmer's horses went lame in this time, he says again that the boy is to blame for it: if he had watched better it would not have happened, and so he is to work off this damage too. In this way the farmers try, under all kinds of empty pretexts, to keep the Hottentots in their service all their lives. The Landdrost gave her a letter to take to the farmer with whom her son is. In the evening Brother Schwinn read the 3rd chapter of Acts and then held the singing meeting in front of our house.

<!-- lines 552-566 -->
At 7 o'clock [10] we held a lovefeast [?] and the partaking of the body and blood of Jesus in Holy Communion. Our 2 sisters Anna Maria and Rahel were both able to partake with us. Anna Maria had been ill for some time, and we did not think she would be able to be there this time. She said, "I long very much for this high good." And the Saviour strengthened her so that she was able to come. On the 8th Brother Marsveld spoke on Psalm 107:3–7. Afterwards 2 farmers came to our Hottentots [to find some] who should help them cart manure. Brother Kühnel was also called to our Sister Catharina, who is ill. She lives a short hour from us.

<!-- lines 566-598 -->
On the 9th 2 farmers came, named Jan Cholman and Stoffel Grunewald. The latter [11] lives about 5 miles and the latter 3 miles from here. Cholman wanted to speak to a Hottentot to whom he had lent money once when he would not go [12]. The Hottentot had promised to work off this debt. He was with the farmer for a while, then asked leave to visit his mother, who is ill, and since then he has not gone back to the farmer. Grunewald, who also looks as though he could not count to three, said: "Baas Teunis has issued an order by which every farmer who has a Hottentot in his service is free, if the Hottentot does not obey him, to go into the kraal and beat the Hottentot to life and death." He said further: "One of them owes me something too. When I plough he must come; if he does not, I shall come with a man and punish him as he deserves." We replied to this report that we did not know that the farmers had such freedom. They then admitted that formerly it had not been so, but that at present it really was so. We could do nothing about it but keep silent, since it is permitted to the farmers by the authorities; for we must take great care not to speak against the authorities. You can imagine how much such circumstances distress us. So we pray all the more to God for mercy on this poor people.

<!-- lines 599-615 -->
On the 10th Brother Schwinn spoke on Mark 16:15. Brother Kühnel held the singing meeting outside in front of the house. On the 11th we heard from several people that the farmers and Hottentots had to go to the Cape again to stand guard, and that our country therefore looks very sad. In the evening Brother Kühnel read the 4th chapter of Acts. On the 12th 2 Hottentots fetched back their sheep, which Juffrouw Teunis had had driven away to her place. They have to pay 2 shillings a head. Baas Teunis sent word to the others that they should fetch the rest too. But they were to drive them no longer here but to such places where they would be safe. The first 2 [obeyed] [13], and they may bring theirs back here; but as for the others, he will answer for it that they do not come here any more. In the evening Brother Kühnel spoke on John 11:25 and 26.

<!-- lines 616-624 -->
On the 13th 2 Hottentots came who live on the Olifants River, 14 days' journey on foot. A farmer drove them away from there and also took cattle from them. They went to the Landdrost about it, but he said he could not help them. Now they had been at the Cape with the Fiscal Reinveld, who gave them a letter to take with them.

<!-- lines 626-630 -->
On the 14th Brother Kühnel was called to our Sister Anna Maria. He found her very weak, but in a gladdening state of heart and wholly resigned to the will of the Saviour. On Sunday the 15th Brother Marsveld spoke on Mark 14:3–9.

<!-- lines 630-677 -->
On the 16th we again had to have a kraal made for our goats and billy goats on the Serschond River, because this side of the Sonderend River is not safe [14], for *Violen* and *boscjes Männer* creep about there, of which much of this livestock dies. It seems we are not meant to become farmers. It goes the same with the oxen: we have already had to slaughter 3 that were sick. We would gladly do away with all the livestock if we saw a way of managing without it. But it is not possible; we must have livestock, especially oxen. How else should we get firewood, corn or flour? Flour is a trouble to us, for in the whole district there is no mill that can grind in summer. There is indeed one 1½ miles from us, but since there is no other within a circuit of 10 miles, everything comes together there. We have had our corn there for 14 days already and still do not know when it will come back. All that time we have to keep 2 people there on board and wages, because otherwise no one gives the threshing a thought. In the rainy season this mill also has much to do [15], for most farmers have mills of their own, where they grind for themselves but for no one else. As soon as the rainy season is over, the grinding stops too. Now we must lay in flour, for in the rainy season we cannot cross the river. [The passage that follows is garbled in the transcription [16]: it says that few mills grind in summer because water is lacking; that the brethren could complain of this trouble, or free themselves from it, if they were secured in the ground they hold; and that they have water enough for a mill even in the dry season, which is highly valued in this country.] From our 5 cows [17] we have not had a drop of milk for our coffee for a long time, and have no prospect of getting any soon.

<!-- lines 677-690 -->
On the 17th Brother Schwinn spoke on Matthew 5:11, 12. Brother Kühnel held the singing meeting. On the 18th Brother Schwinn had his 46th birthday. 4 of our sisters came at daybreak to wake him with singing, and we played the flute with them. Afterwards the others came to wish him happiness. Today there was also the speaking with the candidates for baptism. For several of them we wish more [18] life with God; but of most of them it can be said that their whole mind is to live for the Saviour in this world. In the evening Brother Kühnel read Acts chapter 6. On the 19th Brother Kühnel spoke on Matthew 10:37 and 38. On the 20th Brother Schwinn read chapter 7 of Acts and also held the singing meeting afterwards.

<!-- lines 690-706 -->
On the 21st our dear Lord approved three persons as candidates for baptism [19]. Today some of Baas Teunis's people came, from whom we heard that what the farmer Grunewald had put about on the 9th of this month, concerning the order about disobedient Hottentots, was lies. On the 22nd Brother Marsveld spoke on John 8:46–52. Brother Schwinn was also called to [a sick woman] [20], whose state of soul gave him joy. On the 25th [21] a farmer named Heiziger, who lives close to Swellendam, came here expressly to hire a couple of Hottentots for manure carting, but got none. He stayed the night here. He was born a heathen and was baptised a Christian only 2 years ago. He has lived 10 years in the country. [His wife] was a slave, but is now [free and] also baptised [22].

<!-- lines 706-717 -->
On the 24th we had a fine rain, for which everything had been longing, for the drought was great, since it had not rained for 2 months and was very hot. The cattle were already short of fodder. This drought, however, is said to be all over the country, as one hears. In the evening Brother Schwinn spoke on Revelation 3:13. Tomorrow is [the festival of all the choirs] [23], which is kept in the congregation and which we join, so we have appointed the people for that day. We announced in the meeting that there would be a general meeting at 10 o'clock tomorrow. The singing meeting was dropped because of the rainy weather.

<!-- lines 717-727 -->
On the 25th many people came. Brother Schwinn preached the sermon. The sister to be baptised was given the name Louise. The peace of God made itself powerfully felt during this act. We must say in general that the Spirit of God shows himself mightily in the hearts of those present in all the meetings; but an act of this kind is always especially marked in this respect. This day was an outstanding moment both for us and for our people.

<!-- lines 727-755 -->
But our joy was damped, since today we heard very sad news. Some women came who had been with the farmers for several months. Some said that they had behaved very badly in that time; others asked in tears for forgiveness; others said they had drunk too much; others again that they had then fought one another. Still others accused themselves of lies [24] and said that this man or that, with whom they had kept company, had promised to marry them but had not kept his promise. Because of these offences we had to exclude them from the school and from Communion [25]. We told them that if they wanted to serve the devil and remain his children, we would have nothing to do with them. It would be best for them not to hear the word of God at all, for they would not accept God's grace, but trampled it underfoot. It is sad when the Hottentots are with the farmers so long, for there they get drink and hear nothing but scolding and cursing. When they are here one can often see the powerful work of the Holy Spirit in them; but when they are with the farmers again for a time, many lose everything again.

<!-- lines 756-768 -->
On the 26th Brother Kühnel went into the bush with 7 Hottentots to cut wood, 5 hours from here. We have resolved to build another small room onto our little house, because we are at our wits' end, it is so cramped. Brother Kühnel also means to make all kinds of implements from the wood. The reason Brother Kühnel went with them is this: if one leaves the Hottentots alone, sets them to work in the field, and none of us is there, they do not do much, but spend most of the time smoking tobacco and sleeping. So if one wants them to work diligently, one must be there. In the evening Brother Marsveld spoke on John 9:4 and 5.

<!-- lines 771-790 -->
Beruhen [26]. On the 27th we received the goods we had ordered in January. We also sent a chest of rice [27]. In the evening Brother Schwinn read Acts chapter 8 and held the singing meeting in front of the house. On the 28th we received a letter from Baas Teunis. This time he wrote in so friendly a way as he has never written before. We think this means something. In the evening Brother Kühnel came back from the bush with the Hottentots. Because of the heavy rain they have not been able to cut enough wood, so we must send a couple there once more. On the 29th Brother Marsveld spoke on Philippians 2:6–9. On the 30th we sent people into the bush again to cut some more wood. On the 31st we bought a pig to fatten. In the conference we resolved to read something again from the childhood story of Jesus [28]. We have divided it into 4 parts, to read one each evening, and today, after a prayer by Brother Schwinn, we made the beginning. Brother Kühnel held the singing meeting in the house this time, because of the wind.

<!-- lines 790-805 -->
With this we close our diary of this month, and commend ourselves and our Hottentots to the remembrance of all brothers and sisters and friends in and outside our united congregations, and ask them to entreat the Saviour with us that he may continue to convince people of their misery, so that they may seek him with all their hearts, who has also accomplished a ransom of salvation for them. Few days have passed this month on which some did not come to us and speak with us about the unrest of their hearts and testify to their longing to be saved. On some days these visits were numerous. Thank God, we are all 3 still well, live in love and peace, and remain your poor brothers Hendr. Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn and Joh. Chr. Kühnel.
