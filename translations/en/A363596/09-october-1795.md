<!-- lines 2557-2594 -->
On 3 October we held conference. Since we now have a new government, we had hoped among ourselves that our friends at the Cape would [let us know] what we had to expect [1]; but because this has not happened, we resolved that Brother Marsveld should set out for the Cape tomorrow with our Jacob, to pay our respects to the government and ask for its protection. Because [...] so many Hottentots [...] on our account [2], Baas Teunis is very angry again and has permitted himself expressions so bad that we will not write them down. A crowd of Hottentots are still lying at his place who insist on moving to us, and whom he will on no account let come to us. The Nationals near Swellendam are also said to be banding together again to enforce the right they believe they have over the Hottentots, and in this we too are diligently remembered. Among other things we heard that Bisani, when he drove us away, is supposed to have said: "These people give the right teaching of the true Jesus, and they lead the Hottentots the right way; but I am the devil's once and for all and must be lost, so I will use every means to keep the gospel from being preached to the Hottentots. I must be lost anyway, whether I do this or not." On the 8th some Hottentots whom Baas Teunis had treated so badly set out for the Cape, to ask the new government for help and for freedom to hear God's word.

<!-- lines 2594-2613 -->
On the 13th, late in the evening, Abraham came to tell us that 2 women had just fainted in his father's house. When asked what the cause was, he said: "When we had eaten, we thanked the Saviour in a couple of verses, and one of them said: 'Oh, my heart aches when I hear what the Saviour has done for us.' We sang on, and she fell down, and then the second one too." We went there and helped them with vinegar. They are a couple of quite new people, who visited us on the 14th and said it went too near their hearts to hear what the Saviour had done and suffered out of love for them. For the rest, it is now very much the custom, not only among the baptised but among the people in general, to sing a couple of verses after eating, and also before they lie down to sleep.

<!-- lines 2612-2652 -->
In the evening Brother Marsveld came back from the Cape. He reports the following of his journey: "On the evening of the 6th I arrived at our dear friend M. Schmidt's. On the 7th I went to the English General and the Major-General [3], to tell them who I and my two brothers were, namely missionaries sent by the Evangelical Unity of the Brethren to preach the gospel to the Hottentots. And since all religious meetings were permitted, I now asked permission for us to continue preaching the gospel, and also for their protection in it; to which I received the very favourable answer that we might continue to preach the gospel, and should enjoy protection and shelter. I thanked them for this in the name of my two brothers, and went from there to the Edle Heer Sluysken (the former Edle Heer or Governor), and thanked him in the same way for the favour and protection he had shown us. He said: 'I have done what I could. I wrote to the Major-General on your account, told him how you had been recommended to us by the Company, and asked that he too would grant you his protection; and I have received the answer I wished.' On the 8th I visited good friends I had been with before. On the 9th I meant to travel home again, but since stormy weather had set in, and I hoped to get letters from abroad [4] for us, I stayed on that day, though in vain, because no one was allowed on board. Today the burghers took the oath of allegiance to Great Britain, and everything passed off quite quietly. On the 10th I set out on the return journey and arrived home safely on the 14th." So far his account.

<!-- lines 2653-2669 -->
On the 17th was our Holy Communion, at which 2 persons became candidates. On the 19th the Hottentots mentioned under the 8th came back from the Cape. They had spoken with the English General, who listened to them kindly and told them they could always move to us and hear God's word, and no one should do them any harm. They then said: "But Baas Teunis wants to drive us away." He promised to write to Baas Teunis and the Landdrost of Swellendam that they were to see to it that nothing was put in the Hottentots' way; but should it happen, they need only come back to him, and he would know what to do. On the 22nd a little son of our Sister Catharina was baptised with the name Joseph.

<!-- lines 2669-2680 -->
On the 24th the superintendent of the warm bath told us that this week we would receive a visit from persons of rank, namely from a colonel and a lieutenant-colonel of the English troops [5]. On the 28th these gentlemen sent greetings through a Hottentot from the Baas's place, and word that they would visit us on Sunday.

<!-- lines 2681-2699 -->
On the 29th we began the schools again, which we had suspended since 18 July; but only with the baptised, the candidates for baptism and their children [6]. We intend, however, to take in the others again as well; only each must come to us singly and ask for it, and declare that their whole mind is to live for the dear Saviour in this world out of love, the small as well as the great. We had thought that if the great stumbling block, the school, were removed and we held only meetings, many would then stay away; and Baas Teunis had thought so too. But now we have seen otherwise: although we have held no school for more than 3 months, we know of not one [7] who has moved away, apart from the many on 18 July, whom we advised to go ourselves.

<!-- lines 2700-2718 -->
We closed this month too with praise and thanks to our dear Lord, who had given us rest and peace from outside again. But the main thing, which we look on with thankfulness and shame, is the blessing he lets us see [8]. For he and his Spirit show themselves in the hearts of the Hottentots in a wonderful way, and such as we have not seen before. They come not only singly (others many together) to speak with us, and not only by day but also at night, and say with many tears: "Oh, I do not know where to turn for the anguish of my heart; for I have grieved the dear Saviour so much, and now I have heard his word for so long and yet not lived by it, I must be lost," and so on.
