Of the Hottentots. From the diary of Baviaanskloof, from 1 April to 6 December 1795.

<!-- lines 812-821 -->
On 1 April was the washing of the brothers and sisters [1]. Some of them we wished were beside us ourselves [2]. As for the communicants, we can rejoice over them from our hearts. In our conference Susanna was appointed a confirmand [3]. In the evening we continued the reading of the story of the Passion. On the 2nd we had a blessed partaking of Holy Communion.

<!-- lines 822-845 -->
On the 3rd, Good Friday, we read on in the story of the Passion, and at the passage "He bowed his head and gave up the ghost" we thanked the Saviour in a prayer for his agony and death, and asked him to give us and our people a truly living impression of it; and we had a penetrating sense of the presence of God. To close, we meant then to sing the 2 verses *Da sind wir Armen und Blöden* and *da du in Todes Staube funden* [4], but [we were] interrupted by the loud weeping of the people, and could not keep from tears ourselves. When the people went away, they lay down everywhere in the bushes round about, and much loud praying and weeping could be heard. All the meetings in this week were much blessed and full of grace, but this one was especially marked.

<!-- lines 845-863 -->
On the Great Sabbath [5] we held a lovefeast with our communicants, and afterwards an evening blessing. On the first day of Easter, the 5th, we prayed the renewal [?] with the [baptised] and the candidates for baptism [6]. We had told them to come to us in clean clothes; they came, however, an hour [early] [7]. After the sermon it was announced that there would be another meeting in the evening, in which we would read the story of the Resurrection. The people came for this meeting as early as 2 o'clock and sat down all round our house. On the 6th, early, we sang with our baptised and candidates for baptism the liturgy *zum Opfer* [8], and afterwards there was a sermon.

<!-- lines 863-899 -->
On the 8th the Brethren Marsveld and Schwinn went over the mountains behind our house to visit a farmer. On the 9th Brother Kühnel had a visit from Baas Teunis's son, who laid out the place an hour from here where, as will be remembered from our previous diary, there was so much trouble over the Hottentots' cattle. It will also be remembered that not long ago we had a remarkable visit from a certain Mr Claude [9]. The Hottentots had told him their distress, and since then he had represented to the authorities that they had been deceived in this matter. In particular he had shown the groundlessness of the pretext that cattle could not thrive here, and asserted that 500 head of cattle could very well find a living here. The best of the matter was that Mr Claude had now bought the land, as young Teunis now told us. Also the order that the Hottentots were to take their cattle away had been largely withdrawn by the Cape. Mr Teunis also told us that goods [of Herrnhuters?] had arrived here on a Danish ship; he did not know from where, only that they were not intended for the Cape [10]. No one can imagine how eager this made us. How we now wished to see [Herrnhut] brothers and sisters here!

<!-- lines 899-957 -->
In the evening the Brethren Marsveld and Schwinn came home again. They brought the following from their journey: "[A friend] who lives about an hour away, behind our great mountain [11], had often asked us for a visit, and we would gladly have visited him sooner if we had not been afraid of the mountain; for we heard from all the Hottentots that crossing it was dangerous to life. This time we took our Jacob with us, who showed us the way. Often it seemed impossible to go further, especially near the very top. After long consideration we ventured it all the same and came safely through. Here we had an indescribably beautiful view: on one side the open sea, where we saw what looked like a ship in full sail, and on the other a great plain with grazing cows and oxen. We now climbed down and found this side just as steep as the other. At 11 o'clock we arrived at the friend mentioned above, who received us very kindly. Mr Koster asked us to hold a meeting for him; but we declined, and talked with them in conversation about the way of salvation. In the afternoon another farmer, who lives a good hour further on, came to us and begged us to visit him too, for which he would send us horses tomorrow. We said that tomorrow we would like to be home in good time, so as not to have to climb the path in the middle of the mountain; but as he would not stop asking, we went to him that same evening, at which he was very pleased, and he entertained us well. One of his slave women, of whom our Hottentots had told us much, that she was troubled about her salvation, came to us and poured out her distress with many tears. She said that whenever she wanted to pray, it said within her: 'Depart, thou cursed one, into the everlasting fire prepared for the devil,' and so on. We told her that such people as see themselves as lost sinners are just what the Saviour wants, and pointed her further, as well as we could, to his heart that loves sinners. On the 9th, early at 6 o'clock, our horses were already saddled, and after breakfast we set off; the friend went with us as far as the mountain. It was very hard going again, and we shall probably not climb it again without need. At midday we arrived back in Baviaanskloof." So far their account.

<!-- lines 957-981 -->
On the 10th a friend who lives a mile from here told us that he had brought things for us from the Cape, which we could fetch from him. At the same time he brought confirmation that brothers and sisters were at the Cape, and handed us letters from Brother Stael, Brother Metz and Sister Woltersdorf [12]. She told us that they would gladly visit us; but since the ship was to sail on as early as the 12th, they could not come, though they very much wished to see us and speak with us. We cannot describe how glad we were to know brothers and sisters so near us. Since we could not all [3] get away [13], it was resolved that the Brethren Marsveld and Kühnel should travel there, which happened at once, that same afternoon at 4 o'clock. Brother Schwinn writes further: "Today I had to hold school, mostly for big girls; for the boys, one of our candidates for baptism helped with the school [14]. They had behaved very well during their work with the farmers.

<!-- lines 982-1001 -->
"On the 11th another family of Hottentots applied to stay here and hear God's word. In the afternoon a wagon with 8 horses came towards our house. I was as glad as a child and thought it was the brothers and sisters from the Cape. But it was the superintendent of the warm bath [15] with a party, 11 persons in all, who wanted to attend tomorrow's singing [16]. I was at a loss how to entertain them, but something had to be managed, for that is the custom of the country here. On the 12th they asked to attend the church liturgy and the sermon, and afterwards thanked me kindly and said: 'The Hottentots put us Christians to shame; if we lived nearer, we would come here every Sunday.' In the afternoon they went back again.

<!-- lines 1002-1043 -->
"On the 13th a Bastard [17] said that he did not know what to do for anguish of heart. It had been so with him for a long time, [before he came] to speak with us; since the remarkable death of his brother it had become so great that he could still see him [18]. When asked why he had not come sooner, he said he had been so angry with us. Question: Why, then? Answer: because he had not been one of the first to be baptised. Every time there was a baptism he had become so angry that he could have cursed and run out of the meeting. He had often wished to be far away from us and could not bear the sight of the brothers and sisters. It had vexed him to hear so often in the meeting that whoever does not come to Jesus Christ as a sinner must be lost, since he had been with better Christians who had often told him that whoever lived as he did must be saved. He had known most of our baptised, how they had lived in sin and shame, and yet they were baptised before him. This and much else had so confused him that he had often wished we had never come into the country. But last Sunday in the meeting those who believed themselves better than others because they had not lived in open works of the flesh had been called upon to examine themselves, how things stood with them as to love of God and of their fellow men. That had been like a thunderclap in his heart; it had made him begin to weep, and from that hour he had asked [himself] [19]: 'I am lost, I am going to hell,' and so on. I pointed him to Jesus' love for sinners, and on leaving he said: 'Thank you, and pray for me.'

<!-- lines 1043-1054 -->
"On the 14th I was called late at night to one of our candidates for baptism, who was very ill. I found her in a gladdening frame. On the 15th I read the 11th and 12th chapters of the Acts of the Apostles in the meeting. Afterwards many came and asked what kind of sheet that was, with all kinds of animals, that came down from heaven to Peter. This gave me the opportunity to praise the wonderful love of Jesus for the heathen too, and many tears were shed."

<!-- lines 1054-1111 -->
On the 16th, late in the evening, my dear Brethren Marsveld and Kühnel came back from their journey. They write of it as follows: "Before we left, several of our sisters came, who had quickly heard the news [20], wished us a good journey, and said how very glad they would be to see here the brothers and sisters who had arrived at the Cape; they also sent them warm greetings. From the first there was a real stir about it among the people, and all wished that they might come here. On the 11th we rode on with our Jacob and had all kinds of hardships to bear; among other things we had nothing to eat all day. On top of this, towards evening someone on the road told us that the brothers and sisters had already taken their leave in the town, to set sail tomorrow. What pain that was to us! [And we were] so tired that we could not expect to reach the Cape that day. But we asked the Saviour to help us, and it went better than we had thought, so that at half past 9 we arrived at our friend Schmidt's and with our dear brothers and sisters. They were still sitting at supper. We cannot describe what joy this was on both sides. Tired as we were, little sleep was had that night. To everyone's pleasure they could stay another day, since they were not to go on board until early on the 13th. We spent the morning of the 12th very happily together. Since they had not had Holy Communion for a long time, they asked us to hold it with them. So with Brother Metz, Sister Woltersdorf and the 2 eldest daughters of the latter we held a blessed partaking of the body and blood of Jesus in Holy Communion, in which he acknowledged us very graciously. Early on the 13th, after a moving farewell, they went on board, and Brother Kühnel went with them. Father Schmidt and his children said they had never had such a leave-taking, for it cost them many tears. We spent this day visiting good friends, among others the Lutheran and the Reformed ministers. The brothers and sisters could not yet set sail today, but on the 14th, early, when we got up, they were already gone.

<!-- lines 1111-1130 -->
"Today we visited the Commissioner [21], the commander Rhenius and the commandant Mr Gordon [22], as well as others of the government. We were received kindly everywhere, but the gentlemen were so overwhelmed with work that we could not talk with them much. On the 18th many ships were to sail for Holland. 2 ships from there had arrived in False Bay on the 12th, and we inquired at once whether letters and news had come for us; but there was nothing. Because of the circumstances we had also thought to speak further about our place with the gentlemen of the government, but this was not the right time for it. We therefore decided to set out on the journey that afternoon.

<!-- lines 1131-1151 -->
"Shortly before, a son of Father Schmidt told us that our Jacob had spoken with several Hottentots who are on guard duty and had given them such admonitions that he had been astonished. He told them to seek Jesus Christ, because he was still to be found. They said they wished to be away from guard duty soon, to come to Baviaanskloof and hear God's word again. Jacob told them they should not just blame the guard [23] for that; they could have the dear Saviour there too, for he is present everywhere where one longs for him. [A lady] asked him whether things were so with him as he said [24]. He answered: 'If Juffrouw could see into my heart, Juffrouw would see that it is as I say.'

<!-- lines 1151-1165 -->
"We now travelled with 2 good friends from the Cape to our friend Licken, who was very weak. He was very glad that we showed him this kindness and said he felt strengthened by it. He took leave of us, because he believed we would not see each other again in this life. He is departing this life as a poor sinner who has known nothing but the grace of Jesus. Next morning we hurried on from there, and in the evening we managed so that we did not have to go to bed [out of doors?] [25]. On the way we also visited [...]. In spite of all kinds of hardships and delays, we reached our Brother Schwinn on the evening of the 18th [26]."

<!-- lines 1166-1203 -->
On the 18th 2 persons were chosen as candidates for baptism in our conference. On the 19th, in the meeting for the litany, we delivered greetings from absent brothers and sisters and told the people that for a time we would remember them by name in the litany for their [journey] [27]. All our people wished they had been to the Cape too to see the brothers and sisters, and told our Jacob that he should thank the Saviour for having had that good fortune. He too was so full of stories, and was asked so many questions, that he could not finish. Sister Woltersdorf had given us a cap, which we had our Martha try on. It met with general approval, and the sisters could not see enough of it. "Oh," they said, "if only sisters would come to us too!" Asked what good that could do, they said it would be something quite different to speak with their own kind. In the evening a man visited us who was born in Lobenstein and has been in the country 33 years. He has a 20-year-old daughter by a Hottentot woman, whom he would like to send to school with us, so that she might learn something useful. He asked how long he would have to leave her with us for that purpose. We said we were not schoolmasters; we took no one into the school merely to learn something, but in the hope that they would be converted to the Saviour. Those who did not have this mind we sent away again.

<!-- lines 1204-1224 -->
On the 23rd Brother Schwinn was called to a sick man and found him in a gladdening state. Lena also asked for a visit, because she is so weak with age that she can no longer walk. Brother Marsveld was glad about her. On the 24th Andreas Otto came with a son of Mr Claude, to look at the place bought nearby. On the 25th some more women and children moved here. On the 26th, after the meeting, two came [to us] [28] and spoke with us about the awakening of their hearts. Among them was a man who we thought was having the falling sickness; he was so shaken that he trembled and quaked and could hardly stand. When we asked him what was wrong, for a long time he could not answer. At last he burst into a flood of tears and said: "I hear the great words of God, and I have not lived in the way." [He] was directed to the Friend of sinners [29].

<!-- lines 1224-1240 -->
On the 27th we spoke once more with Susanna, who is soon to go to Holy Communion with us, and on the 29th she was confirmed. On the 30th several of our baptised came for the speaking, and we could rejoice greatly at what they said. Strictly, the speaking was suspended this time, but in such a way that those who were willing could come. In outward things we had much trouble at this time with our 4 oxen, which are so thin that we cannot use them; and since we badly needed our wagon, we had to borrow oxen from the Hottentots.

## Notes

1. *das Waſchen der Geſchwiſter*: the foot-washing, a Moravian rite then held before Holy Communion in Holy Week, after John 13. Easter fell on 5 April in 1795.
2. The transcription reads *Einiges wüſchen wir ſelber neben uns her*; the normalised column reads *wünschten*. The sense is unclear: perhaps the missionaries wished some of the Khoekhoe members could join them in the rite. To be checked against the image.
3. *Confirmandin*: a confirmand, a baptised member being prepared for confirmation and first Communion. *aus gemacht*, "decided", probably means decided by lot.
4. The opening words of two hymn verses, left in German. The first is transcribed *Da ſind wir armen und blöden*, the second *u. da du in Todes Staube funden*; they have not been identified. The verb of the next clause (*wurden ... unterbrochen*) is missing in the transcription.
5. *Großer Sabbath*: Holy Saturday.
6. The transcription reads *beteten wir mit den Gewähl-ten ins Liebesmahl, Tauf=Candidaten die Erneuerung*, which is garbled. It seems to say that the missionaries prayed a "renewal" (perhaps a renewal of vows, or the Easter litany) with the baptised and the candidates for baptism. To be checked against the image.
7. The transcription reads *ſie fanden ſich aber eine Stunde gleich an*; "early" is a guess from the context.
8. *die Liturgie zum Opfer*: not identified. It may be a misreading of one of the Moravian liturgies addressed to the Son; compare *eine Liturgie zum Sohne* in entry 11. To be checked against the image.
9. The earlier trouble over the cattle is in entries 01 and 02, but Mr Claude's visit is not in March; the "previous diary" mentioned here, or an earlier one, is not in this file.
