<!-- lines 1241-1266 -->
On 2 May we had Holy Communion; Susanna partook of it for the first time, and Anna looked on as a confirmand. On the 3rd it was announced after the meeting that the custom, which had become very burdensome to us, of greeting each of us individually before and after the meeting should stop from now on. Until now they had kept it as their duty, and none of them wanted to be behind in it; but we told them we would take it as done every time. Today many came one by one to speak with us, among others the mother of our Louise, who lives 2 hours from here and usually comes here every Sunday. She said: "For some time I have been so restless that I can hardly bear it at home. Whenever I think of this place I feel happy, especially when Sunday is near and I can go to the meeting; and when I am here I feel very happy; but as soon as I have to go home, it is as if I had to go into the fire." Her husband is a slave, and so she cannot come as often as she would like.

<!-- lines 1266-1278 -->
On the 4th we remembered the single sisters in our morning blessing and sang them a few verses for their choir festival [1]. On the 5th [2], before it was day, a woman came and said that she could not sleep for the unrest of her heart. She was told that the dear Saviour had had to suffer so much for the sake of our sins. Many more came today. There is a quite special stirring here, particularly among the multitude. Many come and say that they too wish to be saved, and this is always accompanied by many tears.

<!-- lines 1278-1311 -->
The 7th was a rainy day, which did our gardens much good. In spite of the [rain] [3], those who live an hour from here came punctually to the meeting and to school. On the 8th it rained very hard again. This weather does not agree with our goats: last night 9 of them perished, and altogether, of the 100 we had bought, we have only 36 left. We are assured that in the rainy season they will all perish. On the 13th was the speaking with the candidates for baptism, and we could rejoice over most of them. On Ascension Day, the 14th, a blessed feeling prevailed in the meeting. Of the 6 persons who had lately been excluded from the school, 4 were readmitted. One of them, a woman candidate for baptism, said that she asks the Saviour for forgiveness day and night and cannot sleep for unrest, and that she wished very much to come back into the class. Hannah, who had been excluded a considerable time ago, had often asked to be readmitted, but would never properly confess her offences. Today, however, she did so, and we could not but readmit her, and likewise a candidate for baptism whom we had had to exclude. Both expressed their gratitude with many tears.

<!-- lines 1311-1320 -->
In our conference on the 16th we spoke much about our people and could not thank the dear Saviour enough for all he does for them. 2 men and 4 women were chosen for baptism. In the evening we had them come and asked them once more about their minds, and put the following questions to them [4]: "We desire nothing but to live in peace for the dear Saviour in the world, through his grace." An indescribably blessed feeling prevailed.

<!-- lines 1322-1350 -->
On the 17th it was announced in the meeting for the litany that Hannah repented of her offences and asked the Saviour and the congregation for forgiveness, at which many tears were shed. At 10 o'clock was the baptism, which each of us performed on 2 persons. They were given the names David, Abraham, Juliana, Rosina [5], Johanna and Carolina. We had never before had a baptism of so many at once, and the peace of God made itself powerfully felt. In the afternoon we had the 4 newly baptised [women at a] lovefeast with most of the other sisters [6], which looked much as it does in the congregation when the single sisters go out together in companies at their choir festival. For they too were all dressed in white or nearly white. They are able to do this because they often get cast-off clothes from the farmers' wives instead of pay, which are sometimes still quite good. During the week some of them go about in skins, but on Sundays all are very nicely dressed; and when one of them is baptised, the others help her so that she appears in white. On the 18th and 19th we hired 4 Hottentots to cut firewood for us.

<!-- lines 1351-1367 -->
On the first day of Pentecost, the 24th, the farmer Harme visited us with his wife. We had [expected] many visitors over these holidays, for several parties had sent word...; unfortunately it rained so hard that it stayed at the above, which in fact suited us very well [7]. The farmers' visits on Sundays are a great burden to us, for they take up all the room, and the poor Hottentots, for whose sake we are here after all, have to stand outside the door. Most of them come only out of curiosity, so as to be able to say that they have been in the Hottentot church.

<!-- lines 1367-1381 -->
In the afternoon we held adoration [8] with the baptised and the candidates for baptism. On the second holiday a man was baptised with the name Isaac. Today we looked on our people with quite particular compassion. It was raining hard; the people were already wet when they came, and now most of them had to sit outside in the rain, so that the water ran off their skins in streams. Yet they listened with a devotion that could not have been greater. How we wished to have a proper church for these poor people one day [9]! But we have no prospect of it; the Saviour, however, is mighty and can help.

<!-- lines 1381-1391 -->
On the 27th we heard that a man, one of those who have not been here long, had woken a candidate for baptism at midnight to tell him that he could not sleep for the unrest of his heart, and to ask him to tell him something of what Jesus had suffered for the sins of men; which the other then did as well as he could. Many came to us one by one in these days and testified to their concern about being saved.

<!-- lines 1392-1407 -->
On the 30th we held Holy Communion, at which Anna partook for the first time. Today, to our great joy, we received letters from our dear brethren of the Unity Elders' Conference [10], from April and August of last year. At the end of this month our little Hottentot congregation consisted of:
9 communicants
1 confirmand
1 candidate for Holy Communion
17 baptised adults
1 baptised child
14 candidates for baptism.
Total 43 persons.

<!-- lines 1408-1444 -->
Our Martha had often asked us to tell her when we were writing to the fatherland, so that she could send a report too. She dictated the following to us:
"When the dear sisters were at the Cape lately, I longed very much to see them; but I would have liked it much better still if they had come here and stayed here. Oh, when shall we have the good fortune to have sisters with us? If only the time would come soon! I also send word that I cannot thank the dear Saviour enough for bringing me here. Where else would my poor soul have gone! I have now been here about 2 years. When I think how I was before my baptism (for I have done every sin), and what I feel now! The dear Saviour has also given me the great grace of partaking of his body and his blood in Holy Communion. I do feel very poor and bad, but I have his peace in my heart; and my prayer is that he will keep me with him until I come to him. Then I will thank him very much for the love he has shown me. Greet all of them, you brothers and sisters, and many thanks that you have sent us teachers and that you also support them, for we cannot give them anything; we are a poor people. I also thank the Saviour that he has given me so many sisters and brothers from my nation. Oh, may we all live wholly for him! With this I greet all the brothers and sisters in the fatherland.
Martha."

## Notes

1. The single sisters' choir festival was kept by Moravian congregations on 4 May.
2. The transcription reads *Nachm. d. 5te ſchickte, ehe es Tag war, kam ſchon eine W. ſagte*. *Nachm.* ("in the afternoon") and *ſchickte* ("sent") do not fit *ehe es Tag war* ("before it was day"); they may be misreadings or false starts. *W.* is read as *Weib* or *Frau*, "woman".
3. The transcription reads *Das Ärgernis ungeachtet*, "in spite of the offence", which makes no sense here; probably a misreading of *Des Regens ungeachtet*, "in spite of the rain". To be checked against the image.
4. The questions themselves are not given; what follows in quotation marks is the answer. Something may be missing in the manuscript or the transcription.
5. The transcription has *Juliana / Rosina* across a line break; the normalised column reads "Juliana Rosina" as one name. Six persons were baptised (2 men and 4 women, each missionary baptising 2), so the names are read here as six: Juliana and Rosina are two women. A Rosina is readmitted in February 1796 (entry 13).
6. The transcription reads *Nachmittags hatten wir die 4 neuen Getauften. Liebesmahl mit dem größten Theil der andern Schweſtern Hierzu gaben, welches ohngefähr ſo ausſah, wie wenn in der Gemeine die led. Schweſtern an ihrem Chor feſt in geweſten Geſellſchaften mit einander ausgehen*. The sentence is garbled; the English gives the probable sense. To be checked against the image.
7. The syntax of this passage is broken in the transcription (*und zwar; denn*), and the three dots are in the source. "Unfortunately" is ironic, or the word order is disturbed.
8. *Anbeten*: a Moravian service of adoration, often held on festival days with a particular group of the congregation.
9. The transcription reads *ewig*, "for ever", probably a misreading of *einmal*, "one day".
10. *Unit. Aelt. Conferenz*: the Unity Elders' Conference, the governing body of the Moravian Church, which sat at Berthelsdorf near Herrnhut.
