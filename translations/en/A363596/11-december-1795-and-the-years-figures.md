<!-- lines 2835-2837 -->
Of the Hottentots. Diary of Baviaanskloof, from 7 December 1795 to 14 March 1796.

<!-- lines 2838-2860 -->
After Brother Marsveld's return from Cape Town, where he had received permission from the English General to fell building timber for a church, we considered on 7 December how large we should build it. We decided to make the hall 60 feet long and 18 feet wide, with a room at each side 16 feet square. Since it is high time to begin this work, we sent for the mason who had worked on our house. He is at present with the Landdrost, 3 hours from here, and promised to come to us in 8 days and work for a fair price. The usual rate here for a mason is one rixdollar a day. To speed up the work, he meant to look for an assistant as well. That same day the building site was pegged out, directly in front of our house.

<!-- lines 2860-2872 -->
On the 8th a start was made on digging the foundations, and the watchword struck us greatly: "[They that are far off shall come and] build the temple of the LORD [1]. And none of them does it as if he must." It was fulfilled to the letter: for now 5 Hottentots came with their wives and children from far up country, to hear the word of God, as they themselves said. They rejoiced greatly over the building that was going up and promised to help wherever they could.

<!-- lines 2872-2894 -->
Brother Kühnel rode to Christian Franz to buy boards from him. On the 9th he was at the [post] [2], to show Baas Teunis the written permission we had received to fell building timber, and to arrange with him what was necessary. Today was the speaking [3] with the baptised, and we could rejoice over most of them. On the 10th we had a visit from 2 gentlemen from the Cape, who then went hunting. Today the mason told us that he had not been able to get an assistant. We wrote to the Cape asking them to send us a mason and a carpenter. On the 11th we were visited by a butcher's man who has been on the road for 4 months driving together stock for slaughter. He has over 5000 head of oxen and sheep together. On the 12th we had Holy Communion, at which Maria looked on as a confirmand and Catharina as a candidate.

<!-- lines 2894-2915 -->
On the 13th the mason arrived to begin work on the church. In the last few days we had carried together with the women and children as many stones as possible, to build the foundations with. Yesterday we had also made a start on moulding bricks, of which we shall need about 60000. The side walls will be made of unburnt bricks, after the manner of this country, but for the gables they must be burnt. All this will take work; but at present we are not so much at a loss for bricks as for the timber, which is still growing, since we have no one yet to fell it. Besides, we shall have to fetch the stones from far away. Today we announced in the meeting that early tomorrow morning we would lay the foundation stone of our church, and that everyone, young and old, should come.

<!-- lines 2916-2945 -->
On the 14th, early, at half past 5, everyone was gathered here. We placed the people round the building site and sang a verse, after which Brother Marsveld said that we and they had long wished for a larger meeting place; the Saviour had now so arranged it that we could build one, and now we were gathered to lay its foundation stone and to ask God for his blessing and help. After a verse had been sung, Brother Marsveld knelt down on the foundation stone, the congregation knelt too, and the Saviour was entreated from the heart to make this building a blessing to many people, to let it go forward happily, and to keep all who would work on it from harm. Then the close was made with the singing of a verse. A mighty sense of grace prevailed during this act. The mason said afterwards that when he was about to lay the stone, something came over him that he could not describe. The watchword was: "Them that honour me I will honour. — The choice of grace is past our fathoming; what are we worms, that the Lord so honours us!" [4]

<!-- lines 2946-2964 -->
On the 16th 2 Hottentots came home who had worked in the harvest for a farmer and now meant to go to another farmer. We would gladly have kept them here, because we are short of workers; but we did not want to keep them from the farmer. When they were an hour's way off, they became so anxious that they could go no further; they turned back and offered to work for us. On the 19th many Hottentots who had been at the harvest came home again, and we put them to work at once. Even with the few people we had made good progress in the last week. On the 21st more came home from the harvest, and on the 22nd 15 quite new people, who had lived below Swellendam and now wanted to hear God's word.

<!-- lines 2964-2995 -->
On the 23rd was the speaking with the candidates for baptism. We had been worried about those who had worked for the farmers, because of wine-drinking, but so far we have heard nothing troubling. Today a large family moved [...] [5]. Andreas Otto asked us not to hold the meeting on Christmas Day too early, because he and several others would like to attend it. On the 24th the mason we had asked for came from the Cape. He is a slave. Today [the foundation wall] was finished [6]. Straight after the holidays the walls will be built with dried bricks. Towards evening Messrs Lind and Marx visited us, which was not agreeable to us, since we meant to hold a lovefeast with our baptised on the holy Christmas Eve. We could do nothing but tell them that we would like to be alone with our baptised, and asked them to stay in a room meanwhile. This did not seem to suit them, but they put up with it.

<!-- lines 2996-3028 -->
Before the lovefeast we held a general singing meeting, which we very much enjoyed. The following night it rained so hard that the visitors expected for Christmas Day stayed away, which suited us very well. The weather still turned out such that we could hold the meeting under the trees. A great many Hottentots were here on a visit and stayed here over the holidays. We estimated their number at 500. On the 26th, early, we sang a liturgy to the Son with the baptised and the candidates for baptism. At 10 o'clock we had a baptism, at which 4 persons, named Sara, Regina, Wilhelmina and Benigna, were baptised into the death of Jesus. On the 27th we closed the holidays with praise and thanks to our dear Lord for the blessing he has laid on our testimony of his becoming man and of his death. In all the meetings he showed himself mighty and powerful in the hearts. We believe that many who were only here on a visit will take an impression home with them. This afternoon we sent several Hottentots into the bush to fell timber, and on the 28th more went after them, so that we now have 8 strong men at this work.

<!-- lines 3028-3050 -->
On the 30th a Hottentot captain [7] arrived here with 8 Hottentots from a district about 20 miles from here. They had with them a Hottentot prisoner who had beaten his wife to death and was now to be taken to the Cape. They asked leave to stay here until after New Year. On the 31st, towards midnight, we had a general meeting for the close of the year, to which the Hottentots came from all sides. Brother Schwinn reminded them of the many benefits of God they had enjoyed this year and called on them to praise and thank him, whereupon we entered the new year on our knees in prayer and supplication. What feeling prevailed in this meeting we cannot describe. The prayer was interrupted by loud weeping.

<!-- lines 3051-3084 -->
Now, dear Brethren and Sisters, what shall we say at the close of this year? We have no words to express ourselves. The devil has raged horribly, and he even succeeded in making us leave our sheep for a time. What we went through then we cannot describe. But who would have thought that the Saviour would so arrange and change things, yes, so change them that we now see a wall of our meeting house going up. When we see the mighty work of grace in the hearts of these poor people, we stand ashamed and amazed. For some time new people have come almost every day, so that the number is growing fast. This year 20 adults and 4 children have been baptised; 5 persons have come to Holy Communion, and 4 have become candidates for it. 24 have been received among the candidates for baptism. One sister has gone home. At the close of the year our little congregation consists of:
9 communicants,
1 confirmand,
3 candidates for Communion,
21 baptised adults,
4 baptised children,
27 candidates for baptism,
Total 65 persons, of whom, however, one baptised woman and one woman candidate for baptism are excluded for the time being.

## Notes

1. The transcription reads *Wie man den Samen nennen, die den Tempel des HERRN bauen werden*, which is garbled. The watchword is evidently Zechariah 6:15, "And they that are far off shall come and build in the temple of the LORD" (Luther: *Und es werden kommen von ferne, die am Tempel des HERRN bauen werden*), as the next sentences show; the English supplies the opening in brackets. The second line is a hymn verse, not identified. To be checked against the image.
2. The transcription reads *auf der Jagt*, "hunting", probably repeated by mistake from the next day's entry; the purpose was to see Baas Teunis, who lived at the post. To be checked against the image.
3. The transcription reads *das Sprengen*, a misreading of *das Sprechen*. It then reads *wobey wir uns über die meiſten wundern konnten*, "could wonder at most of them", probably for *freuen*, "rejoice".
4. The watchword is 1 Samuel 2:30. The hymn lines are not identified; *unergründenswerth* is probably *unergründlich*, "unfathomable". *Maden* are maggots or worms.
5. The transcription reads *Heute zog eine ſtarke familie zum Maſ⸗ van Ginger*; the end is unclear, perhaps a place or a person. To be checked against the image.
6. The transcription reads *Heute wurde die Winnower am Gewände fertig*; the normalised column keeps it. *Winnower* is not a German word. The next sentence suggests that the foundations were finished. To be checked against the image.
7. *Capitain*: the leader of a Khoekhoe community, a title recognised by the Dutch colonial government.
