P.A.I. R.5.E. 6. [1]
Diary of the 3 Brethren at the Cape. 1795–96.

<!-- lines 00009-00011 -->
Diaries of the 3 Brethren at the Cape, from 1 to 31 March 1795 [2].

<!-- lines 00012-00020 -->
On 5 March [3] the shoemaker Johs came to the house. He is a German and lives 4 miles from here with another German named Rudolf, who is known in Neudietendorf [4]. He was born in Saxe-Gotha, spent 14 days in Neudietendorf and wanted to become a carpenter; he went away from there and became a shoemaker. He has now been here 10 years. He said he regretted it when he thought that he had not stayed there.

<!-- lines 00020-00048 -->
Afterwards Juffrouw Teunis [5] came too, with her sister and her children. We were much surprised at this visit, because at present she is so very hostile. They all attended the meeting [6], in which Brother Marsveld spoke on Matthew 15, verses 21 to 28. Afterwards Juffrouw Teunis went into the kitchen and began an unbelievable quarrel with our Sister Martha. The cause was this. Martha had lately been with her at the post, and one of her slaves said to Martha that in the house we were often talked about in a dreadful way and very badly spoken of. Martha told us this, and Juffrouw Teunis in turn came to hear of it. She was so angry about it that she said Martha should have told this not to us but to her; then she would have punished the slave woman for it. But Juffrouw Teunis denied everything she is supposed to have said about us, and said it was all lies. She is a woman who flatly denies tomorrow what she says today: no, no and no. This, as she said, was the reason for her coming, to have it out properly for once. As soon as she had finished, she drove straight off to her place and did not stay to eat.

<!-- lines 00048-00072 -->
The shoemaker Johns drove off in the afternoon. Towards evening we saw him and the other man, and the boy he had with him, coming back. The reason was this. It had rained heavily in the mountains in these days, so the Sonderend River had risen high and the oxen had to swim. For this they had taken a Hottentot with them, who swam beside the oxen and guided them. At this point the three of them went over a tree that lies across the river, to look for another Hottentot. The slave who was driving thought the oxen might as well just swim and drove them in. But they came somewhat too far downstream and got into the [gap] with the wagon [7], so that they were stuck fast. So they had to cut all the oxen loose, if they were not to drown. Then the oxen swam out; but they had to leave the wagon stuck there for the night and fetch more people to help. They stayed there for the night and left again the next day, when they heard that the wagon was out.

<!-- lines 00073-00108 -->
Late in the evening Juffrouw Teunis sent to ask that someone [8] might come to her early in the morning. (She had stayed the night at her place.) Brother Kühnel rode to the house on the 2nd. Her complaint was that the Hottentots' cattle had quite ruined the garden: 35 head had got into it that night, and she had had them driven into the kraal. When she asked what she should do, Brother Kühnel answered that we could not help it; she must do what she thought best. While she was talking to me about this [9], 30 head of the Hottentots' cattle came towards the garden. "There, you see now," she said, "what am I to do? There is nobody with them. I will have the whole lot driven to the post, and then they can see what my husband will do with them." Brother Kühnel replied that she should act in the matter as she wished. So she had her slave woman drive the cattle together and take them to the post. I told her that we do not fail to admonish the Hottentots to take better care of their cattle, but that it was hard to bring them to order. She said: "I cannot imagine that the Hottentots would not do what they are told. I have often seen and heard it myself: when they are told to do this or that, they say, 'We do not do that any more, for the gentlemen (or our teacher) have said it is not good and we should not do it any more'; or, 'We will first ask our teacher whether it is good'; and so they will not do it if you forbid it them. Should they not do this too, and take better care of their cattle?"

<!-- lines 00109-00123 -->
On the 3rd Brother Schwinn spoke on John 12:32, and Brother Kühnel held the singing meeting in front of the house. On the 4th at midday, while we were eating, a Hottentot came and said that a man who was making a ditch round his garden had just fainted. Brother Marsveld went with him at once, but it turned out that he was dead. In the middle of his digging [10] he had said, "I don't know, I feel so queer," and was going to leave off; but when he had gone a few steps he fell down and gave no further sign of life. In the evening Brother Kühnel read the 2nd chapter of the Acts of the Apostles. On the 5th the man who had died the day before was buried. In the evening Brother Kühnel spoke on John 13:20.

<!-- lines 00123-143 -->
On the 6th our Sister Susanna came home again. She had been to the Landdrost at Stellenbosch to complain about a farmer named Stoffel Grunewald, in whose service her son is and whom she wanted back; but the farmer would not let him go with her. Her son had hired himself out to the farmer for a few weeks to herd oxen, and because they got into the corn and did damage, he was to serve a whole year more for it. That year is now over, and in that time one of the farmer's horses died. The boy was to blame for that, [the farmer says]; if he had been watchful it would not have happened, and the boy is to work off this damage. In this way a Hottentot never gets free of the farmer as long as he lives. The Landdrost gave her a letter to take to the farmer. In the evening Brother Schwinn read the 3rd chapter of Acts and afterwards held the singing meeting in front of our house.

<!-- lines 143-156 -->
On the evening of the 7th we partook of the body and blood of our Lord in Holy Communion. Our 2 sisters Anna Maria and Rahel were both able to partake with us. Anna Maria has been ill for some time, and we thought she would not be able to be there. She said, "I long very much for this high good." And the dear Saviour strengthened her so that she was able to come. On the 8th Brother Marsveld spoke on Psalm 107, verses 3 to 8. Afterwards 2 farmers came looking for Hottentots to help them cart manure. Brother Kühnel was also called to our Sister Catharina, who is ill too. She lives a short hour from here.

<!-- lines 156-185 -->
On the 9th 2 farmers came, named Jan Molman and Stoffel Grunewald. The first lives about 8 miles from here and the second 3 miles. Molman wanted to buy [11] a Hottentot to whom he had lent money once when he wanted a proper drink. The Hottentot had promised to work it off. He was with him for a while, then asked leave to visit his mother, who is ill; he did so and did not go back to the farmer. Then Grunewald, who looks as though he could not count to three [12], began, and said that Baas Teunis had issued an order that a farmer would be free, if his Hottentot is not obedient to him, to go into the kraals and beat the Hottentot to life and death. He said, "One of them is in my debt too, and when I thresh [13] he must come. If he does not come, I shall come with a man and do this." We said we knew nothing of the farmers having such freedom. They replied that it had not been so before, but that at present it was. We could do nothing about it but keep quiet, since it was ordered by the authorities; for we must take great care never to say anything against the authorities. But think [14], dear Brethren and Sisters, how this must distress us. We pray and call upon God for help and mercy for this people.

<!-- lines 186-194 -->
On the 10th Brother Schwinn spoke on Mark 16:15, and Brother Kühnel held the singing meeting outside in front of the house. On the 11th we heard from several people that the farmers [15] and Hottentots had to go to the Cape again to stand guard, and that things looked very sad in the fatherland [16]. In the evening Brother Kühnel read the 4th chapter of Acts.

<!-- lines 195-204 -->
On the 12th 2 Hottentots fetched back their cattle, which Juffrouw Teunis had lately had driven away. They had to pay 2 shillings a head. Baas Teunis sent word to the others that they should fetch theirs too. But they might not drive them here any more, but to such places where they would be safe. The first 2 belong here, and they might bring theirs back here; but he would watch the others, that they did not come there again. In the evening Brother Kühnel spoke on the Gospel of John 11:25 and 26.

<!-- lines 205-218 -->
On the 13th 2 Hottentots came who live on the Olifants River, 14 days' journey on foot. A farmer had driven them away from there and taken their cattle. They had been to the Landdrost about it, but he said he could not help here. Now they had been at the Cape with the Fiscal Reinveld [17], who gave them a letter to take with them. On the 14th Brother Kühnel was called to our Sister Anna Maria, who was very weak. He found her in a gladdening state of heart and wholly resigned to the will of the dear Saviour. On Sunday the 15th Brother Marsveld spoke on Mark 14, verses 3 to 9.

<!-- lines 219-263 -->
On the 16th we again had to have a kraal made for our goats and billy goats on the Schersand River [18], because this side of the Sonderend River is said to be unhealthy for them: they get the lame sickness and the brand sickness [19], of which most of them die. We are not fortunate with our livestock, for a great many of them die on us. It seems we are not meant to become farmers. It goes the same with the oxen: we have already had to slaughter 3 that were sick. We would gladly do away with all the livestock if we only saw a way of managing without it; but that is not possible. We must have it, especially oxen, for how else should we get firewood, corn or flour, for example? Flour is a hard matter, for far and wide in this district there is no mill that can grind in summer. There is indeed one about 1½ miles away, but because there is no other for so many miles around, everything comes together there. We have had our corn there for 4 days already and still do not know when it will come back. Meanwhile one has to give 2 people board and wages all that time, for the sake of a little flour; if one does not leave them there, no one gives the flour a thought. In the rainy season this mill has little to do, because most farmers have mills of their own and grind for themselves, but for no one else; and as soon as the rainy season is over the grinding stops too. We ought now to lay in flour, for in the rainy season we cannot cross the river. The lack of water is to blame for there being so few mills that grind in summer. We could free ourselves from this trouble if we knew what ground we are living on [20], for we have water enough for a mill even in the dry season, which is a precious thing in this country. From our 5 cows we have not had a drop of milk for our coffee for a long time, and we have no prospect of when we shall get any.

<!-- lines 264-278 -->
On the 17th Brother Schwinn spoke on Matthew 5:11 and 12, and Brother Kühnel held the singing meeting. On the 18th Brother Schwinn had his 46th birthday. 4 of our sisters came at daybreak [21] to wake him with some verses, and we played the flute with them. Afterwards the others came too, to wish him happiness. Today there was also the speaking with the candidates [22]. For several of them we wished more life in their hearts; but of the greater part it can be said that their whole mind is to live for the dear Saviour in this world. In the evening Brother Kühnel read the 6th chapter of Acts. On the 19th Brother Kühnel spoke on Matthew 10:37 and 38. On the 20th Brother Schwinn read the 7th chapter of Acts; before that he held the singing meeting.

<!-- lines 279-289 -->
On the 21st, in the conference, we laid before our dear Lord a 3rd candidate for baptism, whom he also approved [23]. Today some people came from Baas Teunis, from whom we heard that what the farmer Grunewald had said on the 9th of this month about the order was lies: namely, that they had permission, if the Hottentots would not obey them, to go into the kraals and beat them even if they died of it, and to fetch them away from there.

<!-- lines 290-301 -->
On the 22nd Brother Marsveld spoke on John 8:46 to 52. Brother Schwinn was also called to a sick candidate for baptism, whose stripping-bare [24] gave him joy. On the 23rd a farmer named Klerk came, who lives close to Swellendam. He had come here expressly, hoping to hire some Hottentots for a few weeks, but got none. He stayed the night here. He was a Jew and was baptised here only 2 years ago. He has lived in the country 50 years. His wife was a slave but is now free and also baptised [25].

<!-- lines 302-314 -->
On the 24th we had a fine rain, for which everything had been longing, for there was a great drought: it had not rained for 2 months and had been very hot besides. The cattle were already short of fodder. This drought is said to be everywhere, as one hears. In the evening Brother Schwinn spoke on Revelation 3:13. Since tomorrow is the festival of all the choirs [26], and we join the congregation in spirit, we had set the baptism for that day. We announced in the meeting that there would be a general meeting at 10 o'clock tomorrow. The singing meeting was dropped because of the rainy weather.

<!-- lines 314-323 -->
On the 25th the people came in very great numbers. Brother Schwinn performed the baptism. She was given the name Louise. The peace of God made itself powerfully felt during this act. We must say in general that the Spirit of God shows himself powerfully in the hearts in all the meetings; but an act like this is always especially marked.

<!-- lines 323-353 -->
This day was a day of special blessing both for us and for the people; but our joy was stifled, because afterwards we heard of very sad circumstances. Several women came who had been with the farmers for some months, and said that they had behaved badly in that time, and asked forgiveness. Some said that they had drunk too much and then fought with one another. Others, that they had committed fornication with this man and that. Others again: "He promised to take me as his wife, and so I let him come upon my body; now that he has been upon my body, he has left me." We had to exclude them from school and instruction, and told them: if they wanted to serve the devil and remain children of the devil, they should go. It would be better for them not to hear the word of God, for they would not accept God's great grace, but trampled it underfoot. It is sad when they are with the farmers so long, for there they get drink and hear nothing but cursing and scolding. When they are here one can often see the powerful work of the Holy Spirit in them; but when they are with the farmers again for a time, many lose everything again.

<!-- lines 354-366 -->
On the 26th Brother Kühnel went into the bush with 7 Hottentots to cut wood, 5 hours from here. We have resolved to build another small room onto our little house, because we are at our wits' end, it is so cramped. Brother Kühnel also means to make all kinds of implements from the wood. The reason he went with them is this: if one sets the Hottentots to work in the field alone and none of us is there, they do not do much, but spend most of the time smoking tobacco and sleeping. So if one wants them to work diligently, one must be there. In the evening Brother Marsveld spoke on John 9:4 and 5.

<!-- lines 367-384 -->
On the 27th we received the goods we had ordered in January. We also slaughtered a cow that was sick. In the evening Brother Schwinn read the 8th chapter of Acts and also held the singing meeting in front of the house. On the 28th we received a letter from Baas Teunis. This time he wrote in so friendly a way as he has never written before. We thought this must mean something. In the evening Brother Kühnel came back from the bush with the Hottentots. Because of the heavy rain they have not yet been able to cut enough, so we must send a few there once more. On the 29th Brother Marsveld spoke on Philippians 2, verses 6 to 9. On the 30th we sent people into the bush again to cut some more wood. On the 31st we bought a pig to fatten.

<!-- lines 385-391 -->
In the conference we resolved to read something from the story of the Passion in the evening meeting. We have divided it into 4 parts, to read one each evening, and today, after Brother Schwinn had offered a prayer, we made the beginning. Brother Kühnel held the singing meeting, which this time had to be held in the house because of the wind.

<!-- lines 392-408 -->
Now with this we will again close our diary of one month, and commend ourselves and our brothers and sisters to the prayers and remembrance of all the congregations, asking them to pray with us to the dear Saviour that he may continue, through his Holy Spirit, to convince people of their misery, so that they may all seek him who also cried out for them on the cross: "It is finished." Few days have passed this month on which some did not come to us and speak with us about the unrest of their hearts (on some days many), and testify to their great longing to be saved too. We are all 3 still well and live in love and peace, and remain your 3 poor brothers:
Hendrik Marsveld.
Daniel Schwinn.
Joh. Chr. Kühnel.

## Notes

1. *P.A.I. R.5.E. 6.*: an archive shelf mark on the title page, left untranslated. The same mark is mentioned in the notes to A363597.
2. The title page has *Diarium der 3 Brüder / Auf dem Kaap. / 1795-96.*; the normalised column has *Tagebuch*. The heading on page 2 has *Diaria* ("diaries"). This entry is the first of two copies of the March 1795 diary in this file; the second, fuller copy is entry 02. The two differ in many details (names, numbers, whole sentences); the main differences are listed in the notes to entry 02.
3. The transcription reads *Den 5te Merz hauß*. The next dated day is "the 2nd", and the second copy (entry 02, line 414) begins *d. 1 Merz*, so *5te* is probably a misreading of *1te*. *hauß* is unclear; the normalised column reads it as "ins Haus" ("to the house"). To be checked against the image.
4. Neudietendorf: a Moravian settlement in Thuringia, in the duchy of Saxe-Gotha. In the second copy the visitor is *der Schulmeister Lohr*, "the schoolmaster Lohr", not "the shoemaker Johs", and he is said to have become a schoolmaster. To be checked against the image.
5. *Juffr. Teunis*: Juffrouw (Dutch, "Mrs"), the wife of Baas Teunis. The couple lived at "the post" near the mission.
6. The transcription has *das Vorkomst*; the normalised column has "Gottesdienst" and the second copy has *Versammlung*, "meeting". *Vorkomst* is probably a misreading. To be checked against the image.
7. The transcription has a gap here: *geriethen in die [ ] mit dem Wagen*. A word is missing (perhaps "depth" or "current"). To be checked against the image.
8. The transcription reads *niemd*, "no one", which makes no sense with what follows; the second copy has *einer von uns*, "one of us". Probably a misreading of *iemd*, "someone". *morgenhruß* is read as *morgens früh*, "early in the morning".
9. "With me": the writer here is evidently Brother Kühnel, although the passage speaks of him in the third person. The second copy has "with Brother Kühnel".
10. The transcription reads *im beſten Wo graben*; the normalised column's *im besten Wortsinn grabend* is a guess. Read here as *im besten Graben*, "in the middle of digging". The second copy has *mehrmals vom Graben*. To be checked against the image.
11. *kaufen*, "buy", in both columns. The second copy has *sprechen*, "speak to". "Buy" may be a misreading (for example of *suchen*, "look for"). To be checked against the image.
12. *welcher aufsicht als kömme er nicht 3 zahlen*: read as "who looks as though he could not count to three", as in the second copy (*als könnte er nicht auf drei zählen*). The normalised column's *als käme er nicht zu zahlen* is a misreading.
13. *wen ich werde ſchlagen*: *schlagen* here is taken in its farming sense, "to thresh" (compare *Korn ausschlagen*, entry 13). The second copy has *pflügen*, "plough". To be checked against the image.
14. The transcription reads *Sanft aber*, which makes no sense here. Probably a misreading of *Denkt aber*, "but think"; compare *Aber denkt, liebe Geschwister!* in entry 06.
15. The transcription reads *die druren*; the second copy has *die Bauern*, "the farmers". Probably a misreading of *Bauren*.
16. "The fatherland": the Netherlands, then being overrun by the French (see entry 06). The second copy has *unser Land*, "our country".
17. *Fiſcaal Reinveld*: probably Willem Stephanus van Ryneveld, the Fiscal (chief law officer) at the Cape.
18. *Scherſand Rivier*; the second copy has *Serschond Revier*. Not identified. To be checked against the image.
19. *Lam u brand=Seuche*: Cape Dutch *lamziekte* (a paralysing disease of livestock) and *brandziekte* (scab). The transcription has *ungeheud*, read in the normalised column as *ungesund*, "unhealthy".
20. The transcription reads *wen wir wüßten auch was fürs einem Freunde boden wir wohnen*; the normalised column has "on what friendly ground". The sense is probably "whose ground" or "on what terms we hold the land": the brethren could build their own mill if the land were secured to them. The second copy is garbled here. To be checked against the image.
21. The transcription reads *mit Anwuſch des Seges*; the second copy has *mit Anbruch des Tages*, "at daybreak", which is followed here.
22. The candidates for baptism; the second copy has *das Sprechen der Taufkandidaten*.
23. "Approved": the missionaries decided admissions by lot, as was Moravian practice, and read the result as the Lord's decision. The second copy has "our dear Lord approved three persons as candidates for baptism".
24. *Entblößung*: in Moravian usage, being stripped of all trust in oneself and seeing oneself as a poor sinner. The second copy has *zu einem Unkeuschen gerufen, über dessen Seelenlösung*, probably garbled.
25. The second copy names this farmer *Heiziger*, calls him *ein geborener Heide* ("born a heathen") and says he has lived 10 years in the country. To be checked against the image.
26. *das Fest aller Chöre*: a festival shared by all the choirs, the groups by age, sex and marital state into which Moravian congregations were divided.
