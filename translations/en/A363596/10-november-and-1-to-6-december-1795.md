<!-- lines 2719-2735 -->
On 1 November 3 persons were baptised into the death of Jesus: Margaretha, Alida and Esther. We saw a wagon coming towards us across the river, but the water had now risen so high that no one could get through, and the wagon had to turn back. We supposed that it was the English officers who had sent word of their coming. [...] [1]. This was confirmed on the 3rd. At this time we had so much rainy weather that we had to suspend the school. For it has to be held under the trees, because in the house it is utterly impossible for lack of room.

<!-- lines 2735-2747 -->
On 13 November [2] we kept [the day] with, among other things, a lovefeast with our baptised, and Johanna, one of the 4 excluded, was readmitted. On this occasion we also remembered our dear congregations in the fatherland and other mission stations [3], and told our people many things about them. On such occasions the wish often comes to us that our dear brethren in Holland would translate for us something from the accounts of other mission stations, for the use of our little congregation; for we cannot do it ourselves.

<!-- lines 2747-2768 -->
On the 16th the husband of our Margaretha [was bitten] [4] by a poisonous little creature about as thick as a thumb, so that he had to lie down and became almost speechless. Some Hottentots knew very good remedies against such a bite, and someone happened to be at hand. There is a small kind of snake here, hardly as long as a pipe, whose bite is mostly fatal. It is surely a special protection of God that we have not yet come to harm in this way. For it has happened several times, when we have picked something up from the ground or were about to, that the Hottentots, who from their youth pay attention to such things and in general see very sharply, have called out to us: "There is a snake!", which we might not have noticed. There is also a small kind of spider whose bite is fatal unless the proper remedy is used on the spot.

<!-- lines 2768-2788 -->
On the 22nd we were visited by Mr Claude, who has bought the place near us, Mr Hofman and Andreas Otto. They attended the Sunday meeting and were astonished at the great number of people. Messrs Claude and Hofmann said that it was a quite [necessary] [5] thing here to build a large meeting house. We ourselves had often thought about this again, and would gladly have heard the advice of our dear brethren of the Unity Elders' Conference in the matter; but since we can hope for no letters at present, and the season has come when something must be done about the roof [6] if it is not to lie over a whole year, we resolved in a conference on the 28th that Brother Marsveld should travel to the Cape the next day to seek the necessary permission from the General.

<!-- lines 2788-2817 -->
He came back on 6 December and reports of his journey as follows: "On 1 December I went to General Craig and asked permission to fell building timber for a meeting house. He answered that I must speak to the Landdrost about it, and the Landdrost must write to him in the matter. I went to the Landdrost of Stellenbosch, who received me kindly and promised to give me a letter. We received this on the 7th [7], and the General received it very favourably. I spoke with him of the many missions we have under English rule, which enjoy every protection from you [8]. He answered that we should go on confidently in everything, and if anything were put in our way we should let him know. On the 3rd I received from him the written permission to have building timber felled for our meeting house, with the remarkable watchword for the day: 'Thy servants would gladly see Zion built, and would gladly see her stones and lime made ready' [9]; whereupon on the 4th I set out happily on the return journey, and on the 6th I rejoined my dear brothers."

<!-- lines 2818-2831 -->
At the close of this diary our Hottentot congregation consists of:
8 communicants
3 candidates [for Communion] [10]
18 baptised adults
4 baptised children
29 candidates for baptism
Total 62 persons, all of whom, together with the rest of the great multitude and ourselves, we commend to the faithful prayers and remembrance of all the brothers and sisters.
Hendrik Marsveld
Daniel Schwinn
Joh. Chr. Kühnel.

6. The transcription reads *da etwas in dem Dache geſchehen muß*; the building had not yet begun, so *Dache*, "roof", may be a misreading (perhaps of *Sache*, "matter"). To be checked against the image.
7. "The 7th" cannot be right, since Brother Marsveld had the General's written permission on the 3rd and was home on the 6th. Probably *2t*. To be checked against the image.
