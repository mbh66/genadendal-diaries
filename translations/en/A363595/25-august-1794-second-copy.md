Diary of the 3 Brothers Hendr. Marsveld, Dan. Schwinn and Joh. Christ. Kühnel at the Cape, from 1 to the last of August 1794 [1].

<!-- lines 9576-9590 -->
On 3 August [2] we buried [an old woman]. In the evening at the singing meeting it was so full that the people could not all get into the house. If our Lord would give us the joy of soon having a church or a meeting house of our own, we would be very thankful to him for it. On the 4th [a woman] [3] came and complained of great unrest in her heart. A couple of times already, [on the bench] where she had been sitting in the meeting, she had not been able to bear it for anguish, but had had to sit down on the ground. On Sunday the [3rd] the meeting was well attended. Until now we have read the Psalms [4] of David publicly on Mondays and Fridays. Since they are finished, we resolved to read out the 4 Evangelists, and a beginning was made with it on the 4th. The Saviour was present at this reading in a quite special way.

<!-- lines 9590-9604 -->
On the [5th] [5] we slaughtered our last pig to get fat, of which we are often very short. The Hottentots all know well how to go about this work. [There are no butchers] in this country [6]: every farmer has his livestock slaughtered by slaves or Hottentots. For everything they slaughter, be it pig, ox or sheep, they need no other tool than a knife. They are afraid of a pig, and so make much preparation before it comes to the slaughtering. But once it has been stabbed to death, which is done on the ground, they leave it lying until it is scalded [...] [7]. On the [6th] was the speaking of the candidates for baptism, over whom we could rejoice heartily.

<!-- lines 9604-9643 -->
Yesterday evening our Hanna [8] had come home again. She has been with Baas Teunis's son at the stranded ship for several months. In this time we heard much that was bad about her, and have often been very perplexed about it in silence, [since she is not so simple a Hottentot as the others] [9]. Since many people had come to the ship, she was glad not only to put herself on a level with them in many bad things, but [is said to have said]: "I am as good as you, and better still." [...] [10] Yesterday, as soon as she came home, she came to us to greet us. We kept silent and waited to see whether she would tell of her own accord how she had fared since. But she said nothing. When we asked her about it this afternoon, she answered: "Good and bad." We told her what had been reported to us about her. But she would admit nothing, and said this had only been said of her out of envy. We reminded her of what she had promised Jesus Christ at her baptism, and had given us her hand on. We also asked her whether she dared declare before the face of the Saviour what [was] the truth about what we had now put to her [11]; she must consider it well: if she lied to us, she would not escape punishment. She fell silent and would say no more. We advised her to go away and think the matter over earnestly. Besides, she was told that she might not come to the meeting of the baptised until we first knew where we stood with her. This meeting was today, and she had [said] she would come again.

<!-- lines 9643-9664 -->
On the 7th it rained heavily, which was very welcome to us. The corn could not come up for drought. It seemed that we should still get something of a winter in the way of this country. In the evening, [in spite of] [12] the very bad weather, so many people came to the meeting that the house could not hold them all. How it will go with us this year with the oxen (because they are still thin) and the sheep, we cannot imagine. We have to hold the schools as well as the meetings in our living room, for lack of space, and it is now often so full that the Hottentots sit so close to us that we [could] warm ourselves at their breath. Even now we are richly endowed with these [pests] [13]. How will it go when it grows warm, when they are especially [bad]? Today we had to send a girl out of the school because she carried more than 1000 in her skin or kaross. If they [were not so lazy] [14], they could soon be rid of the vermin if they beat [the skins] out well with a stick every day and shook the lice out of the covers. [...]

<!-- lines 9664-9682 -->
On 8 August the [school children] carried small stones [15] for [a building] which we mean to put up for Brother Kühnel as a workshop when the rainy season is over. The men have already brought a considerable quantity of large stones. A deep foundation must be dug because the ground is sandy. We mean to put a loft on the house to [store] corn, for which we lack space. Since the children had helped diligently with this work, we gave each of them a [...] [16]. It is a pleasure to see how they eat it. They all sit together. The big ones beat off the rind with stones, the little ones peel it with their teeth, and then comes the eating; and with what appetite and pleasure this happens, no one can imagine.

<!-- lines 9682-9696 -->
On the 9th an old man [17] was buried who had long been blind. Our sister [18] Eva also came and accused herself of having fallen into a quarrel with her husband, in which she had said words against him, and she was now troubled about it. Her husband is our Brother Johannes. On the 10th our sister Maria came and asked us to make clear to her what had been said in the meeting yesterday, because she had not quite understood it. It had been said, namely, that one must not only confess the Saviour's love with the mouth, but must also feel it in the heart and be convinced of it.

<!-- lines 9696-9723 -->
On the 11th our communicants were told that next Wednesday [we had a festival in our congregations] [19], and that Holy Communion [20] would be held on that day, so we meant to keep it with them too. This time no separate speaking was held, but whoever wanted to talk with us beforehand should visit us tomorrow. We asked them heartily to examine the state of their hearts thoroughly before the Saviour and to talk it out with him. On the 12th all 4 communicants came and were very content [21], as they have never been before. Two of them said that they were very troubled that so many bad thoughts still came to them. We [directed] them to the Saviour as well as we could. Our sister Hanna asked us for forgiveness for having grieved us. [But she will not admit her sin] [22], and so we could not forgive her either. On the 13th we held the festival morning with our baptised, in which [the Saviour] let [himself] be powerfully felt in the hearts of all present. At 10 o'clock we read the address which Brother Reichel gave to the congregation at Herrnhut on 13 August 1727 [23]. In the evening we held Holy Communion with our 4 sisters. That was a day of blessing for us and our baptised.

<!-- lines 9723-9744 -->
On the 14th a man and a woman visited us. The woman complained of the distress of her heart. The man said that for a long time he had not known where to turn for anguish of heart, but now he felt the peace of God within him and was very content. It was our sister Anna's husband. Since we had so often considered together what we should do about the great need of the people, since many come to us and ask for bread, flour and the like, which we have also given them, on the 15th we resolved to put a stop to it, because there are too many beggars. Some bring presents, which are very welcome to us, such as fresh meat; often it is something we cannot eat, but we must accept it so as not to shame them. If one wants to give them something for it at once, they do not accept it. [Afterwards one forgets] [24] it when they come and ask for this and that, which has often put us in a quandary.

<!-- lines 9744-9767 -->
On the 16th Brother Kühnel rode to Mr Teunis to speak with him about building our [house] [25] and to get his advice on how we could meet the cost of this building most cheaply. He returned in the evening. This afternoon we had a visit from the overseer of the warm bath and another man from the Cape, who is at the Castle there. They stayed overnight so that they could attend the Sunday meetings tomorrow. On the 17th they went away again. The man from the Cape said: "I am amazed at the stillness, reverence and devotion of so great a crowd of people. When I heard of it from others, I would not and could not believe it. At the sight of these people, with the tears rolling down their cheeks, I could not hold back my own, but had to let them flow freely. We Christians ought to be ashamed before them." Today we also remembered [the little girls in our congregations], who keep their [choir festival], and commended them to the Lord with the prayer that he would [bless them] [26].

<!-- lines 9767-9792 -->
On the 18th 2 girls came and told us their dreams. One said that she had dreamed the night before that the devil had her and wanted her, which troubled her greatly. She was told that if she asked the Saviour for the forgiveness of her sins and became a child of God, the devil should not get her. We advised her, when she lay down to sleep, to ask the Saviour to fill her heart with the remembrance of his torment and death. Today when she came to school she wept. After a while she said that again she had not slept the whole night, but had thought of our dear Lord, and that her mind was to become a child of God and to live wholly for Jesus Christ. On the 19th our Hanna came and asked that we would take her back. She said that what had been said of her was not so bad; but she would admit nothing. She has also been to Baas Teunis and complained of her plight to him. He let us know through Bedgen that she had not behaved so badly as we had been told, and that we should take her back. But that cannot help us, for she herself still will admit nothing, and [become a sinner] again [27]. On the 20th 2 more children asked leave to come to school. In 8 days 8 children have come to us for this purpose. Our school has grown so strong that it is attended [constantly] [28] by more than 50 children.

<!-- lines 9793-9818 -->
On the 21st Chr. Kune [29] sent us a letter to read which he had received from Martinus Schmidt at the Cape. He asked him to fetch him with the ox wagon on 4 September below the Kloof; he would come that far with his horse wagon and be there on the 4th. This place is 2 days' journey from here. One cannot pass the Kloof with a horse wagon, because it is very steep; so it must be done with oxen. Martinus Schmidt had written [in an enclosure] that he meant to visit us with his wife in September, which makes us very glad, for this dear man and his wife have been parents to us. On the 22nd our Maria came and told us that she had dreamed that a man came to her and said to her that she should partake of Holy Communion with us on the Thursday. She asked what this dream meant. We told her that one cannot rely on dreams. She is a candidate for Communion. On the 23rd we were busy setting vines round our house. We have to fence all of them with thorns, or else the hens eat them off. The 24th was a day of heavy rain; yet so many came to the meeting that the house and the front kitchen could not hold the people.

<!-- lines 9818-9830 -->
On the 25th a Hottentot came and accused himself: he had been at a farmer's and had got drunk there; he was very troubled about it and asked for forgiveness. [On the 26th] Baas Teunis's son [sent] from the post, where he had been living for a time with his wife and child at his parents', a letter to us by an express rider on horseback, [and we] received the news that his child had died the night before [30]. He asked that we would do him the last honour and help accompany it to the grave tomorrow morning at 11 o'clock. Early on the 27th Brother Marsveld and Brother Schwinn rode there; Brother Kühnel stayed [at home] [31]

<!-- lines 9832-9869 -->
[...] one [woman came] [31], wept, and said that she was very troubled. Question: why? Answer: because Jesus Christ had had to bear and suffer so much for the sake of our sins, and nails had been driven through his hands and feet; and she too would gladly be saved. She was told that all this had happened for her. Late in the evening a young man came, trembling and quaking and weeping. He shook so that he could not keep a limb still. He said: "Since the meeting I have been so afraid that I do not know where to hide, for I believe I must be lost. Oh, what shall I do?" The mercy for sinners was commended to him, and he was directed to Jesus, who died for sinners. The strong fire that used to burn seemed to be growing somewhat dim [32], and for this reason we have asked the dear Saviour several times to awaken the hearts through his Holy Spirit and to bring new life among them. For some time now there has been a new life among them, and the Spirit of God is working mightily on the hearts to glorify [33] the torment and death of Jesus, so that hardly a day passes [without people coming] to speak with us, and they usually say that they are so troubled that Jesus had to suffer and bear so much for mankind. With this we close our diary, and commend ourselves and the Hottentots to the prayers and intercession of all our dear brothers and sisters in the congregations, and remain your devoted 3 brothers, Hendr. Marsveld, Dan. Schwinn, Joh. Chr. Kühnel.

## Notes

1. This is the second copy of the August 1794 diary; the first is entry 24 (lines 9283 to 9570), which breaks off on the 22nd. The transcription of this copy is poor in many places, and several dates differ from the first copy; the notes give the first copy's reading where it settles a passage. From the 23rd on, and after the gap noted at line 9830, this copy is the only text.
2. The first copy has 1 August and "a woman was buried". The transcription here reads *wurden wir einen Eulen begraben*, "we buried an owl"; *Eulen* is probably a misreading, perhaps of *eine Alte*, "an old woman". The normalised column has *ein Eule*.
3. The transcription reads *Am 4 kam neune Sada*; the first copy has "On the 2nd a woman came". *neune Sada* is not clear; it may be a name. In the next sentence the transcription reads *auf der Baas*, where the first copy has *auf der Bank*, "on the bench". To be checked against the image.
4. The transcription reads *die Paſſion Davids*, "the Passion of David"; the first copy has *die reihe der Pſalmen Davids*, "the series of the Psalms of David". The transcription also has *Sontags den 6*, but 6 August 1794 was a Wednesday; the first copy has Sunday the 3rd, which is right.
5. The transcription has *d 8*, and the speaking of the candidates below is dated *d 9*; the first copy has the 5th and the 6th. The 8th and 9th of this copy come later in the month, so these are probably misreadings of *5* and *6*.
6. The transcription reads *Eigne Kühe Ochſen giebt es hier im Lande nicht: gute Baas läßt ſein Ohes ...*, "there are no own cows oxen in this country: good master has his [?]". The first copy has "for there are no butchers here; every farmer has all his livestock slaughtered", and the translation follows it.
7. The rest of the sentence is garbled in the transcription: *biß weiches gebrüht u die Haare in der ſcheinend neuen Maſſer als alles dies geſtehen giebt*. The first copy has "and there it is scalded, then scraped with the knife, and that goes well". To be checked against the image.
8. The transcription reads *unſer Mana Hanna*; *Mana* may be a first attempt at the name. On Hanna and the stranded ship, see entry 21, note 10.
9. The transcription reads *u in ihr keinen ſo dummen Hottentotten, wie die Andern ſind, Ran zu ſcheinken*. The first copy has "She is not so simple a Hottentot woman as the others; she can speak well", and the translation follows its sense.
10. A passage here is garbled beyond translation: *Man gab ihr Geſezt, daß ſie deswegen gründlich haben was ſie betrübter geweſen, u dieſes ſoll Sklav vorgekommen ſeyn*. The first copy has "She is also said to have talked filth when she was drunk, and that she is said to have been more than once"; *Sklav* may be a misreading of *öfters*, "often". To be checked against the image.
11. The transcription reads *ob ſie diejenige was wir ihr jeizo vorſtellen hätten auch vor dem Angeſicht des Heilands für Wahrheit erklären dürfe*, "whether she dared declare before the face of the Saviour for truth what we had now put to her". The first copy asks whether she could say before the face of Jesus that it was lies. The sense of both is a challenge to deny it on oath before the Saviour.
12. The transcription reads *Abends wurde im Gnadenthal das ſchlimmſte Wetter*, "in the evening was the worst weather in Gnadenthal". The mission was not called Gnadenthal (Genadendal) until 1806; *im Gnadenthal* is almost certainly a misreading, perhaps of *ungeachtet*, "in spite of". The first copy has "although the weather was very unpleasant". To be checked against the image.
13. The transcription reads *mit dieſen Schmerzen begabt*, "endowed with these pains", and then *wo ſie beſonders rauch ſind*. The first copy speaks of lice throughout, and has "for then they are very bad".
14. From *Wann ſie dumm* to the end of the paragraph the transcription is garbled: *Wann ſie dumm, wo ſie ſollen lernen, nicht ſo fort, ſo kontinurirlich Ungeziefer bald los werden, wen ſie täglich dieſelbe mit dem Stock gut ausklopfen haben u kehren, dreſch die Läuſe aus den Decken. Einer unerſchrocken und dieſe ſcheuen ſie.* The translation follows the sense of the first copy, which ends "and they are no lovers of work". The girl's *Fall oder Loos* is the first copy's *Caroſs (oder Fell)*. To be checked against the image.
15. The transcription reads *d 8 Aug. war die Schule beſſer als ſie kleine Steine trugen*; see entry 24, note 11.
16. The transcription reads *einen Kwaſchtrank*; the first copy has *einen Broutſtück*, "a piece of bread". The description of the rind (*Baſt*) suggests a fruit or pumpkin. See entry 24, note 13. To be checked against the image.
17. The transcription reads *alles Man*; the first copy has *ein alter Mann*.
18. The transcription reads *unſer Schenker Eva*; the first copy has *unſere Schweſter Eva*.
19. The transcription reads *daß wir nächſten Mittwoche in unſerem Bau vorgenommen am Tiſch hätten*, "that next Wednesday in our building [we] had undertaken at the table"; the first copy has "that on Wednesday we have a festival in our congregations, with Holy Communion on it". 13 August 1794 was a Wednesday.
20. The transcription has *das H. Ambt*, "the holy office", here and on the 13th and the 22nd; probably a misreading of the abbreviation *H. AMhl* for *heiliges Abendmahl*, which the first copy of July has.
21. The transcription reads *vergnügt*, "content"; the first copy has *offenherzig*, "open-hearted".
22. The transcription reads *Sie ich will nicht ſündigen*, "she I will not sin". The first copy has "But she is not yet [...] again", and on the 19th "will not yet become a sinner".

25. The transcription reads *wegen des Baus unſers H. Abendmahls*, "about the building of our Holy Communion"; the first copy has *wegen unſres Haußbau*, "about our house building".

The first copy has "We also remembered the little girls in our congregations at their choir festival today, and wished that the dear Saviour would bless them", and the translation follows its sense. 27. The transcription reads *und wieder werden*; the first copy has *und will auch noch nicht Sünderinn werden*, "and will not yet become a sinner".
28. The transcription reads *ſtündlich*, "hourly"; probably *beſtändig* or *täglich*.

The transcription reads *das Schickſal Baas Teunis Sohn von der Poſt*, "the fate Baas Teunis's son from the post", probably a misreading of *d 26 ſchickte Baas Teunis Sohn*, "on the 26th Baas Teunis's son sent". Further on, *an unſren Droſt erhielt die Nachricht*, "to our Landdrost received the news", is probably *an uns, wir erhielten die Nachricht*. To be checked against the image.
31. The text breaks off at the foot of page 202a (*Br. Kühnel blieb*) and resumes on page 202b in the middle of a sentence (*einer werde, weinte*).
