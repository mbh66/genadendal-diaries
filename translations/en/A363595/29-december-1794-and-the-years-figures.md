<!-- lines 12469-12512 -->
On 1 December another farmer came, named Predicam, to hire Hottentots, but he could not get any more. Another large family also moved here, which had lived 10 miles from here. On the 2nd: since several of the Hottentots have sown barley and corn [1], they began to cut it today. On the 3rd Mary [2] came to hire Hottentots, but could not get any more. On the 4th several of the baptised came, quite dismayed, and said that Anna had been at Baas Teunis's and had heard there that things looked [bad] [3] for our place. She had heard that all the Hottentots who have [come] to us [4] would have to go away again, because their cattle had done damage long enough. (Lately he had cattle of the Hottentots driven into the pound, because they had been in the corn, and to redeem them 1 shilling a head had to be paid.) He also meant to come to us on Sunday with several gentlemen from the Cape who are staying with him. Several gentlemen had also wanted to visit us yesterday, but the heavy rain prevented them. This [shows] well enough [that they are] noble gentlemen of the government [5]; for when they left the post, cannon were fired. Through the rumours above, the Hottentots have fallen into great perplexity. We must say that such rumours often put us in great perplexity too, because we know that they would gladly drive us away from here if it were possible. We can do nothing more than ask the dear Saviour for help.

<!-- lines 12512-12549 -->
On the 5th Brother Marsveld was called to [two] sick people [6]. One is a Bastard and has consumption. According to what the others said, he had wanted to speak openly with him, but could not. When Brother Marsveld asked him whether, if he died now, he knew where his soul would go, he answered that he believed in God and prayed day and night for the forgiveness of his sins. Christian Frans also sent word that a small packet and letters for us were at his house, which we should have fetched. We had heard that ships had arrived, and our first thought was that they would be letters from our dear brothers in Europe. We sent someone on horseback at once, and were so eager that we could hardly wait until the Hottentot came back. When he came and we saw the letters, our joy was gone again, for they were letters from the Cape [7]; so we had to be patient again. On the [6th] [8] Baas Teunis let us know that he would visit us tomorrow with several gentlemen. Early yesterday a Hottentot had come with his [wife] [9] to [live here]. He said he had run away; but [we heard] that he had stolen and had run away for that reason. We had him [called], put this to him, and told him that he should see to it that he got away from here again, for we did not want such people living here.

<!-- lines 12549-12597 -->
Early on Sunday the [7th] [10], at 8 o'clock, young Mrs Morgel came to visit us with her daughter and her son's son; she also had 3 slaves with her. Then Baas Teunis came with 3 gentlemen and 4 young gentlemen from the Cape; so today there were 11 persons together, not counting the many slaves, who all want a mouthful of food too. One of these gentlemen was the Vice-Fiscal with his wife; another, named Elias, a cousin of the well-known Mr Elias, a Director [11] in Amsterdam. All these attended the meeting, which we again had to hold in the garden under the pear trees. Brother Marsveld spoke on Luke 22:40-45. After the meeting Baas Teunis had all the Hottentots who have moved here from other places gather in front of the house, and announced to them in the name of the Edle Heer, the Commissioner [12], that all who had moved here from other places must take their cattle back where they had been; and as many of them must move back with them as were needed to look after the cattle. Here they might keep nothing but a few animals for slaughter; when these were eaten, they could fetch more. If they did not obey this, he would take away all the cattle he found in this area. Only one had the heart to raise an objection to him, and said that he might not go back to his place, for the farmer had chased him away. He got the answer that he could go and complain. The Hottentot said: "That has happened more than once, and it has only helped so far that he has now chased me off and driven me away from there." Baas Teunis declared him a man who will not obey the authorities, and said that he would make a report about him.

<!-- lines 12597-12683 -->
Another said: "We have moved here to hear God's word; if I have to go back where I lived and herd my cattle there, I cannot hear God's word." (Where he lived is about 15 miles from here.) Baas Teunis said: "I have told you; if you do not obey, you shall see what will follow." The Hottentots were all quite dismayed that they are to move away again, since God's word matters so much to them. How we felt at seeing such eager people move away can easily be imagined. Nor could we keep silent towards Baas Teunis; we told him that it was a thing to lament before God, and pitiable, how these poor people were treated. They are often treated worse than cattle. Indeed, if it were possible, people would rather exterminate them, or make them wholly slaves. For no one but himself instigated all that he said; he has long wanted it so. One may well say that this Teunis is one of our greatest and most dangerous enemies. Towards us he seems very friendly [13]; but to others he gives it to be understood that if it were possible he would like to destroy the whole thing. He also knows how to turn everything so that he will not be known as an enemy. It is true that both he and his wife showed us much love and friendship at the beginning, and helped us in everything where they knew how and could, for which may God bless them. But our purpose with the Hottentots seemed only laughable to him, as to many, and they believed that nothing would come of our undertaking. She often said so, too, in the time when our house was being built and we were living with them: still, we might try it; but if it did not work, we should consider not going back to the fatherland but staying here; he would look after us. Now they look at the matter with the greatest astonishment, see themselves deceived, and wish that we were who knows where. The enemy sharpens his arrows against us every day. Now the restrictions and the laws are beginning too. He told us today, for example, that the Edle Heer had sent us word that we should accept no Hottentot who had served a farmer unless he brought a certificate from the farmer that his time was up and he was now free. Before, he always told us that we had no need to ask anyone whether he had run away: the order of the authorities was that no farmer might keep a Hottentot who wanted to leave him and learn. So if none of the Hottentots who are with the farmers may come to us except those to whom the farmers give certificates, then certainly few will come, for most have to run away; the farmers will not let them go. God knows how it will go yet. We ask him for help who has taken the power from Satan, who will also help us and have mercy on this poor people. In the afternoon all our guests rode and drove away again.

<!-- lines 12683-12725 -->
(On the 8th another farmer came to hire Hottentots [14]. On the 9th we heard that young Mrs Mary had been at the Cape, and that Mr Heuser had wanted to give her goods to bring which had come from Europe, but he had not been able to get them ashore because of the strong south-east wind. He had also told her that he had already sent a packet of letters to us; and now we also heard that letters for us were lying at the post.) Early on the 10th we sent to the post at once to fetch the letters. Today was also the speaking of the communicants and the baptised; [some] were not at home, however, but away at the farmers'. We could truly rejoice over the declarations of them all, for all testified that it was their whole mind to live only for the Saviour in this world. Towards evening Jacob, whom we had sent to the post to fetch the letters, came back. The letters were from Brother Reichel, which we had long been looking forward to. They were of 23 and 24 April this year, so 7½ months old; several from Amsterdam were with them too. What a joy that was cannot be described, for it was the answer to our first letter from this place, on which we have now lived 1 year and nearly 10 months. Mr Heuser at the Cape writes to us that he has not yet been able to get the goods ashore, and so has sent the letters ahead; he will send the goods too at the first opportunity. Then we shall see what our dear congregations are doing.

<!-- lines 12725-12782 -->
On the 11th young Mrs Mary sent us word that goods from the Cape had arrived for us at her house, which we should have fetched. This message gave us no small joy, to receive them so soon; but today it was too late to have them fetched. Early on the 12th we sent our cart at once to fetch our goods; towards midday the cart came back. With what eagerness we opened the chests, no one can believe. It was a real day of joy for us. We also found letters from our dear brothers at Zeist, from Brothers Rothe, Wagner, Friedlerius and Schneider, and one from Brother Suter [16] in Amsterdam, as well as from other good friends at Zeist, and from good friends at Herrnhut. Now we would treat ourselves properly to the news sent us, and feast our hearts on the beloved addresses we have received. But we were very sorry that we found no Daily Texts for the coming year. (Brother Schmidt [17] writes that we were to receive 3 text books for the year 1795, and 30 Dutch Testaments; but both are missing. Mr Heuser at the Cape writes that Mr Schmidt had written to him that a packet of books for us was to come under his address, which he does not yet have. We received the said goods very quickly from the Cape; and from Holland too it is not bad, for Brother Schmidt's letter is of 7 June 1794; the Zeist letters, though, are of February 1794, and those of Brother Reichel of 23 and 24 April 1794. From all these letters we see that all our dear congregations still live in peace, for which we thanked our dear Lord from our hearts. Several brothers are surprised that the letters are so old [18]; but the letters we received from good friends at Herrnhut are 1½ years old. (On the 13th in our conference we put 4 before our dear Lord as confirmands for Holy Communion, of whom he approved 3; and one as a candidate for Communion, whom he also approved) [19]. In the evening we partook of the Lord's Supper for the last time this year, and he owned it most palpably. On Sunday the 14th there were no Christians here.

<!-- lines 12782-12857 -->
On the 15th a son of Mary came with a farmer who lives close to the Kaffers. He wanted to hire a Hottentot woman, but got none. He too had had to leave his place, and the Kaffers took cattle from him; since it is now fairly quiet again, he means to move back there. He wished that we would come there; perhaps the Bosjesmans (Bushmen) would change if the Gospel were preached to them. The Christians there are very much afraid of them. Several have already expressed this wish. On the 17th the Hottentots' corn, which they had fetched from beyond the Sergeants River, was brought here. Several had sat on the wagon; it overturned, and some were rather badly hurt. Brother Marsveld was also called to a sick man, who said that he was worried: he had walked over a path where poison had been buried, and had fallen ill from it. Brother Marsveld tried to talk him out of this. [A sick woman also came to us] [20] who believed the same of herself. With her we spoke very earnestly, and asked her whether she no longer believed in the Saviour, who is the physician of body and soul, but sought help from the devil. She at once asked earnestly for forgiveness, and said that she also wanted to ask the dear Saviour from her heart to forgive her for having grieved him so; she had not known it; she wanted nothing but what the dear Saviour wants. We had thought that the Hottentots knew nothing of witchcraft; now we have inquired, and heard to our astonishment that they believe in it strongly. Some said: "Since we have been hearing God's word, we do not believe in it so much any more." They told us that further up country [21] there were Hottentots who could take snakes, toads and the like out of the bodies of the sick, which the enemy of mankind had made. Such a one who got well again must give the sorcerer something every year, or else he falls ill again. A woman said: "I have done that until now, but now I will stop." We said that these were all works of the devil, and that they grieved the dear Saviour by them; there was nothing behind it but coaxing the money out of people's pockets. The black slaves are especially known as such sorcerers. On the 18th we had a heavy thunderstorm with much rain, which refreshed everything nicely. On the 19th 2 more women came who spoke with us about the unrest of their hearts. We had to let the singing meeting lapse today because of the rain; for when we hold meetings we must open the door and window, partly for those who cannot get in, partly also because of the warmth and the smell of these people; for their sweat smells strongly, and it is very warm now.

<!-- lines 12857-12899 -->
(On the 20th, in the conference, the dear Saviour directed us to baptise [a candidate] [22]. We also considered what meetings we would hold in these days. Early on the 21st 2 sons of Mary came with 2 slaves. Afterwards a gentleman from the post also came, who also had a slave with him. He was from Flanders, and had left there because of the unrest. He could hardly speak a word of Dutch; he spoke French, English and Portuguese. One of the Marys could speak Portuguese well and served as interpreter. They attended the meeting, which Brother Marsveld held on the Gospel [23]. On the 22nd many Hottentots came home from the harvest, among them 2 of our baptised, about whom we had been worried because of the drinking; but to our joy they behaved well. One brought her husband home with her [...] [24]. For when the corn is cut, the farmer brings a couple of aums [25] of wine onto the land, and everyone gets plenty. When they are drunk, then they must [promise to serve] the farmer for the coming year, and what they promise there they must keep. On the 23rd several more [women] [26] were there who were very uneasy and spoke with us. Today we cancelled all the schools for this year, because we have much writing to do. The singing meeting, which we held in front of the house, was especially lively.

<!-- lines 12899-12947 -->
On the 24th, Holy Christmas Eve, we spoke with the children early in the morning, told them what an important day this was, and urged them to thank the dear Saviour from their hearts that he had become man for their sakes too; and the dear Saviour proved himself most palpably in these young hearts. In the evening at dusk, about half past 7, we had a lovefeast with our baptised [27]. At the lovefeast the story of the birth of Jesus was read; then in a prayer on our knees we thanked the dear Saviour from our hearts for his incarnation, and also for what he has done for us poor people until now, and asked him to continue his mercy. A real feeling of peace prevailed, and many tears of thanks and joy were shed [28]. When it was over, we heard a large number sitting at one kraal singing of the birth of Jesus, at which we felt strangely moved; it went on until after 10 o'clock. On the 25th, the first Christmas Day, there was a meeting at 10 o'clock, in which Brother Marsveld spoke on the birth of Jesus. (We had long been afraid that many Christians would come, for we had heard that many intended to. But in the night it began to rain, and rained on through the morning and nearly all day, so that we thought we would be left in peace. But then Harme came with the Landdrost's servant, a German; then also young Mrs Mary with 4 more women and 4 children; that made 11 persons, not counting the slaves. Still, the rain kept many away. (In the afternoon they all rode and drove away again.) [29]

<!-- lines 12948-12999 -->
On the 26th, the second holiday, we sang the Liturgy to the Son early with our baptised. At 10 o'clock there was again a general meeting, in which Brother Schwinn spoke on the Gospel. Early in the day [the farmer] Gildenheis [30] also came, but soon rode away again. We postponed the baptism to tomorrow, because we were afraid that many Christians might come and we would have no room for it. On these holidays there were more Hottentots here than we have ever seen together; they came from every quarter where they serve the farmers. Since because of heavy rain and wind we could not hold the meeting in the garden under the thorn tree [31], we let as many as possible into our bedroom and kitchen; but the space was far from enough, and so many had to stand before the door in the wind and rain. We can say that the dear Saviour proved himself powerfully and mightily in the hearts of these people, and blessed the testimony that was given of his holy incarnation. But to our sorrow we had to exclude one of the candidates for baptism, who had behaved badly at the farmers' [32] where she had been. On the 27th was the baptism, which Brother Kühnel held; he spoke on the words of the 2nd chapter of Philippians, verse 7. She received the name Catharina. We told her only late in the evening, and had the baptised told only in the morning, because we thought it should not become so full. But these people all gathered again as if they had been summoned. The Spirit of God owned this holy act powerfully; and so we closed these days of blessing and festival with praise and thanks to our dear Lord for all the good that he has let us and this poor people enjoy, and asked him to continue his mercy.

<!-- lines 13000-13054 -->
On Saturday the 28th [33] the whole crowd of people gathered again. Brother Marsveld spoke on the Gospel of the third holiday. Several also came who spoke with us about the unrest of their hearts. Brother Kühnel was called to a sick candidate for baptism, whom he found in a gladdening state of heart; he was also called to a sick girl in the kraals. Today we received a letter from [Mr] Heuser [34] at the Cape, in which he reported that the ship had arrived on which Domine Miller [35] was, who had brought various things for us; but the Domine had ridden to Stellenbosch on a visit, and he had not yet been able to get the things; so one must wait until he came back. He had received from him a letter from Brother Schmidt to us, which he sent us. We were very glad that the Daily Texts for the coming year were among [the things]. We shall probably not have them yet at the beginning of the year, but we hope to get them soon after. Towards evening Doctor Templi's son came; but he looked [36] like no doctor's son, rather like a vagabond. He stayed the night. On the 29th 3 Hottentots came from the Breede River, about 20 German miles from here, who wanted to live here to hear God's word. They brought their oxen and cows with them. They mean to go back [through the little pass] [37] to fetch their families and kraals, and also to bring their sheep. They are still rich in cattle: together they had some 70 oxen and cows, and they said that they also had a fairly large flock of sheep. They had found ostrich eggs on the way, of which they brought us 2 as a present. The 3 of us make a full meal from one such egg. (There are no ostriches where we are.) These eggs are found just so in the field; but even where there are many, one must still pay 1 shilling apiece; for 1 shilling is little money here.

<!-- lines 13054-13116 -->
On the 30th we were busy writing to Europe. In the evening Brother Schwinn spoke in the meeting on 1 John 5:20. He reminded them that tomorrow was the last day of this year, and asked that each one would present himself before Jesus and examine how he had spent this year, and thank God for the great mercy he had shown them this year, in having his word proclaimed to them through his servants and the way to salvation told them; and that each should also fall down at Jesus' feet, ask him for forgiveness, and thank him from the heart for all the good he had given them. This caused a general stirring, and many tears were shed. Then they were told that tomorrow evening at half past 11 we would come together, to thank our dear Lord together for all the love and mercy he has shown us. On the 31st. We will still say something about what Baas Teunis announced on the [7th] [38] of this month, namely that all the Hottentots who have come here since we have been here must take their cattle back where they lived, and as many of them must move with them as were needed to look after the cattle. So far none has moved away. He said that if he found cattle of theirs in this field he would take them and hand them over to the government. The Hottentots say: "If he wants to take our cattle, he can do it; we will not move away because of that; we have come here to hear God's word." At half past 11 we came together for the close of the year. Our Hottentots all came to it. It was a fine evening, so that we could open the door and window. Brother Marsveld called on them all, at the close of this year, to thank God for all his grace and mercy towards us; and so we fell on our knees with them, and thanked God for all that he has done at this place, and passed over into the new year with praise and thanks. There was a general stirring at this close. Afterwards many more came to wish us well, with these words: "I wish you a happy New Year, long life, and a blessed death."

<!-- lines 13117-13171 -->
So we close this year 1794 with praise and thanks to our dear Lord, ashamed and thankful for all the good he has done for us, his 3 poor brothers; and we find very much cause to ask him for forgiveness where we have not acted and walked according to his heart. When we look at the blessing he has laid on the testimony of his servants [39] about his incarnation, torment and death, and how through the Holy Spirit he has awakened hearts and made them perplexed, so that few days have passed without some coming and asking, "What must I do to be saved?", then we are deeply bowed and ashamed. It seems as if there were now a quite different life among these people again. A thousand, yes, a thousand thanks be to him for it! Nor can we thank him enough that this year he has kept us together in love and peace, so that we could share joy and sorrow with one another. May he keep this jewel for us always! With this we commend ourselves and the work of God among the Hottentots to the prayers and remembrance of all our dear congregations before our dear Lord, and especially those who belong to our little congregation, that the dear Saviour may keep them with himself and his wounds, and let them grow more and more in the knowledge of Jesus Christ, so that they may shine as burning lights in this dark land. At the close of this year 1794 our little congregation consists of 4 communicants, 3 confirmands for Holy Communion, 3 candidates for Communion, 9 baptised, with Lena, [and] 17 candidates for baptism: in all 36 persons. Of these, one baptised, Hanna, and one candidate for baptism are excluded. Hendrik Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn, Johann Christian Kühnel.

## Notes

1. The transcription reads *Da verſchiedene von den Hottentotten Gerſte ab, was Korn geſehen haben*; probably *Gerſte und Korn geſäet haben*, "have sown barley and corn".

3. The transcription reads *daß es mit unſerm Platz gemach ausſehe*; *gemach* is probably a misreading, perhaps of *ſchlecht*, "bad".
4. The transcription reads *die von uns nun Boden fangen*, which is not clear; the context (see the announcement on the Sunday) suggests those who had moved here from elsewhere.
5. The transcription reads *Dieſes um den edel Herren von der Regierūng genūg zu ſagen*; the sense seems to be that the cannon salute showed they were gentlemen of rank in the government.
6. The transcription reads *zu de Kranken*, "to the sick"; only one is then described.
7. The transcription has *von der Caſe* here and on the Sunday below, a misreading of *Caap*.
8. 9. The transcription reads *mit ſeinem Peerde gekom̅en, um heu zu mahen*, "come with his horse to mow hay"; perhaps *Weibe* and *um hier zu wohnen*, "with his wife, to live here".
