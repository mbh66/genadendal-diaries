V. Diary of the Brothers Marsveld, Schwinn and Kühnel at the Cape of Good Hope, from 1 to 31 July 1794 [1].

<!-- lines 8766-8788 -->
On 1 July 2 people [2] came. [One of them was] very uneasy because he did not know whether he truly believed in Jesus. The other asked that she might come to the little meeting. On the 2nd an old Hottentot came [and] asked Brother Marsveld, [for] his wife [3] had asked that he would visit her. When he got there, her complaint was about her brother, who would give her nothing because she had lived so badly. On the 3rd a boy who went to school was buried. Early on the 4th a woman, still young, came and said that for anguish of heart she did not know where to turn. She was directed to the dear Saviour. On the 5th we put 4 before our dear Lord as candidates for baptism, and he approved all 4. On the 6th several came again who said that for unrest of heart they did not know where to turn. Question: what were they uneasy about? Answer: that with their sins they had caused our dear Lord Jesus too much pain. After the meeting at 10 o'clock, which was very well attended, we made it known in the evening to 4 that they had come into the number of the candidates. A particular feeling prevailed, and they promised once more to live wholly for Jesus.

<!-- lines 8790-8816 -->
On the 7th another woman came [who did not know] where to turn [4]. Brother Schwinn also got something in his head [5] which moved into his eyes and gave him much pain, so that for the time being he could not hold school. On the 8th a child was buried. Another woman also came and lamented her misery, and a man came with his wife and asked that they too might come to school. It is very cold here now, and we feel the cold badly. During the day it is warm, but in the morning everything is quite white with frost until the sun comes again; then it grows warm. In our garden the beans and potatoes have been frozen quite dead. There is snow on the mountains too. On the 9th, in our morning blessing, we joined our dear boys in the congregations [6] for their holy choir festival. Then came the speaking of the candidates for baptism [7]. Those who came to the speaking for the first time came trembling and quaking. Many of them could say nothing for tears except: "My whole [heart] is now to live for Jesus." There are now also 3 girls among the candidates for baptism, about 15 or 16 years old. They are still very shy of explaining themselves, and answer everything with yes. [Otherwise we can rejoice over them.] [8] On the 10th another child was buried.

<!-- lines 8818-8833 -->
On the 11th our sister Sophia came and complained that she was very troubled, because many abominations rose up in her heart, with which she grieved the Saviour. An old man was also buried. On the 12th, in the conference, the dear Saviour directed us to baptise [one] tomorrow [9]. Another child was also buried. Early on the 13th, while it was still dark, the often-mentioned Jan Prädgen, who is good by nature, Susanna's husband [10], came wanting [to tell] the anguish of his heart and to ask for advice; but for tears and anguish of heart he could hardly say a word. He said he did not know what to do for anguish. It had lately gone the same way with his wife, but she could speak for herself better. At 10 o'clock was the baptism, with the dear Saviour owning it. She received the name Prädgen [11].

<!-- lines 8833-8849 -->
On the 14th our sister Maria came and said that she had been with her husband at a farmer's 2 days' journey from here. The first night she had dreamed that we had taken her book from her in the school and wanted to give her another, but it had nothing written in it. When she woke she had become very troubled about it, and had wanted to turn back and come home. She asked what would come of it. She was told that nothing would come of it; she should only hold fast to the dear Saviour. It matters very much to her not to grieve the Saviour in any way. She is very simple and open-hearted.

<!-- lines 8849-8877 -->
On the 15th another young woman came and complained of the great distress of her heart and her great perplexity about how to be saved. On the 16th an old man sent to ask that one of us would visit him; he would gladly hear words about our dear Lord. Brother Marsveld went, but found him very weak. At 12 o'clock at night our sister Elisabeth sent to ask [one of us] to come to her husband, who was very weak. Brother Kühnel went, but found him very weak and could not talk much with him; yet he wished to ask Jesus Christ for grace and mercy and for the forgiveness of his sins. Brother Kühnel had occasion [to speak of] the necessity and the glory, for every poor and anxious heart, of being sure in healthy days of the forgiveness of sins in the heart [12]. When someone is ill, it is [the custom] that they visit the sick person, and then the house or kraal where the sick person lies is usually so full that many have to stand outside. When one of us is called to a sick person by day and they see it [13], at once everyone comes crowding round the house, and then one often has the chance to commend the dear Saviour.

<!-- lines 8877-8914 -->
On the 17th this man died. Our sister Elisabeth said that to the end, in his pain, he had prayed to God for grace and mercy; he was conscious until his last breath. On the 18th we began to plant the lower half of our garden with a hedge of quinces [14], so that the cattle cannot run in, and to replace those we planted last year that have died. This costs us much work, for we have to fetch the cuttings from an hour away; here one does not have things to hand as in Europe. The time is also coming, namely in August, to plant broad beans [15] quickly, and we mean to plant them too. But whether we [shall get anything] for all the trouble (what of it will be fit to eat), God knows. Yet if we do not enjoy it (so we think), our descendants shall have it to enjoy. Last year we had already planted other beans in front of the ditch [16], but nothing came of them, because we are not able to close it off from the cattle until the hedge is grown, and that will again cost much money and trouble. For even if one sets strong posts in the ground here, they are rotten within a year, so that they fall over; and getting poles and posts here is very difficult and costly. One must have them fetched from the Bay [17], 5 or 6 hours away. Besides, they stand so far in, in the air [17], that one must drag them half an hour, even a whole hour, before they can be loaded on the wagon. In the river there is wood enough for burning, but no poles. So one can easily imagine that it is very costly.

<!-- lines 8914-8929 -->
On the 19th our Elisabeth's husband was buried. It is [remarkable] that when a wife, husband, father or mother dies and is buried here, one [sees] none of the relatives shed a tear [18]; everything goes quietly. Brother Schwinn's eyes are now fairly well restored, for which we are heartily thankful to the Saviour. On the 20th there was a particular stirring in the meeting, which was also very well attended. On the 21st Brother Schwinn rode to the post, and from there to Juffrouw Lind [19], to see whether we could get the small fruit trees for planting. On the 22nd he came home again and had got as many trees as we need.

<!-- lines 8929-8969 -->
On the 23rd was the speaking of the baptised. Two of them were very troubled. One was Prädgen, the last to be baptised. She complained about her husband, who had lived badly with her, especially since her baptism: he did nothing but quarrel with her, and said: "I am a child of the devil and must go to hell; you are a child of God." She asked [us] to speak with him, which was promised her. The other was Anna [20]; she was very troubled. Question: what was she troubled about? Answer: something had happened at her place last night such as she had never heard of in her life. A man and his wife from among her relatives had recently moved in with her, to go to school too. This woman died. When she was dead, because more sick people are lying in the house where they live, they laid the body in a little house beside the big one and shut the door and window fast. This morning the dogs came, quite gorged. People thought that a mare had had a foal in the night which was dead, and that the dogs had eaten it [21]. But alas! they went in, and the dogs had come in at the window where the body lay, had begun at the head and eaten more than half of it, so that not much more than the legs was left to bury. A rare occurrence. In the evening our sister Elisabeth came and wept bitterly because she had missed the meeting this afternoon. She had just been in the bush fetching wood, and when she came back the door was shut. We had much to do to comfort her. In general none of the people living here likes to miss a meeting without the greatest need.

<!-- lines 8970-8991 -->
On the 24th [22] we received 2 letters from the Cape, one from Mr Schönberg, the other from Mr Lücke [23]. Mr Schönberg sent us coffee beans, which we had asked for. A farmer who lived a day's journey from here had brought them with him, and we had to have them fetched from there. We sent one of our Hottentots there today. Mr Lücke asked us to send him a Hottentot who would like to serve with him, and also to buy him 50 goats and some cows, because he had heard that they were very cheap in this area. But this man is quite wrongly informed, and we cannot serve him in anything. On the 25th our Hottentot came back. But the horse was so galled that we shall not be able to use it again for a long time. On the 26th, in the conference, we put our sister Maria before our dear Lord as a candidate for Communion, and he approved it. In the evening we partook of Holy Communion, with the dear Saviour owning us poor ones.

<!-- lines 8993-9004 -->
On the 27th, after the meeting, we spoke with the husband of our sister Prädgen about his bad treatment of his wife; we meant to speak to him earnestly. But when he was told why we really wanted to speak with him, he said that he had come for that very purpose, to accuse himself. It was all true; he had said and done it. But he had already become very uneasy about it himself, though he had not been able to help himself. He also promised to ask our dear Lord that such a thing should not happen again.

<!-- lines 9005-9019 -->
On the 28th Brother Marsveld was called to a sick man half an hour from here. But he could not talk much with him, because he had no desire to be saved and was making a strange noise [24]. Early on the 29th 6 strangers [25] and 2 young men came. 5 of the women already went clothed. They were from the Appel Kraal [26], a long day's ride from here. They asked leave to stay here, to hear what the others hear and to come to school too, which was gladly allowed them. On the 30th another man came who for anguish of heart could hardly speak, but only wept aloud and said: "Oh, I am a great sinner, I have done many sins, but I would still gladly be saved [27]

## Notes

1. The diary is headed *V. Diaria* in the transcription; the normalised column has *Tagebuch*. The numbers 23 and 24 above the heading (lines 8759 and 8762), and the numbers 2 to 7 in the text, are page numbers of the manuscript and are left out. The same July diary follows a second time from line 9022 (entry 23), in a different hand or copy with fuller wording; this first copy breaks off on the 30th. Where the two copies differ, the notes below give the reading of the second.
2. The transcription reads *2 der royde leute*, which is not clear; the normalised column has *2 Hottentotten*. The second copy has "a man and a woman", which fits the rest of the sentence. To be checked against the image.
3. The transcription reads *jener Frau ließ bitten*, "that woman asked"; the second copy has "asked Brother Marsveld in his wife's name to come to her".
4. The transcription reads *die wir weggeſchickt hattens nicht mehr wohin?*, "whom we had sent away ... no longer where to?", which makes no sense. The second copy has "who, as she said, also did not know where to turn for unrest of heart". The words *wir weggeſchickt* are probably a misreading. To be checked against the image.
5. The transcription reads *einen Schlag in den Kopf*, "a stroke (or blow) in the head"; the second copy has *einen fluß in den Kopf*, a rheum or catarrh in the head. The normalised column has *Anfall*, "attack".
6. The transcription reads *ver⸗geſelln wir unſern lieben Knaben*, apparently "we kept company with our dear boys"; the second copy has "we remembered with sympathy our dear boys ... who kept their choir festival today, and commended them to the Saviour's blessing in our morning blessing". The boys are the boys' choir of the congregations in Europe.
7. The transcription has *das Sprechen*, the speaking; the normalised column has *Befragung*, "questioning". The same change occurs on the 23rd.
8. The transcription reads *Sorge kom⸗men wir uns keinen*, which the normalised column takes as "we have no worries". The second copy has *Sonſt kömmen wir uns über ſie freuen*, "otherwise we can rejoice over them", and the translation follows it.
9. The transcription reads *wirb uns der l. Heiland nur am morgen zu taufen*, "the dear Saviour directed us to baptise only in the morning"; the second copy has *morgen 4 Taufte zu halten*, "to hold 4 baptisms tomorrow". Only one baptism is reported on the 13th, so *nur* and *4* may both be misreadings of *eine*, "one". To be checked against the image.
10. The transcription reads *Jan Prädgen, u: zur Suſanna ihr Man*; the second copy has "the good-natured Jan Prädgen" and "with his wife Susanna". Susanna was baptised on 23 March 1794. A man of this name has not been found in the earlier entries, which call him "often mentioned"; he may be mentioned under another name.
11. The transcription reads *wir erhielt den Namen Prädgen*, "we [she] received the name Prädgen". The second copy has "the baptism of a child ... He received the name Prädgen". But on the 23rd and the 27th the last baptised is a married woman called Prädgen, whom the second copy calls *Bedje* and *Bedgen*, and a sister Bedgen appears later in this file (lines 9537, 13370). Her baptismal name was probably Bedgen (Betje), and *Prädgen* is probably a misreading, perhaps influenced by Jan Prädgen's name just before. Who was baptised, and the name, are to be checked against the image.
12. The sentence has no main verb in the transcription (*hatte gelegenheit, die Nothwendigkeit u. Herrlichkeit, die für einen jeden armen und herzens bangen, um bey geſunden Tagen, die Vergebung der Sünden im Herzen gewiſs zu haben*). The second copy has "took occasion to lay it on the sick man's heart how necessary it is for our salvation that each one should be assured of the forgiveness of his sins through Jesus while still in healthy days".
13. The transcription reads *iſt es wahr, daß ſie zu einem kranken beſuchen*, and below *und ſie es ja ſeyn*; the second copy has *iſt es Wahn* and *und ſie es ſehen*, "and they see it". The words translated "the custom" and "usually" (*warſcheinlich*) are guesses at the sense. To be checked against the image.
14. The transcription reads *einer hekke von Quit kerren*; the second copy has *Quitt=birnen*, quince pears. Quince hedges were widely planted at the Cape.
15. *Sau Bohnen*, broad beans. The second copy has "peas and beans".
16. The transcription reads *ſonſt Bohnen gepflanzt, vor dem Graben*; the second copy has "some fruit trees in front of the garden" (*Frucht Bäume vor den Garten*). *ſonſt Bohnen* and *Graben* may be misreadings. To be checked against the image.
17. The transcription reads *aus dem Bay* and *ſo weit in der Luft hinein*; the second copy has *aus dem Boſch*, "from the forest", and *ſo tief in dem Boſch*, "so deep in the forest", which make better sense. The normalised column has *aus der Bucht*, "from the bay".
18. The transcription reads *Es iſt doch nötig*, "it is surely necessary", and *ſo ſoll man von den Angehöri⸗gen, daß keinen gehn, der eine Thräne vergießt*. The second copy has *Es iſt artig*, "it is curious", and says that one hears none of the relatives cry aloud. The translation follows the sense of both.
19. The transcription has *Juffrouw Lind*, the normalised column *Frau Lind*. The second copy reads *Juffrouw Lier, am Caap*, and has *Weinstöcke* ("vines") where this copy has fruit trees. Perhaps a member of the family of Field Cornet Linde (see `glossary.csv`). To be checked against the image.
20. The second copy names the first woman *Bedje* and this one *Debra*, not Anna.
21. The second copy has "that a dog had dropped a lamb in the night"; it also says that the dogs had made a hole in the little house, where this copy has the window.
22. The transcription reads *Am 24te geſtern*, "on the 24th yesterday"; the word *geſtern* is probably left over from a correction.

Where this copy has "50 goats and some cows", the second copy's transcription reads *50 Rds ſagen und alles Schaffen*, probably a misreading of the same words. To be checked against the image.
24. The transcription reads *ein artig ge⸗räuſch dabey hatte*, "had a strange noise with it", perhaps a rattle in his breathing; the second copy has *ein artig Gefuhl*, "a strange feeling". The normalised column has *Treiben*, "doings".
25. The transcription reads *6 frem⸗de*, "6 strangers", and then "5 of the women"; the second copy has "6 women".
26. *Appel Craul* (second copy *Appel Craal*): a kraal or settlement not identified, a long day's ride away.
27. This copy breaks off here, at the foot of the page, in the middle of the sentence (*ich wolte aber doch gern*); the normalised column adds *selig*. The second copy (entry 23) completes it with *ſelig werden* and continues to the end of the month.
