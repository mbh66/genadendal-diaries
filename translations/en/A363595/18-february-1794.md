On 1 February we harvested our flax. Mrs Gordon at the Cape had given us some linseed to see whether it would grow here, and it has done as well as we could wish. The African-born people had never seen this plant, and all travellers wondered that linen could be made from it; they often took stalks home with them to show others. Since several of our people are now beginning to read, we resolved to begin a kind of catechising, so that they may also understand what they read. This was done for the first time on the 3rd, with old and young. Brother Kühnel visited some sick people, among them a girl of about 16 who seemed near her end. We know her as an awakened soul [1], who has shed many a tear in the meetings. Weak as she was, she knew Brother Kühnel at once and greeted him very warmly. He asked her whether she had thought about dying. She answered joyfully: yes. Question: whether she had asked the dear Saviour for forgiveness of her sins? Answer: yes! Question: whether she could believe that he would have mercy on her and accept her? Answer: "Yes, he will have mercy on me." Brother Kühnel prayed over her, with a good feeling. Many were present.

<!-- lines 7838-7885 -->
On the 6th another family moved here to live. At the same time some made known their longing to be saved. On the 7th we received the corn that a Bastard had sown for us; it came to 17 mud, or bushels. On the 8th in our conference Martha was chosen as a confirmand, and Sarah and Eva as candidates for Communion, and one more person for holy baptism. On the 9th our Rahel, who is now Sarah [2], had a somewhat premature delivery; the child also died soon after birth. She sent to ask us how the burial should be done. Early on we had chosen a place for a God's Acre for our baptised; and since this was the child of a baptised woman, which we would have baptised too if it had lived, we resolved to make the beginning with this little body. For the rest they were told that they could keep to their custom of wrapping the body in a sheepskin, since they were poor and could not buy linen, which was not at all necessary either. The burial was on the 10th. On the same day a candidate for baptism, the daughter of our helper Jakob and of Anna Maria, also had a premature delivery and was very weak. Brother Kühnel visited her, and she answered all his questions joyfully. She said: "I am quite resigned to the dear Saviour's will, and only keep asking him to have mercy on me." At midnight we were woken by Jakob with the news that his daughter was very weak. Brothers Marsveld and Schwinn went with him, but she no longer knew anything of herself. At our direction those present sang some verses, and during the singing she passed away. On the 11th was the burial of Magdalena [3].

<!-- lines 7886-7920 -->
At this time we had much to do with visiting the sick; for, young and old, big and small, some 30 persons were ill. When one goes visiting, a crowd gathers at once and goes along from kraal to kraal. On the 13th another child was buried. On the 14th we harvested some of our beans from the garden. We also began drying our peaches. On the 15th a large family moved here from the Tigerhoek to hear God's word. On the 18th we harvested Brother Schmidt's beans [4]; they are large and tasty. Now most of the fruit came all at once, so that one has plenty. In these days 2 children were buried. On the 22nd Brother Marsveld had a severe attack of stabbing and tearing pains all over his body, with chills, fever and headache. We were all the more troubled about it because no doctor is to be had here. We were given a small medicine chest to bring with us, but do not rightly know how to use it. On the 23rd Brother Marsveld was better, but Brother Kühnel had the same attack, which weakened him greatly. We commended ourselves to the Physician of body and soul. On the 24th the schools had to be stopped. Brother Kühnel was a little better today, however. Our prayer to the Saviour is that he would not lay us all on the sickbed at once, so that one can give the other a hand.

<!-- lines 7921-7974 -->
Among the Hottentots the sickness still goes on. 3 of our baptised have been so weak lately that their recovery was doubted; old Lena was among them. But it was a pleasure to visit them, because of the blessed state of their hearts. Martha, who had recently become a confirmand for Holy Communion, said: "I wish from my heart to partake of the body and blood of the dear Saviour too, for I long for it very much; but if he wants to take me to himself, I am quite resigned to his will; I have let go of my 3 children too." This Martha has also borne many a fine testimony to visitors, of the grace of the Saviour and of faith in him. Now all 3 have recovered so far that they can attend the meeting. On the 25th Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel were able to be up again, though both still felt pain. On the 26th another child was buried. On the 28th we closed this month with much thanks to our Lord for having supported us so graciously. In the meetings his Spirit has proved himself powerfully. A great blessing has rested on the catechising too, so that often, at the explanation of the answers they are to learn and of the lovely texts on which the answers are founded, everyone dissolved in tears. This instruction always comes at the end of school. We must say that the Spirit of God is working mightily on the hearts. Through the many illnesses we saw more than we had believed. Until now we had not heard many expressions from the children, for they were too shy; but now that they are ill they speak with us (this concerns mostly the children) in such a way that one wonders what the Saviour has done in them. Altogether it is a pleasure to visit the sick, because of their complete resignation to the will of the Saviour.

## Notes

1. *eine angeſetzte Seele*: probably *angefaßte*, "touched", or *erweckte*, "awakened".
2. *unſere Rahel, die jetzt Sarah iſt*: Rahel was baptised on 13 November 1793, and Sarah (Sara) on 13 August 1793 is a different woman, named as a candidate for Communion in the sentence before. The phrase may be a misreading. To be checked against the image.
3. *die Leiche der Magdalena*: the burial of the young woman who died the night before, or of her child. She was a candidate for baptism, so the name Magdalena may mean that she was baptised on her deathbed, which the diary does not record. Not the Magdalena (Vehettge Tikkuie) baptised by Georg Schmidt, nor the Magdalena of 1804.
4. *Brüder Schmidts Bohnen*: not clear whether these are beans from seed sent by Martinus Schmidt of Cape Town or from plants going back to Georg Schmidt, whose almond and pear trees the diarists mention.
