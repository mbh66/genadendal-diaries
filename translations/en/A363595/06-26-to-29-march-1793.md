The diary now continues [1]: "On 26 March we talked together about now beginning the promised instruction with the Hottentots, since we were now together; they longed for it very much. We announced to them that the instruction, as already said, was to begin tomorrow, an hour before sunset, and only with the old people. Today the Hottentots also first worked on the channel in which the water comes to our house. Many of them said that they seldom did all this for nothing; but the poverty among them is too great; they really cannot [2].

<!-- lines 2812-2834 -->
"On the 27th we began the instruction. Because it was in the days of the Saviour's Passion, we began the 17th chapter of John, read it and then explained it. There were 41 men and women. How quiet they are is hard to believe. We had heard beforehand that it would be hard to get them quiet; they are so of their own accord, without our having said anything. On the 28th, Maundy Thursday, we strengthened ourselves with the body and blood of Jesus for the first time in our house. We had long yearned for this partaking, and we were heartily thankful to the dear Saviour that we now have a house of our own where we can hold our meetings undisturbed. The dear Saviour graciously acknowledged us. The Hottentots came diligently to the instruction again.

<!-- lines 2834-2861 -->
"On the 29th we had a visit from a farmer's wife (her husband has died) with her children and 2 others, 9 persons in all. She lives 2 hours from here; they stayed with us over the midday meal. On the one hand such a visit is welcome to us; but we would rather they did not stay over the midday meal, or even overnight. That is because hospitality is at home here. For wherever one goes, no one expects anything else than to eat and drink there and to stay a day or longer; and if one is polite, one says thank you on leaving; it does not occur to anyone to ask about paying. If such parties were to come to us often, it would be hard, as can easily be supposed; and yet one cannot refuse [3], because it is the custom of the country. If we had to travel much in the country, this could be to our advantage. There came [...]" [4]

## Notes

1. *Es heißt nun im Diario weiter*: a copyist's or editor's words, marking the return to the diary after Brother Marsveld's account of his journey (entry 05).
2. The transcription reads *daß ſie dieſes alles ſelten umſonſt machten*, "that they seldom did all this for nothing". The sense of the next clause is that the Khoekhoe were too poor to work without pay.
3. *und doch kann man es nicht thun*, literally "and yet one cannot do it": one cannot refuse or charge them.
4. The text breaks off at the foot of page 107 of the first diary, with the catchword *kam*. The rest of the first diary (pages 108 to 112 by the archivist's contents list, to 31 March 1793) and the first nine pages of the second diary (from 1 April 1793) are missing: the transcription jumps from image 56 to image 64. The gap is in the source used for this edition.
