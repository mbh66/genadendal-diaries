On 1 December the sermon was fairly well attended, although most are already at the harvest. On the 2nd Brother Kühnel was out hunting with Baas Teunis, on business.

<!-- lines 7102-7161 -->
On the 4th we had the honour of a visit from two gentlemen of the government, Mr Brand and Mr Voor [1] from the Cape. Brother Kühnel had already heard of it at the post the day before yesterday, and had asked Baas Teunis to let us know beforehand, which he promised to do. But the gentlemen had forbidden him, because they wanted to find us going about our usual business. So they came suddenly, with Mrs Teunis and her son, and they could hardly have found us in greater disorder at any other time. We were just busy preparing the clay for our house building, and we looked just like the clay [2]. We had first to get the worst of the clay off our hands before they could sit down. It suited them very well to find us so; only they would have liked to find us at school, to hear the children sing; but it was Wednesday, when we have no school. They asked whether something of the kind could not still be arranged. We said the best singers were away at the harvest (for more than 20 of the bigger children were missing), and those still here were quite small and could not do much yet. But they would not be put off, and so we called the children to a singing school, and Brother Kühnel played the flute for it. However weakly it went, the gentlemen said: "If the best are still missing, we would like to hear them sometime, for this alone has surpassed our expectations." They gave the children good admonitions as well, to learn very diligently. Towards us they showed themselves very friendly, and were glad to find so much eagerness for the word of God among the Hottentots. We used this opportunity to speak with them about our school, and learned that complaints had indeed been made about it to the government. They told us that since our school was not in a church, and we sang between two walls, we could go on building meanwhile; and the Honourable Mr Brand promised to speak further about it with the Governor. At 4 in the afternoon they left again, after showing us every possible friendship.

<!-- lines 7162-7189 -->
On the evening of the 5th, between 8 and 9 o'clock, the house of one of our candidates for baptism burned down. It was the best of all those that stand here, and not as poor as the Hottentots usually build. Putting it out was not to be thought of, for they have no vessels for carrying water. She had some good clothing, but none of it could be saved. We thought she would be very sad because all her goods and chattels were lost; but she said, "The Lord has taken it from me," and was quite content with it. Since some children had behaved badly in school at this time, some parents asked us to punish them with beatings in such cases; and one of the Bastards said: "If my children ever complain that they have been beaten at school, I will give them some more on top." We told them that we did not intend to beat their children; if they deserved punishment, we would leave it to the parents.

<!-- lines 7189-7246 -->
On the 8th several came from the harvest to the sermon and went back again in the afternoon. Two came to us and accused themselves of having done wrong (one could see it on them, for both had their heads bandaged): they had got drunk, then fallen to quarrelling and fought. They were very penitent, and they were spoken to from the heart. With these two, who do not yet belong to the little congregation, nothing more could be done; but with the candidate for baptism who had not taken part in the fight but had taken part in the drinking, we spoke more seriously, and he begged earnestly for forgiveness. Today our Anna Maria and Eva went to Mrs Teunis at the post. The first had been called there, and the other went with her for company; for no woman likes to go alone, especially at harvest time, so as not to fall into the hands of bad people. Afterwards they told us, embarrassed, that they had accepted wine from a farmer on the way; not so much that they were drunk, but still they had not followed our admonitions, namely not to begin drinking at all, since they were not masters of themselves and soon went too far. This matter gives us a great deal of worry altogether. Rahel, the last baptised, and five of our candidates are now at work with the farmers; the other baptised and candidates are still with us. At harvest they get wine four times a day, namely at 8 o'clock, at midday, at 4 o'clock and late in the evening, and it is drunk not from small glasses but from large vessels. We have seen it with one who passes for a miser and gives as little as possible. He had 13 people at work, and each time they got half a bucket of wine, so two full buckets a day, and that was said to be given after meals [3]. They must have something in the great heat, and there is no beer here; but the excessive drinking of wine has more than one bad consequence here, as everywhere. May God have mercy and help in this too!

<!-- lines 7247-7281 -->
On the 10th towards evening our servant Hottentot told us that a tiger was lying in the grass not far from our house. These animals like to lie in wait for young cattle in this way, and since some were grazing in the neighbourhood just then, we thought he had designs on them. It is dangerous to shoot them, because if they are not hit properly on the spot they go for the one who fired and maul him badly. We went out with two guns loaded with ball, and others followed with cudgels. Both guns were fired one after the other, and he lay still. When we went nearer we saw that we had not hit him, but that he had already had a ball in him, with which he had run this far and had now bled to death. On the 11th we spoke with the baptised individually, except Rahel, who is still away. With two we had to speak seriously, because love is lacking between them. Up to now they have lived together in one house; but one of them is now to build a house of her own, and then perhaps things will go better. One of those who were at the post recently [confessed] that she had drunk wine on the way [4].

<!-- lines 7282-7306 -->
On the 12th a number of our people came home again from the farmers. On the 13th Rahel came too, and one of the candidates for baptism about whom we had been so worried. We spoke with them at once, and had cause to believe that they had been kept from pleasures. On the 15th our house could not nearly hold the listeners at the sermon. More and more kept coming from far away. On the 18th Andreas Otto, at Baas Teunis's bidding, brought a load of flour here for the women whose husbands are at the Cape. We have instructions to share it out weekly. On the 19th we heard that the land at the Sergeants River is to be granted to a farmer, which put us in great difficulty. For this land has belonged to the Hottentots until now, and without it they could not subsist here at all. For us too this would be very harmful.

<!-- lines 7307-7332 -->
On the 20th, at the children's request, the singing meetings were begun again; they had been stopped for 14 days, partly because many children were away, partly because of the carelessness of those who stayed behind. They are very cross with one another again, and if one of them so much as makes a friendly face in school, the others say at once: "He [or she] laughed!" On the 21st an old Hottentot told us that he was very uneasy and must tell us the reason: during the harvest he had once got drunk and quarrelled with another man, but it had not gone too far. On the 22nd a woman begged earnestly for holy baptism. She said: "I have long wanted to come, but I was always ashamed to tell of my wickedness, for I have joined in all the wickedness that is done among us; now I am very anxious to be saved and long for holy baptism."

<!-- lines 7332-7381 -->
On the 23rd we closed the schools for the holidays, and in the evening meeting announced that Wednesday was Christmas, and that there would be a sermon on that day and on Thursday as well, and on Wednesday a children's meeting. Today we shared out [the flour] among the Hottentots for the first time. On the 24th, to celebrate Christmas Eve, we held a lovefeast with our baptised, read them the story of Jesus [5] and spoke about it. A very blessed feeling prevailed, and one heard sighing and weeping full of feeling. On the 25th Hottentots came from all sides to attend the sermon. 7 colonists came too, among them the overseer of the warm bath, named Negrini, born in Plauen, who has seen the congregation at Neudietendorf and, by his account, had a brother of his own in the congregation. Of Hottentots there came 130-odd, not counting the children, who had their own meeting. We saw that the sermon could not possibly be held in our house, so we went under the great pear tree that the late Brother Schmidt planted. Before it was the children's meeting. On the second holiday the sermon had to be held under the pear tree again. Most of the men are at home on leave just now; but we had not supposed that so many Hottentots lived in this district. In the evening we had the first liturgy with our baptised, from the Dutch hymn book, no. 200 [6]. We can say that the Spirit of God powerfully proved himself on the hearts at the preaching of the Gospel of Jesus' becoming man.

<!-- lines 7381-7401 -->
On the 27th and 28th we were busy with our house building. On the 29th we announced in the sermon that Wednesday was New Year and there would be a sermon. On the 30th we were told that a girl of 17 or 18 had dreamed that the Saviour had come to her and told her she should be converted and not wait long, for it was time. This happened some days ago, and since then she goes about very thoughtful. A Hottentot told us that while working at the harvest he had often felt very happy and had had fine thoughts. Question: what kind of thoughts? Answer: "I thought about the torment of Jesus and what he has done for people; now I feel that I too would gladly be converted."

<!-- lines 7402-7444 -->
On the 31st we inquired how New Year's night was kept among the Hottentots, and learned that it is the custom not to sleep this night but to spend it in gorging, drinking, dancing and revelry. They learned this from the farmers, for it is the greatest festival of the year in this country. We had thought beforehand of keeping the close of the year with our baptised and candidates towards 12 o'clock; but since everyone stays up in any case, we resolved to hold a general meeting. But we did not want to announce it before the evening meeting at 6 o'clock, since we could suppose that otherwise very many would come and we would be short of room. So it was announced that this night at half past 11 we would have another meeting, to thank God once more for all the good we had enjoyed from him in the year now closed. Immediately after the meeting we saw some here and there galloping off on horseback as fast as they could, and others running on foot as if there were a fire, presumably to fetch their friends from the neighbourhood. By 11 o'clock so many had come from near and far that we were astonished, especially since it was raining and so dark that we at least could not have found any road. Before we rang the bell our house was already full, and the rest had to stand outside; and the greatest stillness prevailed.

<!-- lines 7444-7476 -->
At half past 11 the meeting began. We reminded them of the great mercy God had shown them this year, which we were now happily bringing to a close [7]: for he had let the Gospel be preached to them of the shedding of his blood for the forgiveness of their sins; whoever had not yet thanked him for it and had not yet asked for the forgiveness of his sins should do so in this hour, and so on. At 12 o'clock we stood up and fell on our knees with all present. Dear brothers and sisters! Here words failed us. As in the congregation the New Year is announced with the sound of trombones, so here it was done by a general loud weeping, which broke out all at once as we fell on our knees with them. We call on all our dear brothers and sisters to thank our dear Lord with us for what he is doing here, and to ask him to continue with his grace, and to lead and guide us his poor servants by his Spirit. At the close of the year our Hottentot congregation consists of 7 baptised and 10 candidates for baptism; in all 17 persons.

## Notes

1. *Hrn Brand und Voor*: two officials of the Cape government; not identified.
2. The transcription reads *und der Leim war juſt verſtollen, wie wir ausſahen*, garbled. The sense seems to be that the missionaries looked like the clay they were working. To be checked against the image.
3. The transcription reads *und das hieß nach Eſſen*; the sense is uncertain.
4. The transcription reads *Eine von denen, die neulich auf der Poſt waren, und jetzt noch uner Jahr waren, daß ſie unterweges Wein getrunken hat*. The middle is garbled; the verb, probably "confessed" or "said", is missing. To be checked against the image.
5. *die Jeſus-Geſchichte*: probably the Christmas story from the Gospels, or from the Moravian harmony of the Gospels.
6. *die erſte Liturgie ... aus dem Holländiſchen Geſangbuch Nro 200*: a sung liturgy, apparently from the Dutch Moravian hymn book; the edition is not identified.
7. The transcription reads *welches wir jetzt zu Ende küßen beglückterweiſe*; *küßen* is probably a misreading of *ſchließen*, "close".
