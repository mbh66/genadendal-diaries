<!-- lines 5818-5826 -->
Dear brothers! Here we send you a letter from Mrs Schmidt at the Cape, since this woman appears in the diary and will perhaps appear again. She is one of the pious society; we already have many letters from her, and from others too, which have a good deal of sense in them. So we thought it would not be unwelcome to our dear brothers to have one in her own hand [1].

<!-- lines 5829-5856 -->
Letter from Mrs Smith in Cape Town to the brothers.

"Esteemed and much beloved brothers, wishing you all good in soul and body, I let you know that I and my family are still living to this day under the protection of the infinite love of God, in the enjoyment of good health. I duly received your letter of the 19th of this month of August. To me, the most unworthy of sinners, grace has been shown. How infinitely am I not bound to my divine Redeemer, who has been pleased to count me, an unworthy sinner, worthy to be used as an instrument in providing a sheep of his flock with the first clothing for her body. May he make me heartily thankful for this undeserved [2] grace. May he further give me the spirit of grace and of prayer, so that by it I may go on drawing from his fullness all that I need, to continue my way with joy as a wanderer through this wilderness.

<!-- lines 5857-5880 -->
"May I always find by experience that my heart burns with love towards the Redeemer; may I experience in my soul what our newborn sister experienced, namely the thankful heart, which would be so moved by the manifold benefits I enjoy from him daily; may I keep testing what is pleasing to him, and pray daily: Lord, show me what I have to do. But alas! It goes badly with that. The blessed privilege is beyond price, that a thankful [3] Redeemer stands before the throne to think for his people, to use the expression of Paul, since he ever lives to pray for us. Now, beloved friends, we can [4] all still share in the privilege of the blessings Jesus grants you.

<!-- lines 5880-5905 -->
"I must bear witness that the presence of Jesus is plainly perceived among you; it is astonishing in how short a time four souls have already been won for Jesus. With all our hearts all our prayers and supplications to our Saviour are that in future sons and daughters may be born daily to Zion who will be saved, so that one day the earth may be filled with the knowledge of Christ as the waters cover the bottom of the sea; so that the fullness of the heathen may come in and all Israel be saved. May the Lord give us all the spirit of prayer for it daily, so that the stirrings of his divine mercy may be taken by storm. Do tell the new children of Zion, my dear friends, that we, Miss Schonk and I, are heartily glad at their coming into Zion. Blessed are those who have gone in by the door.

<!-- lines 5905-5949 -->
"He says: I am the door; my sheep know my voice, and they follow me. O my dear friends, I often picture you all as if you were here; your pleasant and contented life is often shown me in the spirit; the peace of God is there, for the progress confirms what I write. O how great is your privilege, to walk thus among simple people. Our friend's husband came to me and thanked me, and I said to him: 'Thank the Husband [5], for he has done great things for your house, and our dear Jesus wants to save you too. Consider what great pains Jesus takes to send you the men, and what all these people have left, and have come over the great water to preach among you poor people the Gospel of a crucified Jesus; they can expect no earthly reward or recompense from you.' To which he answered: 'Yes, Juffrouw.' 'Well,' I said, 'now you must ask dear Jesus to make you so happy too.' I said further: 'Your wife is indeed a poor Hottentot woman, but now she is my sister, for we have one Shepherd and belong to one flock.' To which he answered respectfully: 'Yes, Juffrouw.' Yes, I have been very glad and moved too at the great works of God. I have not yet spoken to Miss van Lier, but I am sure she will be very glad about Marie [6]. If I can be of service to our women friends with anything, they may tell me. I am sending something again for our Anna Marie. I believe that before long men too will perhaps be brought in.

<!-- lines 5949-5970 -->
"Beloved friends, you received the things for our Anna Maria according to the list enclosed. I would have [liked] to send her a better gift [7], but I must now settle something. If the Lord wills and I live, and I have finished this business, I hope then to do it more amply. I will send it to the brothers, and you can then share it out as you see it is needed in the congregation of our King Jesus. But what is done, keep to yourselves, and do not make it known to one or another. Here is also a little letter from a woman friend who sent the girl the striped cloth that was among the first things, so that you may also see what fruit she has enjoyed for her soul.

<!-- lines 5970-6003 -->
"Now, dear friends, I turn to the close, and commend you all, with your little flock, to the Prince of the congregation, which is bought with his blood. May he keep and shelter us all under the wings of his grace. May he raise his banner over us. May he keep, shelter and comfort us with his Spirit and grace; and when we have one day finished serving his counsel, may he take us too into a kingdom of eternally blessed light, so that we may then enjoy his kisses undisturbed, of heartfelt grace, for the sake of his suffering and death. Amen. I commend myself to your daily prayer, for it is an evil time, and that I may not come into temptation; for he goes about as a roaring lion. Be heartily greeted from me and my children, Miss Schonk, van Lier and the Reynevelts, Smit, Vlotman and the other friends. Live in peace, and may the God and Father of our Lord Jesus be the Father of us all. Amen. Cape, 20 August 1793. N.B. On 17 August I turned 44. Your friend, D. W. C. Smith."

## Notes

1. The diarists say they are sending the letter itself, in her own hand. What the diary copy gives is a German text of it; the original was probably in Dutch.
2. The transcription reads *unwurdigende Gnade*; probably for *unverdiente*, "undeserved".
3. The transcription reads *daß ein dankender Erlöſer vor dem Throne ſteht, um für Sein Volk zu denken*, "a thanking Redeemer ... to think for his people". The allusion is to Hebrews 7:25 and Romans 8:34, Christ interceding for his people; *dankender* and *denken* may be slips of the copyist or the transcription for words such as *dienender* and *danken*. Translated as it stands.
4. The transcription reads *wir kommen noch alle Theil nehmen*; probably *können*, "can".
5. *Eheherrn*: Christ as husband of the soul; see the note on *Ehmann* in the previous entry.
6. *die Marie*: Martha, called Mari before her baptism on 13 August, who had nursed a child for the minister van Lier.
7. The transcription reads *Ich haſte, ihr ein beſſeres Almoſen zu ſenden*; perhaps *hätte*, "I would have [liked]". To be checked against the image.
