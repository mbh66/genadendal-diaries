Today, [1 October,] all those whom we recently wrote down also had to go to him to fetch flour. He always gives them enough for 8 days, after which they must go to him again. On the 2nd Brother Marsveld came back. Baas Teunis spoke with him about the present circumstances, and asked that if new Hottentots come forward to live with us, we should first send them to him, so that it might be known who they were. On the 3rd one of the candidates came and lamented greatly that she felt so bad; at the beginning she had not felt so, but had been content; but now she could no longer gather her thoughts together. Many use this expression and mean something by it, but cannot find the right words; and it is almost always accompanied by tears.

<!-- lines 6576-6613 -->
On the 4th young Teunis sent us word that we should fetch with our cart the bell [1] he had brought for us from the Cape. We need it very much, because the bell we had made ourselves out of brass sheet will not do its work properly, since some of the huts are so far off. Our friends at the Cape had long pressed to get us one, and at last an opportunity came at an auction. It is 5½ feet round and 1 foot 1 inch high. As for its weight, the men have enough to do to lift it off the ground. There was great joy among the Hottentots when the bell arrived here. On the 5th another person came among the candidates for baptism. On the 6th the sermon was very well attended, which is really always the case, so that the room in our house is becoming more and more too small. On the 7th some went into the bush to fetch 2 trees between which our bell is to hang. Early on the 8th they were brought here, each tree drawn by 6 oxen. Today holes were dug and lined with stones, and on the 9th the trees were set up with the bell, so that it could already be rung today. The new candidate for baptism declared that she was very happy, and said it was as if she were in another place. At this time we had much work in our garden. It is now spring here, and everything is in full blossom.

<!-- lines 6614-6644 -->
On the 6th [2] our 6 baptised were spoken with. We can say that all of them care from their hearts to live for the dear Saviour; we only wished that they could express themselves more in words. Today Brother Kühnel drove to Baas Teunis, among other things to speak with him about the church we intend to build. The room in our house for the meetings has long been much too small, and not only we but also the friends at the Cape, and Baas Teunis too, saw the need to build a church in which the schools could also be held. All have promised to help with it as much as they can, and believe moreover that the Hottentots should be required to do the labour without pay. Baas Teunis has promised to supply the timber. We believe that in this way it can be done without great cost; only Baas Teunis would have to take it on himself to keep the people at work, for the Hottentot women are very lazy, and without his authority not much would be done. Brother Kühnel spoke to him about this, and he promised to have the people come to him and speak to them himself.

<!-- lines 6644-6686 -->
Early on the 13th we had a visit from 5 persons on horseback. We thought they would want to stay for the sermon, which would not have pleased us because of the cramped room; but after a couple of hours they rode on. Today a person was added to the candidates for baptism. On the 15th Brother Kühnel rode to young Teunis, to be present at the division of a flock of 400 goats, of which 106 belong to us. He came home on the evening of the 16th, and on the morning of the 17th a Hottentot told us that our goats had got as far as the Sergeants River, an hour from here, and that help was needed to get them through the water. We sent to the huts that all the women should go out, and since the children were in school just then, the bigger ones were told that they should help too. But the little ones wanted to be there as well, and so we all went out. Everyone ran as if there were a fire, and the children were first at the water. We thought: what will the little ones do here? But we did not know that almost all of them, the girls as well as the boys, can swim. They were in the water at once; indeed they took a goat, each by one horn, and swam across with it; then back again to fetch another; and so most of the goats were brought across by the children, with much enjoyment on all sides. On the 18th 2 [women] lamented to us the unrest of their hearts and were added to the candidates.

<!-- lines 6687-6732 -->
One of them had first spoken of it to a baptised woman, who announced her to us. In our conference in these days a person was added to the candidates for baptism. At this time we had a good deal to do about some children who go to our school but are kept back by the farmers against their parents' will, and yet we could not achieve much. On the 23rd the candidates for baptism were spoken with, and we found that they are concerned from their hearts to be saved. One said she would never have believed that she was as bad as she now felt, and for that reason she prayed so much the more to the dear Saviour. 3 others also came forward with the unrest of their hearts; only they still lack words far too much to express themselves properly. On the 25th 2 farmers visited us and brought us a packet of letters. One was from Brother Reichel in Berthelsdorf, and the others from brothers in Herrnhut, Zeist and Amsterdam, and even from Labrador. What joy this was, no one can imagine but one who has himself been at so distant a post. One of the farmers was with us for the first time, but we could not attend to him as we should have, for each of us was busy with his letters. Since no ships had arrived for so long, not only we but also the government at the Cape believed that things must look sad in the fatherland. But in these letters we found much cause to thank the dear Saviour for his gracious preservation from so many a danger.

<!-- lines 6733-6760 -->
On the 26th another woman came among the candidates for baptism. We resolved to read something to the baptised every week from the history of Greenland [3], so that they might also become acquainted with other posts among the heathen. On the 28th a beginning was made, with the letters of the Greenlanders, which caused a great stir. (At this time we had made some benches, so that now about 40 persons can sit in the meetings.) On the 31st we had a visit from a true friend from Cape Town, namely the watchmaker Andres, with one of his relatives. This man has already done much for us, and is also one of those who press most for the building of a church. He said that although he could not be away from home more than 8 days, he could not have held out any longer without seeing how we had settled in. There are many more at the Cape who would gladly visit us if their circumstances allowed.

## Notes

1. Both columns read *die Pferde*, "the horses", but the next sentence speaks of the bell, and young Teunis's errand is plainly the bell; *Pferde* is probably a misreading of *Klocke*, the spelling used in the next line. To be checked against the image.
2. *Den 6ten*: out of order after the 9th. Perhaps *16ten* or *10ten*; the 6th was a Sunday, when the sermon is recorded. To be checked against the image.
3. *Grönländiſche Hiſtoria*: probably David Cranz, *Historie von Grönland* (1765), with its continuation (1770), the standard Moravian account of the Greenland mission. The "letters of the Greenlanders" read on the 28th are not identified further.
