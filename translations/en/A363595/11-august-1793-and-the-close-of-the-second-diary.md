<!-- lines 5017-5104 -->
On 1 August, when we came into the garden early, we saw that cattle had been in it and had eaten off a great deal. We had thought we would soon get something to eat; now that hope is gone again [1]. Luckily a Hottentot woman who lives close by had woken up and gone out, and she saw the cattle and chased them out. If that had not happened, not only everything we had planted but also everything we had sown would have been trampled. The ditch that runs round the garden has fallen in at many places from the rain. A ditch that these cattle cannot get over must be deep and wide, for the oxen here jump over ditches so that one thinks it is not possible. On the 2nd we took some of the wood we had cut last and closed up the places where they got over most easily, as far as our wood went. We must try as soon as possible to get poles to make more. On the 3rd God ordained a special preservation for Brother Kühnel. He was fixing a piece of wood across the top of the ladders of our cart, because the ladders always fell together when we drove. He wanted to bore 2 holes through the top and put nails through them, so that it would not break off. He had no borer except one an ell long, so he had to stand on top of the ladders. When the borer was part of the way in, his feet slipped. He tried to hold on to the borer, but it broke in two in the middle. Luckily he fell past the piece that was left standing; he could just as easily have fallen onto it and driven it into his body. Thank God, as far as we know he has taken no harm from the fall, and has only grazed his right side a little. In the night between the 2nd and 3rd it froze, so that there was ice as thick as a taler on the small standing water, and it snowed on the mountains. Our pumpkin plants in the garden, which were growing nicely, were frozen; the other vegetables it did not hurt. We had not imagined it as cold as we now find it, for we were told it was always warm at the Cape. At the Cape it is indeed much warmer, because the town lies under the mountains and the cold winds do not reach it. Here beyond the mountains it is quite different, and further inland it is said to be a good deal colder still. But it is said not to be usual for it to be as cold here as this winter. We have very often wished for woollen stockings, and a stove in the room as well; though this cold is not great in degree [2], it is truly unpleasant. In Europe the month of August is always the warmest, here one of the coldest; they say that the winter ends with this month. We expected more rain. So far there have been few whole days of rain; at least it has not rained longer [than a day or two], and then it has been fine again for a couple of days.

<!-- lines 5105-5112 -->
On the 4th there was a black slave woman in the sermon today [3]. Afterwards she thanked us and said that she would ask her mistress (if she should come to this place) to let her come to church every Sunday, so that she too might know what is good or evil. She has 2 hours to her home.

<!-- lines 5112-5159 -->
On the 5th. Up to now the Hottentots have only driven their oxen and cows together at night in one spot by the kraals, and have not shut them in. So it has already happened a couple of times that some have got into our garden and eaten off what we hoped soon to have for food. To close the whole garden so that they cannot get in at all is beyond us at present. We have often spoken to them about making a kraal together, even if it were only of thorns, and putting the cattle in it at night; if they made themselves gardens, they could not keep anything in them either. They see the need of it very well and always say yes, yes; but there it stays. We thought that if we did not set about it differently, it would never come to a kraal and we would keep little in the garden; and this week some of the men had to go back to the Cape, and then it would not be thought of at all. So yesterday we resolved to tell them that the men should go together into the bush and cut poles; the women and children could drag them out; we would fetch them home with our cart, and we would also go into the bush with them and load, so that if we got some cattle we too would have the right to put them in. The school was to stop until the kraal was finished [4]; since old and young care so much about the school, we thought this would help. So it was done. Everyone set to with a will, and the kraal was finished by evening, so that the cattle could still be put in. That was quicker than we had thought.

<!-- lines 5159-5176 -->
On the 6th: since our flour for bread was used up and we did not know where we could get any, we had written to Baas Teunis and asked him to help us. He wrote that what he had was at our service. Brother Marsveld drove there today to fetch 3 mud, and came back at 7. They sent various other provisions with him as well, which came very much in season. These people do a great deal for us. We also received a small cask with a letter from Mrs Schmidt at the Cape. We had written to our good friends that one [woman] had been baptised, and she felt moved to send a piece of cloth for clothes for her.

<!-- lines 5177-5234 -->
On the 8th there were many strange Hottentots in the meeting, so that the room would not hold them. Some of the Hottentots who are here on leave must leave again tomorrow. They were commended to the dear Saviour in a prayer. On the 9th they went. They came and took leave; we begged them not to forget what they had heard here of Jesus, and they promised to think of it diligently. They all went to Baas Teunis, to take wagons and oxen with them to the Cape. Baas Teunis has received orders to send 300 oxen, with everything needed for that number to draw. He says he is not able to send more than 70 with what goes with them. On the 10th we sent our Hottentot to Baas Teunis to fetch 6 mud of wheat for us; he is willing to let us have as much of his own as we need for the time until slaughtering [5]. But he keeps them at the Tigerhoek, 3 hours further on, because it is not good for sheep at his place but too cold; so we must have them fetched from there. We have no sheep yet. At the beginning we meant to get some at once. The Landdrost at Swellendam had offered to help us to a small flock; soon afterwards we had a letter from the Cape from Mrs Schmidt saying that she had ordered 100 for us, with the lambs that go with them. We thought: what shall we do with all those sheep? But it seems we shall not get them. The farmer who is to bring them lives not far from the Kaffers; who knows whether the Kaffers have not taken all his cattle. Baas Teunis will write to the Landdrost; if he has none for us yet, he will see to it. We also sent letters to Baas Teunis to be forwarded to the Cape. We have had letters from our good friends there.

<!-- lines 5234-5261 -->
Since they take a great interest in us, we told them how we are. In our conference we thought before the dear Saviour of a woman who has been with us a couple of times and has told us of the unrest of her heart, and that she earnestly longs to obtain forgiveness of her sins and to become a child of God; and we received the direction to accept her as a candidate for baptism. On the 11th 2 [men] came from Baas Teunis to visit us; one is his rider, a German [6]. They were in the sermon, which was particularly well attended today, so that the room would not hold everyone, although some of the men are no longer here; and there was a quite special stir. We often wished that our dear brothers and sisters could see and feel how the Spirit of God owns the testimony of Jesus' becoming man, his suffering and death, which we poor people give in weakness. May he only help us further!

<!-- lines 5262-5325 -->
In the afternoon we spoke singly with those who are to be baptised on the 13th; but we did not tell them yet; we meant to do that tomorrow. We asked them whether, if the dear Saviour granted them this grace, it was their whole purpose, from the bottom of their hearts, to renounce heathen customs entirely, to give themselves to Jesus body and soul, and by God's grace to live only for Jesus in this world. The little they could say for tears was: "Yes, yes; that is the wish and longing of our hearts, and our prayer to the Saviour, that he would accept us poor ones as his children out of grace." Towards evening a woman came to us who has not lived here long. When she came we thought she would probably not stay here long (she is a Bastard woman and has a slave for her husband). She believed herself much better than the others and had much good about her. Now she came and said (she could not say much for tears) that she did not know where to turn for the anguish of her heart, because she must see herself as altogether accursed and worthy of damnation. She was directed to the Friend of sinners, who could comfort those in distress. One of those who were to be baptised also told us [7] of a couple who had said to her that they were so uneasy in their hearts, and wished that God would have mercy on them too. She told them they should come to us and speak with us about it. We have often told all of them that if they did not rightly understand something, or were in difficulty about something, or became uneasy in their hearts, they should come to us, or speak with whichever of us they liked, since it made no difference which it was. We have also heard that several have wanted to do so but are ashamed to come to us in their fallen state. We told them that they should have no shyness, whoever they were [8], and had no need to be ashamed: they came with their fall every day to school and meeting, after all. Nothing would please us more than if they came and said that they were in distress about the forgiveness of their sins, and asked how they could be freed from them to become children of God; then we would most gladly advise them, according to the grace the dear Saviour would give us for it, how they might become acquainted with Jesus Christ their Saviour.

<!-- lines 5326-5364 -->
Early on the 12th we had the 3 who were to be baptised tomorrow called, and told them that the dear Saviour had made known his mind to us that they were to be baptised, and this tomorrow; and asked whether they could promise us, and give us their hand on it, that it was their whole purpose by God's grace to live from now on for Jesus Christ and to renounce all heathen practices and customs. What feeling there was at the answer, "Yes, yes, by God's grace," when they added, "with all our hearts," cannot be described with the pen or spoken with the mouth. Afterwards we had the 2 candidates fetched, including the one to whom we had been directed on the 10th, and told them of it, but asked them to keep it quiet. In the evening it was announced in the general meeting, and here too a lovely and pleasant feeling could be perceived. Through this two more came who could no longer hide the unrest of their hearts and lamented their lost condition with tears. There are several who have been powerfully seized, but they are ashamed to say it. With several we have seen in the meeting that they wanted to hold back their tears but could not, and had to let them run freely. What shall we say? It is God's work.

<!-- lines 5365-5410 -->
The 13th. On this festival and day of blessing, so important for us, we held our first morning blessing early. At 10 o'clock was the baptism. It was just as we wished, with no Christian present; since we had announced it only the evening before, no Christian could hear of it. Our Hottentots all came, and it had to be done in front of the house again. What feeling prevailed, and the presence of God that could be felt, cannot be described. They received the names: the eldest Sara, formerly called Frandsina; her daughter Martha, called Mari; the third Eva, called Mitje. Martha, a daughter of one whom Brother Schmidt baptised, is a very clever and diligent person. She was at the Cape a long time and also nursed a child for the minister van Lier. When we came to the Cape, she asked van Lier about us and said that she wished, if she should find out where in the country we were, to go to us, and asked what he advised. He told her that if she wanted to do it he would not hold her back but would advise it, and at the same time urged her to follow everything we told her; if she did, it would be well with her. The 2 [9] Bastards mentioned at the last baptism knelt with us this time. One of the wives of these Bastards said after the baptism that they were happy who had come so far that this great grace was given them; she did not know what kind of heart she had, it was as hard as a stone; but she felt strange all the same, and felt something she could not say.

<!-- lines 5410-5448 -->
In the afternoon we held a small lovefeast with the five baptised (four of ours, and Lena, baptised by Brother Schmidt); we told them its purpose, namely the binding of love among one another. Lena declared that she could not thank the dear Saviour enough for letting her live so long, so that she could hear so much of Jesus our Saviour and could now believe that the dear Saviour would certainly take her to heaven. They all declared something of the happiness of their hearts. Two of the Hottentot women go in proper clothes; the third had borrowed clothes for the baptism. In the evening we partook of Holy Communion, with the Saviour graciously owning us poor ones. This was a true day of festival and blessing for us and for the Hottentots. They all regarded this day as a festival too. Some days ago we had told a few that we had a festival on the Tuesday; they spread it about, and so they all wanted to keep the festival. A couple of children who usually go in clothes here had their best on today. We asked why they were so cleanly dressed today, since it was not Sunday. Answer: "Because the masters are wearing good clothes too."

<!-- lines 5448-5457 -->
On the 14th the overseer [10] Plaisir came to visit us. He seems to be our true friend and is anxious to keep up the friendship. On the 16th another Hottentot woman came to us. She could say nothing for tears and knew no words to express herself, except that she asked to be allowed to come into the little church, that is, into the meeting of the candidates for baptism.

<!-- lines 5458-5483 -->
On the 17th in our morning blessing we remembered the little girls [11] in the congregations and commended them to our dear Lord for his blessing on their choir festival, in some verses. In our conference we thought of 2 [12] as candidates for baptism, to whom we had been directed. Among them was the husband of Eva, who was baptised on the 13th. He is allowed to stay at home because he cannot handle a gun. Besides him 2 old men stay here, who are too old. We have the best hope of several who have gone, and of those who must still go. But the sad thing is that they must go to the Cape. Several said that their greatest heartache was having to go away from here and no longer being able to hear God's word. We resolved to take the five baptised into our litany on Sundays. Early on the 18th we prayed the litany with the five; they were much moved.

<!-- lines 5483-5531 -->
(The slave woman was here again today.) There were again many strange Hottentots in the sermon, so that the room would not hold them. Afterwards Brother Kühnel rode to Baas Teunis's son to visit him, but did not find him at home. On the 19th he sent a letter and wrote that he was very sorry not to have been at home yesterday. He also wrote that if he could help us with some vines, he would do so. He has laid out a very large vineyard. He had also brought a mud measure from his father for us. In the afternoon Brother Kühnel drove there with our two-wheeled cart to fetch it. He promised to come himself tomorrow to show us how vines are planted here. This gave us much work on the 20th; we had to begin early to dig over a piece of ground. The garden costs us a great deal of work; whenever we have a little time we must spend it digging in the garden. We have now, after all, eaten carrots and white turnips a couple of times, and of the white turnips not only the roots but also the leaves, which taste almost like spinach. We have eaten lettuce too. Now the time is coming when the beans are to be put in. Next month almost all garden plants must be planted, and we have little ground ready for them yet; there is still much work to do. The almond tree that Brother Schmidt planted is still half green; this half is in blossom like a white cloth, rather like a cherry tree full of blossom. It is the first of the fruit trees to blossom. The peaches are beginning a little here and there too; we have a good number of them. Apricots and pears are said to blossom at the same time.

<!-- lines 5532-5555 -->
On the 21st, 22nd and 23rd we had much work with the water, leading it so that it can be let into the garden wherever one wishes, to water each part as one wishes. We do not need it yet for the vegetables, but the ground must be well wet, or the *Opütten* [13] will not sprout. Today we led it right round the garden, so that it runs along the whole fence. We must still *flechten* (cut away a fine strip of earth) [14] and bring it to other places where it is so much too deep; this will cost a good deal of work. On the 24th our dear Saviour directed us in our conference to baptise one woman and to accept one as a candidate for baptism. On the 25th, a Sunday, it rained hard early, but it stopped before the sermon, so that the sermon was fairly full.

<!-- lines 5555-5583 -->
On the 26th the husband of our baptised Anna Maria came back from the Cape. We had given him a letter to Mr Gordon (he is the head of the military here and the second person in the government of this country), and had written that this man was the husband of a woman we had baptised; that since the man had gone, the poverty was great, and she and the children had nothing to eat; so we asked, if it were possible, that this man be given leave and sent home, so that he might try to earn bread for his wife and children. We also wrote that since the men had gone things looked sad for all the Hottentots here, for who was now to earn bread for the women and children? Mr Gordon sent us the following letter by this man:

<!-- lines 5583-5635 -->
"Good friend and brother, I have duly received your esteemed letter, and in answer to it I may say that I was much pleased at your well-being and that of the two brothers, and at the blessing on the spreading of the Gospel for salvation [15], and I pray God to grant you further grace in it. With this, as you asked, the Hottentot Mouras comes back, and I will give as many passes as they ask for. But it is necessary for me to do everything to secure this country against a threatened overthrow by men who have become barbarous, who will no longer believe in God, and do everything to make all nations share in their godlessness, and want to turn everything upside down under the fair-seeming name of liberty and equality. But God will help us, and if they come I shall destroy them. Our latest news is that in the middle of last February they attacked Holland, took Breda (through the faint-heartedness of the commandant), and likewise overpowered Klundert and Geertruidenberg; that before Willemstad they were held up and driven back by a small force, through the bravery and upright loyalty of the commandant, Baron van Boetzelaer; and at the same time were beaten by the Austrians and Prussians and chased back into their own country, where through their injustice they murder one another. But in the end they will all receive their reward according to their works. When, brothers, when all is over, I shall [not fail to] come to you [16]; and if I can do you any service, it will always give me pleasure. My wife and children greet you warmly, as I do too, who sign myself with all heartiness your good friend and brother, R. J. Gordon. In the Castle of Good Hope, 23 August 1793."

<!-- lines 5636-5651 -->
Mrs Schmidt also sent something again for clothes for Anna Maria, and knives, forks, a small copper kettle, and two talers in money for a sheep; the man had brought all this with him. In the evening we had her called and gave it to her. She did not know what to say, but began to cry aloud and did not know how to show her gratitude. We told her that she should thank the dear Saviour for it, who had so moved people's hearts as to give her this.

<!-- lines 5652-5676 -->
Since one of us must go to the Cape again to buy various necessities which we cannot well order in writing, we had asked Baas Teunis to let us know when he meant to drive to the Cape, because one of us would gladly go with him again. Baas Teunis must go to the Cape himself four times every year, and if we go with him we can buy what we like; he takes everything for us for nothing, whereas with the farmers one must pay double. Today he sent us word that whoever wanted to come must get ready: when the wagons came from the Cape they would leave at once, perhaps this very week; he would let us know the day. We had meant to baptise on the Tuesday the woman to whom we were directed on the 24th. But since we would gladly all three be together at such an act, we resolved to do it on Wednesday the 28th.

<!-- lines 5677-5726 -->
We wished to spend our choir festival [17], the 27th, entirely by ourselves; last year's we spent mostly at Santiago [18]. On the 27th we had the woman who was to be baptised called and asked her once more whether it was still her whole purpose to renounce everything heathen, to give herself body and soul to Jesus, and to live in this world for Jesus alone. She declared with many tears: "Yes, by God's grace and help." In the evening we announced it in the general meeting. On the 28th the baptism was at 10 o'clock. She received the name Elisabeth. Because of the strong wind we had to do it in the house; those who found no room inside had to stay outside, since only half could get in. We must say that the presence of God could be powerfully felt. In the evening we closed our choir year at Jesus' feet. We asked our dear Lord and Saviour for forgiveness for everything by which we had grieved thy faithful heart in this choir year now past, for not behaving as faithful servants of Jesus, and where we had not thought or acted according to thy heart. We thanked him from our hearts, as well as we could, for everything he had done for us, for which we poor people cannot thank him enough when we consider what he has done for us poor ones in this short time, and with regard to the poor Hottentots; we can say nothing. Early on the 29th we had our choir morning blessing. At 10 o'clock we read to one another the address Brother Reichel gave at our sending off in Herrnhut, and in the evening we partook of Holy Communion. We can say that the dear Saviour graciously and perceptibly owned us poor ones in all the meetings.

<!-- lines 5726-5760 -->
On receiving more definite word from Baas Teunis, Brother Schwinn left us at noon on the 30th for Baas Teunis's, to go with him to the Cape. Brother Schwinn took our cart with him there, so that if he got some provisions from him he could send them back to us. In the evening Brother Schwinn came back again. It had rained hard, so that he could not go on because of the swollen rivers. If it stops raining, Baas Teunis means to leave on 2 September. When Brother Schwinn came to the post, there was something there for us (from the Danish fatherland [?]) [19]; this was why he came home again, and tomorrow he goes back there. We thought it was letters and news, for we longed very much for them. It was a letter from Brother Treschow in Copenhagen, which, as may easily be believed, was very dear and welcome to us. From his letter we could see [20] that he believed we would have received letters and news, which is not so; we long very much for them. The ship that brought this letter had an extraordinarily quick voyage of 3 months and 9 days from Copenhagen to the roadstead here before the Cape.

<!-- lines 5761-5800 -->
At the same time we received a letter from Brother Heuser at the Cape, in which he wrote that if we had anything more to send to the fatherland we should send it to him; he knew of no opportunity yet, but believed one would soon be found. Since we would gladly give you, dear brothers, news of ourselves and of the work of God, and had written a letter which Brother Schwinn meant to take to the Cape, to see whether it could be sent on, we are sending it, on this news from Brother Heuser, together with our diary, in believing confidence in our dear Husband [21] that he will bring this news safely to you, so that praise and thanks may be given to him in the congregations too for his great mercy, which he has shown to us and to the poor Hottentots. We will add that we hold the singing meeting, or singing school, begun with the children, twice a week in the evening at about 8 o'clock. The women attend it too in such numbers that the room is often not large enough. Brother Kühnel always plays the flute for it, and the children listen to it as well as one could wish, and their voices are truly beautiful. They learn verses diligently, and have already learned several to the tune "Einige Sorgen, das soll meine Weide" etc., also "Weil ich Jesu Schäflein bin" etc., which they particularly like to sing. We hear little children of about 3 or 4 years going about singing these verses.

<!-- lines 5801-5817 -->
With this we close our diary, and commend ourselves and the Hottentots to the prayer and intercession of all the congregations before our dear Husband, that he may give us wisdom and grace to serve this work he has begun according to his heart, and counsel us by his Spirit in all circumstances; for this, dear brothers and sisters, we need. Help us to ask it of our dear Husband! We remain your brothers, bound together in the death of Jesus: Hendrik Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn, Joh. Christian Kühnel.

To be checked against the image.

To be checked against the image.
