On 1 November they attended the children's school, and while the children sang the tears rolled down good Andres's cheeks. Our children have now properly learned 22 verses to 18 tunes. Towards midday the visitors left again. On the 2nd Brother Schwinn had an attack of fever, which weakened him a good deal, but on the 3rd he was better. Later he had a special preservation, on a journey on business to the farmer Kuntz [1], 2 hours from here. He was overturned with the cart, in such a way that the wheels stood straight up and he and the Hottentot came to lie under the cart. Had this happened a couple of steps further on, they could have fallen face first onto the sharp stumps of cut-down bushes [2] and hurt themselves badly; as it was, they came away quite unhurt.

<!-- lines 6788-6835 -->
On the 7th there was an auction at a farmer's, 2 hours from here. Since we needed various tools, Brother Kühnel rode there; but he found nothing for us except 2 mills and a churn for butter. Auctions in this country, by the way, are strange affairs. 2 people went round with brandy and 3 with wine and diligently pressed those present to drink; this is done so that they will bid high and drive the prices up. At midday eight kinds of dishes were served in great quantity, and on such occasions everyone helps himself without further invitation. Some come only to eat and drink, which anyone is free to do. One wonders to see so many people together, since they live so far apart. Among others Brother Kühnel got to know a farmer here who asked him to take a young man into our school to learn to read, and offered to give 50 rixdollars for it at once. He himself did not know a letter. Brother Kühnel told him that we were there only for the sake of the Hottentots, and that even with them the school was a secondary matter; our real task was to preach the Gospel to them; others had ministers and schoolmasters at the Cape to see to [3]. He pressed his request hard, and it hurt to have to refuse him. He said: "I cannot read God's word, and where is there a church I could go to, to hear it?" For in this whole great part of Africa, apart from Cape Town, there are only 4 churches. On the 8th and 9th we had much work leading the water in our garden so that it can flow wherever it is needed. The garden work is also becoming very hard now because of the heat.

<!-- lines 6836-6861 -->
On the 11th we held our conference with a view to 13 November, and resolved to keep this day solemnly with our little Hottentot congregation, as we do all the special festival days of the church. At the same time a person was chosen to receive holy baptism on this day. On the 12th we had her called. Asked how she was, she said very happily that she had wanted to come to us yesterday to tell us her wish to be baptised. The question whether it was her whole purpose to live only for the Saviour and to renounce everything that did not please him, she answered with more than one yes. On the 13th we had our morning blessing early; at 10 o'clock was the baptism of our sister, as she now is, Rahel; at 2 o'clock a lovefeast with our 7 baptised; and in the evening we had a special meeting for the covenant [4].

<!-- lines 6861-6895 -->
All three of us must say that hardly ever in our lives have we enjoyed the nearness of the Saviour so perceptibly as on this day. At the baptism, which was very well attended, everyone dissolved in tears. At the lovefeast our dear sister had to [...] each of the [...] [5] had made a deep impression, and they could not declare enough for tears how thankful they were that they could now believe that Jesus Christ was their Saviour too. Afterwards they said one by one that they had fervently kissed his dear hands [and feet] [6], and that they felt inwardly happy; they wanted to ask him with all their hearts to keep them with him and his wounds, and to ground them ever deeper in his torment and death. Those were their own expressions. We have often wished that our dear brothers and sisters could see this little congregation and perceive the feeling that prevails in the meetings. What shall we poor people say! Words fail us. Our prayer to our Lord and Elder [7] is that as the good Shepherd he would ground these little sheep ever deeper in his torment and death, as they ask, and would bring more and more to them, so that his name may be glorified among this nation too.

<!-- lines 6895-6936 -->
On the 14th there was a very blessed speaking with the baptised. They were still full of the blessing of the day before and could not say enough about it. Some said they had begged the dear Saviour with all their hearts to take them into his protection against the world and Satan, so that they might never again come away from him and his wounds. "For we," they said, "are too black to resist Satan, but the dear Saviour can and will help us." Old Lena was much revived that the Saviour had so heartily taken her up again and had now given her so many sisters. She also declared her wish that her children, who are still wandering astray, might be brought in. On the 15th [8], as soon as our door was opened, an old Hottentot came and said that for the unrest of his heart he could no longer put off speaking with us, for he longed from his heart to become a child of God too. As soon as he had gone, another came with the same concern. The last week has made a great impression. In the evening after the singing meeting a crowd of children sat down in front of our house, and when we looked out once more before going to bed, we found them all asleep in a ring, with their heads all together, which looked charming. We had trouble waking them to send them home.

<!-- lines 6937-6997 -->
In our conference on the 16th we had joy in choosing our Martha as a candidate for Communion. She has more knowledge than the others, because she was in service with the late Domine van Lier at the Cape. The husband of our Anna Maria also came among the candidates for baptism. On the 17th Andreas Otto, a well-to-do man living three hours from here on horseback, came to attend the sermon. He seems to be our friend and offered us his services wherever he could. After the sermon he said he had not imagined that the Hottentots were so eager for God's word. "If anyone came into our church," he said, "and looked whether a tear would be shed, [he would look in vain,] preach the preacher as he will; and here one tear follows another [9]." He expressed his wish that the Gospel could be preached to the so-called Bushmen [10] too, for they would certainly be converted, and that would be of benefit to the whole country. He said: "I know them well, for I lived close by them for nine years. If one deals with them properly there is no trouble; but that they are otherwise so wicked is the fault of the Christians themselves, for one commando after another goes out to shoot them. I was once with one myself, when 300 of them were shot at one time. This nation is very strong, far more numerous than the Hottentots. At bottom they are both one nation, and the Bushmen are descendants of Hottentots who were driven out by the Christians and now live in the mountains and forests and live by robbery. When they are hungry they come in great numbers and drive off the cattle of the colonists living nearest to them." Anyone who rides well can get there from here in 18 days, but with a wagon one needs a full month. This man has a brother in that district and means to travel there next year, and he offered to take one of us with him. We could give no definite answer to this.

<!-- lines 6997-7023 -->
On the 18th an old Hottentot lamented greatly the unrest of his heart. Today five men came back from the Cape who had been given a month's leave. The farmers are now in great want of them, because the harvest will soon begin. Baas Teunis, who had been at the Cape, sent us word that someone should come to him, as he had something to tell us. Brother Kühnel went to him on the 19th. At the Cape he had heard much talk of our schools and now inquired about them. He had been told that we might not build without the permission of the authorities; for even the English church at the Cape was not allowed to have a building. The second thing was that he could not take the church building [11] upon himself. The reason is that the post is to be sold or leased, and then he must move away. So nothing will come of our church building for the present.

<!-- lines 7023-7058 -->
On the 20th we took five Hottentots into work, to make our water dam more lasting. Taking people into work does not cost us as much now as at the beginning, because we can give them food from our garden. On the 23rd there was a great stir among the children in the children's meeting [12], so that few eyes stayed dry. There have always been some who seemed to be touched, but this time it was all of them. We wish that they would become a little more trusting, to ask when they do not rightly understand something; but they are still too shy for that. On the 24th there were more people in the sermon than ever before. This is because many men are here on leave now. Many also came now who had been wandering astray. We had already been looking forward to the church, but now that joy is over. Every day now both men and women came to tell us that they longed from their hearts for forgiveness of their sins. On the 26th three came who each told us, separately, that they used to be so afraid of hell, but now they believed they would not go to hell, and they felt so happy at heart when the torment of Jesus was spoken of. On the 27th was the speaking of the candidates for baptism, and several expressed themselves with much feeling.

<!-- lines 7058-7097 -->
On the 28th we considered in our conference how we could make more room for ourselves. For since our living room is at the same time the church and the school, we can have nothing standing in it, and all our things must be kept in our little bedroom. We resolved to build a small side house [13] ourselves, as well as we can, of clay in the Wetterau manner [14]. We made a beginning with this work today. On the 29th most of the Hottentots, and the bigger children too, moved away from here for a time to work for the farmers during the harvest. They can earn a fair amount by it, for a reaper gets three to four shillings a day and good food and drink. But the worst of it is that most of them already owe as much as they earn over the harvest. The farmers have the habit, when the harvest is over, of giving the Hottentots as much wine and brandy as they want; when they are drunk they are hired for the next harvest, and they must keep this promise, even though they often regret it. They also get credit easily from the farmers then. None of ours went away gladly, and we were sorry to let them go, because they are now exposed to every danger. But we could not change it, because the whole order of things here brings it with it.
