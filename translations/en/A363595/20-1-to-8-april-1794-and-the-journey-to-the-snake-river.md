Brother Schwinn writes further: "On 1 April I sent a couple of Hottentots into the bush to cut firewood for us. One must now lay in enough for 4 months, because in winter one cannot get at it for the high water. Today another child was buried. On the 2nd a woman said to me: 'My dear teacher, if only it pleased God to give me the great grace of being baptised into the death of Jesus and washed from sins with his blood! I have grieved the dear Saviour with my sins for so many years; now it is my whole purpose to live only for him in the world.' With this she burst into tears. Hanna told me that when she was recently at a farmer's on business she had watched a dance, and now she had become uneasy about it. She was told what was needful.

<!-- lines 8251-8292 -->
"On the 3rd a Hottentot told me that he had brought a small chest from the Cape for us; he had been strictly told to keep it from the wet on the way, but once he had driven through a river so deep that the water came into the wagon and wet the chest. Supposing that it might hold letters, news and accounts, I closed the school at once, to dry quickly whatever had got wet. But I found myself disappointed: it was a small chest of snuff that Captain Olhof had brought for us. But there were also a couple of letters in it that the minister Vos had brought with him, namely from Brother Marsveld's own brother in Gouda and from Brother Heg there. Pastor [1] Vos wrote with them that he wished he could visit us and see the work of God among the Hottentots, but his circumstances did not yet allow it. He is going to Roodezand, a day's journey from the Cape, and the minister from there is going to the Cape. On the 4th the catechising was especially moving. Afterwards a woman came to me and lamented that she was so troubled, for at one time she could believe and then again not. Another said she had believed she was not as bad as others, because she had not done this and that; but now she must believe that if anyone went to hell, it would be she.

<!-- lines 8293-8337 -->
"On the 5th there was again the burial of a child. Some came and told me of their state. It is becoming more and more plain that a new storm is gathering against us, which does not come unexpectedly, however. For since the Hottentots have been hearing and accepting the word of God, they will no longer let themselves be used for sin, and this causes great enmity. A near neighbour who was truly our friend before is now very angry. Several told me that we had been gravely accused before the government, and that we would have to move away from here to the Kaffers. 'If that happens,' they said, 'we will move with you.' The cause is the Saviour's; if we must move away, he will go before us. He has called us; he will help us through. Meanwhile his holy work goes on and on, and the more people try to keep the Hottentots from us, the more of them come. Today 3 Hottentot families moved here to live from the Salt River, 2 days' journey from here. On Sunday the 6th more people came to the meeting than had ever been here. I sighed to the Saviour that all these people might become thine own. Afterwards several came to me, whose expressions I could not hear [unmoved] [2]. On the 7th 10 women and 7 children, of those who had moved here the day before yesterday, asked leave to come to school. These are not yet all who want to move here; the number is not yet known, but there are said to be more than 30 of them. Until now we have been able to manage on weekdays with the room in our house, but now it is much too cramped every day."

<!-- lines 8337-8373 -->
On the 8th towards evening Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel came home again safe and well. They report of their journey as follows: "On 31 March we left here with our Jacob. At 8 o'clock we were at the post, where we breakfasted and fed our horses. Further on we saw a large copper snake [3] 6 paces from us, and soon afterwards another snake, called a sheep-sticker. Following the example of our Hottentot, we kept out of the way of the latter, so that our horses should not be bitten by it. Further we saw some ostriches, which keep in large troops in this district. At 5 in the evening we came to the farmer Kempe, with whom we spent the night. On 1 April, among other things, we had to cross a mountain so steep that we had to lead our horses. At midday we dined with a farmer named Herz, a baptised Jew. In the afternoon we had 2 rivers to cross, and at 5 o'clock we arrived at the Landdrost of Swellendam's. He received us very kindly and at once ordered our horses to be taken into the stable and well fed.

<!-- lines 8374-8403 -->
"His wife was away at another place, 2 hours off; but he had the wagon harnessed at once to fetch her, and she came home that same evening. She was very pleased at our visit and said: 'At last you have come!' She asked at once how things went among the Hottentots, was glad of what we told her, and showed a great interest in this work. On the 2nd the Landdrost offered to show us some more places in this district, and had a wagon with 6 horses harnessed. His secretary went with us. After our return we told the Landdrost of our plan to travel to the Snake River, to visit those Hottentots and to look at the land. He offered us a led horse for the further journey. On the afternoon of the 3rd we rode on towards the Karremelks River [4], with a letter from the Landdrost to someone who was to put us up for the night and take us to the Snake River the next day. When we got there he was not at home, and it was already dusk.

<!-- lines 8404-8427 -->
"After many difficulties, since we did not know the way and rode astray [5], we at last managed to get a Hottentot as a guide, who got on our led horse and brought us late at night to a farmer Beukes. We found that this is a poor place, among other things because of the water, which is quite salty and does not quench thirst. If one wants fresh water, it must be fetched from 2 hours away. On the 4th we rode on and visited in some kraals, among others the Hottentot who visited us recently and would like to move to us. He and his family were all eager to hear God's word. We told them that we had come in order to make the Hottentots acquainted with their Creator and Redeemer, and then told them the chief content of the Gospel. The [...]" [6]

## Notes

1. *Pf.*: probably *Pfarrer*, "pastor", written at the end of a line before *Voſs*. Vos was going to Roodezand (see `glossary.csv`); he is probably Michiel Christiaan Vos, minister there from 1794.
2. The transcription reads *aber deren Aeuſſerungen ich nicht hören konnte*, "whose expressions I could not hear"; a word such as *ohne Rührung*, "without being moved", has probably been lost.
3. *Kupferſchlange*: the Cape cobra, called *koperkapel* in Cape Dutch; *Schafſtecher* ("sheep-sticker") is not identified, but may be the puff adder.
4. *Karremelks-Revier*: the Karringmelks (Karnemelks) River, east of Swellendam.
5. The transcription reads *da wir nicht Irre auch irre ritten*; translated to the sense.
6. The text breaks off at the foot of page 87 with *Die*. The page images jump from 168 to 176 between lines 8427 and 8428, so seven images are missing. The lost pages held the rest of the journey to the Snake River, the end of the third diary (which ran to 9 April 1794), and the beginning of the next, up to about Whitsun (8 June) 1794, where the next entry takes up the text.
