Diary of the 3 Brothers Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn and Joh. Christ. Kühnel at the Cape, from 1 to 31 July 1794 [1].

<!-- lines 9026-9047 -->
On 1 July a man and a woman came to us. The man was very uneasy because he did not know whether he truly believed in Jesus. The woman asked leave to attend the little meeting. On 2 July an old Hottentot came and asked Brother Marsveld in his wife's name to come to her, because she had something to complain of to him. The complaint concerned her brother, who, as she said, would give her nothing because she had lived so badly. On the 3rd a boy was buried who had gone to school with us. On the 4th a young woman came and said that for anguish of heart she did not know what to do. She was directed to the Saviour. On the 5th we put 4 persons before our dear Lord as candidates for baptism, and he approved all 4. On the 6th several came again who complained very much of great unrest of heart. We asked them what they were uneasy about, and their answer was: that with their sins they had caused our dear Lord Jesus so much pain. After the meeting at 10 o'clock in the morning, which was very well attended, we made it known in the evening to 4 persons that they had come among the candidates for baptism, and a particular feeling prevailed. They promised once more to live wholly for Jesus.

<!-- lines 9048-9072 -->
On the 7th another woman came who, as she said, also did not know where to turn for unrest of heart. Brother Schwinn got a rheum in his head today [2], which affected his eyes so that for the time being he could hold no school. On the 8th was the burial of a child. A man and his wife asked leave to come to our school. It is very cold here now, so that we really feel the cold. In the morning everything is white with frost; when the sun comes, it grows warm again. In our garden the beans and potatoes are frozen, and there is snow on the mountains. On the 9th we remembered with sympathy our dear boys in the holy [congregations] [3], who kept their choir festival today, and commended them to the Saviour for his blessing in our morning blessing. Then came the speaking of the candidates for baptism [4]. Those who had come to it for the first time came trembling and quaking. One of them could say nothing for tears except: "My whole mind is to live for Jesus." There are now also 3 girls of 15 or 16 years in this class. For shyness they cannot explain themselves, and so answer all questions with yes [5]. Otherwise we can rejoice over them. On the 10th another child was buried.

<!-- lines 9072-9091 -->
On the 11th our sister Sophia came and complained that she was very troubled, because such abominations rose up in her, with which she grieved the Saviour. An old man was buried today. On the 12th, in the conference, the Saviour directed us to hold [a] baptism tomorrow [6]. Later in the afternoon was the burial of a child. Early on the 13th, while it was still dark, the often-mentioned good-natured Jan Prädgen came to us in great distress of heart to get advice from us, but for oppression of heart he could hardly bring out a word. He said he did not know what to do for anguish. It had often gone just the same way with his wife Susanna, but she could speak for herself better [7]. At 10 o'clock was the baptism of a child [8], with the Saviour owning it in a way that could be felt [9]. He received the name Prädgen.

<!-- lines 9091-9103 -->
On the 14th our sister Maria came, who has been with her husband at a farmer's 2 days' journey from here. She told us that she had dreamed there the first night that her book had been taken from her by us in the school. We had wanted to give her another, but it had nothing written in it. When she woke she had been very troubled about it, and had wanted to turn back and come home. She asked what would come of it. She was told that she had nothing to fear; she should only hold fast to the Saviour. It matters very much to this sister not to grieve the Saviour. She is very simple and open-hearted.

<!-- lines 9103-9127 -->
On the 15th a young woman came and [said] [10] that she was very troubled and at a loss about being saved. On the 16th an old man sent today to ask that one of us would come to him; he would gladly hear words about our dear Lord. Brother Marsveld went at once, but found him extremely weak. At 12 o'clock at night our sister Elisabeth sent to us with the request that we would visit her husband, who was very weak. For weakness he could say little to Brother Kühnel, but he testified that he was asking Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of his sins. Brother Kühnel therefore took the opportunity to lay it on the sick man's heart how necessary it is for our salvation that each one should be assured of the forgiveness of his sins through Jesus while still in healthy days. If anyone here is ill [...] [11], and then the house [12] or kraal where the sick person lies is so full of people that many have to stand outside. When one of us is called to a sick person by day, and they see it, everyone comes to the house, and then one often finds the chance to commend the Saviour.

<!-- lines 9127-9158 -->
On the 17th the man mentioned above [13] died. Our sister Elisabeth said that he had cried to God for grace and mercy until his last breath, and was conscious to the end. On the 18th we began to fence in the [lower] half [14] of our garden with a hedge of quince pears, so that the cattle cannot get in. This work gives us much trouble, because we had to fetch the stakes from an hour away; for in this country one does not have things to hand as in Europe. In August is the time to plant peas and beans. We mean to do it too, but whether we shall enjoy anything of it for all our trouble, God knows. We comfort ourselves with this: if we do not enjoy it, our descendants shall enjoy it. Already last year we planted some fruit trees in front of the garden, but nothing came of it, because we are not able to keep the cattle off until the hedge is grown. This costs us much money and trouble; for even if one sets strong posts in the ground, they are rotten within a year, so that they fall over. Poles and posts are very costly here and hard to get. One often has to have them fetched from the forest 5 or 6 hours away, and besides [15], they sometimes stand so deep in the forest that one must drag them half an hour, even a whole hour, before they can be loaded on the wagon. In the river there is [firewood] [16], but there are no poles there. One can imagine that getting them is very costly.

<!-- lines 9158-9171 -->
On the 19th our Elisabeth's husband was buried. It is curious that when a husband, wife, father or mother dies and is buried here, one hears none of the relatives cry aloud [17]; everything goes quietly. Brother Schwinn has now almost fully recovered from his trouble with his eyes, for which we are heartily thankful to the Saviour. On the 20th a particular stirring of hearts could be felt in the meeting, which was very well attended. [On the 21st] [18] Brother Schwinn [rode] to the post, and from there to Juffrouw Lier [19], to inquire whether we could get [small trees] for planting at these places. On the 22nd he came home again; he had got as many trees as we need.

<!-- lines 9172-9208 -->
On the 23rd was the speaking of the baptised [20]. Two of them were very troubled. One, the baptised Bedje [21], complained about her husband, that he was especially quarrelsome [22], and also lived very badly with her and was especially rough, and sometimes spoke out against her like this: "I am a child of the devil and am going to hell; you are a child of God." She asked us to speak with him, [which was promised her] [23]. The other was called Debra [24]. We asked her the cause of her distress. She answered: something had happened at her place last night such as she knew she had never heard of in her life. [Recently] a man [25] with his wife and her relatives had moved in with her, to go to school too. The woman has since died. Because more sick people are lying in the house where they live, they laid the body in a little house beside the big one and shut the window and door fast. In the morning the dogs came and had gorged themselves. At first people thought that a [ewe] [26] had dropped a lamb in the night, which the dogs had eaten because it was dead. On closer search it was found that the dogs had got into the little house by a hole (where the body lay) and had eaten it from the head down, more than half of it, so that nothing was left to bury but the legs. A rare occurrence! In the evening our sister Elisabeth came. She wept bitterly because she had missed the meeting this afternoon. She had been up in the bush [27] to fetch wood. When she came back, the hour was over. We had much to do with her to comfort her over it. In general none of the people here misses one of our meetings without the greatest need.

<!-- lines 9208-9230 -->
On the 24th we received 2 letters from the Cape, one from Mr von Schonberg and a second from Mr Lier. Mr Schonberg sent us coffee beans, which we had asked for. A [farmer] [28] who lives a day's journey from here brought them with him, and we have to have them fetched from there. We sent one of our Hottentots there for the purpose. Mr Lier asked us to [send] him one of our Hottentots who would like to serve with him, and also [to buy him 50 goats and some cattle] [29], because he had heard that [they] were very cheap in our area. But this man is quite wrongly informed, and so we cannot serve him. [On the 25th] the Hottentot we mentioned above [came back]; but the horse [he had taken] was so galled that we shall not be able to use it again for a long time [30]. On the 26th our sister Maria was approved by our [Lord] as a candidate for Holy Communion [31]. In the evening we partook of [Holy Communion], with the Saviour owning us poor ones [32].

<!-- lines 9231-9254 -->
On the 27th, after the meeting, we spoke with the husband of our sister Bedgen. We meant to speak to him earnestly about the bad treatment of his wife. But when he was told what we really wanted to talk to him about, he declared that he had come for that very purpose, to accuse himself. Everything we had heard was true; he had already become very uneasy about it himself, but so far he had not been able to help himself. He also promised us to ask Him [33] that such a thing should not happen again. On the 28th Brother Marsveld was called to a sick man who lives an hour from here. Brother Marsveld could talk little with him, because the sick man showed no desire and gave no answers, and had a strange feeling with it [34]. Early on the 29th 6 women and 2 young men came; 5 of the women already went clothed. They were from the Appel Kraal, a long day's ride from here. They asked leave to stay here, to hear what the others hear and to come to [school] too [35], which is gladly granted them.

<!-- lines 9255-9281 -->
On the 30th another man came who [...] only [...] to hear the words of God [36]: "I am a great sinner, I have committed many sins, but I would still gladly be saved." In the night a sick woman sent to us and asked that one of us would come to her. Brother Marsveld went. The sick woman longed greatly for mercy and asked the Saviour for grace as well as she could. Early on the 31st her husband came to us again and said: "Now I no longer know where to turn for anguish." The great love of Jesus for sinners was commended to him, and he was told that he should lay himself at the Saviour's feet and ask [for grace]. The woman died today. With this we close this diary, and commend ourselves and the whole work of God among this people to the faithful prayers and remembrance of you and of all our brothers and sisters and friends, and remain your brothers, bound to you in Jesus, Hendrik Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn, Joh. Christ Kühnel.

1. This is the second copy of the July 1794 diary; the first is entry 22 (lines 8763 to 9019), which breaks off on the 30th. This copy has fuller wording in places but is harder for the automatic transcription, and many of its misreadings can be put right from the first copy; the notes say where. The stray word *Hendr* above the heading (line 9022) and the numbers 2 to 10 in the text are left out; the numbers are the manuscript's page numbers.

3. The transcription reads *in heilligen Bundesgem..n*, with letters lost; perhaps *Brüdergemeinen*, "Brethren's congregations". The boys are the boys' choir of the congregations in Europe.
