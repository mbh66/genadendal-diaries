<!-- lines 7975-8006 -->
On 2 March Brother Schwinn had an attack of back pain, which wore him out greatly. Today a farmer from the Tigerhoek was here to fetch back 2 girls who had been with him but have now moved here. They are children of one of his slaves by a Hottentot woman, and by the law of the country such children formerly had to serve the owner of the slave until their 25th year. But this has now been abolished by the authorities, and the children belong to the mother and are therefore free. He had a German with him, who had studied at Jena and was to support him with his eloquence; but they achieved nothing with us, for we appealed to the orders of the authorities. We reported the matter to Baas Teunis, moreover. On the 3rd we renewed ourselves with thankfulness in remembrance of moving into our house a year ago today, and of all the benefits we have enjoyed since, beyond all expectation. On the 4th Baas Teunis wrote to us that we had done very well to turn away the farmer with his request; for if we had allowed it to one, more and more would come.

<!-- lines 8006-8032 -->
In these days 3 more children were buried. Of those who have gone home so far, all were still very small, apart from the schoolchildren [1]. On the 6th a boy of about 10, who lives an hour from us, sent to ask Brother Kühnel to visit him. When he came, the boy could no longer speak, but gave Brother Kühnel his hand and pressed it. Shortly before, he had been looking forward to the next Saturday and believed he would be well by then, to go to the children's meeting. He was commended to the Saviour in a heartfelt prayer. On the 8th Anna Maria and Elisabeth were added to the candidates for Communion. We had a separate meeting with Martha, in which she was blessed with the laying on of hands for her first partaking of Holy Communion. In the evening we had this enjoyment, with our dear Lord powerfully owning us. On the 9th another child was buried.

<!-- lines 8033-8066 -->
Among the sick who are still lying ill is a woman whose whole body is covered with sores and who is in great pain. But it is a true pleasure to visit her. She says: "I have much pain, but the Saviour suffered much more pain for my sake." Question: whether she can take to herself the merit of Jesus' pains, and has certainty in her heart that he will take her to himself into his kingdom out of grace? Answer: "I am not worthy of it, I know that; but I believe that he will have mercy on me, and I ask him for it day and night." And we were told [2] that she does spend day and night in prayer. The Hottentots set great store by dreams; and so this woman recently dreamed that we had told her that when she died (and she expects it) we must give her a letter to the dear Saviour to take with her, and that it must not be forgotten to tie the letter under her head. They dream a great deal about us altogether, and tell us strange things about it; but the content is always that we pointed them to the Saviour.

<!-- lines 8066-8095 -->
On the 10th a Bastard woman who lives half an hour from here sent to ask for a visit in an illness. Some time ago she was much tried, and bore patiently much ill-treatment from her father, who is still quite dead in sins, because she would no longer join in the old things; but afterwards she became quite indifferent again. Brother Kühnel now reminded her of what she had heard in the meetings, and asked her whether she had sought and found forgiveness of sins. She said she still felt lost and could not believe that she would be saved, and so on. She was heartily directed to the Saviour, to whom prayer was also made for her. Today at midday a young woman passed away here, of whom we can believe that the Saviour has accepted her in grace. On the 13th another young woman was buried with her newborn child, and also a boy of about 16. 22 have now died of this sickness [3], and very many are still lying ill.

<!-- lines 8095-8131 -->
At midday a doctor who lives 4 hours from here came to examine the nature of the sickness raging here. He found that it was a bilious fever, and said an emetic must be used against it. He made up a bottle of medicine, whose ingredients he had brought with him, and gave us directions for giving it to the sick. We also asked him about Brother Schwinn's leg, in which he has much pain. He declared the complaint to be gout, and advised us to use a certain herb that grows plentifully here against it. On the 14th another child was buried. We announced to our people in the evening meeting that if they felt ill they could get medicine from us; and 5 persons came to us. On the 17th Trin, the wife of a Bastard, sent to lament to us the unrest of her heart. She said she did not know where to turn for anguish, for she believed she must be lost. This woman and her husband have thought much of themselves until now, and said they had lived well from childhood and had grown up among the Christians. They could not understand that they had to seek grace and forgiveness of sins like the others. Now the woman has come to know herself.

<!-- lines 8131-8157 -->
On the 18th another child was buried, and so again on the 20th. On the 21st we brought in part of our maize [4], and were sorry that we had not planted more, since it is good fodder for the pigs, of which we have now got a few. On the 22nd in our conference 2 persons were chosen for holy baptism and one for the class of candidates for baptism. Since because of the raging sickness we can expect no visits from farmers now, it was resolved to hold the baptism on Sunday the 23rd. Towards evening, when we wanted to have the 2 who were to be baptised called, we heard that one of them had fallen ill [5]; and when we went there we found her quite bedridden. So we let the matter stand over with her, and spoke with the other, who declared herself moved and longing. On the 23rd she received holy baptism with the name Susanna.

<!-- lines 8157-8206 -->
Today we received from Mr Heuser a packet with some letters from Zeist and Amsterdam, which were very welcome to us, and also a letter from Captain Olhof [6], who brought us here from Holland. He wrote that he would gladly visit us, but his ship had to hurry on to Batavia. Mr Heuser also wrote that he had news that the ship *Noord-Holland*, which has not yet arrived, has a small chest and a bag for us on board. We are now waiting eagerly for this ship to arrive, in the hope of getting more letters, and perhaps our accounts too [7], for we still lack these for this year. On the 25th a Hottentot came from the Snake River [8], 3 days' journey from here, and said he would like to move here to hear God's word. He told us that the Hottentots in his district had been very glad at the news that teachers had come into the country for them. Asked whether many Hottentots lived there, he said there were many, and they were not so poor as those here; they all had at least a few sheep, cows and oxen. Some, he said, were still somewhat afraid because of the talk that they would be taken to Batavia as soon as they had learned something. For the rest, it was now generally said that when we had been a year in Baviaanskloof we would move to them, since Baviaanskloof was a poor place where not many could live. He said that they were waiting for us there with longing, but for him it had lasted too long, and he had decided to move here, and perhaps more would follow him.

<!-- lines 8206-8227 -->
On the 27th we received a letter from Mrs Schmidt at the Cape, with some rice for the sick Hottentots. She told us that the new minister Voss [9] had arrived in the place of the late van Lier, and had asked her about us and the progress of the mission, and had been very glad of it. On the 29th in our conference a person was chosen as a candidate for baptism. We also considered a visit to the Snake River, since we had heard much about the Hottentots there, but sometimes one thing and sometimes another, and would gladly see everything for ourselves. It was resolved that Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel should travel there, and Brother Schwinn will carry on the work here meanwhile, as far as he can. On the 30th these brothers set out on their journey.

## Notes

1. The transcription reads *Von denen, die bißher heimgegangen ſind, waren die Schulinder, die übrig da waren alle noch ſehr klein*, garbled. The sense seems to be that apart from schoolchildren all who had died were very small children. To be checked against the image.
2. The transcription reads *Und wir erzählten uns auch*, "and we told ourselves"; probably *man erzählte uns*, "we were told".
3. The transcription reads *an dieſer Krankheit, Schweiß und Stein*; the last words are garbled, perhaps a name for the sickness. To be checked against the image.
4. The transcription reads *wandten wir einen Theil von unſerm Wäſchhaus ein*, "we turned in part of our washhouse". What follows, a crop that is good fodder for pigs, suggests *Welſchkorn*, maize. To be checked against the image.
5. The transcription reads *daß die eine Braut geworden ſey*, "that one had become a bride"; since she was then found bedridden, probably *krank*, "ill". *wollen ruhen laſſen* is probably *wollten rufen laſſen*, "wanted to have called".
6. *Capitain Olhof*: master of the ship that brought the missionaries from Holland in 1792; not identified further.
7. The transcription reads *vielleicht den uns auch Rechnungen*; translated to the sense.
8. *Schlangen-Revier*: probably the Slang River (Slangrivier) near present-day Heidelberg in the Western Cape, about three days' journey east of the mission.
9. *der neue Predikant Voſs*: said here to replace the late Helperus Ritzema van Lier, but in the next entry he is going to Roodezand, whose minister moves to Cape Town. Probably Michiel Christiaan Vos.
