[...] [1] resolved to speak with the brothers about it. This woman came very early one morning, before we were up (she lives half an hour from us), in such great anguish of heart that she could say little except: "I must be lost for ever!" We saw that the dear Saviour meant to bring her away from her notion of being good. Afterwards she came often and told us how it stood with her. Her husband is still lying ill and could not be at the baptism. As was said before, he has now for once become a sinner [2], and his supposed goodness is gone. We closed these festival days with praise and thanks to our dear Lord, who had proved himself powerfully in a remarkable way at this festival of the outpouring of the Holy Spirit [3], on us and on the hearts of the poor Hottentots too.

<!-- lines 8451-8498 -->
On the 10th Brother Kühnel had his 33rd birthday; we commended him to the dear Saviour in some verses in our morning blessing. He said of himself: "I have had many birthdays in the congregation, but I remember none so blessed, and none like today's, in which I have felt such a thing in my heart. When I was woken today with verses by several of our sisters (for which Brother Schwinn played the violin), I did not know where I was, because I had not imagined anything of the kind. When I came to myself and thought who it was: heathen, who a short time ago knew nothing of the dear Saviour, and now praised and glorified him. What I felt at it I cannot say. In the morning almost all the Hottentots came together to congratulate me. I felt strange, because it was all unexpected and I had not imagined such words from these people. They congratulated me with these words. Some said: 'I wish God's blessing for the Brother's birthday.' The other words that belong to it I do not know. Others said: 'I wish the Brother for his birthday that the dear Saviour may bless him and give him what he needs for the dear Saviour's service among us,' and so on. What I felt in my heart at this I cannot describe." One of our baptised had come to Brother Schwinn and told him that they meant to wake me with some verses. He told her that when they did it, he would play for it.

<!-- lines 8498-8521 -->
(On the 11th Brother Kühnel went to the post [4] to speak with Baas Teunis about a horse for a grinding gear [5], which we wanted to have made at his place, because we could not get it so cheaply anywhere. Then Baas Teunis has an old, handy pan for distilling brandy, which he got in that state [6]; it has many holes and can no longer be used, and he cannot get it mended here, which matters a great deal to him. Brother Kühnel had told him that he would come some time and try to mend it. This was now done. Today was also the speaking [7] of the candidates for baptism, and we could rejoice over it from our hearts. Many of them had not yet been to the speaking and came trembling and quaking. When they were spoken to lovingly and told its purpose, their anxiety vanished.)

<!-- lines 8522-8559 -->
On the 12th a sad thing happened with Dorothea, our last baptised. She went into the mountains to look for wood for a kraal, and there she began to sing a Hottentot song and to dance to it as well. At once some who had heard and seen it came to tell us. We had her called at once. When we put it to her, she was quite indifferent and said: "Yes, I did that." We put to her what she had promised us before her baptism, giving her right hand on it. She had also said very often that she had renounced the devil and all his works. So she had lied about that, and her purpose was to serve the devil. She should go; we could not own her as our sister. On the 13th Dorothea, who had been excluded, came again and asked forgiveness, because she had sinned. She was directed to the dear Saviour, whom she had greatly grieved by it. On the 14th it was announced in the meeting of the baptised, with sorrowful hearts, that Dorothea was excluded from this meeting, because she had sinned against the dear Saviour and his congregation. They were all asked to bring this poor sheep before the dear Saviour in prayer.

<!-- lines 8560-8606 -->
(Towards evening Brother Kühnel came home again. Baas Teunis had had people see to the pan [8] already, but no one could mend it. Brother Kühnel did mend it so far that it can be used, which made Baas Teunis very glad. We owed him for various things; since Brother Kühnel took nothing for his work, he also took nothing for all we owed him.) That same evening we held our conference. Brother Kühnel told us that Baas Teunis had said that the church council had met at Stellenbosch [9], but he had not been able to learn the reason. A document had been drawn up, however (which was presumably to be presented to the government), and when it was to be signed, 2 of the church councillors said that they could not and would not do so; so nothing came of this document. On the 15th, a Sunday, it rained the whole day; yet so many people were at the meeting that the room and the kitchen were full. On the 16th the woman recently excluded came again. (She had already been there a couple of times.) She wept much and asked for forgiveness. But we told her that she should lay herself at Jesus' feet and ask for forgiveness, for she had sinned greatly, and so on. When she could say in truth that the dear Saviour had forgiven her, we would forgive her too. But for now we could not own her as our sister. Of our Hanna, who is still at the stranded ship [10], Baas Teunis said that she was behaving very well there and no one could say anything bad of her.

<!-- lines 8606-8645 -->
On the 17th: at midday our sister Elisabeth brought an old Hottentot woman who did not know what to do for the anguish of her heart. When she came she trembled and quaked, and said that for the anguish of her heart she did not know where to turn. We directed her to the dear Saviour. On the 18th a woman came who was very troubled that she was still so far behind, although she had now heard God's word for so long. It remains to be said that in the conference on the 14th we resolved to make the children's singing into a proper singing meeting, namely on Tuesdays and Fridays. Besides, the house would not hold them [11]. We also resolved to hold an evening blessing twice a week with our communicants, namely on Mondays and Thursdays. (On the 19th: we had sent our cart with corn to Christian Franz on the 16th, to be ground. But in these days the river had risen so high that it will not be possible to cross it for a long time. A tree lies across it, which serves as a footbridge, but it is only a pole. When the water does not go over it, people crawl across. We needed flour and sent our man to bring it across here, for it is still an hour from our house.

<!-- lines 8645-8666 -->
On the 20th we began to dig over a piece of the garden with our people, where we want to plant trees. This costs much work, for a kind of weed grows there whose roots are 1½ ells deep. One must dig as deep as that too, because they must come out if we do not want the trees to be choked. On the 21st the wife of Hans Mathens came with a letter from Juffrouw Teunis. She had complained about the Hottentots, although she had not been to us, and we knew nothing of the matter. We spoke very seriously to her about going there to complain without saying a word to us about it.)

<!-- lines 8666-8692 -->
On the 22nd Magdalena came and lamented. She said that for a time now she had been able to keep her thoughts on the dear Saviour. Yesterday things had come into her mind again that had happened many years ago. This had made her so uneasy in the night that she had not been able to sleep. She was told that as soon as a bad thought came to her she should at once hold to the dear Saviour and ask him to free her from it, and to fill her heart with his torment and death. (On the 23rd we received a letter from Brother Heuser; he sent us various dried fruits, which served us well in these winter months. This present was very welcome to us.) On the 24th, St John's Day, in our morning blessing we commended all our dear children in the congregations, and their festival today, to the Saviour for his blessing in some verses.

<!-- lines 8692-8724 -->
On the 25th was the speaking of the baptised; we could rejoice in them heartily. Sophia too, one who was recently excluded, came to the speaking. She asked with many tears for forgiveness and said: "For the anguish of my heart I do not know where to turn. At night I cannot sleep, and during the day I cannot stay anywhere. That Satan brought me into such a thing I cannot understand now." She said she had deserved to be sent away by God and by her teachers. But she begged for mercy from the dear Saviour and from us. When we had spoken together, we had her called in the afternoon to forgive her, after first reminding her of her baptismal covenant; and in the evening we announced it in the meeting of the baptised and asked each one to hold close to the dear Saviour, and so on; and a very loving feeling prevailed. In the Communion meeting our sister Anna Maria was blessed for her first partaking of Holy Communion on the coming Thursday. How the peace of God can be felt in such a meeting we cannot describe.

<!-- lines 8725-8757 -->
On the 26th a boy who went to school was buried. (On the 27th one of our cows had a calf, but it was so weak that we had to take it into the kitchen at night, because it was now so cold at night that everything was white with frost in the morning, so that the poor creature would certainly have died if it had had to lie under the open sky.) On the 28th we had Holy Communion with our four sisters, with the dear Saviour owning us poor ones. (On the 29th one of our oxen had been left lying in the field, more than half an hour from us. We sent at once to cut its throat. But the meat was only fit for the dogs. On the 30th we sent our cart to the post [12] to fetch flour, for we cannot cross the river now to have corn ground.) With this we close our diary, and commend ourselves and the work of God here to the prayers and remembrance of all our dear brothers and sisters in the congregations. Hendr. Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn, Joh. Christn. Kühnel.

## Notes

1. The text resumes after the gap in the page images (168 to 176) in the middle of a sentence, at page 10 of a new pagination. The lost pages held the end of the journey to the Snake River, the rest of April and May 1794, and the beginning of this diary; the date here is Whitsun week, June 1794 (Whit Sunday fell on 8 June). The baptism mentioned in this passage, perhaps of Dorothea, "our last baptised", took place in the lost part. The parentheses round some passages are in the manuscript; their purpose is not stated.
2. The transcription reads *Er iſt, wie ſchon geſagt, zum Mal ein Bruder geworden*, "has for once become a brother"; with *ſein vermeint gut ſeyn iſt weg* following, probably *Sünder*, "sinner". To be checked against the image. *ihrem Guthſagn* in the sentence before is probably *Gutſeyn*, "being good"; the normalised column has *Selbstgerechtigkeit*.
3. Whitsun, the festival of the outpouring of the Holy Spirit.
4. Both columns read *auf die Jagd*, "hunting"; probably *auf die Poſt*, where Baas Teunis lived. The transcription also has *ging mit Br. Kühnel*, "went with Brother Kühnel", with no subject.
5. *wegen eines Pferdes zu einem Schleiſ-Zeug*: perhaps a horse-driven grindstone or mill; the word *Pferdes* may be a misreading, as in lines 6577 and 7736. To be checked against the image.
6. The transcription reads *die ſo hat er gekriegt*, "which he got so"; the sense is uncertain.
7. Both columns read *Speiſen*, "feeding"; the next sentences show it was the speaking, *Sprechen*. The same misreading occurs on the 25th.
8. The transcription reads *Wegen der gedachten Schlange*, "the snake mentioned"; it must be the brandy pan (*Pfanne*). *hat Baas Teunis ſchon Gehabt* seems to lack a word such as *Leute*.
9. The transcription reads *daß auf Stellenbüsch auferhegen der Kirchen-Rath zuſammen gekommen*; *auferhegen* is not clear, perhaps "extraordinarily". The Stellenbosch consistory had authority over church matters in the mission's district.
10. *bey dem geſtrandeten Schiffe*: a ship wrecked on the coast, where Hanna (baptised 28 January 1794) was evidently working; the account of it was in the lost pages.
11. The transcription reads *Gehören wollte das Haus auch nicht zulangen*; *Gehören* is probably a misreading, perhaps of *Ohnehin*, "in any case".
12. Both columns read *auf die Joſt*; probably *Poſt*.
