Diary of the three Brothers Hendr. Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn and Joh. Christn Kühnel, at the post of Good Hope [1], from 1 November to the end of the year 1794.

<!-- lines 11712-11731 -->
On 1 November there was such a great stirring in the children's meeting as there has hardly ever been; for few eyes stayed dry, and because of the loud weeping we had to close. (Then we brought our goats across the river [2]. Yesterday we had asked the Hottentots to help us, and a great crowd of people came together for it, for each goat has to be dragged through singly. Other Hottentots took the opportunity to have their goats brought through at the same time. About 500 were brought across, of which only 125 are ours. Towards evening the overseer [3] of the warm bath came to visit us. In the conference the dear Saviour assigned us 2 men and a woman as candidates for baptism.

<!-- lines 11731-11786 -->
Early on the 2nd Baas Teunis's wagon master (a German) also came. He came to take leave, because his time of serving the Company is now up; he is now going to the Cape. With him came the son of a gentleman from the Cape whose father is a member of the Council of Justice. He and the overseer of the warm bath attended the meeting at 10 o'clock, which Brother Marsveld held; he spoke on the Gospel. In the afternoon they all rode away again. On the 3rd another farmer came who wanted people to weed his corn. Some also came who spoke with us about the unrest of their hearts. Early on the 4th our Dorothea came again and asked for forgiveness. We could believe that it came from her heart. She said that she would ask the dear Saviour to keep her from everything bad in future, so that she might not grieve him so again. 2 more also came who wanted to live here, and a little girl who was very troubled and said with many tears that she too would gladly be saved. On the 5th we sent our cart into the forest to Baas Teunis, to get some wood for weaving shuttles, axe handles and all kinds of necessities. If one wants wood of this kind, one must first have permission. Baas Teunis is the overseer of the forest, and it is now so strict that if anyone is caught in the forest, the punishment is flogging and branding [4]. In the evening we announced in the meeting of the baptised that Dorothea heartily repents of her misdeeds, has asked the dear Saviour for forgiveness, and by his grace now wants to live wholly for him; and that she has also begged us earnestly to forgive her and take her back into our fellowship. Since we have forgiven her from our hearts, we asked each one to forgive her too, and to thank the dear Saviour that he has set this strayed sheep right and brought her back to his flock, and so on. There was great emotion at this.

<!-- lines 11786-11835 -->
On the 6th a Hottentot came who had served the minister at Swellendam (that is some 20 German miles from here) and had now fallen out with him. He asked us to write his name down, for he meant to go and fetch his things, to live here. We told him that we write no one down. If his purpose was to hear God's word and he wanted to live by it, he could come here; but whoever did not live by it must leave here and might not live here. He said that this was his mind. Several others also came again and spoke with us; among them a woman who said that for unrest she could sleep little, and when she did fall asleep a little, she dreamed of us. On the 7th. In these days we heard from a gentleman from the Cape that a packet boat which was to go from Holland to the Cape was taken by the French on the third day after it had sailed. Mr Frederik had been its commander. This Mr Frederik was lieutenant on our ship [5], and showed us every possible love and friendship then. We are very sorry that he should now find himself in such circumstances; for, as people say, he is now said to be imprisoned in Paris. We firmly believe that there were letters on this packet boat from our dear brothers and sisters at least, which we long for so much, but which we can only regard as lost. What should and can we do, but ask the dear Saviour to let what our dear brothers send to us, and what we send to them [6], come safely into our and their hands, and soon to make an end of the unrest of war?

<!-- lines 11835-11875 -->
On the 8th we resolved in the conference to set up a separate meeting for the candidates for Communion on Saturdays, and to move the meeting of the baptised from Wednesday to Monday, and the general meeting, in which the New Testament is read, from Monday to Wednesday. On the 9th Brother Marsveld had his 50th birthday. Yesterday Martha came and asked whether we would not give her a pleasure: there were some who wanted to wake [7] Brother Marsveld with some verses on his birthday, and we should play for it. We gladly did this. Already before 4 o'clock in the morning some who sing well gathered for it; afterwards all the baptised came, and besides these a large part of the other Hottentots, to congratulate him. We were amazed at how quickly it had become known. At 10 o'clock in the meeting Brother Marsveld spoke on the Sunday Gospel. Afterwards 4 more came and spoke with us about the state of their hearts. On the 10th we sent our cart with 2 Hottentots into the forest at Baas Teunis's again to fetch wood, so that it can dry; these 3 [8] will spend until the end of this week at it. It is called a forest, but the wood must be dragged to the wagon half an hour and further before one can load it; and that is a way that one must crawl on hands and feet even without dragging anything.

<!-- lines 11875-11910 -->
On the 11th several came and said that in the kraals some still often talk as badly as they did before, and yet they hear something better now: "Our dear Lord has done so much for us, and still such bad things can be said, with which he is grieved." On the 12th the speaking of the baptised was to have been held, but we let it lapse this time. On 13 November, the Elders' Festival [9], we had a festival morning blessing early with our baptised, in which the purpose of this festival was told them. Then in a prayer on our knees we thanked him for all his protection and help, and asked forgiveness where we had not met his mind; and we did homage to him anew and promised him obedience and new faithfulness, at which he let us all feel his peace powerfully. At 10 o'clock we read the address which Brother Blüher [10] gave at Herrnhut on 13 November 1792. At 2 o'clock in the afternoon we held a lovefeast with our 17 baptised. Hanna was still missing, who is still going astray. At this lovefeast love for one another, and honesty towards us, were commended to them most heartily.

<!-- lines 11911-11962 -->
On the 14th the farmer Marx [11] came to visit us with his brother-in-law, who lives at Stellenbosch. Several Hottentots also came to speak with us about the wishes of their hearts. Early on the 15th, as soon as the meeting of the candidates for Communion was over, another farmer came; he wanted to hire Hottentots to help him with the harvest, which will soon begin. As he rode away, another came who had come from the Cape and lives close to Swellendam. He was a Hessian by birth. He had been 5 years at Batavia, then gone back to Europe, and has now been here again for 12 years. He was released by the Company 2 years ago, and is a wheelwright by trade. He stayed until after midday. He brought us no cheering news from the Cape, which he had heard there, concerning the French. He said that they were said to have taken many ships, English as well as Dutch, and that they held the whole of Brabant. In the evening we had Holy Communion, with the dear Saviour owning us poor ones. Early on the 16th at 7 o'clock another farmer came already. But [12] the Communion liturgy was already over. Until now we have remained undisturbed in such meetings, because they are early. These farmers [keep] no fixed time, but go by the daylight: when it is light early, they are early. Now the candidates for baptism are there for their meeting already at half past 5, and often earlier; it is on Wednesdays and Saturdays. After it comes the children's meeting, and on Saturdays, after the children's meeting, the meeting of the candidates for Communion. The farmer mentioned above stayed for the meeting in which Brother Marsveld spoke on the Sunday Gospel; after the meal he rode away again.

<!-- lines 11962-12058 -->
Early on the 17th a girl came and said that she was quite faint with sorrow over her sins, and she would gladly be saved, which she declared with many tears. Afterwards a man came with his wife, and each accused the other. The man said that she would not obey him at all, but wanted him to obey her, which was not fitting at all, and now she had been away from him for 3 days at her brother's. The woman said that several came to them and talked badly, as they had always lived, and she had gone away so as to hear the words of our dear Father and live by them, and not hear such bad talk any more. Since after several questions both were at fault, they were told that the one was as much to blame as the other, and that they should ask each other's forgiveness and ask our dear Lord to forgive them too; and the people who came and talked so badly they could tell to leave the house. They promised to do all this. 2 farmers also came again to visit us; one was young Mrs Morgel's son. On the 18th we received a letter from Baas Teunis's son, whose place is a good hour from here. When we came, as can be seen from our first diary from here, he had not yet finished his house. This place belonged to the Hottentots, as his father has often told us himself. The Edle Heer Rhenius [13] had given it to him on condition that he did no harm to any Hottentot, much less drove them away; as soon as a complaint came that he did that, he must leave the place. Until now he has also said nothing about the Hottentot Dirk [14] not grazing there. Hottentots also lived close by him, who now live a quarter mile from us. Now he writes that we should tell the Hottentots to watch their cattle well: he was not obliged to let them graze on his ground and let their herds do him damage; if they did him damage, he could give them a good few blows and have the damage paid. This had been told him by the government, and if they would not believe it, they could go to it and ask. That the Hottentots do not look after their cattle is the truth. We have already talked much with them about it, that they should keep a herdsman with them, and have taken all possible trouble; they say "Yes, yes", but it stays at "yes, yes". This now puts us in perplexity: if he has got this place in such a way that he may chase away the Hottentots' cattle as far as belongs to a farm, then we are so hemmed in that the Hottentots' cattle must die of hunger. God knows how and what is yet to come of it. We can say with truth that the devil is angry. We hear from all sides that the farmers are very incensed against us and wish us who knows where, and talk so badly of us to the Hottentots that it is astonishing what we are supposed to be doing with the Hottentots. Well, it is God's work. May he only give us the grace to stay close to him and his wounds, and not to lose heart through the schemes of Satan. Yet when a farmer comes to us, we are always very friendly. The letter from Baas Teunis's son we read out in the schools of both the men and the women, and urged them most earnestly to keep a herdsman with the cattle.

<!-- lines 12059-12118 -->
On the 19th. Since for some time the Hottentots, when they have been at the farmers' and done something bad, have come at once of their own accord and told it, and at the same time asked forgiveness, strange statements and accounts often come up, at which we are often ashamed, for they name the thing plainly, without one having to ask much. (We will give the statements of the two who were here today. The first: "While I was at Frans's just now, bad things happened, for which I ask forgiveness." Question: what, then? Answer: "There was a fellow from the fatherland there [15], and when I went out to the cattle in the field he came to me and fell upon my body." When she was asked whether she had then committed fornication, though she had heard God's word, and so on, she answered: "I would not let him upon my body, and said he must keep away from me; but he took me, and I had to hold still for him. Then he came to me again in the stable; he shut the door and laid me down, and came upon my body again, and I had to hold still for him again." [16] Stefaanus Sophia's daughter [17] (she has had a husband who has been away for a time but has not released her, so that she still belongs to him) now wants to keep company with another man ("zuhalten", as they call it). Her mother does not want this, nor do his parents, because she has a husband. Today 3 of the baptised came (her mother was here yesterday and told it), and also several others who do not belong to us, and said that this was not right and was not approved among them either; and since they now heard God's word, they must also live differently. So her book was taken from her in the school, and she was told that we did not want to teach whores. She answered that he had not yet been upon her body. Soon afterwards the fellow came and said that he would leave her alone. He said: "She did creep into my lap, but I have not yet been upon her body, and have not done anything bad with her yet." Many such complaints come now, which, as is easy to imagine, is no easy thing for us; yet we must listen to them when they come, and admonish and rebuke them too [18].

<!-- lines 12118-12171 -->
On the 20th the farmer Louis Frie came to visit us; he stayed over midday. In the afternoon Plaisir also came to visit us; he now lives 4 miles from here. He stayed the night. We now get many visits from the farmers, most of them to hire Hottentots. If they come at mealtime they must be given food, for which very few give thanks, because it is the fashion. This is hard for us, because we have to buy bread, meat and wine. We treat them as kindly as possible; yet it is said of us that when someone visits us we do not offer him a chair. Since we had no more than our 3 ship's chairs, we have always (when someone came) given him a chair and sat on the bench ourselves. We think: let them say what they will; we lose nothing by it. On the 21st a Hottentot came and said that a farmer had asked him who created the world. Answer: God. Question: what was the first man called? Answer: Adam, and the woman Eva. At this the farmer said: "That is not true; the first man was called Noah. These people teach you wrongly." The Hottentot replied: "These people do not teach us wrongly, they teach us right; perhaps you were not rightly taught" [19]; at which the farmer grew angry. Then another asked him: "From what was the woman made?" Answer: "From a rib of Adam." "Quite right," said this one. A woman also came (she was at a farmer's) and told him that she now wanted to go to the school too. The farmer said to her: "Stay with me; what do you want there? I will give you a book and teach you myself." The woman answered him: "If Baas had wanted to teach me, Baas could have done it long ago; now I do not want to be taught by Baas, but I want to move to Baviaanskloof." On the 22nd Hanna came and asked again to be taken back; but we have not yet heard a good report of her, and so could not take her back for the time being.

<!-- lines 12171-12232 -->
On Sunday the 23rd we had many visitors from among the Christians, and it really seemed as if they meant to bankrupt us with eating and drinking. Young Mrs Teunis had sent word yesterday that she wanted to come to church with her daughter and her daughter's husband. Early at 7 o'clock 6 came on horseback, whom we waited on. Among these was Mary [20], who was here last Sunday too; now he brought his family. Then came young Mrs Teunis with 6 more persons; so now there were 12. Today the weather was calm, so that we could hold the meeting under the pear tree; if it had had to be in the house, it would have been half filled by the visitors, for the young women will not sit so close. When the meeting was over, another farmer came, named Mildekok, [from] 5 miles from here. Brother Marsveld held the meeting and spoke on the Sunday Epistle. The matter of room in the meeting had worked itself out; but now we were in difficulty about the food. With the guests mentioned first we had no difficulty in dealing, but only with young Mrs Teunis and her company. They noticed that we were [put out] [21] on their account, so they had the horses saddled before the meal. They rode away, and we were glad. After all, we have come for the Hottentots, and not for the Christians. It almost seems as if the Sunday meeting were to be for Christians, for our Hottentots have to stand outside, and with [the visitors] [22] it is mostly only curiosity. Yet if we had room, and they did not want food and drink, they might well come; perhaps one or another would sometimes get a blessing for his heart. This time we still got off well; but if one reckons only 2 glasses of wine for each, how much wine is used up! And yet we cannot do otherwise (or else it would happen to us, when we go anywhere, that no one would keep us overnight, as happened to Brother Schwinn when he rode to the Cape lately). At 4 o'clock young Mrs Teunis drove away again with her company.)

<!-- lines 12232-12255 -->
On the 24th. Since we have often heard from our good friends at the Cape, and now again from Martinus Schmidt, that the minister Vos at Roodezand greatly wishes to speak with one of us (we have heard that he is much stirred that the poor Hottentots accept the Gospel so and are so eager for it), we had resolved that Brother Marsveld should ride there. So he rode off there early today, accompanied by our good wishes [23], with one of our Hottentots. On the 25th several came who spoke with us about the perplexity of their hearts. 3 more farmers also came to hire Hottentots. Today many Hottentots also went to the farmers to cut barley and then corn. One of our candidates for baptism also came and told [us]:

<!-- lines 12255-12317 -->
When he was hired at a farmer's lately, another farmer, who lives with him, stayed there overnight. When this man heard that there was a Hottentot there who goes to school, he wanted to speak with him. The Hottentot was already asleep. He was called and had to come into the room. This farmer had put endless questions to him, which the Hottentot had answered astonishingly well; we will give some of them here. Question: "What are you taught, good or bad?" Answer: "Good is taught us, Baas." Question: "What is good?" Answer: "God's word is good, and that is taught." Question: "Who created the first people?" Answer: "God." Question: "What were they called?" Answer: "Adam and Eva." Question: "Where was Christ born?" Answer: "Baas must wait a little for me; I cannot say the name of the place at once." He said this twice, then: "At Bethlehem in the Jewish land." Question: "Who visited Jesus first?" Answer: "The shepherds in the field." Farmer: "That is not true; the wise men from the East." Question: "Are you taught high or low?" Answer: "I thank Baas that we are taught high, for what is higher and more than God's word?" Question: "Now that you are taught, you are surely also taught that you need no longer be subject to the Christians, but are to become baas over them?" Answer: "We are taught that our dear Lord says that we must be obedient to all to whom we owe obedience, and no longer do such bad things as we have done until now." Question: "Do you want to do good?" Answer: "With God's help, yes. Of ourselves we cannot, but God will give us the strength for it." Question: "Can you read?" Answer: "No!" Question: "How long have you been going to school?" Answer: "About a year." Farmer: "A year, and still cannot read? What a stupid person you are!" Answer: "A man is by nature unfit for anything good. We must receive all that we have as a gift from our dear Lord, for of ourselves we cannot even think anything good, much less do anything good," and so on. He had asked many more questions, which the Hottentot answered. Then the Hottentot noticed that the farmer meant to go too far with his questions, and so the Hottentot said: "My Baas wants me to go back to my work early tomorrow, and so I must sleep first. Good night, Baas!"

<!-- lines 12317-12366 -->
On the 26th several went to the farmers again to cut corn. When we say corn, wheat is always to be understood by it. Rye grows well too, but it is not the fashion to bake bread from it. Several also came who spoke with us about the unrest of their hearts. Today [the speaking] of the candidates for baptism [24] was also to have been held, but we let it lapse this time, announced it early in the meeting, and said that whoever wanted to speak with us could come; and several came at once. On the 27th the husband of our sister Rahel [25] came and said with many tears that he felt as if he must go into eternal damnation. The mercy of the Saviour was [commended] to him [26]. On the 28th several of the baptised came [saying] that they longed greatly to have the grace to go further. Others also came who talked with us about the distress of their hearts. We also heard that our sister Anna has a very hard time with her husband. He wants to know what we do with her when we take her alone. When she tells him that they had meetings, he will not believe it, but says that if it were so, others could hear it too. Lately, when she became a candidate for Communion, he was especially incensed, and wanted to know what we were up to with them in the evenings. He has used very bad words against her; but she begged us earnestly to say nothing to him, or else she would get beaten [27]. On the 29th many went to the farmers again; to one farmer alone went 17 together, men, women and big children. The schools and meetings will not be so large for the time. Towards evening Brother Marsveld came home again from his visit to Roodezand, well and in good health. He gives the following account of his visit:

<!-- lines 12367-12382 -->
"I travelled from here on the 24th, with the blessing of my dear brothers [28], to the minister Vos at Roodezand. At 6 o'clock in the evening I came to Sybrandt de Beer, whom I found in his field. He asked whether I would not come into his house; and when I answered that I still wanted to reach the warm bath and spend the night there, he said: 'That is still nearly ¾ hours away [29]; stay the night with me,' which I did; and we had fine conversations about our salvation.

<!-- lines 12378-12380 -->
[Footnote to the 28th, marked # in the manuscript:] In the evening Brother Schwinn read the 17th and 18th chapters of Luke. The [singing meeting] [30], which he also held, had to be in the house because of the strong wind.

<!-- lines 12383-12466 -->
"On the 25th I rode on, and in the evening arrived at the minister Vos's at Roodezand, who was glad of my visit. This evening we had much to talk of about the fatherland; 'but tomorrow,' he said, 'we will talk together again'; whereupon I asked him to question me about everything. A chapter from Paul's letter to the Romans was read, after which we fell on our knees and he made a long prayer. On the 26th before breakfast we knelt down likewise, and he again made a fairly long prayer. After breakfast we went into the garden. Here he said: 'Now, my Brother Marsveld, how many baptised have you?' Answer: '16.' Question: 'Do they also make a public confession of faith?' Answer: 'Before baptism a couple of questions are put to them, which they answer before the whole meeting.' Question: 'How often do you hold meetings?' Answer: 'Every day.' Question: 'What is done in them?' Answer: 'Twice there is a Bible reading and twice an address; on Wednesdays and Saturdays there is a children's meeting, and on Saturdays we also speak on some verses from the Bible.' Question: 'How do you baptise?' Answer: 'In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost.' In the morning there was catechising for young people; the minister Vos asked me whether I would attend it, and I accepted. In the evening he held instruction for negro slaves, at which I was also present at his wish, as was my Hottentot. He asked him: 'Who made the first people?' Answer: 'God.' Question: 'What were they called?' Answer: 'The man Adam, and the woman Eva.' Question: 'Out of what did God make people?' Answer: 'The man out of earth, the woman out of a rib of Adam.' Towards evening a farmer sent a slave woman to me and asked me to visit him on my way back. After supper the minister Vos asked me whether I would not hold the evening prayer; but I asked him to spare me that, since he was a minister. He pressed me hard, but I would not agree to it. On the morning of the 27th he wanted me to say the morning prayer; but I asked him not to take it amiss that I declined altogether. He said: 'But you do it in your meeting.' I answered: 'Yes, there I have Hottentots before me, and to them I am called.' At 9 o'clock I took a friendly leave and began my return journey. My visit had not turned out as I had expected. In the evening I came to Jacob Hugo, with whom I spent the night. On the 28th I rode on and came to a woman who was over 70 years old. She told me that Brother Schmidt of blessed memory [31] had once stayed with her for 3 days and had many conversations with her; why did we not do the same? I said: we have not the time for it. I spent the night with Daniel Tooy. On the 29th I arrived at Baviaanskloof with my dear brothers, heartily thankful to the dear Saviour. On the whole I have found better Christians on this journey than there are in our area." Now the diary continues:

<!-- lines 12467-12469 -->
On the 30th the meeting was so large that one could hardly notice that so many are away.

## Notes

1. The transcription reads *auf der Poſt der guten Hoffnung*, "at the post of Good Hope", here and in the heading of the January 1795 diary (line 13177); elsewhere the headings have *auf der Caap* or *am Kap*. It may be a misreading of *Caap*, or the diarists' own phrase. To be checked against the image.
2. The opening parenthesis here has no closing parenthesis in the transcription. On the parentheses, see entry 26, note 14.
3. The transcription reads *der Vorſchlag von dem warmen Bad*, "the proposal from the warm bath"; probably *der Aufſeher*, "the overseer", who is named again on the 2nd. Probably Negrini (see `glossary.csv`). The next sentence reads *In der Conferenz wir uns der l. Heiland ... an*, with *wies* missing.
4. The transcription reads *Gießbald⸗ und Brandmarken*; probably *Geißeln* or *Geißelung*, "flogging", and branding.
5. The ship that brought the missionaries to the Cape in 1792, whose master was Captain Olhof (see `glossary.csv`). Mr Frederik is not otherwise identified.
6. The transcription reads *was unſere lieben Brüder an uns geſchehen, wie auch dasjenige, was wir an ſie geſchehen*; probably *geſchickt*, "sent", as the translation takes it.
7. The transcription reads *wärden*, probably *wecken*, "wake".
8. The transcription has *dieſe 3*, "these 3", after mentioning "2 Hottentots"; perhaps the cart is counted, or a third man went.
9. 13 November, the Moravian festival of Christ as Chief Elder of the church, kept since 1741.
10. *Bruder Blüher* is not identified. To be checked against the image.
11. *Bauer Marx*, probably the same as the *Mary* who came on the 23rd with his family "who was here last Sunday too", and perhaps of the *Marcis* or *Mareis* family of September and October.
12. The transcription reads *Noch war die Abendmahls⸗Liturgie ſchon aus*; probably *Doch*, "but". The next sentence lacks a verb (*Dieſe Baurn keine gewiſſe Zeit*).
13. The transcription gives Rhenius the title *der Edle Herr*, which elsewhere belongs to the head of the government (see entry 27, note 13). In 1792 and 1793 Rhenius was acting head of the government (see `glossary.csv`).
14. *Dirk*, a Khoekhoe man whose cattle grazed near the place of Baas Teunis's son; not otherwise identified.
15. *ein Vatterländiſcher Kerl*, "a fellow from the fatherland", that is, a man from Europe. *Frans* is not identified.
16. The woman's account, as the diarists record it, is of being forced; they record it among the confessions of sins.
17. The transcription reads *Stefaanus Sophia Tochter*; perhaps "the daughter of Sophia, wife of Stefanus", or a name. Not identified.
18. The parenthesis opened before "We will give the statements" is not closed in the transcription.
19. The transcription reads *vielleicht ſagt ihr nicht recht gelehrt werden*; probably *ſeid ihr nicht recht gelehrt worden*, as the translation takes it.
20. *Mary*, probably the farmer Marx of the 14th; see note 11.
21. The transcription reads *daß wir ūm ſie nicht verlegen waren*, "that we were not at a loss about them"; the sense requires the opposite, and *nicht* may be a misreading, perhaps of *ſehr*.
22. The transcription reads *bey nūhterem*, which is not clear; perhaps *bey jenen*, "with those", meaning the visitors.
23. The transcription reads *mit unſerm Wagenbūchſen begleitet*, "accompanied by our wagon boxes"; probably *mit unſern Segenswünſchen*, "with our good wishes", as when Brother Schwinn left on 28 September. Marsveld's own account below has *mit dem Wagen meines lieben Bruders*, probably *mit dem Segen meiner lieben Brüder*.
24. The transcription reads *Auch hielt heute das Ehepaar der Tauf⸗Candidaten an*, "today the married couple of the candidates for baptism also held"; probably *das Sprechen*, "the speaking".
