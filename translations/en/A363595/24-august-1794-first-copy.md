Diary of the 3 Brothers Hendr. Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn and Joh. Christ Kühnel, from 1 to the last of August 1794, from the Cape [1].

<!-- lines 9286-9304 -->
On 1 August a woman was buried. In the evening at the singing meeting it was so full that the people could not all get in. If [our Lord] would give us the joy [2] of a church or meeting house in the meantime, what a joy that would be! On the 2nd a woman came and complained that for anguish of heart she did not know where to turn. A couple of times already in the meeting, when she was sitting on the bench, she had not been able to bear it, but had had to sit down on the ground. On Sunday the 3rd the meeting was very well attended. On the 4th, since we had now read through the Psalms of David in the meeting and they were finished, we resolved to read the 4 Evangelists again, and today a beginning was made. The Saviour owned this reading in a quite special way. We read twice a week, on Mondays and Fridays.

<!-- lines 9305-9321 -->
On the 5th we slaughtered our last pig, for lack of fat, for we are often short of it. The Hottentots all know well how to go about slaughtering, for there are no butchers here: every farmer has all his livestock slaughtered by slaves or Hottentots. For everything they slaughter, be it pig, ox, sheep or whatever, they need no other tool than a knife. They are afraid of a pig, and make [much] preparation when it is to be slaughtered. When it has been stabbed to death, which is done on the ground [3], it is left lying there, and there it is scalded, then scraped with the knife, and that goes well.

<!-- lines 9321-9365 -->
On the 6th was the speaking of the candidates for baptism [4], over whom we could rejoice throughout. Yesterday evening our Hanna had come home again, who has been with Baas Teunis's son at the stranded ship [5] for several months. In this time we heard much about her, and most of it bad, so that we have often been very perplexed over it. She is not so simple a Hottentot woman as the others; she can speak well. Since many people had come to the ship, she not only put herself on a level with them, but is said to have told several: "I am as good as you, and better still." She is also said to have talked filth when she was drunk, and that she is said to have been more than once. Yesterday, as soon as she came home, she came to greet us. We kept silent, thinking we would see whether she would come of her own accord and tell how things had gone; but no. This afternoon we had her called and asked her how it had gone in the time she was away from us. The answer was: "Good and bad." Then we told her what we had heard of her. She would not admit it, and said it was said of her out of envy. We reminded her of her promise at her baptism, what she had promised our dear Lord and Saviour in her spirit [6], and had given us her hand on it. We asked her whether she could say before the face of Jesus that what we had put to her was lies; but she should consider well and bear in mind that if she lied to us she could certainly expect punishment from the dear Lord. She fell silent and would say nothing. She should go for now [and think the matter over]; and she was told [7] that today (for there was a meeting for the baptised, and she had said she would come again) she might not come to this meeting until we first knew where we stood with her.

<!-- lines 9365-9395 -->
The 7th was a day of heavy rain, which was very welcome for everything, for the corn cannot come up for drought. It seems that we shall still get something of a winter (in the way of this country). Although the weather was very unpleasant, towards evening so many people came to the meeting that the house could not hold them. How things will go with us this year as regards [...] and lice [8], we have poor prospects, because we have no other place than our room [9] to hold the schools and meetings in, and it is now so full that they sit close around us and brush against us with their karosses. We already get many of these creatures now; how it will go when the warm season comes we do not know, for then they are very bad. Today we also had to send a girl out of the school whose kaross (or skin) was quite full; she had more than a thousand in it. If they were not so lazy, they could well rid themselves of these little creatures, if they beat the skin (or kaross) out well with a stick every day. But it takes beating to get the lice out of the thick hair, and they are no lovers of work [10].

<!-- lines 9395-9416 -->
On the 8th we had the school children carry small stones [11] for the little house which we mean to build for Brother Kühnel as a workshop when the uncertainty [12] is over. The men have already brought a good quantity of large stones, for a deep foundation must be dug because the place is sandy. We mean to put a loft or floor on it where we can store corn, because we have no place to keep corn. When the children had brought a good quantity of stones, we gave each of them a piece of [bread] [13]. It is a pleasure to watch: they gather together, the big ones crack [it] with stones, the little ones with their teeth, then comes the eating, and with what appetite and pleasure no one can believe.

<!-- lines 9416-9432 -->
On the 9th an old man was buried who had long been blind. Our sister Eva also came and complained that she had had an exchange of words with her husband, in which she had said words against him, and she was now troubled about it. Her husband is our Brother Johannes. On the 10th our sister Maria came and asked us to make clear to her what had been said in the meeting yesterday, because she had not rightly understood it. It had been said in that meeting that one must not only confess the Saviour's love with the mouth, but must also be convinced of it in the heart and feel it.

<!-- lines 9432-9462 -->
On the 11th, in the evening blessing, our communicants were told that on Wednesday we have a festival in our congregations, with Holy Communion on it, and that we would hold it with them on that day too. But we would not hold a separate individual speaking; whoever still wanted to speak with us should come tomorrow. We asked them heartily to examine themselves before the dear Saviour and to clear out their hearts thoroughly. On the 12th all 4 came and were very open-hearted, as they have never been before. Two of them lamented and were very troubled that so many fleshly thoughts still came to them. We directed them to the dear Saviour as well as we could. Our Hanna was also with us and asked forgiveness for having grieved us. But she is not yet [...] again [14], and so we could not yet forgive her. Early on the 13th we had a special morning blessing with our baptised, in which the dear Saviour proved himself powerfully in every heart. At 10 o'clock we read the address which Brother Reichel gave in the congregation meeting at Herrnhut on 13 August 1792 [15]. In the evening we had Holy Communion with our 4 sisters, with the dear Saviour owning it. That was a day of blessing for us and our baptised.

<!-- lines 9462-9488 -->
On the 14th a man and a woman came. The woman complained of the distress of her heart. The man said that for some time he had felt the peace of God in his heart and was very content, whereas for a long time he had not known where to turn for anguish of heart. He is our sister Anna's husband. On the 15th: we had often talked together about the poverty of the people and what we should do, since many come to us, one asking for bread, another for flour; and since we had long given many pieces of bread, we saw ourselves obliged to put a stop to it, because there are too many people. Others bring presents, which are often very welcome to us, for example fresh meat; and often they bring something that we cannot eat, but we must accept it so as not to shame them. If one wants to give them something else for it at once, they do not take it, and afterwards one forgets it when they come and ask us for this or that. This has often put us in a quandary about what we should do.

<!-- lines 9488-9512 -->
On the 16th Brother Kühnel rode to Baas [16] Teunis to speak with him about the house we mean to build, and to get good advice from him on how we could do it most cheaply. He came home in the evening. This afternoon the overseer of the warm bath [17] came to visit us, with another man from the Cape who lodges in the Castle. They stayed the night, and in the morning, as it was Sunday, [they stayed] to attend the meetings. On the 17th, after the meeting, they went away again. The man from the Cape said: "I am amazed at the stillness of so great a crowd of people, and at the reverence and devotion they have. When I heard of it I did not believe it, and could not believe it. When one sees these people so, how the tears roll, I too could not help letting my tears flow freely. We Christians ought to be ashamed." We also remembered the little girls in our congregations at their choir festival today, and wished that the dear Saviour would bless them.

<!-- lines 9512-9549 -->
Yesterday, the 18th, 2 girls came who had both had dreams. One said that she had dreamed the night before that the devil wanted to have her, which troubled her greatly. She was told that if she asked the dear Saviour for the forgiveness of her sins and wanted to become a child of God, the devil should not get her. When she lay down to sleep she should ask the dear Saviour to fill her heart with his torment and death. Today when she came to school she wept. After a while she came and said that she had not slept the whole night but had thought of our dear Lord, and that her mind was to become a child of God and to live wholly for Jesus Christ. On the 19th our Hanna came and asked that we would take her back. She said that what had been said of her was not so bad, but she would not admit anything further [18]. She has also been to Baas Teunis and complained of her plight to him. He sent us word through Bedgen that she had not behaved so badly as we had been told, and that we should take her back. But this cannot help us, for she herself will still admit nothing, and will not yet become a sinner. On the 20th two more children came and asked to go to school. In 8 days 8 children have come. The school is growing strong; there are now over 50 children in it.

<!-- lines 9549-9570 -->
On the 21st Chr. Kune [19] sent us a letter to read which he had received from Martinus Schmidt at the Cape. He wrote to him that he should fetch him with the ox wagon on 4 September below the Kloof [20]; he would come that far with his horse wagon [and be there on the 4th] [21] (that is 2 days' journey from here). With horses one cannot get over the Kloof because it is so steep; there one must drive with oxen. Martinus Schmidt had written to us lately that he meant to visit us with his wife in September, which made us very glad, for this dear man and his wife have been father and mother to us and have already shown us much kindness. Now, the Saviour will grant that we see these dear people with us. On the 22nd our sister Maria came and said that she had dreamed that a man had come to her and said to her that on [Thursday] [22] she [should partake] with us as a fellow [...] [23]

## Notes

1. This is the first of two copies of the August 1794 diary; the second is entry 25 (lines 9573 to 9869). This copy breaks off on the 22nd; the second runs to the 27th and then, after a gap, to the end of the month. The notes give the reading of the second copy where it settles a doubtful passage. The heading says *von der Caap*, "from the Cape", as the July diary does, although the diary was written at Baviaanskloof.
2. The transcription reads *bei uns unter deſſen wollter die Freunde ſchenken*, "with us meanwhile the friends would give". The second copy has *Wen uns unſer Hrr die Freude machen wollte*, "if our Lord would give us the joy", which shows that *Freunde* is a misreading of *Freude*.
3. The transcription reads *welches auch da Hut geſchieht*; the second copy has *welches auf der Erde geſchicht*, "which is done on the ground".
4. The transcription has *das Waſchen*, "the washing", and the normalised column follows it; the second copy has *das Sprechen*, the speaking.
5. On Hanna (baptised 28 January 1794) and the stranded ship, see entry 21, note 10.
6. The transcription reads *in ſr Geiſte*, "in her spirit"; the sense is uncertain.
7. The transcription reads *Sie ſollte vor jezt gehen u darin bey den treuen Nachbar würde ihr geſagt*, "she should go for now and in it with the faithful neighbour she was told". The second copy has "We advised her to go and think the matter over earnestly. Besides, she was told ...", and the translation follows its sense. To be checked against the image.
8. The transcription reads *Wie es uns dieſes Jahr wird, wegen Läuſe (doch dieſe müſſen wir zäugen) aber wegen Kaͤſe gehen wird, dazu haben wir ſchlechte Ausſichten*, "how it will go with us this year as regards lice (but these we must [?]), but as regards cheese, for that we have poor prospects". The words *Läuſe* and *Kaͤſe* may have been misread or exchanged: the second copy has "How it will go with us this year with the oxen (because they are still thin) and the sheep, we cannot imagine", and then goes on to the lice. To be checked against the image.
9. The transcription reads *in unſerm Rivier*, "in our river"; the second copy has *in unſrer Wohnſtube*, "in our living room".
10. The transcription reads *das koſtet aber klopfen die Läuſe aus den dicken Haaren heraus zu klopfen, worin ſie (nemlich von Arbeit keine Liebhaber ſind*. The sense is clear, the wording garbled.
11. The transcription reads *Am 8 t war der Schul der Kinder lieber wir ſie kleine Steine tragen*; the second copy has *d 8 Aug. war die Schule beſſer als ſie kleine Steine trugen*. Both are garbled; the sense is that the school children carried small stones. To be checked against the image.
12. The transcription reads *wenn die Ungewißheit vorbey iſt*, "when the uncertainty is over"; the second copy has *wenn die Regnezeit vorbey iſt*, "when the rainy season is over". The transcription also lacks *für Br.* before *Kühnel*.
13. The transcription reads *einen Broutſtück*, "a piece of bread"; the second copy has *einen Kwaſchtrank*, which is not clear. The description that follows (the big ones beat or crack it with stones, the little ones use their teeth; the second copy says they beat off the rind, *Baſt*, with stones and peel it with their teeth) suggests something with a hard rind, perhaps a pumpkin or a fruit, not bread. To be checked against the image.
14. The transcription reads *Sie iſt aber noch nicht wieder ein*, which breaks off; a word such as *Sünderin* may be missing. The second copy has *Sie ich will nicht ſündigen*, also garbled. On the 19th she "will not yet become a sinner", that is, will not admit her sin.
15. 13 August is the Moravian memorial of the renewal of the church at Herrnhut in 1727. The second copy dates the address *d 13 Augt. 1727*, perhaps mixing the date of the event with the date of the address. Brother Reichel is probably Johann Friedrich Reichel of the Unity Elders' Conference. To be checked against the image.
16. The transcription reads *Cpt. Teunis*, "Captain Teunis"; the second copy has *Mr Teunis*. Elsewhere he is always Baas Teunis.
17. Probably Negrini, the overseer of the warm bath, who visited in December 1793 (see `glossary.csv`).
18. The transcription reads *ſie wollte aber weiter nichts gedenken*, "she would not think anything further"; the second copy has *wollte aber nichts geſtehen*, "but would admit nothing". The same misreading occurs a few lines later, *will noch nichts geſchehen* for *geſtehen*.
19. Probably Christian Kuns, the rich farmer two hours from the mission (see `glossary.csv`).
20. The Kloof is probably Hottentots Holland Kloof, the pass over the mountains on the road from the Cape.
21. The transcription reads *und mit da ſager (das iſt 2 Tage Reiſen von hier) an einem Morgen mit Pferden kam man nicht über die Cloof*; the second copy has "and be there on the 4th. This place is 2 days' journey from here. With a horse wagon one cannot pass the Kloof".
22. The transcription reads *auf den Donnerberg*, "on the thunder mountain"; the second copy has *auf dem Donnerſtag*, "on the Thursday", and says that the man told her she should partake of Holy Communion with us. Maria had been approved as a candidate for Communion on 26 July.
23. This copy breaks off here at the foot of the page (*mit uns als Ge*); the normalised column completes the word as *Gefährtin*, "companion". The second copy follows on the next page.
