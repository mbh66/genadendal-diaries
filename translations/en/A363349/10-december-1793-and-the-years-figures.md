<!-- lines 3614-3617 -->
On 1 December [1] the sermon was fairly well attended, although most are already at work at the harvest.

<!-- lines 3617-3672 -->
On the 4th we had the honour of being visited by 2 gentlemen of the government at the Cape [2]. Brother Kühnel, being at Baas Teunis's, had asked him to let us know of it; but these gentlemen had forbidden him to do so, because they wanted to find us going about our usual business. So they came quite unexpectedly with Mrs Teunis and her son; and they could hardly have found us in greater disorder at any other time, for we were just busy preparing the clay for our coming little house. Now one can imagine how we looked. We first had to clean thoroughly the planks [3] that had been soiled by the splashing of the clay before these gentlemen could sit down. It was just right for them that they found us so; but they would have liked it better if they had found us busy keeping school and could have heard the children sing. It was Wednesday, when no school is held for the children. They asked whether something of the kind could not be arranged after all. We said that the best singers were away (for more than 20 of the biggest children were missing), and those who had stayed at home were small and could not do much yet. But they would not be put off, and so we had the children [come to a singing school, and Brother Kühnel] played [the flute] [4]. However weakly it went, these gentlemen were pleased to declare: "If the best are still missing here, we would like to hear them all together some time, for even this has surpassed our expectation." They also gave the children good admonitions to learn really diligently. Towards us they showed themselves very friendly, and they were very glad that they found so great a desire for God's word among the Hottentots. We used this opportunity to speak about our bell, and learned that complaints had certainly been made about it to the government. They gave us this solution: since our bell hung not in a church but between 2 trees, we could make use of it meanwhile, and one of the gentlemen promised to speak further about it with the Governor [5]. After midday, at 4 o'clock, they left again, after showing us every possible friendliness.

<!-- lines 3672-3681 -->
Since some children had not behaved well in school about this time, some parents asked us to chastise them in such cases; one of the Bastards even said: "If my children ever complain that they have been beaten at school, I will give them more for it." We told them that we did not intend to beat their children; but if they deserved punishment, we would leave it to the parents.

<!-- lines 3682-3699 -->
On the 8th some of those who are at work at the harvest came to the sermon and went back again after midday. Two of them came to us and accused themselves of having done wrong; one could see it on them, too, for both had their heads bandaged. They had got drunk, then fallen to quarrelling and fought. They were very penitent about it, and we admonished them warmly. With these two, who do not yet belong to our congregation, nothing more could be done; but with the candidate for baptism, who had not taken part in the fighting but had in the drinking, we spoke more seriously, and he begged earnestly for forgiveness.

<!-- lines 3699-3721 -->
Today our Anna Maria went to Mrs Teunis in company with Eva. They do not like to go alone, above all at harvest time, so as not to fall into the hands of bad people. Afterwards they told us with embarrassment that on the way they had accepted wine from a farmer; not so much that they had become drunk, but they had not followed our admonition, namely not to begin drinking at all, because they are not masters of themselves and very easily go too far. This matter caused us much concern in general. Rahel, the last baptised, and 5 more of our candidates are now at work with the farmers; the other baptised and candidates are still with us. During the harvest the farmers give wine to drink plentifully, as much as 4 times a day, which here too, as everywhere, has more than one bad consequence. God have mercy on them and help in this too.

<!-- lines 3721-3727 -->
On the 11th [6] we spoke with the baptised individually, except Rahel, who is still away. With two we had to speak seriously, because love was lacking between them. Until now they have lived together in one house, but one of them is now to build a house of her own. Then perhaps things will go better.

<!-- lines 3728-3735 -->
On the 12th some of our people came [home] from the farmers, [and on the 13th Ra]hel [7] and one of the candidates for baptism about whom we had been very worried. We spoke with her at once, and had cause to believe that they had been kept from sinning.

<!-- lines 3735-3739 -->
On the 15th our house could not nearly hold all the hearers at the sermon. More and more keep coming who live far from here.

<!-- lines 3739-3745 -->
On the 18th Andres Otto, by order of Baas Teunis, brought a load of meal here for the women whose husbands are at the Cape. We have instructions to distribute it weekly.

<!-- lines 3745-3752 -->
On the 19th we heard that the land at the Sergeants River was to be given to a farmer, which put us in great difficulty. For this land has belonged to the Hottentots until now, and without it they cannot subsist here at all; for us too this would be very harmful.

<!-- lines 3752-3756 -->
On the 20th the singing meetings began again; they had not been held for 14 days, partly because of the absence of many children, partly because of the carelessness of those who had stayed at home.

<!-- lines 3756-3761 -->
On the 21st an old Hottentot told us that he was very uneasy and must reveal the cause of it to us: at the harvest he had got drunk and quarrelled with another man, but it had not gone too far.

<!-- lines 3761-3767 -->
On the 22nd a woman begged earnestly for holy baptism. She declared: "I have long wanted to come to reveal to you the bad things, for I have joined in doing all the evil that is done among us; but I was ashamed. Now I am very troubled about being saved, and I long for holy baptism."

<!-- lines 3768-3777 -->
On the 23rd we announced in the evening meeting that Wednesday was Christmas, or the day of remembrance of the birth of our Saviour, on which festival day, and also on Thursday, sermons would be held, and on Wednesday a children's meeting as well. Today we distributed meal among the Hottentots for the first time.

<!-- lines 3777-3781 -->
On the 24th we held a lovefeast with our baptised, read them the history of the festival and spoke about it. A blessed feeling prevailed among us, and one heard heartfelt declarations about it.

<!-- lines 3781-3804 -->
On the 25th Hottentots came from all sides to attend the sermons. 7 colonists came too, among others the overseer of the warm bath [8]. Of the Hottentots there were 130, without the children, who had a special meeting. We saw no possibility of holding the sermon indoors, so we went under the pear tree that the late Brother Schmidt planted [9]. Afterwards the children had a meeting. On the second holiday the sermon had to be held under the pear tree again, for most of the men are now at home on leave; but we had not supposed that so many Hottentots lived in this district. In the evening we held the first liturgical singing with our baptised, from the Dutch hymn book, no. 200 [10]. We can testify that the Spirit of God proved himself powerfully on the hearts of the hearers at the preaching of the Gospel of the becoming man of Jesus Christ.

<!-- lines 3804-3807 -->
On the 29th we announced in the sermons that next Wednesday was New Year's Day, and that there would be preaching.

<!-- lines 3807-3820 -->
On the 30th we were told that a girl of 17 or 18 years had dreamed that the Saviour came to her and said to her that she should be converted and not wait long with it, for it was time. This [happened some days ago, and since then she goes about] [11] very thoughtful by herself. A Hottentot told us that while working at the harvest he had felt very well, and had had good thoughts. To the question "What thoughts?" he answered: "I thought about the suffering of Jesus, and what he has done for people. Now I feel that I too would gladly be converted."

<!-- lines 3820-3830 -->
On the 31st we inquired how New Year's night is kept among the Hottentots, and learned that they are accustomed not to sleep this night but to spend it gorging, swilling, dancing and shouting. They have learned this from the farmers, for in this country it is the greatest festival of the whole year.

<!-- lines 3830-3871 -->
We had thought beforehand of closing the year with our baptised and candidates alone. But since everyone stays up, we resolved to hold a general meeting. We did not want to announce it, however, before the meeting at 6 o'clock, since we could suppose that otherwise very many would come uninvited [12] and we would lack room. So it was announced that this night at half past eleven we would hold another meeting, to thank God once more for all the good we have enjoyed from him in this past year. By 11 o'clock so many had already come from near and far that we had to wonder at it, all the more because it was raining and so dark that we at least could have found no road. Before we had rung, our house was already so full that the rest had to stand outside the house; for the rest a great stillness prevailed. At half past eleven the meeting began. We reminded them of the great mercy of God that he had shown them in this year, which we now wished to close before the face of Jesus: for it was he who had let the Gospel be preached to them, of the shedding of his blood for the forgiveness of their sins. Whoever had not yet thanked him for it should do so while it was still this year, and so on. At 12 o'clock we stood up with all who were present and fell on our knees. Here, dear brothers and sisters, words fail us! As in the congregation the year is announced with the sound of trombones, so here it was done by a general and loud weeping, which broke out all at once when we fell on our knees with them. We call on all our brothers and sisters to thank our dear Lord with us for all that he does for us here, and to pray him to go on with his grace, and to lead and govern us his poor servants by his Spirit!

<!-- lines 3871-3873 -->
At the close of the year our Hottentot congregation consists of 7 baptised and 10 candidates for baptism, 17 persons in all.

## Notes

1. The transcription reads *Den 1n Oct:*, but the entry falls between 29 November and 4 December, and the German diary has "On 1 December". *Oct:* is probably a misreading of *Dec:*. To be checked against the image.
2. The German diary names them *Hrn Brand und Voor*; not identified.
3. *die Pranken*: probably *Planken*, "planks", here perhaps the benches, which had to be cleaned before the visitors could sit. The German diary has "we had first to get the worst of the clay off our hands". To be checked against the image.
4. Line 3648 is missing. The transcription runs *waarom wij dan de kinderen lieten ter* / *Speelde*. The German diary has "and so we called the children to a singing school, and Brother Kühnel played the flute for it". The words in square brackets are supplied from there.
5. The German diary has "our school" here where the Dutch has *onze klok*, "our bell", and "we sang between two [trees]" where the Dutch has "hung between 2 trees". The Dutch fits the complaint about the bell in November (entry 09).
6. The transcription reads *Diste ſpraaken wij die gedoopten bijzonder*; the German diary has "On the 11th we spoke with the baptised individually". *Diste* is probably a misreading of *Den 11ten*.
7. Line 3730 is missing. The transcription runs *kwamen eenige onzer lieden van de Boeren* / *hel en eene van de Doopcandidaaten*. 8. The German diary names him Negrini, born in Plauen. See the glossary.
9. The pear tree planted by Georg Schmidt, the first missionary (1737 to 1744), which the brethren found still standing when they arrived in 1792.
10. *het eerste liturgiſche gezang ... uit het Hollandſche gezangboek N 200*: a sung liturgy from a Dutch hymn book; the edition is not identified.
