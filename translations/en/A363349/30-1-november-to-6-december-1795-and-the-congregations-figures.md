<!-- lines 11310-11327 -->
On 1 November 3 persons were baptised into the death of Jesus, named Margaretha, Alida and Esther. Today we saw a wagon coming towards us across the river; but the water in the river being swollen exceedingly high, no wagon could get across, and so this wagon too had to turn back. Our guess was that the English officers would be in it who had sent word to us of their coming, and whose visit would have been very welcome to us [1]. The rainy weather was so lasting that we have had to let the school drop. For it has to be held under the pear tree, since for want of room it could not possibly be held in the house.

<!-- lines 11328-11337 -->
On 13 November we celebrated, among other things, with a lovefeast with our baptised. One of the 2 excluded was accepted again [2]. At it we remembered our dear congregations in Europe and the mission posts among the heathen, of which we told our Hottentots a great deal. This gave occasion to a repeated wish that our brothers in Holland might be urged to translate accounts of our mission posts for the use of our Hottentots, which we are not able to do [3].

<!-- lines 11338-11358 -->
On the 16th the husband of our Margaretha was bitten in the leg by a poisonous creature, so that he had to go and lie in bed, and almost lost his speech [4]. The Hottentots know good remedies for healing such a poisonous bite. There is a kind of small snake here whose bite is fatal most of the time, and it is a special protection of God that we have suffered no such harm. For we have several times been in the case where, when we wanted to pick something up from the ground, the Hottentots, who have learned from their youth to pay attention to it and have sharp sight, have called out to us: "Beware of a snake that is lying there!", which we would otherwise not have noticed. So there is [also a small kind of spider, whose bite] is fatal if the remedies known to the Hottentots are not used at once [5].

<!-- lines 11359-11378 -->
On 22 November we were visited by the gentlemen Hoffman, Andreas Otto, and Claude, who has bought the place in our neighbourhood. They attended the Sunday meeting, and were amazed at the crowd of hearers. The gentlemen Claude and Hoffman said that it seemed to them absolutely necessary to build a larger meeting house [6]. We ourselves had also let our thoughts go over this many times, and wished very much to learn the thoughts and advice of our dear brothers in the Unity Elders' Conference about it. But since at the present time we cannot expect letters from them, and it is now the season in which something of this kind must happen, if it is not to lie undone for a whole year [7], we decided in a conference on 28 November that Brother Marsveld should leave for the Cape tomorrow, to seek to obtain the necessary permission for it from the English General.

<!-- lines 11378-11411 -->
He came back on 6 December and gave us the following report of the carrying out of his commission: "On 1 December I waited upon General Craig, and asked him to allow us to fell building timber for a meeting house. He said that I must speak with the Landdrost about it, and he must give him his advice on it. At this I went to the Landdrost of Stellenbosch, who received me kindly, and promised to give me a letter to the General. I received this letter on 2 December [8], and handed it to the General, who was well satisfied with it. I took the opportunity at this to make the General acquainted with our many missions among the heathen in the English islands, which all enjoy the favourable protection of the King of England [9]. He said that we should go on with our work without fear, and if we were hindered in it, we should just let him know. On 3 December I received from him a written permission to have building timber felled for a meeting house. The watchword on this day was very remarkable, running thus in the High German translation: 'Thy servants would gladly see Zion built, and her stones and lime made ready.' Psalm 102:15 [10]. With this joyful permission I set out on 4 December on my journey back from the Cape, and on the 6th arrived again with my dear brothers."

4 baptised children

Total 62 persons,

## Notes

1. *en welker bezoek ons zeer welkom zoude geweeſt zyn*. The Dutch settles the German diary's garbled page break (its note 1): the visit would have been welcome. The Dutch does not have the German diary's "This was confirmed on the 3rd".
2. *Eene van de 2 uitgeſlootene werd wederom aangenomen*; the German diary has "Johanna, one of the 4 excluded". Two baptised persons were excluded in August (entry 27).
3. *Berigten van onze HeidenPoorten*: probably *Heidenpoſten*, "mission posts", as above.
4. *door een vergiftig gedierte zoodaanig in het been gebeeten*. This supplies the verb missing in the German diary (its note 4); the Dutch does not have its "about as thick as a thumb", or the snake "hardly as long as a pipe".
5. Line 11356 is missing. The transcription runs *Zoo is ’er* / *doodlyk is, als men niet aanſtonds de geneesmiddelen*. The German diary has "There is also a small kind of spider whose bite is fatal unless the proper remedy is used on the spot". The words in square brackets are supplied from there.
6. *dat het hun volſtrekt noodzaaklyk ſcheen te zyn*. This settles the German diary's *erſtaun⸗dige* (its note 5). See Hoffman and Claude in the glossary.
7. *en thans doch het Saiſoen is, waarin iets diergelyks geſchieden moet*. This settles the German diary's *Dache*, "roof" (its note 6).
8. *Ik ontving dien brief den 2 Dec:*. This settles the German diary's "the 7th" (its note 7).
9. *onze veele bezendingen onder de Heidenen op de Engelſche Eilanden ... welke allen de gunſtige protectie van den Koning van England genieten*: the Moravian missions in the British West Indies. This settles the German diary's *durch Sie* (its note 8).
10. *naar de hoogduitſche vertaaling*: the Dutch translator notes that the watchword is given as it reads in the German (Luther) Bible, Psalm 102:15; in the Authorised Version it is Psalm 102:14.
11. *3 Avondmaals Candidaaten*. This settles the German diary's *3. Tauf⸗Candidaten* (its note 10).
