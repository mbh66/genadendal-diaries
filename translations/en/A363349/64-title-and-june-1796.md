On 25 June Brother Schwinn drove to the post, to Baas Teunis, to fetch flour; and at the same time took with him our letters and diaries, which the latter promised to send on.

<!-- lines 16190-16205 -->
On the 28th 6 Hottentots came from the Cape on leave for 14 days to visit their wives and children. By their account the General, who [...] has, wished that still more Hottentots would let themselves be enlisted; even if their number rose to 1000, it would not be too many [3]. They also told us that a corps of 300 men was to have come here, with the aim of making an expedition further inland; but on the rumour that the French were to be expected, nothing had come of this undertaking.

2. The parenthesis is a note by the Dutch copyist or editor. The last diary communicated is the one that ends on 13 March 1796 (entry 34). The church was consecrated on 24 March 1796 (see entry 61).
3. Line 16194 is missing. The transcription runs *wenſchte de Generaal, die* / *heeft, dat nog meer Hottentotten zich mogten laaten aanwerven*. The lost line probably said that the General (Major-General Craig) had already raised a corps of Khoekhoe soldiers; compare entry 33 (February 1796), where he planned a corps of 300 to 400. The words in the translation are only those that survive. *belief* is read as *beliep*, "rose to".
