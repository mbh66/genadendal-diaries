<!-- lines 3067-3073 -->
Diary of the Brethren Hendrik Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn and Johan Christian Kühnel of Baviaanskloof in Africa, running from 1 September 1793 to 9 April 1794 [1].

<!-- lines 3075-3075 -->
[On 1 September Brother Schwinn went to the post, to travel from] there with Baas Teunis to Cape Town [2].

<!-- lines 3076-3083 -->
On the 2nd we had the 2 Bastards come to us, to speak with them and consider how the corn, which by order of the authorities we had had to sow for the Hottentots who have marched to the Cape, could be protected against the wild horses and other cattle. They took the watch on themselves until Baas Teunis should have made further arrangements.

<!-- lines 3084-3121 -->
In our conference we had often thought earnestly about the individual speaking [3] and had seen that it was necessary; but since our little congregation consists of women whose husbands are absent, we had not yet put it into practice. In our last conference, on 31 August [4], we came back to this matter again, and resolved to make a beginning with our 6 baptised, and in such a way that the individual speaking with them one by one should be done by the three of us together, or at least by two. This was made known to them in their meeting on the 7th, and at the same time they were given an idea of this speaking, and warmly admonished to search their hearts thoroughly in these days, so that its purpose might be reached. The first speaking is fixed for the 11th. At this time we had arranged our meetings in the week as follows. Every evening we hold a general meeting, except on Saturday, when the baptised meet on their own. On Wednesdays and Saturdays the candidates for baptism have a meeting in the morning and the children at midday, and on Tuesday and Friday evenings a singing meeting, or singing school, is also held. The general meetings are only for the grown-ups, for want of room; the singing meetings, on the other hand, are attended by everyone, although our room cannot hold them all, for they are very fond of singing, and sing so well that there is little to correct.

<!-- lines 3121-3137 -->
On the 8th, when Brother Marsveld went out, he was called into a hut in which lay a sick woman who had consumption, so that her end could be expected soon. He asked her whether she was afraid of death. She answered no, since she must die some time. At this he reminded her of what she had heard in the meetings, and asked her whether she believed that the Saviour had redeemed her too, and whether she had prayed to him for the forgiveness of her sins. At this she was silent. But towards evening she had Brother Marsveld called again and asked him to repeat what he had said. Then she said that she could believe it, and called on the Saviour for mercy, to whom she was also commended in a heartfelt prayer.

<!-- lines 3138-3144 -->
On the 9th she died [5]. The others told us that in the night before she had often prayed: "My God! My Lord Jesus! Forgive me my sins!" She had lived here about a quarter of a year, and as long as she could go out she had missed no meeting, and many a tear had been seen rolling down her cheeks.

<!-- lines 3144-3162 -->
On the 10th she was buried, which we attended. We asked beforehand about their customs, and learning that before the body was carried out of the house it was kissed by all, we told our baptised that they should leave this off, since kissing a dead person serves no purpose in the world. They are accustomed to leave the bier, made of poles, lying on the grave; we explained to them that this too served no purpose, and that they would do better [to use the poles for something else. This was] followed [6], as was another admonition concerning the gorging and swilling after the burial. Only a goat was slaughtered [7], of which each received a very small piece. For the rest, the burial passed off as quietly and orderly as one could wish.

<!-- lines 3162-3190 -->
On the 11th was the individual speaking with the 6 baptised. We had supposed that they would not know how to say much to the purpose; but having thought about themselves thoroughly, they were as open-hearted as one could wish. They could not express enough their gratitude to the Saviour for all the mercy shown them. Old Lena said again and again: "Ah, thanks be to the good Saviour that he has sent us teachers to instruct us in his word." At the beginning it seemed as if they had forgotten everything they heard from the late [Brother] Schmidt [8]; but now one thing after another comes to mind, so that we often wonder at it. She said that in Brother Schmidt's time it had not gone so far that meetings such as these were arranged; but before he left he had said that he would send them 3 brethren from the fatherland. "And for these," she said, "we have been waiting all the time." We have heard several times before that there has been a certain tradition by which they have expected teachers, and indeed under the name of 3 brothers; and especially in the last years before our arrival there was much talk of it.

<!-- lines 3190-3202 -->
On the 12th we had a distinguished and numerous visit, among others from Mrs Teunis and her children, the Commissioner of Plettenberg Bay [9] with his wife and children, a captain-lieutenant [10], and so on. This company with its attendants consisted of 29 persons. They came after midday, and many left towards evening. Had they come for the midday meal, we would have found ourselves in great difficulty; but Mrs Teunis knew well that we have nothing, and had arranged the journey accordingly. They were all very friendly and well satisfied with everything.

<!-- lines 3202-3210 -->
On the 14th a Hottentot woman visited us and declared her heartfelt longing for holy baptism, and said with many tears that her whole mind was to live only for Jesus Christ in this world, and that wherever she went or stood her prayer to him was that he would make her a child of God.

<!-- lines 3210-3218 -->
The 13th [11], being a special day of remembrance for the servants of the congregation, we joined them in spirit, prayed for forgiveness of our debts, and promised our dear Lord new faithfulness. To our joy we received today a letter from our Brother Schwinn. He had sent various things to the post, which we are to fetch with our cart.

<!-- lines 3218-3222 -->
On the 19th we heard singing in a hut, and going towards it unnoticed we found that it was some children singing together, well and sweetly, the verses they had learned at school.

<!-- lines 3222-3225 -->
On the 20th Brother Schwinn came back to us from the Cape, healthy and safe. He reports the following of this journey [12]:

<!-- lines 3225-3232 -->
"On 2 September we set out from the post. We had an extremely difficult journey, on which God visibly saved us from destruction more than once. On the 6th we arrived safely in Cape Town, where I was received with much love by our friend M[artinus] Schmidt [13].

<!-- lines 3232-3259 -->
"On the 7th I went to Colonel Gordon, who was very glad at the news that the Hottentots were uncommonly eager for God's word. The Commandant [14] showed the same joy, and advised me [to pay] my [respects to the Governor] at once [15]. This last-named gentleman was exceedingly friendly, and inquired closely into all our circumstances. He said: 'It is a hard but praiseworthy undertaking,' and gave me 120 Rixdollars for our support, adding that I should visit him every time I came here. I thanked him heartily and commended our mission to his protection. After this I visited the other good friends, who all took a heartfelt share in the progress of God's work among the Hottentots, and some of whom gave me clothes to take for the baptised. On the 9th I was with 3 ministers, and with two of them I had spoken [16]. On the 10th I dined with Colonel Gordon. He and his wife take a great interest in the mission. Among other things they gave me various kinds of seed, and 50 Rixdollars in money to buy sheep with.

<!-- lines 3259-3281 -->
"On the 12th Mr Schmidt accompanied me to our Hottentots, who are camped an hour from the town. As soon as we got there they all ran up to me and were very glad. Besides this they had often visited me in the town. I reminded them of what they had heard of the Saviour with us, and they promised not to forget it. I had already spoken with the Governor about our Hottentots who must now serve at the Cape, and about their poor wives and children, who must suffer the bitterest famine because of it; Baas Teunis had done the same. In consequence the Governor had me called to him on the 13th, and gave me 75 Rixdollars for the said women [17] and children, so that they should not have to suffer want, with the instruction that we should let him know when that money had been spent. On the 14th I took leave of our good friends, and on the 15th we began our journey back; after which I arrived safe and well with my dear brethren on the 20th."

<!-- lines 3281-3281 -->
So far Brother Schwinn.

<!-- lines 3282-3287 -->
On the 21st we told the candidates for baptism that the individual speaking would now begin with them too, and on the 25th.

<!-- lines 3287-3293 -->
On the 22nd, after the sermon, we had all the women whose husbands are at the Cape come together, and asked them one by one how much meal they needed a week with their children. This was done at the request of Baas Teunis, who had orders from the Governor to provide the meal.

<!-- lines 3293-3295 -->
On the 23rd we sent a Hottentot to Baas Teunis to fetch the goats he had got for us.

<!-- lines 3295-3303 -->
On the 25th the candidates for baptism were spoken with. They were 4 in number, for a man who belongs to them is away. Two declared, with especial feeling, how troubled they were that they did not always have the sufferings of Jesus before their eyes as they would wish, and that they also could not keep everything they heard in the daily meetings [18].

<!-- lines 3303-3326 -->
On the 28th we heard an unpleasant rumour of an impending revolt among the Hottentots, which was to break out at the signal shot that is fired from the Lion's Hill [19] when several ships are seen at sea. This gave Baas Teunis occasion to have Brother Marsveld come to him. He made an arrangement with him about these circumstances, and asked that if any Hottentots should apply to live with us, we should first send them to him, so that he could find out whether they belonged to those suspected of revolt, of whom 6 have already been taken prisoner, while about 40 are still keeping themselves in the mountains. Baas Teunis also admonished us to keep [a watchful eye] [20]. But we do not expect anything of the kind from our Hottentots, all the more since almost all the men are at the Cape; we promised, however, to be attentive to it, and as soon as we noticed the least trace of it, to make it known to him at once.

## Notes

1. This second Dutch diary is a shorter version of the German diary for the same months (A363595 entries 13 to 20). It leaves out much of the German, including the hunt of 13 to 15 September, the cattle in the garden on the 18th and nearly all of Brother Schwinn's account of his journey to the Cape; it also settles several readings that the German leaves unclear. The numbers 4 to 10 that appear in the transcription at the foot of the pages are the manuscript's own page numbers.
2. Line 3074 is missing, and with it the start of the entry for 1 September. The words in square brackets are supplied from the German diary: "On 1 September Brother Schwinn went to the post, to drive from there to Cape Town with Baas Teunis."
3. *het perſoneele ſpreeken*: the individual speaking; see *Sprechen* in the glossary.
4. The German diary has "on 19 August". To be checked against the images.
5. *Overleed zij*: "she died". The Dutch uses a plain word here; the German diary has *verschied*, "passed away".
6. Line 3156 is missing. The transcription runs *en zij beeter deeden dat* / *gevolgd; zoo ook nog eene andere Vermaaning*. The German diary has "they could use the poles for something else. All this was followed". The words in square brackets are supplied from there. The repeat of this page in entry 11 keeps the lost line (line 3940): *Hout tot iets anders te gebruiken. Dit alles werd op-*, which confirms the reading.
7. *er werd slegts een Bock geslagt*: "only a goat was slaughtered". The German diary has "only one loaf was cut". One of the two is probably a misreading. To be checked against the images.
8. *van den zal:* / *Schmidt*: *zal.* for *zaliger*, "the late", as in the German diary. Georg Schmidt left the Cape in 1744.
9. *Comiſſarius van de Blettermans Baaij*: the Company's official at Plettenberg Bay. See the glossary.
10. *een Captain Leutenant*: perhaps a captain-lieutenant, a Dutch naval rank; the German diary has "a captain and a lieutenant".
11. The German diary dates this remembrance to the 16th. The Dutch places it after the 14th but calls it *De 13te*, which is probably a misreading of *16te*. To be checked against the image.
12. The Dutch gives only the start and end of Brother Schwinn's journey. The German diary has a long account of the crossing of the rivers and of the Houhoek pass.
13. The transcription reads *van onzen Vriend m...* at the foot of page 52a; the German diary has "our old friend Martinus Schmidt".
14. *de Commendant*: the German diary has *Landeshauptmann*, not identified (A363595 entry 13, note 7).
15. Line 3238 is missing. The transcription runs *die mij radde, om ten eersten mijne op-* / *ken*, probably *mijne op[wagting bij den Gouverneur te maa]ken*. The German diary has "advised me to pay my respects at once to the new Governor". The Governor was probably Abraham Josias Sluysken, who took office in September 1793; see the glossary. The repeat of this page in entry 11 keeps the lost line (line 4022): *wachting bij den nieuwen Gouverneur te gaen maa-*, which confirms the reading.
16. The transcription reads *met twee van deezen van hun had ik aangeſproken*, which is garbled. The German diary has "with two of them, Mr Colver and Mr Fleck, I had good conversations".
17. *Wijven*: "women"; the word was not yet disparaging in eighteenth-century Dutch.
18. The German diary says there were 10 candidates and that "ten" declared themselves moved (A363595 entry 13, note 12). The Dutch has 4 and two, which fits the number of candidates in the diary so far.
19. *den Leeuwenberg*: Lion's Hill (Signal Hill) above Cape Town, from which a gun was fired when ships were sighted.
20. Line 3320 is missing. The transcription runs *Baas Tennis vermaande ons ook* / *te houden*. The words in square brackets are supplied from the sense. *Tennis* is the transcription's spelling of Teunis in this diary. The repeat of this page in entry 11 keeps the lost line (line 4104): *om een Waakzaam oog op onze Hottentotten*, which confirms the reading.
