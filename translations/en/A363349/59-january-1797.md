1797.

On 1 Jan. there was preaching in the forenoon, on which occasion the hall could hold the people no more than on the evening before. When the visitors had left, we held a special meeting with our baptised, in which we worshipped our Lord. With the beginning of this year we made the first trial of holding societies with the baptised adults and with the communicants, in the hope that the Saviour will bless this arrangement; as it has also shown itself since [1].

<!-- lines 15751-15781 -->
On the 6th the minister Vos arrived with us in the company of four persons. He had already told us before that he would preach not here, as he had first meant to, but at Baas Teunis's at the post; which was very welcome to us for the reasons given above [2]. He showed himself very friendly, and declared that he had long wished to visit us and to be an eyewitness of the work of God in this place. In the evening he attended the meeting with his party, and on the 7th we went round the kraals with them and visited the Hottentots, to whom they put all kinds of questions, for example: why they had come here? whether it was their whole mind to live for Jesus Christ? whether they also had experience of the grace of Jesus in their hearts? They wondered greatly at the answers of several, and said that one could see very well that God was working powerfully by his Spirit in the hearts of these people. Towards evening several wagons with visitors arrived here, and some on horseback too, to attend the sermon at Baas Teunis's tomorrow. We had about 30 persons to lodge for the night.

<!-- lines 15782-15802 -->
On the 8th they all went to the post, from where the minister in the e[vening ...] him so many people that we did not know how to get them all under a roof: for there were 60 persons of them [3]. Some of them had to spend the night on the wagons, and others under the open sky. The minister took these people on especially, spoke with each of them separately in his room, as he had also done yesterday, and prayed with them on his knees morning and evening. The conversion of the heathen lies just as much at his heart; so that, when he is at home, he gives a not small number of slaves instruction in Christianity a couple of times a week, and holds meetings for them.

<!-- lines 15803-15814 -->
On the 9th, the day of his departure from us, he said a prayer at breakfast in which he besought the Saviour to command his blessing further over the Hottentots, so that many more might be converted to him, and to strengthen us in our work among them. At 9 o'clock he left, together with all the other visitors, except young Schmidt from the Cape, whose father gives us so many proofs of his true friendship towards us; and him we kept with us a few days more [4].

## Notes

1. *gezelſchappen*: "societies", small gatherings of baptised members for conversation about their hearts, as in the German *Gesellschaften* of the Moravian congregations.
2. *om boven gemelde redenen*: see entry 58 (18 December 1796), where the brethren wished another place had been chosen for the minister's sermon. *bij Baas Teunis op de poſt*: at Soetemelksvlei (see the glossary).
3. Line 15784 is missing. The transcription runs *van waar de Predikant des a⸗* / *hem zoo veele menſchen*. The lost line began *-vonds*, "evening", and probably said that the minister came back with a crowd of people following (*bracht met*) him. The words in the translation are only those that survive.
4. *de jonge Schmidt van de Kaap*: a son of Father Schmidt in Cape Town, with whom the brethren lodged (see Schmidt in the glossary).
