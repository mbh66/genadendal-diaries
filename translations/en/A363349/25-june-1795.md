<!-- lines 10012-10034 -->
On 3 June a family came from about 20 miles away to live here and hear the word of God. We also heard of someone in another district who would like to come here, but whom the farmer he serves will not let go. It is a district where the farmers are very displeased that the Hottentots are taught. We were told, as a certain truth, that a commando of farmers from the Strandveld would come here to fetch us away and take us over to Batavia, thinking that they would then soon be done with the Hottentots. The Hottentots stand amazed that such threatening rumours do not make us afraid; but when they speak with us about it, we tell them that nothing can happen to us without God's will, and therefore we are not afraid. Even the Hottentots are no longer as fearful as they were at the beginning.

<!-- lines 10035-10055 -->
On 8 June Jacob was at the post. Baas Teunis held him responsible for not having forbidden the Hottentots to tell Mr Claude that his son had impounded the Hottentots' cattle, for which reason he has now had to leave that place. Jacob answered: "We are taught that we may not lie, but must speak the truth." Baas Teunis replied: "I know that you are taught so; but all the same you [should not have] said it [1]; for now I must give up that place, and now I have come into bad credit with the authorities," and so on. On the 9th the youngest child of our Martha was baptised, at the mother's urgent and repeated request [2].

<!-- lines 10053-10081 -->
On the 13th we heard 4 cannon shots, which we judged to be signal shots, and this was confirmed the following day too. Such a shot shows that all Hottentots who can bear arms must go quickly to Cape Town. Since our Hottentots had no great wish to do so, we thought it necessary to remind them that it was their duty to obey the commands of the authorities. At this Jacob and Isaac left for there straight after the meeting, and were soon followed by the others. Abraham and David were ill and stayed at home for that reason. Isaac asked whether, if he came to close quarters with the enemy, he might shoot him dead. We knew nothing to answer to this but that he was bound to comply with the commando. We added the admonition to commend himself always to the Saviour, soul and body. We received a letter from Baas Teunis which made known to us that all the Hottentots with us must set off for the Cape; but this had already happened before his letter was received. We are very eager to know how things look at the Cape. It is generally believed that a French fleet is approaching.

<!-- lines 10082-10124 -->
On 17 June Mrs Teunis sent us a letter to learn from us whether we knew any news from the Cape. But we knew nothing, for at present neither a wagon nor any other message comes from there. On the 19th a tiger was discovered not far from us, which had bitten to death 5 goats belonging to the Hottentots [3]. Some went out with flintlocks to look for it, but they did not meet it. On the 20th we came to know that it was not the French but the English who were at the Cape. On the 21st we heard that a Hottentot from Stellenbosch, who was to go to the Cape, had taken refuge with us to get free [4]. This made us remind our people in the meeting not to give this Hottentot shelter. Not long after, Baas Teunis came and treated us very kindly. He had received orders to send to the Cape all the Hottentots he could lay hands on. In accordance with this order all the Hottentots still present here were called to gather in front of our house, not even excepting the old, the sick and the lame. We had not thought to find so many here still, since many had already left. Their number still came to 35 persons, of whom Baas Teunis picked out 18, and told them that they must set out on the road to the Cape very early tomorrow. Then he told us that things now stood so badly with us in this country that they could not be worse. The English lay with their fleet before the town, and in the countryside the farmers were in full revolt against their authorities, which went so far that they were deposing and appointing their Landdrosts, as has already happened with the Landdrost at Swellendam and his secretary. The farmers who did this were 125 in number [5].

<!-- lines 10125-10145 -->
On 22 June we again heard many [disagreeable rumours. Many farmers who] will not make common cause with the rebels are fleeing to the Cape, being no longer safe at home, since they are threatened with death [6]. We did not feel easy at this, knowing that the farmers are not at all content with our work among the Hottentots. Some of our Hottentots had heard a farmer's wife say that Baviaanskloof would soon be seen burning. On the 23rd we heard that the rebels were already on this side of Swellendam, and would go first to Baviaanskloof, and from here to the Cape. On the 24th the rumours became still more alarming, and we thought this way and that of a friend who could give us good advice.

<!-- lines 10146-10172 -->
No one else occurred to us but Andreas Otto, a field corporal, to whose ward we properly belong. He lives 3 miles from here, and the Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel rode to him for that purpose. Brother Schwinn, who stayed at home alone, declares that he has never yet known such a day. One rumour followed another that the rebels were drawing near. The baptised said: "If only they would spare our teachers!" and asked Brother Schwinn "whether we would flee?" Brother Schwinn answered that he did not yet know that, and would first wait for the report that his two brothers would bring him. At this they declared that wherever we went they too would go with us, and if we stayed here, they too would stay with us; for they wanted to live and die with their teachers. The candidates for baptism and many others said the same. Among others a candidate for baptism said: "Would God, who created heaven and earth, allow the rebels this deed? He is surely mighty enough to hold these people back, so that they do not come to us; let us only call upon him for it without ceasing."

<!-- lines 10173-10191 -->
"Towards evening," [writes Brother Schwinn,] "when I was in my room and pondering the present circumstances, I suddenly saw a crowd of people standing in front of my windows who looked to me as if they were armed. I was not a little frightened at this sight, thinking that it was the rebels; but behold, they were Hottentots, 12 in number, who carried on their shoulders a stick with a bundle hanging from it. They came from the Snake River, to come and live with us and hear the word of God. They had already been with Baas Teunis a couple of days, who had tried to make them change their purpose [7]; and since he did not succeed in this, he has ordered them to the Cape. Their wives and children are still with Baas Teunis, who would on no account let them go to Baviaanskloof, but insisted that they should go back again to their former dwelling place."

<!-- lines 10192-10227 -->
On 25 June the Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel came home again, and told the following of their journey: "When we came to the farmer Mary, we heard to our great joy that the rebels had drawn back and had not advanced any further. At the farmer Gildenheis's this news was confirmed. At Otto's we learned nothing new, but only the confirmation of what we had heard, that the rebels are said to have said that the first thing they would set about would be to destroy Baviaanskloof. We stayed the night with him. The next morning we rode home again. At Gildenheis's we stopped a little again. When we [wanted to go] on [our way] [8], they called us back quickly, to hear for ourselves what news the messenger, whom they saw coming from far off, and who probably came from the Cape, could tell us from there. And indeed he came straight from there, and brought letters in which it is reported that the English were making ready to attack the Cape, but that it had been resolved to defend it to the last man. The English already held False Bay. We also learned here that the rebels were said to have said that they would not kill the baptised Hottentots, but take them with them, and then indeed overpower them [9]. But Martha they meant to hack to pieces."

<!-- lines 10222-10251 -->
At Baviaanskloof the 12 Hottentots who had arrived yesterday stayed on one more day, so as to hear the word of God at least once, and attended the evening meeting. 8 women who belong to them arrived here on 26 June, after getting leave from Baas Teunis to visit here, but not to stay. Why, and by what authority, Baas Teunis does this, we do not know. The 12 men set out on the road to the Cape this morning. They went away very unwillingly, and would rather have stayed longer, to hear more of the word of God. The others told them that when their service at the Cape was done, they could come back. 27 June was the day for partaking of Holy Communion. We found it advisable, however, to put it off for this time, since both we and our communicants were too much distracted in mind by the present circumstances. The many rumours still go on, but one cannot rely on them. On the 29th the superintendent of the warm bath came to visit us. Of news he knew nothing more than that nothing decisive had yet happened at the Cape [10]. He stayed the night with us.
