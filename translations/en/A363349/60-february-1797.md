On 11 Feb. we were looking forward very much to Holy Communion, since it was just the day on which we had been appointed to this our post 5 years before [1]. But we were disturbed in this joy we had in view: for towards evening 3 farmers who live not far from the shore came to spend the night with us; for which reason we had to put off Holy Communion. In the evening we fell into a very pleasant conversation with these people. They declared that they had come to us for no other reason than to receive something for their hearts. They spoke very openly of their lost state, and were in want of grace and of the forgiveness of their sins. Since the minister Vos preached at the post, many people on the coast, as they told us, had been awakened; for in that sermon they had heard words such as had never yet come to their ears.

<!-- lines 15840-15849 -->
In the meeting on the 5th these people were much moved, and on taking leave they asked to be allowed to come again, and to bring their wives with them too [2]. "Ah!" said one of them, who otherwise did not speak much, "if only we might be so fortunate as to have one of you with us; for that we could never thank God enough in eternity."

<!-- lines 15850-15870 -->
On the 11th we had a visit from an English major with his wife, by name Thomas Edwards [3]. He came from Bengal, and was now taking the waters at the warm bath. He intended to go back at the first opportunity to England, where his father is a minister. He liked it very much with us, and he asked us, too, to give him a letter to take to our brothers in England, to whom he would then also tell by word of mouth how he had found things here. Besides this party [...] four others, so that we had to lodge 23 people: which gave us much trouble, since each party wanted a separate room for itself [4].

## Notes

1. The diary dates the brethren's appointment to this mission to 11 February 1792. 11 February 1797 was a Saturday, and the brethren usually held Holy Communion on Saturday evenings.
2. *In de vergadering den 5*: "in the meeting on the 5th". The farmers came on the evening of 11 February and the meeting must have been the Sunday meeting the next day, 12 February; *5* is probably a misreading of *12*. To be checked against the image.
3. *Den 11*: a second 11th in the same month, after the 12th; probably a misreading of a later date. To be checked against the image. *een Engelſchen Majoor met zijne vrouw, met naame Thomas Edwards*: not identified; an officer of the East India Company's army in Bengal on his way home.
4. Line 15866 is missing. The transcription runs *Behalven dit gezel⸗* / *ſchap* at the foot of page 263a and *vier andere, zoodat wij 23 menſchen moeſten beherbergen* on the next page, whose first line is lost. The lost line probably said that further visitors came, among them the four others. The words in the translation are only those that survive.
