Diary of January and February 1798.
After the meetings on New Year's Day the colonists who had kept the holidays here set out on the journey back, in part with a deep impression and a thankful heart, and so too the Hottentots who do not live here, but had come on a visit. [On 3 January the mason who works on] our mill, and is a slave, was called back [1]. He had just got so far with his work that we no longer needed him.

<!-- lines 12837-12861 -->
On the 6th a snake five feet long crept into our pigsty. Happily the pigs were not in it; otherwise they could all have been killed; for even horses are often bitten to death by this kind, which is very dangerous. It was shot dead with a gun. On the 13th a large party came here in 5 wagons, and stayed the night with us, partly in our house, partly under a tent. In this party was also the Governor's secretary, who is at present travelling through the country, and made inquiry, closely and with sympathy, into all the circumstances concerning our mission [2]. He, as well as others, [pointed out] to us a district where we could with advantage lay out a second place for our mission: which has already been said to us several times [3]. This whole party attended the meetings on Sunday the 14th; but after them went on with its journey.

<!-- lines 12862-12885 -->
Today we received a letter from the Landdrost of Stellenbosch, stating that we had been accused by the brothers Morgel over the running away from their service of a couple of Hottentot women who live with us. Seeing from this letter that many circumstances had been falsely given, Brother Marsveld set out on the 15th on the journey to Stellenbosch. The Landdrost declared that he did not in the least doubt the truth of our account, and added: the Morgels are restless fellows; which is why they must now keep 15 dragoons as well [4]. From there Brother Marsveld travelled to Cape Town, and had the pleasure of finding a packet from Europe there, which he brought us on the 21st. In it were reports from the congregations, Daily Texts, Brother Joseph's addresses to children [5], together with a letter from Brother Reichel, and two from Brother Latrobe; but still no further news of Brother and Sister Kohrhammer. We begin to fear that they will have been taken by a privateer.

<!-- lines 12886-12917 -->
Today the water came streaming down from the mountains so strongly and abundantly that our mill dam broke through. We called all the Hottentots to help, and in the space of 3 hours the damage was so far repaired that we could grind again. We had a still stronger flood on the 24th, in which we saw God's protection most plainly [6]. We found ourselves between two waters, namely the Sonderend River, and a new stream, which had washed out a bed for itself a good 6 ells deep. In this way our buildings were spared, although much was laid waste in our garden, and 15 of our sheep lost their lives as well. On the 30th, when the water had gone down fairly well, we could first rightly see how great the damage was which this flood had caused. Our mill dam had been washed away entirely, so that we are forced to make a new one. We had many visits from the Hottentots, especially from the newcomers, who are much awakened. But some girls cause us much concern, and [two of them had to be] sent away [as seducers] [7]. The soldiers, who are quartered everywhere in the country, are to blame for much mischief.

<!-- lines 12918-12952 -->
On 3 February we celebrated Holy Communion, which two sisters enjoyed for the first time. In these days we heard much of the damage the floods had done in the whole country. Many a farmer has lost 3, 4, even 600 sheep. On the 12th we saw ourselves obliged to exclude young Martha from our fellowship, which was made known in the meeting of the baptised, and caused great emotion and loud weeping [8]. On the 20th four adults were baptised into the death of Jesus. On the 25th we had the joy of receiving a consignment of goods that had been sent to us by way of Copenhagen, and which we had held to be lost, together with some letters, some of which were a good five years old. On the 28th we remembered our moving into our house 5 years ago, and thanked the Saviour for all that he has done for us since that time. In this month 5 persons have come among the candidates for baptism. Herewith we greet all our dear brothers and sisters in and outside the congregation, and commend ourselves anew to their remembrance and intercession.

## Notes

1. Line 12832 is missing. The transcription runs *maar ten bezoek gekomen waren.* / *onze molen werkt, en een ſlaaf is, te rug gevorderd*. The second copy (lines 13581 to 13582) has *Den 3. Jan:r werd de metselaar, die aan onze molen werkt*; the words in square brackets are supplied from there.
2. *de Secretaris van den Gouverneur, welke tegenwoordig het land doorreist*: perhaps John Barrow, private secretary to Governor Macartney, who travelled through the colony in 1797 and 1798. Not identified in the text.
3. *Hÿ zoowel als anderen aan den ons eene landſtreek, waar wÿ met voor deel eene tweede plaats ... zouden kunnen aanleggen*: the verb is garbled, perhaps *wezen ons ... aan*, "pointed out to us". The second copy (lines 13601 to 13605) has *Hy zoo wel als anderen roemden onze landstreek, maar wy met voordeel eene tweede plaats*, "praised our district", which does not fit the rest of the sentence. To be checked against the images of both copies.
4. *de Gebroeders Morgel*: see Morgel in the glossary. The second copy (lines 13612 to 13614) has *twee Hottentottinnen*, "two Hottentot women", and *dat my*, a slip for *wy*. The dragoons were quartered on them.
5. *Br: Joſephs kinderreden*: Brother Joseph was August Gottlieb Spangenberg; his addresses to children.
6. *den 24*; the second copy (line 13643) has "the 26th".
7. Line 12914 is missing. The transcription runs *veroorzaaken ons veel kommer, en* / *weggezonden worden*. The second copy (lines 13662 to 13664) has *en twee van dezelve moeſten als verleidereſſen weggezonden worden*; the words in square brackets are supplied from there.
8. Martha: probably the Martha baptised on 13 August 1793 (see the glossary), but the text calls her "young Martha", and she may be another. To be checked.
9. Pages 222a to 223b (lines 12953 to 12956) are blank in the transcription. The *6* at line 12957 stands before the second copy of these reports, which begins at line 12958; it is probably a page or quire number.
