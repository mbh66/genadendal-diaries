The year 1796.
On 1 January the farmer Grunewald with his wife and 4 children attended the New Year meeting, which we held in the open air. On the 2nd Brother Kühnel was at Daniel Dohi's, to buy a load of flour, which he promised to deliver in 8 days. Many of our Hottentots have been at the harvest both with him and with his brother, and both gave them the testimony that they had never had better people; they wished they could have the service of such every year. On the 4th Mrs Teunis visited us with 2 more ladies from the Cape, who behaved very kindly. On the 9th we held Holy Communion, which Maria partook of for the first time. On the 11th it was rainy, so that we had to stop our brick-making. Until now we have had the weather we wished for building; but what troubles us now is that we still have no carpenter. Towards evening Daniel Dohi brought the flour we had ordered. It was high time, for we were eating our last bread, and had no flour left at all. We have a great deal to do at present, since every day we have to provide more than 20 workmen with bread and meat [1].

<!-- lines 11761-11783 -->
On the 12th 2 Hottentot captains came here, and said that they meant to go to the Cape with part of their men, to [speak] with the General about the [farmers of the lower country], who are again very rebellious, and have also uttered threats against us and our Hottentots [2]. Several Hottentots who wanted to leave them are said to have had the sinew above the heel cut, to keep them from walking [3]. Since our carpenter has still not come, and we have got so far with the building that the carpenter must begin his work, we sent our cart to the Cape on the 13th, both to fetch him and the necessary ironwork.

<!-- lines 11783-11809 -->
On the 17th young Teunis visits us with 2 other gentlemen. One of them, named Paling, formerly a captain, but now, like all the others, out of service, has often been in Zeist, and here too he liked everything very much [4]. On the 18th Brother Kühnel rode into the bush, to see how far the felling of the timber had got, and also to hire a wagon from a farmer to bring the timber here. He got a wagon for 2 loads. It gives us much trouble to get wagons; for they suffer a great deal in the mountains, and since iron is so dear at present, a wagon now costs 300 rixdollars. If a farmer lets us have his wagon for one load, or two at most, that is a lot: and we shall have more than 10 loads to bring. The Hottentots give us the oxen for it.

<!-- lines 11809-11830 -->
On the 19th our cart came back from the Cape, and our carpenter with it. On the 20th he began his work at once. We sent 20 Hottentot women to a farm 1½ hours from here to thresh corn, so that we could get straw for the roof of our church. We get the straw from the farmer for nothing; but the threshing will cost us a great deal, since we have to keep those who do this work [5]. To hasten this labour, which will last at least 3 weeks, we decided that from now on one of us should always be there, each in his turn, however hard we might find it to stand the whole day in the great heat in the open air. We now had over 30 people at work.

## Notes

1. The Dutch does not have the German diary's last sentence, "Fortunately the brick-making will soon be finished."
2. Line 11766 is missing. The transcription runs *om met den Generaal over de* / *die wederom zeer oproerig zijn*. The German diary has "to speak with the General about the farmers of the Unterland" (its note 1); the words in square brackets are supplied from there.
3. *Verſcheidene Hottentotten, die van hun van hun wilden weggaan*: *van hun* is written twice. *de pees boven den hiel*, "the sinew above the heel"; the German diary has "the sinews under their calves".
4. *met naame Paling*; the German diary has *van Baling*. Not identified. See Zeist in the glossary.
5. *daar ^diegenen wij, die dit werk verrichten, moeten bekoſtigen*: the words are out of order, with *diegenen* inserted above the line; the sense is as translated. The German diary has "since we have to feed the people".
