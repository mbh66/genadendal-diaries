Now the following is further reported in the diary broken off above [1]:

<!-- lines 15340-15349 -->
On 26 March, being together again, we agreed to begin the instruction promised to the Hottentots, which they longed for very much. Accordingly we made known to them that a start would be made with it tomorrow, an hour before sunset, for the old people only. Today the Hottentots worked for nothing on the water channel that is to be restored. Many offered to go on with this work for nothing; but their poverty is so great that we could not ask it of them [2].

<!-- lines 15351-15376 -->
On the 27th we made the beginning of our instruction; and since these were just the days in which the suffering and dying of Jesus Christ is remembered in the Christian churches, we began by reading to them the 19th chapter of the Gospel of John and explaining it [3]. There were 41 men and women, who listened to it with such quietness and attention as one could hardly have imagined [4]; for we had heard not long before that it is hard to make the Hottentots keep quiet, and now they were so without our having spoken to them about it. On the 28th, being Maundy Thursday, we had for the first time the grace of refreshing and strengthening our hearts by partaking of Jesus' body and blood in Holy Communion, which we had long yearned for, and have only now been enabled to do by having a house of our own; for which we are most humbly and thankfully grateful to our gracious Lord and Saviour, since this also gives us the opportunity of holding our other meetings in rest and peace. The dear Saviour was [graciously near] us as we partook of [his body and blood. At the] instruction in the truths of the Gospel many Hottentots were present again [5].

<!-- lines 15377-15397 -->
On 29 March we had a visit from a farmer's widow with her children and two other persons, 9 in number altogether. They stayed with us through the afternoon. Now although such a visit, looked at from one side, is very dear and welcome to us, yet we would not wish that they should often stay over the midday meal, or even over the night besides; which, in our needy state, would be rather hard and costly for us, since hospitality is general in this country, and it is not unusual for people who come on a visit to stay one or two days and freely eat and drink with one, without a thought of payment. Most of them do not even think [to give thanks] on leaving, which the well-mannered among them do all the same [6]. Perhaps this may also come in useful to us when we travel through the country, since we shall not need to take money with us for it [7].

<!-- lines 15398-15432 -->
On the 30th we held a conference to consider matters concerning the Hottentots [8]. The school, which had been kept for 4 weeks, could no longer be held as it had been till now, since the number of pupils had increased greatly and still increases daily; most of them already know all the letters and are beginning to spell, for which more time is needed than before. Since the school with the children is also about to begin, we agreed to arrange the school hours after the Easter festival in the following way. At 8 o'clock in the morning the men, and after them the children until midday. In the afternoon the children come to school first, and are followed by the women. Then comes the catechising for the old people, in which something is read from the Bible and spoken about. So we are engaged in teaching the Hottentots in 3 divisions, each division being taken by one of us [9]. I wish that my dear brothers and sisters could attend the last meeting in the evening, to see with what reverence and with what longing the Hottentots listen to what is said and preached to them of the meritorious suffering and dying of the Saviour. Many a one of them listens with such longing as if he would take the word out of our mouths. With others the tears trickle down their cheeks. We must confess that the presence of God is to be felt most powerfully in all the meetings. We do not know what the Lord has in mind to do; but it truly looks very much as if the hour of salvation of the Hottentots has come.

<!-- lines 15433-15453 -->
On 31 March, being Easter Sunday, we prayed the Brethren's Easter liturgy early in the morning in our room [10]. At 10 o'clock there was the sermon on the usual Gospel for Easter Day. 44 Hottentots were present, as attentive and quiet as one hardly finds in a Christian church. Among these 44 there were no children. We did wish, though, that the children over 12 years old could also be present, both at the sermon and at the daily instruction in the truths, which is held in the evening; this would be very necessary. But the space in the room, nine ells in length and 7 in breadth, is not big enough for 50 listeners, whose number is set to grow larger still, as we hear that many wish to come and live with us. When we came here, not a single house stood here yet, and now there are already 13 in the neighbourhood of our dwelling.

<!-- lines 15454-15470 -->
With this we close our diary for this time, trusting in faith that God [...] [will not let it be] put out, but that it will burn so that his name may be glorified by this despised people too [11]. Help us poor ones, my dear brothers and sisters, to beg of our kind Lord and Saviour what we need for carrying out his service among these poor blind Hottentots. To this end we commend ourselves and our Hottentots to the prayers and remembrance of the whole Unity of Brethren, as your 3 brethren united with you.

<!-- lines 15471-15474 -->
(Signed) Hendrik Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn and Johann Christian Kühnel [12].

<!-- lines 15475-15477 -->
Dated at Baviaanskloof on the Sergeants River in Africa, 31 March 1793.

<!-- lines 15478-15486 -->
Postscript. One more thing should be noted here. Great lovers of dancing as the Hottentots are, and used as they are to dance every evening in their dwelling places, we have yet noticed nothing of it among those who now live with us, without our having said anything to them about it.

## Notes

1. *Nu wordt verder in het boven Afgebroken Dagverhaal, dit nog gemeld*: the copyist's or editor's words, marking the return to the diary after Brother Marsveld's report (entry 55). The German diary has *Es heißt nun im Diario weiter* at the same place (A363595, entry 06).
2. This settles the German *daß ſie dieſes alles ſelten umſonſt machten* (A363595, entry 06, note 2): the Khoekhoe offered to go on working on the channel without pay, but the brethren would not ask it of them.
3. *met het 19.t Hoofdſtuk van Joh*: John 19, the crucifixion. The German diary has "the 17th chapter of John", Jesus' prayer before the Passion. The two copies differ; John 19 fits the days of the Passion. To be checked against the images.
4. The transcription garbles this sentence with repeated words (*dat men* / *Volk eene Stilte en aandacht het zich naauwlyks* / *het zich naauwlyks* / *zoo had kunnen voorſtellen*). The English gives the sense.
5. Line 15374 is missing. The transcription runs *De Lieve Heiland, was ons by het Genot van* / *Onderrigt der Evangeliſche Waarheden, waren*. The German diary has "The dear Saviour graciously acknowledged us. The Hottentots came diligently to the instruction again"; the words in square brackets are supplied from its sense.
6. *De meesten van hun denken by 't weggaan niet eens*, "most of them do not even think on leaving": a word such as *danken*, "to thank", is missing. The German diary has "and if one is polite, one says thank you on leaving; it does not occur to anyone to ask about paying". The German adds that the widow lived 2 hours from the mission.
7. The German diary breaks off at this point, at the foot of its page 107 (A363595, entry 06, note 4). The rest of the entry, from 30 March to the end of the diary, is known only from this Dutch copy.
8. The transcription reads *D. 30 hadden wy Conferentie, om te overleggen.* / *betrekkelyk de Hottentotten te overleggen*, with *te overleggen* written twice.
9. The parenthesis that opens before *Elke Afdeeling* is not closed.
10. *de Liturgie der Broeder Gemeente op den Eerſten Paaſch dag*: the Easter morning liturgy of the Brethren, prayed at daybreak.
11. Line 15456 is missing. The transcription runs *geloovig vertrouwende, dat God* / *uitblusſchen, maar zoo brandende worden*. The lost line probably said that God would not let the fire he had kindled among the Khoekhoe be put out; the words in square brackets give the sense of the surviving words only. There is no German text for this passage.
12. *(Was geteken)*: "(signed)", the copyist's note that the signatures were in the original. The transcription reads *Joh: Chriſtian Kühn*, cut short.
13. Line 15487 is the marker of page 257a, and lines 15488 to 15489 are the docket on the outside of the copy: *Dagverh. van de Kaap. van 23 Nov. 92 tot d. 31 Mrt. 93.* Line 15491, *N. XII. A. 1797.*, belongs to the next diary in the file (1 November 1796 to 19 May 1797).
