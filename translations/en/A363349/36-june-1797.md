On 4 and 5 June we celebrated Whitsun, and can testify that all the meetings, especially those of the baptised and candidates for baptism, were crowned with grace and blessing. We were glad that it had rained hard in the preceding days; otherwise we would have had many visitors, which would have caused us many hindrances. On the 7th we spoke with the candidates for baptism, who gave us joy. The blessing they had enjoyed during the holidays could be clearly seen in them. In the evening Brother Schwinn was called to a sick woman. To the question whether she knew where she would go when she had died, she answered: I shall go to the Saviour! And why? he asked her again: because he shed his blood for me. At present many are sick, especially with pain in the side [1].

<!-- lines 12285-12322 -->
On the 11th some farmers attended the Sunday preaching; among others also a millwright, whom we had asked to come to us some time, so as to be able to speak with him about building a mill. For we meet with so many difficulties in getting our corn ground that we are forced to build a mill of our own. We also have the best opportunity for it on our place, and many a farmer has told us that he would gladly give 400 rixdollars for it if he had one like it on his land [2]. We did think of putting this off until we had received reinforcement; but a new circumstance persuaded us to make haste with this matter. And it was this. The farmer Kuns, who until now had supplied us with flour, though sparingly enough, must now grind for the English soldiers who lie at the post, and cannot think of us at all any more [3]. A mill on our place will also be of great benefit to the Hottentots. For at present they have to take their corn four, even six German miles to the mill, and pay dearly, and wait a good eight to fourteen days for it besides. Yet we must still have patience: for the carpenter, who intends to go to New Holland, where he thinks he will become a rich man, had no wish to take on this work first [4].

<!-- lines 12323-12333 -->
On the 12th seven adults, namely two men and five women, were baptised, at which act the congregation was exceptionally numerous, and the presence of God was very perceptible. On the 18th we received a letter from Mr Heuser at the Cape, and in it one from Brother Latrobe in London, from which we saw, to our great joy, that we were to expect Brother and Sister Kohrhammer as our reinforcement [5].

<!-- lines 12335-12357 -->
On the 22nd Brother Marsveld was fetched to a girl who was sick. She declared to him that she wished to go to the Saviour, and called on him day and night for grace. [On the 23rd a Hottentot woman who] had lately come among the candidates for baptism departed this life happily [6]. Her father told us that she had prayed to the Saviour until her end. Another sick woman, to whom Brother Kühnel had been called, said that she had no fear of death at all, and that she prayed God for the forgiveness of her sins. On the 24th we celebrated Holy Communion, which Elisabeth enjoyed with us for the first time. On the 26th an account of the Eskimos in Labrador was read in the meeting of the baptised, by which they were particularly moved.

## Notes

1. *bijzonders aan Lydemee*; the second copy (lines 13032 to 13033) has *aan Zÿde mee*. Both are probably *zijdewee*, "pain in the side", pleurisy.
2. *wel 400 Rijkdd:*; the second copy (line 13046) has *1000 Rÿksd:*. To be checked against the images of both copies.
3. *De boer Kunz*: probably Christian Kuns; see the glossary.
4. New Holland: Australia.
5. *Br: J. Z. Kohrhammer*; the second copy (line 13082) has *Br: en Zr: Kohrhammer*, "Brother and Sister Kohrhammer", which is followed here. The same misreading occurs in entry 38. Brother Latrobe is probably Christian Ignatius Latrobe, secretary of the Moravian mission society in London. Mr Heuser: see the glossary.
6. Line 12340 is missing. The transcription runs *en Hem dag en nacht om genade aanriep.* / *onlangs onder de Doopcandidaaten gekomen was, zalig uit de tijd*. The second copy (lines 13089 to 13090) has *Den 23 ging eene Hottentottin, welke*; the words in square brackets are supplied from there. *ging zalig uit de tijd*: literally "went happily out of time"; see entschlafen in the glossary.
