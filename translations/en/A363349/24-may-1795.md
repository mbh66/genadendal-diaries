<!-- lines 9795-9823 -->
On 1 May we partook of Holy Communion [1]. Susanna went with us for the first time, and Anna looked on as a confirmand. On the 3rd we announced after the meeting that the custom until now, of coming before and after the meeting to greet us one by one, should from now on be left off, since it was subject to various difficulties. We knew that they had regarded it as a declaration of duty in which no one wanted to fall short; but we could assure them that we would not regard their not coming as a neglect of duty, and would gladly release them from it. Today many came to speak with us one by one; among others the mother of our Louise, who lives two hours' walk from here and comes here every Sunday to hear the sermon. She said: "For some time I have felt so uneasy that I cannot bear it at home. As soon as I think of Baviaanskloof I have a blessed feeling, especially when Sunday draws near; and when I come here I am glad and blessed; but when I go back home, it is to me as if I were going into the fire." She is married to a slave, and so cannot come as often as she would like.

<!-- lines 9823-9839 -->
On 4 May we remembered in our morning blessing the single sisters in the congregations, and sang them some verses of blessing for their festival. On the 5th, early, before it was day, a Hottentot woman came and said "that she did not know where to turn for the unrest of her heart." To the question "why?" her answer was: "because the Saviour had had to suffer so much for the sake of her sins" [2]. Many more followed her today. In general there has been a great stirring among the Hottentots since Passion Week. Many come to tell us that they too would gladly be saved, and each time this goes with many tears.

<!-- lines 9840-9872 -->
On the 7th it rained all day, to the great benefit of our garden. The Hottentots, even those who live an hour's walk from here, did not let this rain keep them from coming to school and to church. For our goats, on the other hand, the rainy weather is very harmful: on 8 May nine of them died. Altogether, of the 106 goats that we bought, we have only 36 left [3]; and we are told that in lasting rainy weather we shall lose them all. On the 13th we spoke with all the candidates for baptism, most of whom gave us joy. On the 14th, Ascension Day, a blessed feeling was to be perceived in all the meetings. Of the 6 persons who had lately been put out of the school, 4 were accepted again. One of them, who was a candidate for baptism, said that she implores the Saviour day and night for forgiveness and cannot sleep for unrest, and wished very much to be allowed back into her class. Hanna, who had already been excluded for a considerable time, had indeed asked several times to be accepted again, but had not been able to bring herself to confess her guilt openly. But today it came so far with her, and we could no longer refuse to accept her again; which was also the case with an excluded Hottentot man. Both showed their thankfulness with tears.

<!-- lines 9873-9910 -->
On 16 May in our conference we talked about our Hottentots, and could not thank the Saviour enough for all that he has done for them. 2 men and 4 women were appointed to be baptised. In the evening we had them come to us once more, to ask them how things stood in their inmost hearts; [their answer, that they desired nothing] else than to live in this world for the Saviour alone through his grace, was accompanied by an unspeakably blessed feeling [4]. On the 17th it was announced in the meeting at the church prayer that Hanna had painful repentance over her transgression, and implored the whole congregation for forgiveness, at which many tears were shed. At 10 o'clock was the baptism. Each of us baptised two of them, with the names David, Abraham, Juliana, Rosina, Johanna and Carolina. We had never yet baptised so many at once, and the peace of God was powerfully to be felt in our midst. In the afternoon we saw the 4 newly baptised sisters taking a walk in company with most of the other sisters, which brought to our minds the walk of the single sisters in the congregations on their choir festival in white clothes; for our baptised Hottentot women were also dressed in white, and the other Hottentot women give all they possibly can to put them in a state to do so [5]. It helps them in this, too, that when they have worked for the farmers they often receive, instead of payment in cash, cast-off clothes of the women, which are many times still quite good.

<!-- lines 9911-9943 -->
On the 18th and 19th we hired 4 Hottentots to cut firewood for us. On the 24th, the first day of Pentecost, the farmer Harme visited us with his wife. We had expected more visitors on this day, since several parties had sent word; but the heavy rain prevented it, which was really welcome and pleasant to us; for the farmers' visits on Sunday are very burdensome to us, because they take up most of the room, and the poor Hottentots, for whose sake we are here after all, have to stand outside the door. Most of them also come only out of curiosity, so as to be able to say that they have been in the church of the Hottentots. In the afternoon we had the adoration with the baptised and the candidates for baptism. On the second day of Pentecost a man was baptised with the name Isaak. Today we looked on our people with the greatest compassion. It was raining very hard, and the people were already wet when they came, and now the greater part of them still had to sit outside in all the rain to hear the sermon; and yet they heard it with the greatest attention. How much we wished to have a roomy church for these poor people, which could hold them all! As yet it is not to be foreseen; but God is mighty to help.

<!-- lines 9944-9956 -->
On 27 May we heard that a man, one of those who have not been here long, had woken a candidate for baptism to tell him that he could not sleep for the unrest of his heart, asking him to tell him something of what Jesus had suffered for sinners; which he also did as well as he was able. Many came to us one by one in these days and made known to us their concern to be saved.

<!-- lines 9957-9970 -->
On the 30th we had Holy Communion, which our Anna partook of for the first time. Today we also received letters from our dear brothers of the Unity Elders' Conference, of April and August of last [year. At the end of this month the little] congregation of the Hottentots [consisted] of [6]:

9 communicants
1 confirmand
1 candidate for Communion
17 baptised adults
1 baptised child
14 candidates for baptism
Total 43 persons.

<!-- lines 9971-10011 -->
Our Martha had often asked us to tell her when we would write to the fatherland, so that she could send a letter too, which runs as follows as she dictated it:

"When some sisters had arrived at the Cape lately, I longed very much to see them; and I would have wished still more that they had come to us and stayed with us. Oh, when will the good fortune come to us of seeing sisters with us, to stay with us? Oh, if only that time might come soon! I let you know that I cannot thank the Saviour enough that he has brought me to Baviaanskloof. Where else would my poor soul have ended up! Now I have been here about 2 years. When I think over what I was before my baptism, when I lived in all sins, and what I feel now, I stand amazed and ashamed, since the dear Saviour has also given me the great grace of partaking of his body and blood in Holy Communion. I do feel myself very poor and bad, but also his peace in my heart; and my constant prayer is that he may keep me with him until I come to him. Receive, all of you, my dear brothers and sisters, the most heartfelt thanks that you have sent us teachers and also support them; for we, being a people poor to the bone, have nothing to give them for their living. The dear Saviour be praised too, that he has given me still more brothers and sisters from my nation who love him. Oh, if only we might all live to his honour! Greet all the brothers and sisters in the fatherland.
Martha."
