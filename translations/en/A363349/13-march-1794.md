<!-- lines 4604-4605 -->
On 2 March Brother Schwinn had an attack of pain in the back, which wore him out greatly.

<!-- lines 4605-4620 -->
Today a farmer from the Tigerhoek was here to demand back 2 girls who had been with him and have now come here. They are the children of one of his slaves by a Hottentot woman, and formerly, by the laws of the land, such children had to serve until their 25th year the man to whom the slave belonged. But this has now been abolished by the authorities; the children now belong to the mother and are therefore free. He had also brought with him a German, who studied at Jena, to support him with his eloquence. But it was in vain with us, for we simply appealed to the orders of the authorities. For the rest, we informed Baas Teunis of this incident.

<!-- lines 4620-4624 -->
On the 3rd [1] we remembered with thankfulness moving into our house a year ago today, and the benefits we have enjoyed since, beyond our expectation.

<!-- lines 4624-4631 -->
On the 4th Baas Teunis wrote to us that we had done very well to refuse the request of the farmer mentioned above; for if we had allowed it to one, more would always come.

<!-- lines 4631-4633 -->
In these days 3 more children [were buried. Of those who have died, apart from the school]children, the rest were all still very young [2].

<!-- lines 4633-4642 -->
On the 6th a boy of about 10 years, who lives an hour from us, sent to ask Brother Kühnel to visit him. When he came there, the boy could no longer speak, but gave Brother Kühnel his hand and pressed it. Shortly before, he had been looking forward to the coming Saturday, and believed that he would then be well and able to attend the children's meeting. He was commended to the Saviour in a heartfelt prayer.

<!-- lines 4642-4648 -->
On the 8th Anna Maria and Elisabeth were accepted as candidates for Communion. With Martha we came together once more by ourselves, to bless her with the laying on of hands for her first partaking of Holy Communion. In the evening we had this enjoyment, with our Lord powerfully owning us.

<!-- lines 4649-4665 -->
On the 9th another child was buried. Among the sick who still lie ill is a woman whose whole body is full of sores and who suffers great pain. Yet it is a rare pleasure to visit her. She says: "I have much pain, but the Saviour endured much more pain for my sake." Question: whether she could then take to herself the merits of Jesus' pains, and was assured in her heart that he would take her to himself into his kingdom out of grace? Answer: "I am not worthy of it, I know that; but I believe that he will have mercy on me, and so I pray to him day and night." Others also told us that she spends day and night in prayer [3].

<!-- lines 4666-4680 -->
On the 10th a Bastard woman who lives half an hour from here, and lay ill, sent to ask for a visit. Some time ago she had been much awakened, and bore with patience much ill-treatment from her father, who is still dead in sins, because she would no longer join in the former things; but afterwards she fell asleep again and became quite indifferent. Brother Kühnel reminded her of what she had heard in the meetings, and asked her whether she had sought and found forgiveness of sins. She said that she still felt lost and could not believe that she would be saved, and so on. She was heartily directed to the Saviour, to whom prayer was also made for her.

<!-- lines 4680-4682 -->
Today a young woman passed away here, of whom we can believe that the Saviour has accepted her in grace.

<!-- lines 4682-4690 -->
On the 13th another child was buried, and also a boy of about 16 years. [So far] 22, big and small, have died of this sickness, and very many are still lying ill with it [4].

<!-- lines 4690-4702 -->
Towards midday a doctor who lives 4 hours from here came to examine the nature of the sickness raging here. He found that it is a bilious fever, and that an emetic must be used against it. He made up a bottle of medicine, whose ingredients he had brought with him, and gave us directions as to what we should give of it to the sick. We also asked him about Brother Schwinn's leg, in which he has suffered much pain. He declared his complaint to be gout, and showed us a certain herb that grows here in plenty as a good remedy against it.

<!-- lines 4702-4706 -->
On the 14th another child was buried. We made known to our people in the evening meeting that if they felt unwell they could get medicine from us, and at that 5 persons came.

<!-- lines 4706-4718 -->
On the 17th early the wife of a Bastard came to us, driven by the unrest of her heart. She said that she did not know where to turn for anguish, for she believed that she must be lost. This woman and her husband have until now had much confidence in themselves, and said that they had believed in God from childhood and grown up among the Christians. [They] could not understand that they, like the others, had to seek grace and forgiveness of sins [5]. Now the woman is coming to know herself.

<!-- lines 4719-4720 -->
On the 18th another child was buried, and so on the 20th.

<!-- lines 4720-4731 -->
On the 22nd in our conference 2 persons were chosen for holy baptism, and one for the class of candidates for baptism. Since because of the sickness raging here we have no visits from strangers to expect at present, it was resolved to perform the baptism on Sunday the 23rd. Towards evening, when we wanted to have the 2 who were to be baptised called, we heard that one of them had fallen ill. So we let it be put off with her, and spoke with the other, who declared herself moved and longing. She received holy baptism on the 24th [6] with the name Susanna.

<!-- lines 4731-4748 -->
Today we received from Mr Heuser [7] a packet with some letters from Zeist and Amsterdam, which were very welcome to us, and likewise a letter from Captain Olhof, who brought us here from Holland. He wrote to us that he would gladly have visited us, but his ship had to hurry on to Batavia. Mr Heuser also wrote to us that he had news that the ship *Noord Holland*, which has not yet arrived, has a small chest and an anvil [8] on board for us. We now wait eagerly for this ship to come, and hope that we shall receive more letters, news, and also Daily Texts, for we lack these last for this year [9].

<!-- lines 4748-4769 -->
On the 25th a Hottentot came from the Snake River, 3 days' journey from here, and said that he would like to live [here] so as to be able to hear God's word. He told us that the Hottentots in his district had been very glad at the news that teachers had come into the country for them. To the question whether many Hottentots lived there, he said that there were many, and not so poor as those who live here; they all still had some sheep, cows and oxen. Some, he added, were still somewhat afraid because of the rumour that they would be carried off to Batavia as soon as they had learned something. Further, the general talk was that when we had been a year in Baviaanskloof we would come to them, because Baviaanskloof was a poor place where not many can live. He said further that they were waiting for us there with longing; for him it had lasted too long, so he had resolved to move here, and perhaps more would follow him.

<!-- lines 4769-4784 -->
On the 29th in our conference a person was accepted among the candidates for baptism. Further we considered whether we should pay a visit to the Snake River [10], since we heard so much about the Hottentots living there, but now this and now something else again, and we would gladly look into it ourselves. So it was resolved that Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel should travel there, and Brother Schwinn should carry on the work here meanwhile, as far as was in his power. On the 31st the brothers set out on their journey [11].

## Notes

1. The transcription reads *Den 31tn*, which cannot stand between the 2nd and the 4th; the German diary has "On the 3rd". The brethren moved into their house early in March 1793. To be checked against the image.
2. Line 4632 is missing. The transcription runs *In deeze Dagen werden wederom 3 Kinderen* / *Kinderen, de overigen waren alle nog Zeer Jong*. The German diary has "In these days 3 more children were buried. Of those who have gone home so far, all were still very small, apart from the schoolchildren". The words in square brackets are supplied from the sense.
3. The German diary goes on here with the dreams of the Khoekhoe of the mission, and this woman's dream that she must be given a letter to the Saviour to take with her when she died. The Dutch does not have it.
4. The transcription reads *Eer Zijn van deeze Ziekte groot* / *groot en klein 22 geſtorven*; *Eer* is probably *Er*, and "So far" is supplied from the sense. The German diary has "22 have now died of this sickness". Where the German diary has "another young woman was buried with her newborn child", the Dutch has only "another child".
5. Line 4714, the last on page 71a, is missing, and page 71b begins *Zij konde niet verſtaan*, "she could not understand". The plural that follows (*moeſten*) and the German diary ("They could not understand") suggest *Zij* means both husband and wife. The German diary names the woman Trin.
6. The Dutch has Sunday the 23rd for the planned baptism and then *den 24tn* for the baptism itself; the German diary has the 23rd for both. See Susanna in the glossary.
7. *den Heer Heuſſen*: Mr Heuser of Cape Town, who forwarded the brethren's letters; see Heuser in the glossary.
8. *een kiſtje en een Ambeeld*: *aambeeld*, "anvil". The German diary has "a small chest and a bag".
9. *Looſingen*, the Daily Texts; see Losungen in the glossary. The German diary has *Rechnungen*, "accounts", probably a misreading; the Dutch settles it (compare note 7 of entry 19 of A363595). The German diary also records on the 21st a harvest of maize and on the 27th a letter from Mrs Schmidt about the new minister Vos; the Dutch has neither.
10. The transcription reads *of wij in Slangen Rivier eene Zonder bezoeken*, probably *eens zouden bezoeken*, "whether we should once visit". Translated to the sense.
11. The German diary has "On the 30th".
