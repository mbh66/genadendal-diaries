On 1 September we had in the night such a heavy thunderstorm here as we have not yet known in this country. The rain was very heavy, but we did not expect such a flood as came upon us afterwards. At 8 o'clock in the morning the water came down from the mountains with violence, and flooded the greater part of our garden, and washed away much soil; and it will cost no small trouble to repair the damage again. On the 3rd we finished reading the [protocol of the] Ministers' Conference of 1796, which we have read with great blessing [1]. On this occasion we greet its members most heartily, and commend ourselves and the poor Hottentots to their intercession and remembrance.

<!-- lines 12459-12477 -->
On the 5th we let 5 Hottentots go into the bush to fell timber for building our mill; but the next day one of them came back and told us that Baas Teunis had forbidden them, and shown them another place, where there was nothing but useless timber to be found. Two days before he had said just the opposite; for which reason Brother Kühnel rode to the post to speak with him and the Major about it. The latter gave him complete freedom, and a pass for our people as well. Today a girl of about 13 years was buried [2]. Shortly before her death she said: the Saviour has forgiven me my sins; now I wish to go to him, and I rejoice at it very much.

<!-- lines 12478-12499 -->
On the 7th there blew so violent a storm as we have not yet had here [3]. We feared greatly for the roof of our church, and called the Hottentots to help; but nothing was damaged except a window frame, which was smashed. The damage to our fruit trees in the garden was more considerable. On the 9th a sick little boy of about 9 years had one of us called to him. To the question what he had to say, he answered: I shall go to the Saviour. Question: do you believe, then, that he will accept you? Answer: yes, I have offered him my heart! A girl of 11 spoke in the same way. On the 16th we enjoyed Holy Communion, at which Petrus looked on for the first time. In this month some children were baptised.

<!-- lines 12500-12525 -->
On the 18th we had a visit from the minister Krisch from the Cape, with four other gentlemen [4]. On the 19th this party went round the kraals with much pleasure, [and also attended the meeting. The] minister was much moved, and much amazed at the stillness and attentiveness, as also at the singing of the Hottentots, and said: that is God's work! [5] On the 20th this party went away again. The Sunday meeting on the 22nd was attended by 15 strangers [6], and shortly after, together with the Major and 2 more officers, Mr Sommerfeld, commissary over the posts, came to us. The latter stayed the night with us, and we took this opportunity to ask him whether we could not make more use of our bell [7]. He promised to speak with the Governor about it. On the 31st a man named Nathanael was baptised.
