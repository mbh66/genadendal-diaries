<!-- lines 11435-11441 -->
Diary of the Brothers Marsveld, Schwinn and Kühnel, [at] Baviaanskloof, from 7 December 1795 to 14 March 1796 [1].

<!-- lines 11442-11466 -->
After Brother Marsveld's return from the Cape, where he had received leave from the English General to fell building timber for a church, we considered on 7 December how large we should build it. We agreed to make the hall 60 feet long and 18 feet wide, with a room at each side 16 feet square. It being now the highest time to begin this work, we sent at once for the mason who had worked on our house. He is at present with the Landdrost, 3 hours from here, and promised to come to us within 8 days, and to work for a fair price. The usual fixed wage for a mason here is 1 rixdollar a day. To hasten the work he meant to bring help with him. That same day the building site, right opposite our house, was pegged out.

<!-- lines 11466-11487 -->
On the 8th we began to dig the ground, and the watchword struck us greatly: "They shall come from far that shall build the temple of the Lord. And no one does it there as if he must!" [2] It was fulfilled to the letter; for just today 5 Hottentots came here with wives and children from far up country, to hear God's word, as they themselves said. They rejoiced greatly at the sight of the work we had begun, and promised to help in whatever they could. Brother Kühnel rode to Christian Kuns to buy boards from him [3]. On the 9th he was at the post, to show Baas Teunis the written leave we had received to cut building timber, and to come to an agreement with him about what was needed.

<!-- lines 11488-11499 -->
Today we spoke with the baptised, and could rejoice over most of them. On the 10th we had a visit from 2 gentlemen from the Cape, who then went on to the post [4]. Today the mason told us that he had not been able to get help. So we wrote to the Cape for a mason and a carpenter.

<!-- lines 11500-11530 -->
On the 12th we celebrated Holy Communion, at which Maria was present as a confirmand and Catharina as a candidate. On the 13th the mason arrived here. With the women and children we had already brought together as many stones as we could, to build the foundation with them. Yesterday we had also begun to make bricks, of which we shall need about 60000. The side walls are made of unburnt bricks, after the manner of this country, but for the gable they must be burnt. All this will cost labour; but at present we are not so much at a loss for bricks as for the timber, which is still growing, while we have no one to fell it, [and besides we] shall have to fetch [the stones from far away] [5]. Today we announced in the meeting that we intended to lay the foundation stone for our church early tomorrow, and asked everyone, great and small, to be present at it.

<!-- lines 11531-11563 -->
On the 14th, at half past 5 in the morning, everyone here was gathered. We placed them round the building site and sang a verse, after which Brother Marsveld said that we had long wished to have a larger place for our meetings; that the Saviour had now so ordered it that we had received leave for it: now we had come together to lay the foundation stone for it, and to implore God for his blessing and help. After a verse had been sung, Brother Marsveld knelt down on the foundation stone, which the congregation followed, and called on the Saviour from the heart to let this building be a blessing to many people, and to keep all who would work on it from harm. Then the close was made with the singing of a verse. A mighty sense of grace showed itself during this act. The mason confessed afterwards that when he had to lay the stone, he had felt something he could not describe. The watchword on that day was: "Them that honour me I will honour." The election is worthy of our reverence; what are we worms, that the Lord so honours us! [6]

<!-- lines 11564-11586 -->
On the 16th 2 Hottentots came home who had worked in the harvest for a farmer, and now meant to go to another. We would gladly have kept them with us, since we are at a loss for workmen; but we might not keep them back from the farmer. When they had already gone an hour on their way from us, such fear came over them that they could go no further; so they turned back, and offered of their own accord to work for us. On the 19th many Hottentots who had been at the harvest came home again, and we put them to work at once. Even with these few people we had made good progress in the last week. On the 21st still more came home from the harvest, and on the 22nd 15 more quite new Hottentots, who had lived under Swellendam and now wanted to hear God's word.

<!-- lines 11586-11600 -->
On the 23rd the speaking was held with the candidates for baptism. We had been anxious about those who had worked for the farmers, as regards wine-drinking; but so far we have found no cause for concern. Today a large family came here to live [7]. Andreas Otto also sent to ask us not to hold the meeting on Christmas Day too early, since he and several others would like to attend it.

<!-- lines 11601-11622 -->
On the 24th the mason we had asked for came [8]. Today the stone wall was also finished. Straight after the holidays the building will go on with dried bricks. Towards evening the two women Lind and Mary visited us, which was not agreeable to us, because on this festive evening we meant to hold a lovefeast with our baptised Hottentots [9]. We could do nothing but tell them that we would like to be alone with our baptised, and asked them to stay in our room meanwhile. They did not seem very pleased with this, but they put up with it. Before the lovefeast we held a general singing meeting, at which we played the flute [10].

<!-- lines 11623-11656 -->
In the following night it rained so hard that nothing came of the visit we were to expect on the first holiday, which suited us very well. The weather turned out, however, such that we could hold the meeting in the open air. A great many Hottentots were present here, who also stayed here over the holidays: we reckon their number at about 500. On the 26th, early in the morning, we sang the hymn of praise in honour of our Saviour with our baptised and candidates for baptism [11]. At 10 o'clock 4 persons, named Sara, Regina, Wilhelmina and Benigna, were baptised into the death of Jesus. On the 27th we closed the holidays with praise and thanks to our dear Lord for the blessing with which he had crowned our testimony of his becoming man and his death. In all the meetings he showed himself in his power to the hearts of all who were present. We believe that many who were here only on a visit will have taken an impression home with them. This afternoon we sent several Hottentots into the bush to clear trees, and on the 28th we sent them a couple more, so that we now have 8 strong men at this work.

<!-- lines 11657-11665 -->
On the 30th a Hottentot captain arrived here with 8 Hottentots. They live a good 20 miles from us, and had with them a Hottentot prisoner who had beaten his wife to death and was now to be taken to the Cape. They asked leave to stay here until after New Year.

<!-- lines 11666-11680 -->
On the 31st, towards midnight, we had a general meeting to close the year, to which the Hottentots came running from all sides. Brother Kühnel reminded them of the many benefits of God they had enjoyed this year, and urged them to praise and thank God for them, whereupon we began the new year with prayer and supplication [12]. What feeling prevailed in this meeting we are not able to describe. The prayer was broken off by loud weeping.

<!-- lines 11681-11704 -->
Now, dear brothers and sisters! what [shall we say of this] year! [13] We lack words to express ourselves. The devil has raged horribly, and he has even succeeded in making us leave our sheep for a time. What we went through then we are not able to describe. But who would have thought that the Saviour would have changed everything so quickly, yes, so changed it that we now see a wall of our church already above the ground! When we behold the mighty work of grace in the hearts of these poor people, we stand ashamed and rapt in wonder. For some time new Hottentots have come almost every day, so that their number is growing noticeably.

<!-- lines 11705-11714 -->
This year 20 adults and 4 children have been baptised; 5 persons have come to Communion, and 4 have become candidates for it. 24 have come among the candidates for baptism. One sister has died. At the end of this year our little congregation consists of [14]:

<!-- lines 11715-11723 -->
9 communicants
1 confirmand
3 candidates for Communion
21 baptised adults
4 baptised children
27 candidates for baptism
Total 65 persons, of whom, however, one baptised person and one candidate for baptism are excluded for the time being.
