<!-- lines 16206-16210 -->
On 6 July we spoke with our candidates for baptism, whom we found in such a state that we could rejoice over them. One of them we had had to exclude, who, having now moved away from here, is no longer counted as one of us.

<!-- lines 16211-16214 -->
Today we received a letter from our friend Heuser, the watchmaker at the Cape, with a request for 3 Hottentots to go with him on a journey for his health that he meant to make [1].

<!-- lines 16215-16238 -->
On the 8th we also received a letter from the Landdrost of Stellenbosch, together with a copy of a long letter to him from the English Governor, Major-General Craig, who wrote that the Landdrost had brought forward the complaints of the privy councillor Clüde about the cattle of our Hottentots, and had at the same time offered to give up another 20 minutes of land for them, so that they could now count on a pasture of 50 minutes; and that he now wished to fix the boundaries with stones [2]; but (the General wrote) that then all the cattle that came over those boundaries would be taken from them. In this letter we were also ordered to admonish the Hottentots that in future they must herd their cattle better and behave well towards their neighbours: for otherwise they would fall into the hands of justice. As for this admonition, we can hardly do more than we have done until now. Nevertheless we read the letter to them, and urged obedience to what it said on them as earnestly and as strongly as was possible.

<!-- lines 16238-16254 -->
Meanwhile this turn of events came upon us unexpectedly; because we had ourselves heard from the Landdrost, not long ago, that this place of Mr Clüde's was to be reckoned only as a garden that had to be fenced in [3]. We therefore decided that the Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel should ride with this letter to our friend Mr Wolfrum to ask him for advice: which was done. This friend advised us to wait until the Landdrost came over himself in person, as he had promised; that we should then tell him our thoughts freely and openly; but that at the same time we should turn to the General.

<!-- lines 16255-16260 -->
In the meantime Pieter Dohij came to spend the night here with a party of 10 persons, with the aim of attending our meetings on the Sunday; which caused no small difficulty for Brother Schwinn, who was now at home alone [4].

<!-- lines 16261-16263 -->
On the 12th 1 man and 2 women were baptised under a blessed feeling of the Saviour's presence [5].

<!-- lines 16264-16285 -->
On the 13th 2 women went to the Cape; the one to visit her son there. He had moved here last year to hear God's word. The farmer with whom the son was in service wounded him in one leg with a knife, wanting, as he said, to break him of running away. But when the farmer came to the Cape with this lad, he ran away from the farmer and took service with the English. The farmer wanted [to claim] him back from the Gene[ral ...] his wagon could not get on; but he got the answer that the Hottentots were free people and could serve whom they wanted [6]. Something similar happened lately too, when a couple had driven to the Cape to be married, who lost 3 Hottentots in the same way and achieved nothing by their complaints. They had to set out on the journey home with great difficulty, on their own.

<!-- lines 16286-16293 -->
In these days the overseer of the warm bath, Negrini, was here to make a pair of bellows for Brother Kühnel, which we had long wished for but had not been able to get. This tool is very dear in this country. There are farmers who have paid as much as 40, even 50 rixdollars for one; though these are good for nothing. Our man, however, understands his work.

<!-- lines 16294-16299 -->
On the 17th the Hottentots rode out on a wolf hunt, in the company of Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel; but nothing was shot [7]. On such occasions we wished we had more than our three guns; since one has to spread out in the mountains.

<!-- lines 16300-16303 -->
On the 20th we spoke with our baptised, and we had cause to rejoice over their declarations on the whole. On the 23rd we held Holy Communion.

<!-- lines 16304-16333 -->
On the 24th we asked some Hottentots to fetch trees from the forest that could be laid over the river. The old ones are already so rotten that one cannot cross over them any more without danger. 20 of them came and offered themselves for this work; but at the same time they asked that one of us might go with them, since otherwise they would not be finished with it in three days. So Brother Kühnel went with them. The forest lies two hours from here, and has plenty of wood. But what makes it extremely hard to get it from there is that it grows between the rocks, which are so steep that we have to let ourselves down with the help of ropes to be able to get to it, and by the same means have to haul the trees up over the rocks as well. We felled two trees; the smaller, meant for one arm of the river, was brought over the rocks before evening: but when darkness overtook us we had to stop the work for that day. The next day we overcame all difficulty and trouble with the bigger tree as well, and both came without any mishap to the place they were meant for; so that the overseer of the warm bath mentioned above could set out on his journey home [8].

<!-- lines 16334-16340 -->
30 July reminded us of the painful event of a year ago today, when we had to leave our poor people [9]. We found many reasons to thank the Saviour that he has helped us through all difficulties this year too, and has blessed our testimony of him to so many hearts.

## Notes

1. *Huiſer, den Horologiemaaker op de Kaap*: Mr Heuser, the watchmaker in Cape Town who forwarded the brethren's letters (see Heuser in the glossary).
2. *20 minuuten Land* and *eene weide van 50 minuuten*: land measured in "minutes", perhaps minutes of walking or riding along the boundary. The measure is not clear; the German column also has *Minuten*. *den geheimraad Clude*: Mr Clüde (see Hendrik Cloete in the glossary), who is called a privy councillor here.
3. *ſlechs als een hof, die omtuind moest worden*: that is, as a garden or orchard to be fenced, and not as grazing land closed to the Khoekhoe's cattle.
4. *Pieter Dohij*: Pieter Dohi (see the glossary). The Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel were away at Mr Wolfrum's.
5. 12 July 1796 was a Tuesday. The date may be misread; to be checked against the image.
6. Line 16276 is missing, the first line of the page after 16274 *De Boer wilde hem van den* / *Gene=*. The transcription goes on *zijn wagen niet voord konde komen*. The lost line held the end of *Generaal* and probably said that the farmer asked for the lad back because without him he could not get his wagon home. The words in square brackets are supplied from the grammar only.
7. *wolvejagt*: a hunt for "wolves", the name the colonists gave to hyenas.
8. The overseer, Negrini, had probably been waiting for the bridge before he could take his wagon back across the river.
9. On 30 July 1795 the brethren took leave of the Khoekhoe on the order of the rebel commandant, and left on 31 July (entry 26).
