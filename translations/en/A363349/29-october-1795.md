<!-- lines 11154-11187 -->
On 3 October we held a conference. The government at the Cape having changed, we had hoped to be informed by our friends there what we had to expect from it; but since this had not happened, we took counsel that Brother Marsveld should go to the Cape with our Jacob to wait upon the new rulers and ask for their protection for us. As the number of Hottentots who come to us increases, Baas Teunis is again very displeased with it, and has allowed himself such bad expressions that we think twice about writing them down [1]. There are still many with him who stand firm in wanting to live with us, and whom he will on no account let go to us. The Nationals near Swellendam are also beginning to band together again, to enforce the right they think they have over the Hottentots, in which we too are an object of their deliberations. Among other things we were told that Bisani, when he drove us from Baviaanskloof, is supposed to have said: "The Brothers have the right teaching, and lead the Hottentots on the right way; that I know. But I am the devil's now once and for all, and must be lost; so now I will also use all my power to prevent the Gospel from being preached to the Hottentots; for I shall be lost anyway, whether I do this or do not do it" [2].

<!-- lines 11187-11211 -->
On 8 October some Hottentots whom Baas Teunis had treated badly went straight to the Cape, to ask the new rulers for permission and support to be free to go and hear the word of God. On 13 October Abraham came to tell us that two women had fallen into a faint at his father's house. To the question what the cause of it had been, he told the following: "When we had eaten, [we thanked the Saviour in a couple of verses, and] one said: 'Oh, I feel a deep pain in my heart when I hear what the Saviour has done and suffered for us' [3]. As we went on singing, the other woman fainted too." On receiving this news we went there directly, and came to their help with vinegar, by which they both came round again. They are two newcomers, who visited us on 14 October and said to us: "It went so painfully to their hearts when they heard that the Saviour had done and suffered so much for us." On the occasion of this case we must remark here that now as a rule not only the baptised but the other Hottentots too sing some verses after eating, and so also before going to bed. In the evening Brother Marsveld came home again, and told us the following of his visit to the Cape.

<!-- lines 11212-11246 -->
"On 6 October I arrived at the Cape at our dear friend Martin Schmidt's. On the 7th I went to pay my respects to the two English Generals, with the aim of giving them a true idea of our persons, and of our calling by the Evangelical Unity of the Brethren to preach the Gospel to the Hottentots; asking them at the same time to grant us too the undisturbed freedom promised to all religions, and to let us enjoy their protection. To this I received the most favourable answer, and the most complete assurance of their protection. I thanked them for it in the name of my two brothers, and then went from them to the former Governor Sluiske, to declare our thankfulness to him too for the favour and protection he had shown us. He said: 'I have done what I could, and have written to the English Generals that the Brothers were recommended to us by the Company, and that I now commend them in turn to their favour and protection; to which I have received a favourable answer.' On the 8th I visited several good friends. On the 9th I intended to travel home; but hearing that a Danish ship had arrived, and thinking that it might perhaps bring me letters, I stayed one more day, but in vain, because no one was allowed to go on board that ship [4]. Today the burghers had to take the oath of allegiance to Great Britain, which happened in complete quiet. On 10 October I set out on my journey back, and on the 14th reached Baviaanskloof in good health."

<!-- lines 11247-11271 -->
On 17 October we partook of Holy Communion, and 2 Hottentots became candidates for Communion [5]. On the 19th the Hottentots of whom mention was made under the 8th came back from the Cape. They have spoken with the English General, and received the friendly answer that they might go freely to Baviaanskloof to hear God's word there, and that no one would do them any harm for it. They replied that Baas Teunis had threatened to chase them away; at which the General answered "that he would write to Baas Teunis and the Landdrost of Swellendam to put nothing in the Hottentots' way; and if it should happen after all, they had only to come to him again, and he would then know well what he had to do." On 27 October the superintendent of the warm bath told us that we would get a visit this week from a distinguished gentleman, namely a lieutenant-colonel of the English troops [6]. He himself had us told on the 28th by a Hottentot, with the message that he would come to visit us on Sunday with a party.

<!-- lines 11272-11292 -->
On the 29th we began to hold school again, which we had suspended since 18 July. But this happened [only with the baptised, the candidates for bapt]ism and their children [7]. We do intend to take in others too, but only such as come and ask for it, declaring that they are wholly set on living for the Saviour in this world; which the smallest as well as the biggest must declare. We had thought that if the school, the great stone of stumbling, were to stop, and we held only meetings, many Hottentots would stay away, and Baas Teunis thought so too; but that has not proved true. For although no school has been able to be held for three months, we do not know of a single Hottentot who has moved away, except those who left for Swellendam on 13 July on our advice and with our approval [8].

<!-- lines 11293-11309 -->
We closed this month with praise and thanks to our beloved Lord and Saviour, who has given us rest and peace from outside again. And if we fix our minds on the main thing, we stand ashamed and amazed at the blessing with which God has crowned the preaching of the Gospel to the Hottentots [9]. The work of the Holy Spirit in the hearts of the Hottentots is so visible that it goes beyond all our thinking. They come not only one by one, but also several together, to speak with us, and that not only by day but also in the night, and say to us with many tears: "Many a time I do not know where to turn for the fear and anguish of my heart. For I have grieved the Saviour so often; I have now heard his word so long already, and yet not lived by it; I must be lost," and so on.

## Notes

1. *Het getal der Hottentotten, die tot ons komen, vermeerderende*. This settles the German diary's garbled sentence (its note 2). *die wy bedenkelijk zyn, om neder te ſchryven*: the transcription has *heeft zich* twice; the sense is "which we hesitate to write down". The Dutch does not say that Marsveld was to leave "tomorrow".
2. The German diary has "These people give the right teaching of the true Jesus"; the Dutch has "The Brothers have the right teaching".
3. Line 11192 is missing. The transcription runs *Toen wy gegeeten hadden,* / *zeide een: ach ik gevoel eene diepe Smart*. The German diary has "When we had eaten, we thanked the Saviour in a couple of verses, and one of them said". The words in square brackets are supplied from there.
4. *maar hoorende, dat een Deenſch Schip aangekoomen is*. This settles the German diary's "stormy weather" and "letters from abroad" (its note 4). The two Generals are James Henry Craig and Alured Clarke; Sluiske is Abraham Josias Sluysken (see the glossary).
5. *2 Hottentotten wierden aanſtaande Avondmaals⸗leeden*. The Dutch does not have the German diary's baptism on the 22nd of Sister Catharina's little son, named Joseph.
6. *een OverſtLuitenant der Engelſche troupen*. The German diary has the 24th, and "a colonel and a lieutenant-colonel" (its note 5); the Dutch has one gentleman, who sends word himself on the 28th.
7. Line 11274 is missing. The transcription runs *Doch geſchied⸗* / *ten en hunne Kinderen*. The German diary has "but only with the baptised, the candidates for baptism and their children". The words in square brackets are supplied from there; *Doop⸗Candidaaten* ends in *-ten*.
8. *den 13 July*; the German diary has 18 July, as does the Dutch on 10 September (entry 28) and above. The Khoekhoe from the district of Swellendam left on 18 July (entry 26).
9. This settles the German diary's garbled sentence (its note 8).
