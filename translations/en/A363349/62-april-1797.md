On 9 April an unpleasant accident befell a Hottentot whom we had hired with 12 oxen to fetch wood for us from the forest. A quarter of an hour from us he had got stuck with the wagon. 2 oxen tore themselves loose, and as they were very wild and he could not get hold of them again, he had to unyoke the others as well. Meanwhile darkness overtook him, and he drove them all into the scrub. As soon as it was day he looked for them; but behold, they had been driven straight into the kraal of Mr Clüde, as if they belonged there [1]. The Hottentot went at once to this man to give him an account of what had happened, and also to offer compensation for the damage. But Mr Clüde only asked him whether the oxen were his own property or ours? And on the answer that they were his own, he was simply sent quietly away, and the oxen were declared forfeit. After the farmer had told us this, two of us went with the Hottentot to Mr Clüde, who then let himself be persuaded, and gave the oxen back, without even being willing to accept the compensation offered for the damage done to his garden [2]. From all the circumstances we saw clearly enough that if we had not stepped into the breach for the poor Hottentot, he would as surely as anything have lost his oxen: as happened only lately with 38 oxen [...] had driven off, and which were afterwards sold by his master [3].

<!-- lines 15951-15960 -->
On the 12th an English soldier who had deserted arrived here. He now had a letter of pardon, and another from the Landdrost at Swellendam, in which everyone was asked to bring him from place to place to the Cape. He stayed the night with us, and had to dry his clothes with us, since he had fallen into the water. The next day we had a Hottentot take him on to the nearest place.

<!-- lines 15961-15976 -->
On the great sabbath of our Saviour, Saturday before Easter, the 15th, the lovefeast was enlivened by a very pleasant conversation. Old Lena, who for a long time had not been able to come to the meetings any more, was carried here today by some brothers. She was especially thankful that the Saviour had let her live to see this day, on which she had once more been able to join with her sisters in love. Shortly after we held with our baptised the evening blessing that is usual on this day.

<!-- lines 15977-15993 -->
On the 16th, being Easter Day, we went early in the morning in procession to our burial ground for the prayer of the Easter litany, and remembered by name the child who has been buried here in this year [4]. All the Hottentots living with us were present at this meeting; indeed, already at 3 o'clock in the morning they were sitting in crowds before the church door, so as not to miss this occasion. At the sermon, by the count of a Hottentot, 415 people were present, and among them were only 2 strangers, and 5 of our schoolchildren, who now make up a number of 188 [5]. For want of room these have 2 separate meetings weekly, to which, however, many adults come as well.

<!-- lines 15994-16018 -->
On the 18th 3 women were baptised into the death of Jesus, and with this we closed these festival days, of which we must testify that our Saviour made himself known to us and our people in an outstanding way, in Passion Week as well as at the Easter festival. Of this not only the great stirrings of hearts during the meetings are a proof, but also that many come to us to speak with us about the state of their hearts, when sorrow and tears often keep them from speaking. From many a one we heard these and similar declarations: "When I think that my sins have caused the dear Saviour so much pain, this goes so through my heart that I cannot put it into words. I feel moved to offer myself wholly to the Saviour with body and soul: I want nothing but what he wants, by his grace": and more of the like.

<!-- lines 16019-16031 -->
Ah! dear brothers and sisters! if you could once see how eager these poor people are to hear something of the Saviour's suffering and dying, and how they drop everything so as not to miss a meeting; you would surely double your praise and thanks to the Saviour! We now wish from our hearts that the blessing that so many have lately shared in may be lasting, and that the reward of the Saviour's [...] be increased [6].

<!-- lines 16032-16067 -->
On the 19th we were called to one of the candidates for baptism, who has lain in consumption for some time. He declared that he [wished] to be taken up to the Saviour, but first to be baptised into his death and washed from sin [7]. We had no reason to refuse him his request, which he made several times and urgently, and decided to do this tomorrow. But that same evening his brother came to tell us that the sick man was very weak. So we went to him at once, and had some baptised brothers and sisters called too. When we had asked him once more about his desire, this was accompanied by so piercing a feeling that all those present burst into tears: upon which we baptised him with the name Samuel. At our visit on the 29th he believed that his end was now near. After he had answered the questions put to him in a weak voice, and declared that he loved the Saviour because he shed his blood for him, and that he now wished to come to him, he was blessed for his departure under a special feeling of the peace of God. On 5 May his blessed end followed [8]. Before this he had asked those present to sing verses for him, and shortly before his death he gave out the verse himself: "My dearest Jesus, come!" [9]

<!-- lines 16068-16077 -->
On 30 April we held a special meeting, in thankful remembrance of the grace they had received, for all those who have been baptised since Easter of last year, and who have come to Holy Communion. The first were 14 adults and 8 children, and the latter 6 persons.

## Notes

1. *de Craal van den Hr Clüde*: Mr Clüde, the neighbour whose land bordered the mission's grazing (see Hendrik Cloete in the glossary, and entries 65 and 67 of the 1796 diary).
2. *de aangebodene vergoeding voor de ſchade aan zijnen tuin veroorzaakt*: the oxen had evidently broken into Mr Clüde's garden or orchard.
3. Line 15948 is missing. The transcription runs *zooals onlangs nog met 38 oſſen heeft plaats* / *ontreeven had, en naderhand door deszelfs Heer zijn verkocht geworden*. The lost line probably said whose slave or servant (*deszelfs Heer*, "his master") had driven off (*ontdreven*) the 38 oxen, and from whom. The words in the translation are only those that survive; the story is not otherwise known.
4. *de Paaſch Litanei*: the Easter morning litany of the Brethren, prayed on the burial ground at daybreak. The child is not named; the year's figures for 1796 give one child as having died (entry 58).
5. The transcription reads *en 5 van onze ſchoolkinderen, die thans een getal van 188 uitmaaken*. It is not clear whether only 5 schoolchildren were at the sermon, or whether a word or figure is garbled. To be checked against the image.
6. Line 16030 is missing. The transcription runs *en dat de loon van ’s Heilands* / *vermeerderd worden. ——*. The lost line probably finished a phrase such as "the reward of the Saviour's sufferings may be increased among this people"; "the reward of his sufferings" is a common Moravian phrase for souls won for Christ. The words in the translation are only those that survive.
7. The transcription reads *dat hij gaarn tot den Heiland* / *te opgenomen te worden*; a verb such as *wenſchte*, "wished", is missing. There is no gap in the line numbers.
8. The death on 5 May is recorded here, out of order, at the end of the account of Samuel's illness.
9. *Mijn allerliefſte Jeſus! kom!*: a Dutch hymn verse, not identified.
