<!-- lines 15561-15569 -->
On 3 Dec. 13 Hottentots arrived here from the Cape on leave for 3 weeks; by this opportunity we received several letters that had come from Europe. We cannot describe what joy they gave us, and how thankful we were to the Saviour that he had once again let something come into our hands.

<!-- lines 15570-15575 -->
At this time many of the Hottentots living here, especially the women (for there are only a few men here), went to the farmers to work in the harvest: so that for a good while it was fairly empty of people here.

<!-- lines 15576-15591 -->
On the 18th we received a letter from Pieter Fooy, in which he told us that the minister Vos of the Roodezand intended to make a visit to these parts after the new year, and also to preach at a place suited to it [1]. Now since there was nowhere a more suitable place for this than with us, we were asked to let the said minister preach in our church, and to lodge him too. We could not refuse this request, although, in view of the circumstances that go with it, we would rather have wished that another place had been chosen for it.

<!-- lines 15592-15601 -->
On the 21st we spoke with the candidates for baptism, over the greater part of whom we could rejoice. They declared that they were minded to live for the Saviour alone in this world. They still lack words too much to be able to express themselves properly about the state of their hearts.

<!-- lines 15602-15618 -->
On the 24th we would gladly have held a meeting with our baptised in the evening, as last year; but suspecting that visitors would come, we held this meeting at 3 o'clock in the afternoon. And in this we had judged it well: for that same evening we had 13 guests, among whom was Mr Wolfrum with 3 English officers. The next day still more came, in carriages and on horseback: so that their number came to 31, not counting the slaves. In the night, too, many Hottentots had come here from all sides for the festival meetings, which were then so fully attended that our church could not nearly hold all the people.

<!-- lines 15619-15635 -->
On the 26th, among other things, the [...] at which so many adults also appeared that the hall was quite full [2]. In the meeting of the baptised we had to make known a painful occurrence, which was already talked about widely enough apart from this, and at the same time to announce that a person who had until now belonged to us has had to be excluded from the congregation; and we commended her to the heartfelt intercession of those present [3]. This caused great stirring of hearts, and this case made many shed tears.

<!-- lines 15636-15638 -->
On the 27th we closed the festival days, so blessed for our poor people, with the baptism of 4 adults.

<!-- lines 15639-15648 -->
On the 28th we heard that 2 Hottentots were here who had deserted from the guard. We had them called and asked for their pass. As they could not show one, and looked for all kinds of excuses, we made it clear to them that they must go back to the Cape this very day: for we could not suffer such people, who were disobedient to the authorities, on our place.

<!-- lines 15649-15656 -->
On the 29th, to our no small joy, we got into our hands the goods that had been sent to us from Europe for the third time, two consignments having been lost, and for which we had had to wait so long. We were very thankful to the Saviour for them.

<!-- lines 15657-15691 -->
On the 31st 15 persons came to visit us, who explained the purpose of their coming thus: that at other times they had closed the old year with sinful merrymaking and begun the new one in the same taste; but now they wished to do it before the face of our dear Lord. They did then attend the meeting at half past eleven, in which we recalled with praise and thanks the proofs of grace of our Lord and Saviour, and at the entry into the new year brought him our thank-offering on our knees, at the same time praying him for the forgiveness of all our debts and for the renewal of his grace. Among the particular benefits that we have received from the hand of our good Lord in the past year we count also that he so graciously preserved us in the dangers that hung over our heads in the month of Feb. [4]; that he has given us a house in which we can come together for the preaching and hearing of his Word; that he has blessed the testimony that we bear of him to so many people; and that he has built us up and strengthened us through health, and love, and unity among ourselves [5]. We call on all our dear brothers and sisters to thank the Saviour with us for all this, and to pray him that he may go on showing us grace and having mercy on us.

<!-- lines 15692-15713 -->
The number of those living with us has increased greatly, especially in November and December; for many who lived here at the beginning, but afterwards moved away again, or whom we ourselves advised to do so in those distressing circumstances, have come to us anew, and among them many new people. [...] number might not be so great, since we cannot oversee the multitude as we ought [6]. But what shall we do? When they come here, driven by hunger for God's word and by longing to be saved, and ask us for leave to live with us: then we cannot turn them away: we are here, after all, for the sake of such as want to learn to know the way of salvation.

<!-- lines 15714-15736 -->
We cannot give the number of those who live here now. The children who go to school make up a number of 173 at the end of the year, namely 93 boys and 80 girls. In this year 12 adults and 6 children have been baptised. 6 persons have been admitted to Holy Communion. 13 have become candidates for baptism, and 6 persons candidates for Communion. The congregation from among the Hottentots consists at the close of the year 1796 of:

15 communicants
5 candidates for Communion
29 baptised adults
9 baptised children
and 25 candidates for baptism

in all 83 persons [7]; 20 more than in the previous year. One adult and one child have died.

## Notes

1. *Pieter Fooy*: probably the neighbouring farmer Pieter Dohi (see the glossary), whose name the transcription spells several ways; he appears as *Pieter Dohij* in July 1796 (entry 65). To be checked against the image. *de Predikant Vos, van 't roode zand*: the minister of Roodezand (Tulbagh); see the minister Vos in the glossary. His visit is told in entry 59.
2. Line 15620 is missing. The transcription runs *Den 26 hadden onder anderen de* / *ke zich ook zoo veele bejaarden lieten vinden, dat de Zaal geheel vol werd.* The lost line probably named a meeting on 26 December (perhaps that of the children) and began *wel-ke*, "at which". There is no other copy to settle it.
3. *eene tot hier toe onder ons behoorende perſoon*: "a person who had until now belonged to us". The Dutch *perſoon* is feminine, so "her" may only follow the grammar; the person is not named. Line 15628 is empty in the transcription and loses no text.
4. The farmers' commando against the Khoekhoe of Baviaanskloof in February 1796 (entry 33).
5. *opgebourd*, read as *opgebouwd*, "built up".
6. Line 15702 is missing. The transcription runs *zyn op nieuws tot ons gekomen, en daaronder veele nieuwen.* / *tal niet zoo groot mogt zijn*. The lost line probably said that the brethren almost wished that the number (*ge-tal*) were not so great; the words in the translation are only those that survive.
7. The figures are written as a column. After *29 Bejaard gedoopten* the transcription has *9* at the foot of page 261a and *-9 Gedoopte kinderen* at the head of page 261b: the 9 is the catchword. 15 + 5 + 29 + 9 + 25 = 83, which agrees with the total. At the end of 1795 the total was 65, of whom 2 were excluded for the time being (entry 31); 83 is 20 more than the 63 who remained.
