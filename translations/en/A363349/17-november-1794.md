<!-- lines 6290-6294 -->
Diary of the three Brothers Marsveld, Schwinn and Kühnel at the Cape, from 1 November to 31 December 1794 [1].

<!-- lines 6295-6299 -->
On 1 November there was such a great stirring in the children's meeting as there had never been: few eyes stayed dry, and because of the loud weeping we had to close [2].

<!-- lines 6299-6308 -->
Early on the 2nd Baas Teunis's wagon maker (a German) came to take leave, because his time of serving the Company was now up; he is now going to the Cape. With him came the son of a gentleman from the Cape whose father is a member of the Council of Justice. He and the overseer of the warm bath attended the meeting at 10 o'clock, which Brother Marsveld held; he spoke on the Gospel. In the afternoon they all rode away again.

<!-- lines 6308-6311 -->
On the 3rd another farmer came to look for people to weed his corn. Some also came who spoke with us about the unrest of their hearts.

<!-- lines 6312-6322 -->
Early on the 4th our Dorothea came again and asked for forgiveness. We could believe that it came from her heart. She said that she would pray to the dear Saviour to keep her from all evil in future, so that she might not grieve him any more. 2 more also came who wanted to live here, and a little girl who was very troubled and said with many tears that she too would gladly be saved.

<!-- lines 6323-6343 -->
On the 5th we sent our cart into the forest to Baas Teunis, to fetch wood for all kinds of necessary things. If one wants wood of this kind, one must first have permission for it. Baas Teunis is overseer of the forest, and it is now so strict that if anyone is caught in the forest, flogging and branding are the penalty. In the evening we announced in the meeting of the baptised that Dorothea heartily repented of her unfaithfulness and had asked the dear Saviour for forgiveness, so as by his grace to live only for him now, and had also begged us earnestly to forgive her and take her back into our fellowship. Since we now forgave her from our hearts, we would ask each one to forgive her too, and to thank the dear Saviour that he had brought this strayed sheep back to his flock. There was great emotion at this.

<!-- lines 6345-6360 -->
On the 6th a Hottentot came who had served the minister at Swellendam (which is some 20 German miles from here) and had now fallen out with him. He asked us to write his name down; then he would go and fetch his things and come and live here. We told him that we write no one down. [Whoever came] to hear God's word and wanted to live by it [could come here; but whoever did not want to] live by it could not stay living here [3]. He said that this was his mind. Several others also came again to speak with us; among them was a woman who said that for unrest she could sleep little, and when she fell asleep, she dreamed of us.

<!-- lines 6361-6382 -->
On the 7th. In these days we heard from a gentleman from the Cape that a packet boat which was to sail from Holland to the Cape had been taken by the French. Mr Frederik had been on it as its commander. This Mr Frederik was lieutenant on our ship, and showed us then every possible love and friendship. We were very sorry that he now finds himself in such circumstances; as people now say, he is to be held in Paris. We firmly believe that there were at least letters and news from our dear brothers on the packet boat mentioned, which we long for so much, and which we can only regard as lost. What shall we do but pray to the dear Saviour that he will let all that our dear brothers send to us, and what we send to them, come safely into their hands and ours, and soon make an end of the troubles of war?

<!-- lines 6382-6389 -->
On the 8th we resolved in our conference to set up a separate meeting on Saturdays for the candidates for Communion, and to move the meeting of the baptised from Wednesday to Monday, and the general meeting on Monday, in which the New Testament is read, to Wednesday.

<!-- lines 6389-6406 -->
On the 9th Brother Marsveld had his 50th birthday. Yesterday Martha came and asked whether we would not give her a pleasure: there were some who wanted to wake Brother Marsveld with some verses for his birthday, and we were to play for it. This we gladly did. Some who could sing well gathered already before 4 o'clock in the morning; afterwards all the baptised came, and after these a great part of the other Hottentots, to congratulate him. We wondered how it had become known so quickly. At 10 o'clock in the meeting Brother Marsveld spoke on the Sunday Gospel. Afterwards 4 more came and spoke with us about the state of their hearts.

<!-- lines 6406-6414 -->
On the 10th we sent our cart with three Hottentots into the forest at Baas Teunis's again to fetch wood, so as to dry it; we were busy with this until the end of this week [4]. It is indeed called a forest, but the wood must be dragged over the mountains half an hour and further before one can load it; and that is a road one has to crawl on hands and feet without dragging anything.

<!-- lines 6415-6421 -->
On the 11th several came and said that in the kraals some still often talked as badly as they had formerly done, and yet they heard something better now: "Our dear Lord has done so much for us, and still such bad things can be spoken, at which he is grieved."

<!-- lines 6422-6423 -->
On the 12th the baptised were to have been spoken, but we let it lapse this time.

<!-- lines 6424-6445 -->
On 13 November, being the Elders' Festival, we had a festival morning blessing early with our baptised, in which the cause of this festival was told them. Then in a prayer on our knees we thanked the Lord for all his help, and asked forgiveness where we had not been obedient to his voice. We did homage to him anew and promised him obedience and new faithfulness, at which [he let us all feel his peace powerfully] [5]. At 10 o'clock we read to ourselves the address which Brother Blüher gave at Herrnhut on 13 November 1792. At 2 o'clock in the afternoon we held a lovefeast with our 17 baptised. Hanna, who is still going astray, was still missing. At this lovefeast particular love for one another, and honesty towards us, were most heartily commended.

<!-- lines 6446-6451 -->
On the 14th the farmer Mary [6] came to visit us with his brother-in-law, who lived at Stellenbosch. Several Hottentots also came to speak with us about the unrest of their hearts.

<!-- lines 6451-6454 -->
On the 15th in the evening we had Holy Communion, with the Saviour owning us poor ones.

<!-- lines 6454-6470 -->
Early on the 16th another farmer came already; but the Communion liturgy was already over. (Until now we have remained undisturbed in such meetings, because they are early. They [the farmers] have no fixed time, but go by the day: if it is light early, they are early. At present the candidates for baptism are there for their meeting already at half past 5, and often earlier still; it is on Wednesdays and Saturdays; after it comes the children's meeting, and on Saturdays, after the children's meeting, the meeting of the candidates for Communion.) The farmer mentioned above stayed for the meeting, in which Brother Marsveld spoke on the Sunday Gospel. After the meal he rode away again [7].

<!-- lines 6471-6500 -->
Early on the 17th a girl came and said that she was quite faint with sorrow over her sins, and she would gladly be saved, which she declared with many tears. Afterwards a man came with his wife, and each accused the other. The man said that she would not obey him, which after all was not fitting, and now she had been away from him for 3 days, at her brother's. The woman said that several came to her and talked badly, as they had formerly done, and so she had come here to hear the words of our dear Father and live by them, and could no longer listen to such bad talk. Since, after further questions, both were at fault, they were told that the one was as much at fault as the other, and that they should ask each other's forgiveness and pray our dear Lord to forgive them too; and to the people who came and talked so badly they could say that they must leave the house. They promised to do all this. 2 farmers also came again to visit us; one was young Mrs Morgel's son [8].

<!-- lines 6500-6558 -->
On the 18th. Yesterday we received a letter from Baas Teunis's son, whose place lies a good hour from here. When we came, he had not yet finished his house, as can be seen from our first diary from here. This place belonged to the Hottentots, as his father himself has told us. The Edle Heer Renius had given it to him on condition that he did no harm to any Hottentot, much less drove them away; should it come about that he did this, he must leave the place [9]. (Until now he has also said nothing to the effect that the Hottentots' cattle might not graze there. Hottentots have also lived close by him [... Now he writes] that we should tell the Hottentots to watch their cattle: he was not obliged to let them graze on his ground and to let himself be done damage by them; if they did him damage, he would give them a good load of blows and have the damage paid him [10].) This had been told him by the government, and if they would not believe it, they could go to it and ask. That the Hottentots do not look after their cattle is all too true. We have already spoken much with them about it, that they should keep herdsmen with the cattle themselves, and have taken all trouble [11]. They too say "Yes, yes"; but it stays at "yes, yes". This has now put us in perplexity: if he has got this place in such a way that he can drive away the Hottentots' cattle as far as belongs to a farm, so that they dare not come on his land, then we are so hemmed in that the Hottentots' cattle must die of hunger. God knows who and what is yet to come of it. We can say with truth that the devil is angry. We hear from everywhere that the farmers are very incensed against us and wish us away, and also talk so badly of us to the Hottentots that one stands amazed at what we are supposed to be doing with the Hottentots. Well, it is God's work. May he only give us the grace to stay close to him and his wounds, and not to lose heart through the attacks of Satan. Yet whenever a farmer comes to us, we are always very friendly. We read the letter from Baas Teunis's son aloud in the school of the men as well as of the women, and urged them most earnestly to keep a herdsman with the cattle.

<!-- lines 6558-6567 -->
On the 19th. Since for some time the Hottentots, when they have been at the farmers' and done something, come of their own accord at once and tell it, and at the same time ask for forgiveness, strange declarations often come up, at which we are often ashamed; for they name the thing plainly, without one having to ask much. Yet we must listen to them when they come, and admonish and rebuke them too [12].

<!-- lines 6568-6587 -->
On the 20th the farmer Louis came to visit us and stayed over midday. In the afternoon Plaisir also came to visit us; he now lives 4 miles from here. He stayed the night with us. We now get many visits from the farmers, most of them to hire Hottentots. If they come at mealtime they must have food, for which very few give thanks, because it is the fashion. This is hard for us, because we have to buy bread, meat and wine. We treat them as kindly as possible. Yet it is said of us that when someone comes to visit us, we do not even offer him a chair. Since we had no more than our 3 ship's chairs, we have always (when someone came) given him a chair and sat ourselves on the bench. We think: let them say what they will; we lose nothing by it.

<!-- lines 6587-6609 -->
On the 21st a Hottentot came and said that a farmer had asked him who created the world. Answer: "God." Question: what was the first man called? Answer: "Adam, and his wife Eva." At this the farmer said: "That is not true; the first man was called Noah. These people teach you wrongly." The Hottentot then said: "These people do not teach us wrongly, they teach us right; perhaps you have not been rightly taught"; at which the farmer grew angry. Then another asked: "From whom was the woman made?" Answer: "From a rib of Adam." "Quite right," said this one. [A woman also came (she had been at a farmer's) and told him that] she now wanted to go to school too [13]. The farmer said to her: "Stay with me; what do you want there? I will give you a book and teach you myself." The woman gave him the answer: "If Baas had wanted to teach me, Baas could have done it long ago. Now I do not want to be taught by Baas, but I want to move to Baviaanskloof."

<!-- lines 6609-6612 -->
On the 22nd Hanna came and asked to be taken back again; but we had not yet heard a good report of her, and so could not take her back for this time.

<!-- lines 6613-6624 -->
On the 24th [14]. Since we have often heard from our good friends at the Cape, and now again from Martinus Schmidt, that the minister Vos at Roodezand [15] greatly wished to speak with one of us (we have heard that he is very glad that the poor Hottentots accept the Gospel so and are so eager for it), we had resolved that Brother Marsveld should ride there. He rode there early today, accompanied by our good wishes, with one of our Hottentots.

<!-- lines 6625-6642 -->
On the 25th several came who spoke with us about the perplexity of their hearts. 3 more farmers also came to hire Hottentots. Today many Hottentots also went to the farmers, to cut first barley and then corn. One of our candidates for baptism also came and told us that he had now been at a farmer's who lives far out in the veld, and had stayed the night there. When this man heard that there was a Hottentot there who goes to school, he wanted to speak with him. The Hottentot was already asleep. He was called and had to come into the room. This farmer put endless questions to him, which the Hottentot answered astonishingly. We will give some of them here:

<!-- lines 6642-6688 -->
Question: "What are you taught, good or bad?" Answer: "Good is taught us, Baas." Question: "What is good?" Answer: "God's word is good, and that is taught." Question: "Who created the first people?" Answer: "God." Question: "What were their names?" Answer: "Adam and Eva." Question: "Where was Christ born?" Answer: "Baas must wait a little; I cannot say the name of the place at once." This he said twice, then: "At Bethlehem in the Jewish land." Question: "Who visited Jesus first?" Answer: "The shepherds in the field." Farmer: "That is not true; the wise men from the East." Question: "Are you taught high or low?" Answer: "I think, Baas, that we are taught high, for what is higher and more than God's word?" Question: "Since you are taught like this, you are surely also taught that you need no longer be subject to the Christians, but are to become baas over them?" Answer: "We are taught that our dear Lord says that we are to be obedient to all to whom we owe obedience, and no longer do such bad things as we have done until now." Question: "Will you do good?" Answer: "With God's help, yes. Of ourselves we cannot, but God will give us the strength for it." Question: "Can you read?" Answer: "No!" Question: "How long have you been going to school?" Answer: "About a year." The farmer: "A year, and still cannot read? What a stupid person you are!" Answer: "A man is by nature unfit for all good things. We must receive everything we have from our dear Lord for nothing; we cannot even think anything good, much less do anything good," and so on. He asked many more questions, which the Hottentot [answered. Then the Hottentot noticed] that the farmer meant to go too far with his questions [16], and the Hottentot said: "My Baas wants me to go back to my work early tomorrow morning, and for that I must sleep first. Good night, Baas!"

<!-- lines 6689-6701 -->
On the 26th several went to the farmers again to cut corn. When we say corn, wheat is always meant by it. Rye grows well too, but it is not the fashion to bake bread from it. Several also came who spoke with us about the unrest of their hearts. Today the candidates for baptism were to have been spoken, but we let it lapse this time, announced it early in the meeting, and said that if anyone wanted to speak with us about anything, they could come. Several came at once.

<!-- lines 6701-6706 -->
On the 27th the husband of our sister Rahel came and said with many tears that he felt as if he must go into eternal damnation. The mercy of the Saviour was commended to him.

<!-- lines 6706-6729 -->
On the 28th several baptised came to us, longing to have the grace to go further. Others also came who spoke with us about the sorrow of their hearts. We also heard that our sister Anna has a very hard time with her husband. He wants to know what we do with her when we take her alone. When she tells him that we hold a meeting, he will not believe it, but says that if it were so, others could hear it too. Lately, when she became a candidate for Communion, he was especially incensed, and wanted to know what we were up to with her in the evening. He has used very bad words against her; but she begged us earnestly not to tell him, or she would get a beating. In the evening Brother Schwinn read the 17th and 18th chapters of Luke. The singing meeting, which he also held, had to be held in the house because of the strong wind.

<!-- lines 6729-6736 -->
On the 29th many went to the farmers again; to one farmer alone went men, women and big children, 17 in number. The school and meetings will not be so large for this time. Towards evening Brother Marsveld came home again from his visit to Roodezand. He gives the following account of it:

<!-- lines 6737-6762 -->
"I travelled from here on the 24th, with the blessing of my dear brothers, to the minister Vos at Roodezand. In the evening at 6 o'clock I came to Sybrandt de Beer, whom I found in his field. He asked whether I would not come into his house; and when I answered that I still wanted to reach the warm bath and spend the night there, he said that it was still about 3½ hours away [17]; I should stay the night with him, which I did; and we had fine conversations about our salvation. On the 25th I rode on, and in the evening arrived at Mr Vos's at Roodezand, who was glad of my visit. This evening we had much to speak of about the fatherland. 'Tomorrow,' he said, 'we will talk together again'; whereupon I asked him just to question me about everything. First a chapter from the letter of Paul to the Romans was read, after which we fell on our knees, and he made a long prayer. On the 26th before breakfast we knelt down likewise, and he made a fairly long prayer.

<!-- lines 6763-6798 -->
"After breakfast we went into the garden. [Here he said: 'Now, Brother Marsveld, how] many baptised have you?' [18] Answer: '16.' Question: 'Do they also make a public confession of faith?' Answer: 'Before baptism some questions are put to them, which they answer before the whole meeting.' Question: 'How often is a meeting held?' Answer: 'Every day.' Question: 'What is dealt with in it?' Answer: 'Twice there is reading from the Bible, twice speaking; on Wednesdays and Saturdays there is a meeting for the children, and on Sundays we speak on some texts from the Bible [19].' Question: 'How do you baptise?' Answer: 'In the name of the Father, of the Son and of the Holy Ghost.' In the morning he held catechising for the young people. Mr Vos asked me whether I would attend it, and I accepted. In the evening he held catechising for Negro slaves, at which at his wish I was also present, together with my Hottentot. He asked them: 'Who made the first man?' Answer: 'God.' Question: 'What were they called?' Answer: 'The man Adam and the woman Eva.' Question: 'Out of what did God make people?' Answer: 'The man out of the earth, and the woman out of a rib of Adam.' Towards evening a farmer sent a slave woman to me and asked me to visit him on my way back. After supper Mr Vos asked me whether I would not hold the evening prayer; but I asked him to spare me that, since he was a minister. He pressed me hard, but I would not agree to it.

<!-- lines 6799-6823 -->
"On the 27th in the morning he wanted me to say the morning prayer; but I asked him not to take it amiss that I declined it altogether. He said: 'But you do it in your meeting.' I answered: 'Yes, there I have Hottentots before me, and to them I am called.' At 9 o'clock I took a friendly leave and set out on my journey again. My visit had not turned out as I had expected. In the evening I came to Jacob Hugo's, where I stayed the night. On the 28th I rode on and came to a woman who was in her seventies. She told me that Brother Schmidt had often stayed with her for 3 days at a time [20], and she had had many conversations with him; why did we not do the same? I said that we had no time for it. I spent the night at Daniel Jooÿ's [21]. On the 29th I arrived with my dear brothers in Baviaanskloof, heartily thankful to the Saviour. On the whole I have found better Christians on this journey than there are in our neighbourhood."

## Notes

1. The German diary has "at the post of Good Hope" (its note 1); the Dutch has *van de Caap*, "of the Cape".
2. The German diary goes on with the goats brought across the river, a visit from the overseer of the warm bath, and 3 persons chosen as candidates for baptism; the Dutch does not have these.
3. Line 6354 is missing. The transcription runs *Wien het niet daarom te doen is, om Gods Woord te hooren, en wie daarna wilde* / *daarna leeven wilde, die konde hier niet blyven woonen*; *niet* in the first clause does not fit the sense. The German diary has "If his purpose was to hear God's word and he wanted to live by it, he could come here; but whoever did not live by it must leave here". The words in square brackets are supplied from there.
4. *met drie Hottentotten*; the German diary has "2 Hottentots" and then "these 3" (its note 8). The Dutch makes the brethren themselves busy with the wood until the end of the week.
5. Line 6436 is missing. The transcription runs *beloofden hem Gehoorzaam en nieuwe Trouw, waarby* / *Om 10 Uur verlazen wÿ*. The German diary has "at which he let us all feel his peace powerfully". The words in square brackets are supplied from there.
6. *de Boer Marÿ*; the German diary has Marx here and Mary on the 23rd (its notes 11 and 20).
7. The German diary also has, on the 15th, a farmer who came to hire Khoekhoe workers for the harvest and a wheelwright from Hesse with news of the French; the Dutch does not have them.
8. *der Jonge Vrouw Morget haar Zoo[n]*; the German diary has Morgel. Perhaps of the family of the secretary Morgel of September (entry 26 of A363595).
9. *De Edele Heer Renius*: Johan Isaac Rhenius, acting head of the government in 1792 and 1793; see the glossary and note 13 of entry 28 of A363595.
10. Line 6518, the first after the page number on page 107b, is missing, and the right edge of several lines on pages 107b to 112b is cut off in the transcription (*gew* for *gewoond*, *Bri* for *Brief*, and so on). The transcription runs *E hebben ook Hottentotten digt bÿ hem gew* / *hÿ, dat wÿ den Hottentotten zouden zeggen*. The German diary has "Hottentots also lived close by him, who now live a quarter mile from us. Now he writes that we should tell the Hottentots". The words in square brackets are supplied from there. The German diary names a Khoekhoe man, Dirk, whose cattle grazed there. The Dutch closes the bracket after "paid him", not at the end of the entry.
11. *wÿ hebben ook al veel met hun (des aangaande daarover gesproken, dat ze Wachters bÿ het vraagde) zelve zouden houden*: the words are out of order in the transcription, perhaps because of an insertion. Translated to the sense, with the German diary.
12. The German diary gives here the statements of two women who came that day, one of them an account of being forced by a man from Europe, and the case of a young woman who wanted to live with a man while her husband was away. The Dutch leaves them out.
13. Line 6600 is missing. The transcription runs *regt zoo, zeide deeze; Ook* / *ze nu ook School wilde gaan*. The German diary has "A woman also came (she was at a farmer's) and told him that she now wanted to go to the school too". The words in square brackets are supplied from there.
14. The Dutch has no entry for the 23rd. The German diary has a long account of Sunday the 23rd, when young Mrs Teunis and 11 other visitors came to the meeting and the brethren struggled to feed them.
15. *Rothensand*: Roodezand; see the glossary. The minister was Michiel Christiaan Vos.
16. Line 6682 is missing. The transcription runs *dewelke de* / *Hottentot, dat de Boer te ver met Vragen*. The German diary has "which the Hottentot answered. Then the Hottentot noticed that the farmer meant to go too far with his questions". The words in square brackets are supplied from there.
17. *omtrent 3 ½ Uur*; the German diary has "nearly ¾ hours", which its note 29 doubted. The Dutch figure explains the invitation to stay.
18. Line 6764 is missing. The transcription runs *Na het ontbÿd gingen wÿ in den Tuÿn.* / *veel gedoopte heeft Uwe?* The German diary has "Here he said: 'Now, my Brother Marsveld, how many baptised have you?'" The words in square brackets are supplied from there.
19. The German diary has "on Saturdays we also speak on some verses from the Bible".
20. *dikwils 3 Dagen lang*, "often for 3 days"; the German diary has "once". Georg Schmidt worked at Baviaanskloof from 1737 to 1744; see the glossary.
21. *Daniel Jooÿ*; the German diary has Daniel Tooy. Not identified.
