<!-- lines 12114-12136 -->
On 2 March Louise was confirmed in the evening meeting, and on the 5th she enjoyed Holy Communion with us. In these days a start was made on thatching the roof, for which we have only one man who understands this work. On the 6th Pieter Dohi visited us. He too had been called to the meeting of the rebellious farmers; but he would have nothing to do with the matter; yet to find out what would happen there, he let his brother go to it. That meeting, however, did not take place; why, he did not know. He told us in confidence that the farmers' purpose had been to ask us whether we would leave the Hottentots and go back to the fatherland; and in case we refused, to shoot us dead at once: the young Hottentots they meant then to take with them, but to murder the old ones likewise.

<!-- lines 12137-12168 -->
On the 7th we heard that all the field corporals in this district have been called to the Cape. On the 13th we had a visit from more farmers, among others from young Teunis, who after the meeting began a great row with the Hottentots, because they had gone to the General and told him lies about his father. There are some among the Hottentots who can answer well: and the quarrel rose so high that we had to step in between them. He then made known, as he said, by order of the General, that the Hottentots were to pay off their debts to the farmers, or must work for them; further, that the General intended to raise a corps of soldiers made up of young Hottentots. A gentleman from the Cape was present at this, who told him that he had behaved badly in the quarrel with the Hottentots, and if a complaint should be made about it, it would not go well with him. At last he admitted this too, and his leave-taking then went off fairly amicably after all [1].
