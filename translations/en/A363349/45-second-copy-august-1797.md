> Machine translation, not yet checked by a reader of the Dutch. Translated from the Dutch diplomatic transcription and checked against the German translation in the normalised column. This is the second Dutch copy of the reports for May 1797 to February 1798; the first copy is translated in entries 35 to 41, and the notes compare the two. There is no German diary for these months. The diarists' words for Khoekhoe people ("Hottentots") and Xhosa people ("Kaffers") are kept as they wrote them; see `glossary.csv`. Square brackets mark editorial additions.

<!-- lines 13146-13156 -->
On 9 August Brother Kühnel made the journey to the farmer Redlichhausen, who has got us some sheep. He had made a journey of three and a half months into the land of the Caffers to buy oxen and sheep [1]; and now we have a small flock of the latter, which, though we had spared no pains, we had not been able to manage before.

<!-- lines 13157-13176 -->
On the 18th we celebrated with some solemn meetings, among other things with the administering of baptism to 2 elderly women, and with the holding of Holy Communion [2]. On the 25th, with the help of a good 10 Hottentots, we got our sheep, 200 in number, safely across the river, which had been much swollen in the preceding days. Some of them already have lambs. On the 26th we also got our new garden, or rather vineyard, in order. We have planted 2600 vines in the hope that they will bear fruit after 3 years have passed [3]. At this time too we took much trouble to get millstones, and looked for them in many places, but in vain.

<!-- lines 13177-13188 -->
On the 29th we celebrated our choir festival, and read among other things a choir address that Brother Reichel had given to the choir of single brothers at Herrnhut in the year 1795, and which served all the more to rouse us, since mention was made of us in it twice. In our morning blessing we also remembered Brother and Sister Kohrhammer, as we also do every Saturday in our evening meetings, since we have heard that they have been called here [4].

## Notes

1. *tot in het land der Caffers*: the country of the Xhosa. *Redlichhäuſer* is Redlichhausen; see the glossary.
2. *Den 18.*; the first copy (line 12407) has "the 13th", probably right, since 13 August was a Moravian festival day (entry 38, note 2). Line 13160 is missing between *aan 2 bejaarde* and the page break; the text runs on without a gap (*vrouwen*, line 13162), so the line probably held only a catchword.
3. The first copy (line 12420) has 2000 vines.
4. *In onzen morgenregen*: a misreading of *morgenzegen*, "morning blessing", as the first copy (line 12434) has it.
