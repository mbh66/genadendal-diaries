<!-- lines 15871-15878 -->
Now since the smithy for Brother Kühnel is to be made ready shortly, and he [had to provide himself] with several tools from the Cape, just as we have to provide ourselves from there with all kinds of necessities for the winter, the Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel travelled to Cape Town with a wagon on 14 March [1].

<!-- lines 15879-15887 -->
On the 19th Brother Schwinn was called to old Sister Lena, who was ill [2]. She said to him that she thought of nothing but the Saviour; how he had lain in the manger as a child, and on the cross had shed his blood for the sake of her sins. For this she thanked him, and believed for certain that when she should die she would be taken up to him.

<!-- lines 15888-15893 -->
On the 22nd the 2 brothers mentioned above came back from the Cape; but the wagon had to stay the night in the middle of the kloof: for it could be brought no further, although 14 oxen were yoked to it.

<!-- lines 15894-15911 -->
On the 24th, when it was just a year since we had consecrated our church, we kept this memorial day in the following way [3]. Early in the morning we held a solemn morning blessing with our baptised and candidates for baptism, in which we thanked the Saviour heartily that he had now for a year let his word be preached in rest and peace in this church, and that he had blessed it to so many hearts. At the same time we prayed him to go on further with these proofs of his grace. In the afternoon we had a joyful lovefeast with the baptised, and in the evening there was a general meeting in which an address was given.

## Notes

1. The sentence lacks a verb in its second part: *en hij zich nog met verſcheidene gereedſchappen van de Kaap, zooals wij ons met allerlei noodwendigheden tegen den winter van daar moeten voorzien*. The words in square brackets are supplied from the parallel *moeten voorzien*, "must provide".
2. Old Lena, baptised by Georg Schmidt (see Lena in the glossary). The German column has *die alte Schwester Lena*.
3. The church was consecrated on 24 March 1796. The Dutch diary of 1795 to 1796 ends on 13 March 1796 (entry 34), and the diary of June to November 1796 says that the reports of the months between were lost (entry 64).
