<!-- lines 15009-15032 -->
On 15 March we had a Hottentot who has a wagon fetch two loads of poles, partly to use as firewood, partly to fence our garden, in which we are now very busy sowing and planting. On the 16th a wagon had to go to the farmer who visited us on the 9th and had invited us to visit him soon. We made use of this good opportunity and drove with it. He met us very kindly and invited us to stay the night with him; but not wishing to, we drove home again in the evening. On the 17th our pupils came diligently to school again. Their number is growing, and they are making astonishing progress in learning: most of them already know all the letters, and they apply themselves to it with much effort and diligence. On the 18th a Hottentot came who had served a farmer with his son. When the agreed time of their service had run out, the son asked the farmer how long he was still to serve him. The farmer beat him violently at this question, and the son ran away, and he did not know where he had gone, though 4 weeks had already passed [1]. We could do nothing about it but advise him to go to the Landdrost and lay his trouble before him.

<!-- lines 15033-15049 -->
On the 19th we received a letter from the town from Mr Teifseur [2]. He wrote that since we had left the Cape so quickly, and he had not been able to do us any kindness there as he wished, he was sending us here half an aum of wine [3], and wished us abundant blessings from God. We have no acquaintance at all with this gentleman. Today the Hottentot whom we sent away, as told above, because he had beaten his wife so cruelly, came begging very urgently for forgiveness and to be taken back, because he could bear it no longer; and he promised never to do it again. So now he is coming to school again [4]. On 20 March we had wood cut by the rivers, because in winter they become so full of water that this can no longer be done then.

<!-- lines 15050-15069 -->
On the 21st we had to keep a day of rest, because in the days before we had worked so hard in our garden that we were quite tired out and weakened, not being used to such heavy work. We had to root out, in a fierce heat that surpasses the greatest heat in Germany, the bushes with which the garden was choked [5]. On the 22nd we had a pleasant and distinguished visit from the Landdrost of Swellendam, with his wife and daughter, in the company of the minister of that place [6], and of the Baas of a Company post which lies 30 days' journey from us [7], with his wife and daughter, and Baas Teunis's wife: 8 persons together. The Landdrost asked us to visit him, and knowing that we had no horses of our own, he promised to have us fetched with a wagon as soon as we had the wish and the time for it. One needs 2 days to get there with a wagon drawn by horses. He treated us very kindly and promised to help us in all things wherever he could. They stayed some hours with us, with pleasure.

<!-- lines 15070-15098 -->
On 23 March we made a start on leading the water from the river, which is a good way from us, into our garden, so as to be able to water it [8]. Brother Schmidt, the former missionary, had already done this, but in the time between the water channel had become so filled with stones that one could hardly see anything of it. Yet several of the Hottentots assured us that they had seen this channel. Five of them began that same day to put the channel in order again. Both on 23 March, being the first day of Passion week, which is kept so solemnly in the congregation, and on 25 March, being the day of the Annunciation, when the angel told Mary that the Saviour of the world would be born of her [9], we were with the congregation in spirit, imploring our gracious Lord and Saviour to bless us too, his poor and wretched ones, with the feeling of his nearness; to let the light of his meritorious wounds shine brightly in our hearts in this darkened country; and to set forth his form in death in us in a moving way. Among the so-called Christians in this country things look very bad as regards religion. Many children of the Christians do not know a single letter; they grow up so, and grow old, without being able to learn the word of God. To hear it preached in a church also happens very seldom, since they have to ride a couple of days or longer to be able to get to a church, which is most sad.

## Notes

1. The German diary adds that the farmer beat him with the sjambok.
2. *den Heere Teifseur*. The German diary has *von dem Eß⸗Sieur* here (line 2243) and *vom Eß-Le sieur* in Brother Marsveld's report (line 2375), which its translation reads as perhaps Le Sueur (A363595, entry 04, note 14). The Dutch name may be a misreading of the same name; the gentleman has not been identified. Compare the Mr Lesueur of 1794 in `glossary.csv`. To be checked against the image.
3. *een half Aam Wijn*: half an aum; the aum was a measure of wine of about 150 litres.
4. Line 15046 is missing, between *Hy komt dan nu ook weder ter School.* and *Den 20 Maart*. The German diary has nothing more here, so the gap probably loses no text.
5. The transcription reads *de Struiken zwaar mede de Thuin opgepropt was*; *zwaar* is probably a misreading of *waar*, "with which".
6. *den Predikant daar ter Plaatze*: the minister at Swellendam, whom the German diary names *Domine Magrer*. The name has not been identified; compare *den Heer J Predikant te Zwellendam* on 28 January (entry 51, note 27).
7. The German diary has "10 days' journey from here".
8. *het water uit de Rivier, die een geheel einde van ons af is*, "the river, which is a good way from us". This settles the German *von unſerer Kirche*, "from our church" (A363595, entry 04, note 17): the mission had no church, and the German words are probably a misreading of words for "a good way off".
9. *de dag der Boodſchap een's Engels aan Maria*: the Annunciation, 25 March. This settles the German *als am Feſt aller Thore* (A363595, entry 04, note 19), which is probably a misreading of the name of the feast of the Annunciation (*Mariä Verkündigung*). 23 March 1793 was the Saturday before Palm Sunday, on which the Brethren began the reading of the Passion week story; Easter fell on 31 March.
10. *In deeze Nagt*: Brother Marsveld's own account (entry 55) puts his arrival on the 25th at 12 o'clock at night. The German diary has "In this week".
