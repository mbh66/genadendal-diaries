Diary of January and February 1798.
After the meetings on New Year's Day the colonists who had kept the holidays here set out on the journey back, in part with a deep impression and a thankful heart, and so too the Hottentots who do not live here, but had come on a visit. On 3 January the mason who works on our mill, and is a slave, was called back. He had just got so far with his work that we no longer needed him.

<!-- lines 13586-13609 -->
On the 6th a snake five feet long crept into our pigsty. Happily the pigs were not in it; otherwise they could all have been killed; for even horses are often bitten to death by this kind, which is very dangerous. It was shot dead with a gun. On the 13th a large party came here in 5 wagons, and stayed the night with us, partly in our house, partly under a tent. In this party was also the Governor's secretary, who is now travelling through the country, and made inquiry, closely and with sympathy, into all the circumstances concerning our mission [1]. He, as well as others, praised our district, but we could with advantage lay out a second place for our mission: which has already been said to us several times [2]. This whole party attended the meetings on Sunday the 14th; but after them went on with its journey.

<!-- lines 13610-13634 -->
Today we received a letter from the Landdrost of Stellenbosch, stating that we had been accused by the brothers Morgel over the running away from their service of two Hottentot women who live with us [3]. Seeing from this letter that many circumstances had been falsely given, Brother Marsveld set out on the 15th on the journey to Stellenbosch. The Landdrost declared that he did not in the least doubt the truth of our account; and added: the Morgels are restless fellows; which is why they must now keep 15 dragoons as well. From there Brother Marsveld travelled to Cape Town, and had the pleasure of finding a packet from Europe there, which he brought us on the 21st. In it were reports from the congregations, Daily Texts, Brother Joseph's addresses to children [4], together with a letter from Brother Reichel, and two from Brother Latrobe, but still no further news of Brother and Sister Kohrhammer. We begin to fear that they will have been taken by a privateer.

<!-- lines 13634-13666 -->
Today the water came streaming down from the mountains so strongly and abundantly that our mill dam broke through. We called all the Hottentots to help, and in the space of 3 hours the damage was so far repaired that we could grind again. We had a still stronger flood on the 26th, in which we saw God's protection most plainly [5]. We found ourselves between two waters, namely the Sonderend River, and a new stream, which had washed out a bed for itself a good 6 ells deep. In this way our buildings were spared, although much was laid waste in our garden, and 15 of our sheep lost their lives [as well] [6]. On the 30th, when the water had gone down fairly well, we could first rightly see how great the damage was which this flood had caused. Our mill dam had been washed away entirely, so that we are forced to make a new one. We had many visits from the Hottentots, especially from the newcomers, who are much awakened. But some girls cause us much concern, and two of them had to be sent away as seducers. The soldiers, who are quartered everywhere in the country, are to blame for much mischief.

<!-- lines 13667-13701 -->
On 3 February we celebrated Holy Communion, which two sisters enjoyed for the first time. In these days we heard much of the damage the floods had done in the whole country. Many a farmer has lost 3, 4, even 600 sheep. On the 12th we saw ourselves compelled to exclude young Martha from our fellowship, which was made known in the meeting of the baptised, and caused great emotion and loud weeping [7]. On the 20th four adults were baptised into the death of Jesus. On the 25th we had the joy of receiving a consignment of goods that had been sent to us by way of Copenhagen, and which we had held to be lost, together with some letters, some of which were a good five years old. On the 28th we remembered our moving into our dwelling 5 years ago, and thanked the Saviour for all that he has done for us since that time. In this month 5 persons have come among the candidates for baptism. Herewith we greet all our dear brothers and sisters in and outside the congregation, and commend ourselves anew to their remembrance and intercession.

## Notes

2. *Hy zoo wel als anderen roemden onze landstreek, maar wy met voordeel eene tweede plaats voor ons Zendelingſchap zouden kunnen aanleggen*: the sentence does not hold together; "praised our district" does not fit what follows. The first copy (lines 12852 to 12858) is also garbled (*aan den ons eene landſtreek, waar wÿ*); together they suggest "pointed out to us a district where we could with advantage lay out a second place for our mission" (entry 41, note 3). 3. *dat my van de Gebroeders Morgel*: *my* for *wy*. The first copy (line 12866) has "a couple of Hottentot women". See Morgel in the glossary.
4. *Loofingen*: a misreading of *Looſingen*, the Daily Texts, as the first copy (line 12880) has it. *Br. Joſephs kindere⸗den*: the addresses to children of August Gottlieb Spangenberg; see August Gottlieb Spangenberg in the glossary.
5. *den 26*; the first copy (line 12894) has "the 24th".
6. Line 13652 is missing. The transcription runs *en ook 15 van onze ſchaapen om 't leven* / *Den 30*. The first copy (line 12903) has *om 't leven kwamen*; the lost line probably held only *kwamen*.
