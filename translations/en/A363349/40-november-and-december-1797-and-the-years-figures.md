Extract from the diary of the months of November and December [1].

<!-- lines 12530-12573 -->
That in carrying out our plan of building a mill for our own use, and for that of the Hottentots who live with us and in our neighbourhood, all kinds of obstacles would be laid in our way by the farmers who have mills of their own, was easy to suppose: for the owners of the mills had until now found it far too much to their advantage to keep the Hottentots dependent on them, to favour such an undertaking, or even merely to look on at it indifferently. To frustrate the thing itself, only one way lay open to them, namely to prevent, as far as was in their power, our getting anyone who would take on this work and carry it out. A good while before, a man who understood the work had promised us that he would come to us at the end of October and see to the building; but in the beginning of November this man let us know that it was not possible for him before New Year; and in case we could find no other master in the meantime, he would then see whether it would be possible for him to help us. From this we saw that we could place no reliance on this man's promises, and so found ourselves in no small perplexity. But two days later Negrini, of whom mention has several times been made in our diaries, gave us his word, on the occasion of a visit to us, that he would come back after 14 days and make the mill wheel, with everything that belongs to it. Now Baas Teunis too made us the offer of lending us a mason for some time, which we accepted with joy.

<!-- lines 12574-12598 -->
Accordingly the little mill house was built, not far from our dwelling, by the small brook that runs past it, from the ground up to the first storey of hewn stone, and the rest up to the roof of moulded and dried bricks of clay. We had the millstones fetched from some days' journey away. Negrini appeared at the appointed time, and carried out the work; a German too, who is a skilful wagon[maker, gave us good] advice [in many respects] [2]. So everything went as we wished, and went forward so happily that on 22 December we could grind on our mill for the first time. This work is put together very simply, and consists only of a driving wheel, which lies outside the mill house on the surface of the water, and by means of a leather belt sets in motion another wheel, fixed to the axle that goes through the millstone.

<!-- lines 12599-12632 -->
Even before the mill was ready, another difficulty quite unexpectedly vanished for us, which until now had existed with regard to the supply and the buying of corn [3]. On a supply we had indeed made no reckoning; for we had to fetch the grain ourselves; and what the Hottentots bought came dear to them because of the work they had to do for the miller besides. But just at present, now that grain is so scarce that people will gladly make a long journey to get it, a farmer from the coast brought a load to our door, and sold it to us for a price at which we can buy it nowhere now. And what makes this circumstance still more remarkable is that this farmer is one of those who 2 years ago meant to come here with a commando and lay everything waste, and when this did not succeed, bound himself with the other farmers not to sell to us and the Hottentots, so as to be able to starve us out by it. When the other farmers learned that this farmer had brought us corn, they vied with one another, as it were, to supply us with this and other kinds of provisions; for, they said, if he has broken his word, we need not keep ours.

<!-- lines 12633-12646 -->
The farmers had made a like agreement among themselves when our smithy was being built, and had even said openly that they would not buy the least thing of what we made. But the smithy was not yet quite finished before they were already asking for knives, and ordering them; and at present Brother Kühnel has so much to do that he cannot get the fourth part of what is wanted ready. For this reason he has taken a young Hottentot as an apprentice, from whom, however, he has as yet little help.

<!-- lines 12647-12677 -->
Besides this, God has also given his blessing on our farming. In our garden the vegetables have not failed the whole year through, and have all stood so fine that many who visited us wondered at it, and said that they had never yet seen such a garden, and that God's blessing on us was visible in this matter too. It would be very much to the advantage of the Hottentots, both outwardly and inwardly, if we had more opportunity to provide them with work, so that they did not need to earn their bread with the farmers; which is not only very hard for them, but also bound up with much danger of suffering harm to their souls, above all in the present time of war, while [the men of this nation are for the] most part in English service at the Cape [4], and the English dragoons who are quartered with the farmers commit many excesses, over which we often grow very sad. Here on our place the dragoons who come here now and then behave very orderly, since the officers who often visit us keep good discipline.

<!-- lines 12678-12701 -->
Nor have we lacked visits of every sort in this time. We mention only those that came with some particular circumstances. On Sunday 19 November 5 strangers came to the meeting, and after it 4 English officers as well, who at once let us know that they wished to eat with us. We told them that we had not much; to which they answered by saying that a soldier is content with anything. This assurance already lessened our perplexity, but still more the unexpected departure of the first guests, who grew so afraid of the officers that shortly after their arrival they rode off at full gallop, without first waiting for the midday meal. So instead of 9 persons we had only five to lodge [5].

<!-- lines 12702-12719 -->
On 23 December a dragoon brought us a letter from the warm bath, in which we were asked to keep a room free for several English officers and their wives, who wished to come here for Christmas. This party then arrived with us on the 24th, together with still more people, some with wagons, some on horseback; so that we would have lacked room to get all these under a roof, had the officers not taken the precaution of bringing a tent with them, which they pitched in front of our house, and slept in. On the first holiday still more visitors came, and because of the crowd it looked almost as if a fair were to be held here.

<!-- lines 12720-12759 -->
So that this crowd should not take up the room in the meetings which is properly meant for our Hottentots, and for such as had come from far to attend the festival, we had so arranged it that we placed the guests in our two rooms, which are at either side of the hall, declaring that it would grieve us if we had to see the poor sheep from among the Hottentots, for whose sake we were here after all, standing outside the hall; to which they answered that they too held this to be unfitting, and would gladly stand back. So the Hottentots occupied the hall alone at the preaching, and even so many of them had to stand outside. We did not even take the trouble to count the Hottentots, since their number was too great, but tried to do so with the other hearers; but this too was in vain, since everyone ran about too much among one another. So much is certain, that their number was far above a hundred. With such numerous visits we must then limit the law of hospitality as far as possible, the necessity of which [the visitors themselves also see. After midday] they all left, except for a few, who still attended the children's meeting [6]. We were very glad when we saw ourselves alone again, not only because of the bustle and the burdens such a visit brings with it, but also because of the administering of baptism, which we had appointed for the following day.

<!-- lines 12760-12783 -->
On the 26th, as the 2nd holiday, the preaching was at 10 o'clock, and after it 9 persons, namely 2 men and 6 women, were baptised [7]. We must confess that God's Spirit proved himself very active not only in this meeting, for such baptisms are always exceptionally blessed, but also in all the others during these holidays. Many of the strange Hottentots who had come here declared their thankfulness with tears for what they had seen, heard and enjoyed, and it grieved them very much that they had to go away again. The visitors were just as numerous on the last day of the year, on which we found very much cause to praise and thank our Saviour for the grace, goodness and faithfulness with which he has blessed us and our Hottentot congregation the whole year through.

<!-- lines 12784-12809 -->
It is still to be noted that while we were busy building our mill, two Hottentots were kept from harm in a special way. Over the foot of the one, while he was breaking stones, crept one of the most poisonous snakes, without hurting him. The other, who had been moulding bricks and had laid his jacket on the ground meanwhile, noticed a strange movement on his body after he had come home from work, and to both meetings [8]. As this movement grew stronger, he took off his jacket when he came into the kitchen to eat, to search it. And behold! a snake had crept into it, of which only the head with its tongue put out was to be seen. When a shout went up in the kitchen at this, we ran to it, and Brother Schwinn trod on the snake's head before it had harmed anyone. It was of a poisonous kind, and more than half an ell long.

<!-- lines 12810-12821 -->
In this year 1797, 24 adults and 11 children have been baptised. 3 persons have come to Communion. At the end of the year the congregation of the Hottentots consisted of [9]:

18 communicants
5 candidates for Communion
42 baptised adults
19 baptised children
52 candidates for baptism
Total 136 persons, 53 more than at the end of the previous year.
