<!-- lines 6828-6838 -->
On 1 December another farmer came, named Predicam, to hire Hottentots, but he could not get any. A large family also moved to us, which had lived 10 miles from here. On the 2nd: since several Hottentots have sown some corn this year, a beginning was made today with cutting it. On the 3rd Mary came to hire Hottentots, but he could not get any.

<!-- lines 6839-6868 -->
On the 4th several of the baptised came, quite dismayed, and said that Anna had been at Baas Teunis's and had heard there that things did not look well for our place. She had heard that all the Hottentots who were here from other places [must go away again, because their cattle had] done damage [1]. (Lately his son had some of the Hottentots' cattle driven into the pound, because they had been in the corn, and to redeem them a shilling a head had to be paid.) On Sunday he meant to come to us with several gentlemen from the Cape who are staying with him. Several gentlemen had also meant to visit us yesterday, but they were prevented by the heavy rain. These must surely have been gentlemen of the government, for when they rode away from the post, the cannon was fired [2]. Through the rumours mentioned above, the Hottentots had fallen into great perplexity. We must say that such rumours often put us in great perplexity too, because we know that they would gladly drive us away from here if it were possible. We can do nothing more than pray to the dear Saviour for help.

<!-- lines 6869-6877 -->
On the 5th Brother Marsveld was called to 2 sick people. One is a Bastard and has consumption. According to what the others said, he had wanted to speak openly with him, but could not. When Brother Marsveld asked him whether, when he died, he knew where his soul would go, he answered that he believed in God, whom he prayed day and night for the forgiveness of his sins.

<!-- lines 6877-6891 -->
Christian Kuns [3] also sent word that a packet and letters for us had arrived at his house, which we should have fetched. We had heard that ships had arrived, and our first thought was that these would be letters from our dear brothers in Europe. We sent someone there on horseback at once, and were so eager that we could hardly wait until the Hottentot came back. When he came and we saw the letters, our joy was gone again, for they were letters from the Cape; so we had to give ourselves to patience again.

<!-- lines 6892-6902 -->
On the 6th Baas Teunis let us know that he would come and visit us tomorrow with several gentlemen. Yesterday also a Hottentot had come with his wife to live here [4]. He said that he had run away; but today we heard that he had stolen and had run away for that reason. We had him called, put this to him, and told him that he should see to it that he got away from here again, for we did not want such people living here.

<!-- lines 6902-6919 -->
On Sunday the 7th [5], early at 8 o'clock, young Mrs Morgel came to visit us with her daughter and her son's son; she also had 3 slave women with her. Then Baas Teunis came with 3 gentlemen and 4 young ladies from the Cape; so today there were 11 persons together, not counting the many slaves, who all want a mouthful of food too. One of these gentlemen was the Vice-Fiscal with his wife; the other, named Elias, an uncle of the well-known Elias, a Director in Amsterdam [6]. All these attended the meeting, which we held again in the garden under the pear tree. Brother Marsveld spoke on Luke 22:40, 41 [7].

<!-- lines 6919-6946 -->
After the meeting Baas Teunis had all the Hottentots who have come to live here from other places gather in front of the house, and made known to them in the name of the Edle Heer, the Commissioner, that they must take their cattle back where they had been, and as many must move back with them as [were needed to look after the cattle; here they might] keep nothing but a few animals for slaughter [8]; when these were eaten, they could fetch more. If they did not obey this, he would take away all the cattle he found in this area. Only one of them all had the heart to answer him back, and said that he might not go back to his place, for the farmer had chased him away. He got the answer that he could go and complain. The Hottentot said: "That has happened more than once, and it has helped no more than that he has now chased me away and driven me from there." Baas Teunis declared him to be one who would not obey the authorities, and said that he would make a report about him.

<!-- lines 6946-6979 -->
Another said: "We have moved here in order to hear God's word. If I must go back where I lived, to herd my cattle there, I cannot hear God's word." (Where he lived is about 15 miles from here.) Baas Teunis said: "I have told you; if you do not follow it, you shall see what will follow." The Hottentots were all quite dismayed that they are now to move away again, since God's word matters so much to them. How we feel at seeing such people, longing for salvation, move away from us again can easily be imagined. Nor could we keep silent towards Baas Teunis; we told him that it was a thing to lament before God, and pitiable, how these poor people are treated. They are often treated much worse than the cattle; indeed, were it possible, people would exterminate them altogether or make them wholly slaves. But all this that he said no one but he himself set on foot, for he had long wanted it so. One may well say that this Teunis is now one of our greatest and worst enemies. Towards us he is now very friendly, and for our cause; but to others he lets it be known that if it were possible he would like to destroy the whole thing.

<!-- lines 6980-7023 -->
He also knows how to twist everything so that he will not be known as an enemy. It is true that both he and she showed us much love and friendship at the beginning, and helped us in everything where they could, for which we wish them God's blessing. But our purpose with the Hottentots seemed to him, as to many, only laughable, and they believed that nothing would come of our purpose. She often said so, too, at the time when our house was being built and we lived with them: still, we could try it; if it would not go, we need not go back to the fatherland for that, but could stay here; he would look after us. Now they look at this matter with the greatest astonishment, see themselves deceived, and wish that we had rather never been here. The enemy sharpens his arrows against us every day. Now the restrictions and laws are beginning too. He told us today among other things that the Edle Heer sent us word that we might accept no Hottentot who had served a farmer unless he brought a certificate from him that he was now free and his time was up. He has always told us that we [need not ask] anyone [whether he had run away; it was the] order of the authorities that no farmer might keep a Hottentot who wanted to leave him and learn [9]. If now none of the Hottentots who are with the farmers may come to us except those to whom the farmers give a certificate, then certainly few will come; for most have to run away, since the farmers will not let them go. God knows how it will go yet. We pray for help to him who has taken the power from Satan, and who will also help us and have mercy on this poor people.

<!-- lines 7023-7027 -->
In the afternoon all our guests rode away again. In the evening Brother Schwinn spoke on 1 John 4:16, 17. The singing meeting, which Brother Kühnel held, was in front of the house [10].

<!-- lines 7027-7054 -->
Early on the 10th we sent to the post at once to fetch the letters [11]. Today was also the speaking of the members and the baptised; two were not at home, but with the farmers at the harvest. We could rejoice over the declarations of them all, for they all testified that it was their whole mind to live for the Saviour in this world. (Towards evening Jacob, whom we had sent to the post to fetch the letters, came back. These were the letters from Brother Reichel which we had long been looking forward to. They were of 23 and 24 April this year, 7 months old; there were also several letters from Amsterdam with them. What a joy this was cannot be described, for there was an answer to our last letter about this place, on which we have now lived about 1 year and 10 months [12]. Mr Heuser [13] at the Cape writes to us that he has not yet been able to get the goods ashore, and so has sent the letters ahead; he will send the goods too at the first opportunity. Then we shall see how things look in our dear congregations.

<!-- lines 7054-7086 -->
On the 11th young Mrs Mary sent us word that goods from the Cape had arrived for us at her house, which we should have fetched. This message caused us no small joy, to receive them so soon; but today it was too late to have them fetched. Early on the 12th we sent our cart to fetch our goods; towards midday the cart came back. With what eagerness we opened the chests no one will believe. It was a real day of joy for us. We also received letters from our dear brothers at Zeist, from Brothers Rothe [14], Wagner, Friedlezius and 2 Schneiders, also from Brother Suiter in Amsterdam, as well as from other good friends at Zeist and from those at Herrnhut. Now we mean to treat ourselves a little to the news sent us, and refresh our hearts with the addresses we have received. But we were sorry that we found no Daily Texts for the coming year with them [15]. From all these letters we could see that all our dear congregations still lived in peace, for which we were very thankful to our dear Lord. Several brothers wonder that our letters are so old; but the letters we received from our good friends at Herrnhut are already 1½ years old.

<!-- lines 7086-7089 -->
On the 13th we held the Lord's Supper for the last time this year, at which he made himself palpably known. On Sunday the 14th there were no Christians here.

<!-- lines 7090-7101 -->
On the 15th a son of Mary came with a farmer who lives close to the Kaffers. He [wanted to hire a Hottentot woman, but got] none [16]. He too had had to leave his place, and the Kaffers have taken his cattle; since it is now fairly quiet again, he means to move back there. He wished that we would come there; perhaps the Bosjesmans (Bushmen) would change if the Gospel were proclaimed to them. The Christians there are very much afraid of them. Several have already expressed this wish.

<!-- lines 7101-7106 -->
On the 17th the Hottentots' corn, which they have sown beyond the Sergeants River, was brought here [17]. Several had sat on the wagon, but it overturned, and some were rather badly hurt.

<!-- lines 7106-7143 -->
Brother Marsveld was also called to a sick man, who said that he was bewitched: he had walked over a place where poison had been buried, and had fallen ill from it. Brother Marsveld tried to talk him out of this. A short while ago one of our baptised also came who believed the same of herself [18]. With her we spoke very earnestly, and asked her whether she no longer believed in the Saviour, who is the physician of soul and body, but sought help from the devil. She at once asked earnestly for forgiveness, and said that she would also pray the dear Saviour from her heart for forgiveness, because she had grieved him so; she had not known it; she wanted nothing but what the dear Saviour wants. We had thought that the Hottentots knew nothing of witchcraft; now we had inquired about it, and heard to our astonishment that they have a strong belief in it. Some said: "Since we have been hearing God's word, we do not believe in it so much." (They told us that further up country there were Hottentots who take snake bones [19] out of the bodies of the sick, which had made the people ill. Such a one who has got well again must give the sorcerer something every year, or else he falls ill again.) A woman said: "I have done that until now, but now I will stop." We said that these were all works of the devil, and that they grieved the dear Saviour by them; there was nothing to it but to lure the money out of people's purses. The black slaves are especially known as such sorcerers.

<!-- lines 7143-7145 -->
On the 18th we had a heavy thunderstorm with much rain, which had refreshed everything nicely.

<!-- lines 7146-7155 -->
On the 19th 2 more women came who spoke with us about the unrest of their hearts. We had to let the singing meeting lapse today because of the rainy weather; for when we hold a meeting both door and window must stand open, partly for those who cannot get in, and partly because of the heat and also the smell of the people; for their sweat smells strongly, and it is very warm at present.

<!-- lines 7155-7167 -->
On the 22nd many Hottentots came home from the harvest, among them 2 of our baptised, about whom we were worried because of the drinking; but to our joy they behaved well. One of them brought home the wine she had been given when they finished [20]. For as soon as the corn is cut, the farmer brings some buckets of wine onto the land, and everyone gets plenty there. When they are drunk, the farmer hires them for the coming year, and what they promise then they must keep.

<!-- lines 7167-7172 -->
On the 23rd several more women were there who were very uneasy and spoke with us. Today we had called off all schools for this year, because we had much writing to do.

<!-- lines 7172-7201 -->
In the singing meeting, which we held in front of our house, we played [21], since [... On the 24th, being the eve of] Christmas night, we spoke early with the children, told them what an important day this was, and called on them to thank the Saviour right from their hearts that he had become man for their sakes too; and the dear Saviour proved himself very palpably to these young hearts. At dusk, about half past 7, we had a lovefeast with our baptised. Since we had given nothing but the meal for the rolls, they gave the fat for it among themselves, to bake them a little better [22]. At the lovefeast the story of the birth of Jesus was first read; then in a prayer on our knees we thanked the dear Saviour from our hearts for his becoming man, and also for all that he had done for these poor people until now, and asked him to go on with his mercy. A real feeling of peace was to be felt, and he received many tears of thanks and joy. When it was over, we heard a great number sitting in front of a kraal singing of the birth of Jesus, at which we felt deeply moved; it went on until after 10 o'clock.

<!-- lines 7201-7204 -->
On the 25th, Christmas Day, there was a meeting at 10 o'clock, in which Brother Marsveld spoke on the birth of Jesus [23].

<!-- lines 7204-7236 -->
On the 26th, the second holiday, we sang the Liturgy to the Son early with our baptised. At 10 o'clock there was again a general meeting, in which Brother Schwinn spoke on the Gospel. Early the farmer Gildenheuser [24] came too, but he soon rode away again. We put the baptism off to tomorrow, because we feared that many Christians might come and we would have no room for it. For these holidays more Hottentots had come than we have ever seen together; they came from everywhere they are in service with the farmers. Since because of the heavy rain and wind we could not hold the meeting in the garden under the pear tree, we let as many as possible into our bedroom and kitchen. The room was far from enough, and many had to stand in the rain and wind before the door. We can say that the dear Saviour proved himself powerfully and mightily on the hearts of these people, and blessed the testimony that was given of his holy becoming man. (But to our sorrow we had to exclude one of the candidates for baptism, who had behaved too badly at the farmer's where she was [25].

<!-- lines 7236-7252 -->
On the 27th was the baptism, which Brother Kühnel held; he spoke on the words of the 2nd chapter of Philippians, verse 7. She received the name Catharina. We told her only late in the evening, and had the baptised told only in the morning, because we thought it would not become so full. But these people all gathered again as if they had been summoned. The Spirit of God owned this act of holy baptism powerfully; and so we closed these days of blessing and festival with praise and thanks to our dear Lord for all the good that he has let us and this poor people enjoy, and asked him to go on with his mercy.

<!-- lines 7252-7279 -->
On Sunday the 28th [26] the whole crowd of people gathered again. Brother Marsveld spoke on the Gospel of the 3rd holiday. Several also came who spoke with us about the unrest of their hearts. Brother Kühnel was called to a sick candidate for baptism, whom he found in a gladdening state of heart; he was also called to a sick girl in the kraals. Today we received a letter from Mr Heuser at the Cape, in which he reports that the ship with Domine Miller on board, who has brought various goods for us, has arrived; but the Domine had ridden to Stellenbosch on a visit, and he had not yet been able to get the goods; so we must wait until he came back. He had received from him a letter from Brother Schmidt, which he has sent us. We were very glad that the Daily Texts for the coming year were among [the goods]. (We shall probably not have them yet by the beginning of the year, but we hope to get them soon after.)

<!-- lines 7279-7300 -->
On the 29th 3 Hottentots came from the Breede River, about 20 German miles from here, who wanted to live here to hear God's word; they brought their oxen and cows with them. After New Year they mean to go again, to fetch their families and kraals and also to bring their sheep [27]. These are still rich in cattle; together they had some 70 oxen and cows, and they said that they also had a large flock of sheep. They had found ostrich eggs on the way, of which they brought us 2 as a present. From one such egg the 3 of us have a full meal. (There are no ostriches where we are.) These eggs are found in the veld, where there are many of them; yet one must pay 1 shilling apiece, for 1 shilling is little money here.

<!-- lines 7300-7326 -->
On the 31st [28] we were busy writing to Europe. In the evening Brother Schwinn spoke in the meeting on 1 John 5:20. He reminded them that tomorrow was the last day of this year, and asked that each one would examine himself before Jesus how he had spent this year, and thank God for the great mercy he had shown them this year, in having his word proclaimed to them through his servants and the way of salvation made known; and that each one should also fall down at the feet of Jesus, ask him for forgiveness, and thank him from the heart for all the good he had given them. This caused a general stirring, at which many shed tears. Then it was made known to them that tomorrow evening at half past 11 we would come together, to thank our dear Lord together once more for all the love and mercy he has shown us.

<!-- lines 7326-7341 -->
On 31 December. We will still say something about what Baas Teunis made known on the 7th of this month, namely that all the Hottentots who have moved here since we have been here must take their cattle back where they have lived, and as many must go back with them as would be needed to look after the cattle. (So far no one has gone away.) He said that if he found cattle of theirs in the veld, he would take it and [hand it over to the government. The Hottentots] say: "If he will take our cattle, he may do it; we will not move away for that; we have come here to hear God's word" [29].

<!-- lines 7342-7355 -->
At half past 11 we came together for the close of this year. Our Hottentots all came. It was a fine evening, so that we could open door and window. Brother Marsveld called on them all, at the close of this year, to thank God with us for all his grace and mercy; and we fell on our knees with them, and thanked God for all that he has done at this place, and passed over into the new year with praise and thanks. There was a general stirring at this close.

<!-- lines 7356-7359 -->
Afterwards many came to wish us well, with these words: "I wish you a blessed New Year, long life, and a blessed death."

<!-- lines 7360-7395 -->
Now we close this year 1794 with praise and thanks to our dear Lord, ashamed and thankful for all the good he has done to us, his 3 poor brothers; and we find very much cause to ask him for forgiveness where we have not acted according to his heart. When we look at the blessing the Saviour has laid on the testimony of his 3 poor brothers about his becoming man, suffering and death [30], and how through the Holy Spirit he has awakened hearts and made them perplexed, so that few days have passed without some coming to ask, "What must I do to be saved?", we are deeply ashamed at it. It seems as if there were now a quite new life among these people. A thousand, yes, a thousand thanks be to him for it! Nor can we thank him enough that in this year he has let us stay together in love and peace, so that we have been able to share joy and pain with one another. Now may he keep this for us! With this we commend ourselves and the work of God among the Hottentots to the prayers and remembrance of all our dear congregations before our dear Lord, and especially those who make up our congregation, that our dear Saviour may keep them with himself and his wounds, and let them grow more and more in the knowledge of Jesus Christ, so that they may shine as burning lights in this dark land.
