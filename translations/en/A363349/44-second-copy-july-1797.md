This is the second Dutch copy of the reports for May 1797 to February 1798; the first copy is translated in entries 35 to 41, and the notes compare the two. There is no German diary for these months. The diarists' words for Khoekhoe people ("Hottentots") and Xhosa people ("Kaffers") are kept as they wrote them; see `glossary.csv`. Square brackets mark editorial additions.

On 9 July the Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel went to the post, where they had been invited by the Major, who had already visited them the month before, and now wished to make friends. They could not speak with him himself, but with another Major from the Cape, who was also present, and understood some German. When the brothers learned that the latter was charged to travel through the whole country, and to propose improvements to the government where they might be needed, they would gladly have laid the circumstances of our Hottentots before him: but this would not succeed for the present; meanwhile he promised that when he came back from Swellendam he would call on us and inquire into everything [1].

<!-- lines 13126-13145 -->
On the 16th the said Major came to visit us. We then set before his eyes all the oppressions the Hottentots had to suffer from the farmers; to which he answered by saying that we must turn to the Governor; and he meant to speak with him about it himself, as soon as an opportunity for it should arise. On the 23rd a dragoon came from the post to fetch various vegetables from us in the name of the Major, who expected the General in these days. Before this some of them had been with us for seed and plants. Some eighty men now lie at the post, and it seems that they will settle there.
