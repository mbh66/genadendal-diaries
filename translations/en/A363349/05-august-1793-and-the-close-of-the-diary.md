<!-- lines 2228-2249 -->
[On] 1 August [we had an unpleasant experience], in that the cattle had got into the garden in the night and had eaten a great deal; indeed, had they not luckily been chased out of the garden by a Hottentot woman who lives close by and had woken up and gone out of her kraal, the cattle would have trampled and destroyed everything sown and planted. So our hope of getting something from the garden for our kitchen had come to nothing. The ditch round the garden, to keep the oxen from getting into it, must be deep and wide, since the oxen in this country jump over ditches in a way one can hardly imagine possible. [On the 2nd we took the last-cut] wood [1], as the most suitable help for making still thicker and firmer those places in our hedge where the oxen can most easily break through into the garden.

<!-- lines 2250-2292 -->
On 3 August Brother Kühnel experienced a special preservation by God. He was busy nailing a crosspiece over the two side ladders of our cart, to keep them from knocking together so easily while driving. He wanted to bore the holes for the nails in one of these ladders, and to do this he had to stand on top of both ladders. One of his legs slipped, and as he tried to hold on to the borer, which was an ell long, it broke in two pieces, and he was in danger of falling with his body onto the broken-off piece of iron of the borer. But by God's goodness and providence he fell beside it, off the cart onto the ground, without suffering any harm of consequence, except a slight injury to his right side.

In the night between 2 and 3 August it froze so hard that the little streams were closed with ice and the mountains were covered with snow. We had not expected to find it so cold here, having heard that the weather at the Cape was always warm. It is much warmer at the Cape than in this region, because the town lies under the mountains, and the cold winds do not strike it as they do here over the mountains; and further inland it is said to be colder still [2]. But it is not usual for it to freeze so that it makes ice, as it has done this winter. For this reason we have often wished for woollen stockings and a stove in our room. For although the degree of cold here is not as great as in Europe, it is still piercing. In Europe the month of August is the warmest, and here one of the coldest. It is said here that the winter ends with this month.

<!-- lines 2293-2299 -->
On 4 August there was a Negro slave woman in the sermon [3], who came afterwards to give us her thanks and to say that when her mistress, who lives two days' journey from here, came here, she would ask her whether she might attend our service every Sunday, so as to learn too what is good or evil.

<!-- lines 2300-2348 -->
On 5 August one of our wishes, which we have long had, was fulfilled. We had already spoken to the Hottentots many times about building a kraal together, in which the oxen and cows could be shut up at night, so as to keep them from pushing through the hedge into our garden (which we have not yet been able to make thick enough), and so getting in and either eating up or trampling the vegetables we need for our food. So we spoke to them about it again yesterday, seeing that their repeated promises were not carried out. This had so good a result that they went into the bush with wife and children to fetch the stakes needed for this purpose, in which we helped them with our hands and our cart; and when that was done they went today to build the kraal, and finished this work before evening began to come on, so that the cattle could spend the night in it. So it was done even more quickly than we had expected.

[Since our flour was used up] [4] and we did not know where we could get more, we wrote to Baas Teunis asking him to help us. He was willing at once to let us have as much as he had. So Brother Marsveld rode there today to fetch 3 mud, and came back on 7 August, with still more provisions given by Mrs Teunis, which came very opportunely for us. These good people truly do us many kindnesses. We also received a letter from Juffrouw Schmidt at the Cape, who, on hearing that a Hottentot woman had been baptised, sent us material for her clothing [5].

<!-- lines 2349-2404 -->
On 8 August there were many strangers in the meeting, so that there was not room enough. Some of the Hottentots who have been given leave for a short time to be here must leave again for the Cape tomorrow, which was done on 9 August. As they took leave of us we admonished them above all not to forget what they had heard here of the Lord Jesus, which they promised to do. They had first to go to Baas Teunis, who had received orders to send 300 oxen with all the necessary draught gear to the Cape; but he was not able, as he said, to supply more than 70 with what was wanted with them.

On 10 August we sent our Hottentot to Baas Teunis to fetch 10 sheep for us [6], since he had offered to let us have as many of his as we would need for slaughtering. But as his flock of sheep was at pasture 3 hours further on at the Tigerhoek, we had to have them fetched from there. From the beginning we had indeed set our minds on a small flock of our own, and had received a promise from the Landdrost at Swellendam to provide us with one; but shortly afterwards, having received a letter from Juffrouw Schmidt at the Cape saying that she had arranged 100 sheep for us, we hesitated to accept the Landdrost's offer, because of the far too great number that would come to us. Meanwhile the sheep promised by Juffrouw Schmidt have not come, and who knows whether we can still expect them, since they are to come from the borders of the Kaffers [7]. We have told Baas Teunis of this, and he will write to the Landdrost about it and provide us with the sheep we need in one way or another. At the same time we sent letters to Baas Teunis for the Cape, to give our good friends there news of our welfare, in which they take great interest.

In our conference we thought of a Hottentot woman who has visited us a few times already and has told us of the unrest of her heart and of her urgent longing to receive the forgiveness of her sins and to become a child of God. The Saviour indicated to us that we should take her into the class of candidates for baptism.

<!-- lines 2405-2438 -->
On 11 August two of Baas Teunis's people came to visit us; one was a saddler [8] and a German by birth. They attended the sermon, whose hearers were so many in number that the room was not enough, although a great number of the Hottentot men were absent. The sermon was heard with special emotion, and we often wish that our brothers and sisters could see and feel how graciously the Spirit of God owns the testimony of Jesus' becoming man, his suffering and death, which is given in weakness by us his poor witnesses. May he go on with it in grace.

After midday we talked one by one with those who are to be baptised on the 13th, without telling them yet today, since we meant to do so tomorrow. We asked them whether, in case it should please the Saviour to let the grace of holy baptism come to them, their whole heart was set on renouncing entirely all heathen customs and usages, and on giving themselves soul and body to the Lord Jesus, and on living [9] for him alone in this world by his supporting grace. They answered with tears in their eyes: "Yes, yes, that is the desire and longing of our heart, and we pray and plead with him to accept us poor ones as his children out of grace."

<!-- lines 2438-2492 -->
Towards evening a Hottentot woman who has not lived here long came to visit us. When she came here we did not think she would stay with us long. She is a Bastard and married to a slave. At first she imagined herself much better than the others; but now she came and confessed, with a flood of tears, that she did not know where to turn for anguish and distress of heart, since she felt herself wholly worthy of curse and damnation. She was pointed to the great Friend of sinners and of the wretched, who could abundantly comfort the weary and heavy laden and give rest to their souls.

One of those who are to be baptised named a couple of other Hottentot women who had said to her that they were very uneasy in their hearts and earnestly wished that God would have mercy on them too; whereupon she had advised them to go to us and speak with us about it, in keeping with what we have told them all: that whenever they did not rightly understand something, or became uneasy in their hearts and troubled about being saved, they should just come to whichever of us they had most confidence in, to pour out their need. We have also heard that several have wanted to do so, but have been held back by shame at appearing in their skins. On that occasion we told them that they should come freely and had no reason to be ashamed, since we saw them daily coming to school and to the meeting covered only with their skins, and had not yet sent anyone away for that reason. It would be still more welcome to us if they came to us as weary and heavy laden, troubled about their salvation, and came to ask advice how they might get out of their distress and become children of God. In that case they would find us most willing to give them the best advice according to the measure of grace given us, since we had come into this country for this very purpose, to make them acquainted with Jesus Christ, their Redeemer and Saviour.

<!-- lines 2493-2526 -->
On 12 August we had the 3 Hottentot women who are to be baptised tomorrow come to us, [to tell them that the grace of holy baptism] would come to them [tomorrow] [10], repeating our question of yesterday, whether they could promise it and give their hand on it that their heart was wholly set on living, by God's grace, only for Jesus Christ, and on saying farewell to all heathen traditions. The feeling that went with their answer, "Yes, yes," and above all with the words "with our whole heart," can be expressed neither with the mouth nor with the pen. After this we had the two first candidates, together with the one who was indicated to us on 10 August, come to us as well, to make it known to them, asking them not to tell any other Hottentots; after which it was made known in the general evening meeting, at which a very lovely and pleasant feeling could also be perceived.

On this same day two other Hottentot women came who could no longer hide the unrest of their hearts, and with tears lamented their lost condition. There are still more who have been powerfully seized, but are still ashamed to reveal it. In the meeting we have noticed that many have tried to hold back the tears that sprang into their eyes, but in vain, the tears trickling all the more abundantly down their cheeks. What shall we say to this? It is plainly the work of God.

<!-- lines 2527-2601 -->
On 13 August, the important festival and day of remembrance of all the Brethren's congregations, we held our festival morning blessing early in the morning. At ten o'clock was the baptism. It was just as our hearts wished, that we could perform this solemn act in quiet among our Hottentots alone, without a crowd of strangers. It took place outside the house again, and the feeling of the nearness and presence of God was the same as at the first baptism, beyond all description. The eldest of those baptised, formerly called Trancina [11], received the name Sara; her daughter, formerly Maria, the name Martha; and the third, formerly Mietje, the name Eva.

Martha, a daughter of the Hottentot Jonas, baptised by Brother Schmidt, is a very hard-working and diligent person. At the Cape she was nurse to the child of the minister van Lier. When we arrived at the Cape she asked the minister van Lier about us, and asked him whether he could and would advise her to join us when she had heard that we had settled in the country. He answered that if she had a mind to it he would not only not dissuade her but would much rather advise her to it, and for that purpose would gladly release her from her service with his child; at the same time he urged her to observe carefully everything we would tell her, and if she did so she would fare very well by it.

The two Bastard Hottentots who at the first baptism had not been willing to bend their knees did so this time, and one of their wives declared after the baptism that she judged those happy who were counted worthy of this great grace. As for her own heart, she did not know what to say of it: on the one hand it was as hard as a stone, and on the other she did feel something that she could not put into words.

After midday we held a lovefeast with our 4 baptised and old Lena, baptised by Brother Schmidt, and made them understand its purpose, namely that it was meant to draw the bond of mutual love still closer. Old Lena declared that she could not thank the Saviour enough for having still granted her life and spared her so long, so as to be able to hear so much good about Jesus her Saviour, and to believe that the dear Saviour would take her to himself in heaven. All of them declared that they felt an inward blessedness of heart. Two of those baptised today had clothes of their own to put on, and the third had borrowed clothes from another woman for it. In the evening we partook of Holy Communion, at which the dear Saviour graciously made himself known to us. So this day has been a true festival for us and our Hottentots, whom we had told a couple of days before that we 3 brothers had a festival on 13 August, in which they now all wished to share. Even a couple of children who usually go unclothed put on their best clothes, and when asked why they did so, they answered: to follow the example of the old people and to keep the festival too.

<!-- lines 2602-2638 -->
On 14 August the farmer Plaisir came to visit us. He seems to be our true friend and to wish to keep up the friendship with us.

On 16 August a Hottentot woman came with tears in her eyes to beg and plead with us to let her too into our little church, as she called the special meetings of the candidates for baptism.

On 17 August we remembered the little girls in our congregations, who are keeping their festival today [12], and wished them the best blessings in some verses. In our conference two were approved by the Saviour as candidates for baptism. One was the husband of the baptised Eva, who has been given leave to stay at home because he does not know well how to handle a gun. Besides him there are two more who may stay at home because of their age. Of several who have already gone, and of others who still have to go, we have the best hope. Many of them said that it grieved them to leave us, and to be deprived by their absence of the privilege of hearing the word of God.

On 18 August our 5 baptised attended our church litany [13] before the first sermon, and were much moved. In the sermon, besides the slave woman mentioned above, who visited us again, there were so many strange Hottentots that the room was not enough. After the sermon Brother Kühnel rode to Baas Teunis's son to visit him, but did not find him at home.

<!-- lines 2639-2668 -->
On 19 August we received a letter from him, in which he expressed his regret at not having been at home yesterday, and told us at the same time that he was willing to make us a present of some vines if we wanted them, and that he had brought a mud of meal for us from his father. To fetch the latter Brother Kühnel rode there this same afternoon; and the former moved Baas Teunis's son to come to Baviaanskloof on 20 August to show us how the vine is planted in this country, which made us a great deal of work and kept us busy digging all day. So our garden costs us much labour, and we must spend all possible time on it. We have at last been able to eat yellow and white turnips from our garden, and salad too. The leaves of the white turnips taste almost like spinach. Now comes the time and the month in which everything [must be planted and sown] [14], which still requires much work, since little land has yet been got ready. The chief and most necessary work is to lead the water round the whole garden, so as to water the garden with it; we kept busy diligently with this work on the 21st, 22nd and 23rd.

<!-- lines 2669-2699 -->
On 24 August it was indicated to us in our conference to baptise another Hottentot woman, and to take one into the class of candidates for baptism.

On 25 August, in spite of heavy rain, there were still a fair number of hearers in our church meeting.

On 26 August the husband of our baptised Anna Maria came back. We had given him a letter to Mr Gordon (the head of the military and the second person in the government of this country), telling him in it that the bearer of this letter was the husband of a woman baptised by us, who through her husband's absence had fallen into the utmost poverty and was without the necessary means of life for her children. We therefore asked His Honour, if it were in any way possible, to give this man leave to return home, so as to earn bread for his wife and children. It was no better with the other Hottentot women who, bereft of their husbands, were also bereft of the help they so needed to provide their bare living. Mr Gordon had the following friendly answer given us by the same man:

<!-- lines 2700-2733 -->
"Good friend and brother! I have duly received your letter, which does me honour, and the following serves as answer to it. I have been much gladdened by the news of your well-being, and of the blessing granted by God for the spreading of the Gospel among the Hottentots, and I pray God with my whole heart to bless your work abundantly further. At your request the husband of the baptised Anna Maria is coming back, and I will have as many passes of leave [15] made out as you may wish. Yet it is my dearest duty to do everything to secure this country against being overpowered by people who have become barbarous, who now renounce faith in God and use every effort to make all nations share in their godless purposes, to turn everything upside down under the fair-seeming name of liberty. But I trust in God's help, and if they come I shall devour them. Well then, brother! When all this is done I will come and visit you, and if I can be of any service to you, it will be a great pleasure to me. Hearty greetings from my wife and children, as well as from me, being with the most sincere affection, at the Castle of Good Hope, your good friend and brother, Fr. I. Gordon [16]. 23 August 1793."

<!-- lines 2733-2769 -->
Juffrouw Smidt also sent on this occasion something to clothe Anna Maria with, as well as knives, forks, a small copper tea kettle, and 2 dollars in money to buy a wether. Anna Maria's husband brought all this with him. We had her called in the evening to hand it over to her. She was struck dumb at the sight, wept aloud [17], and did not know how to express and show her gratitude enough. We told her she ought to thank [the Saviour] [18], who had inclined the heart of this Juffrouw to make her this present.

Our outward circumstances required that one of us should go to the Cape again, to buy necessities that we could not well order by letter. So we had asked Baas Teunis to let us know when he meant to ride to the Cape, so as to make the necessary journey there with him. This man has to be at the Cape 4 times a year, and coming there under his escort we can buy what we like, and he is so kind as to take what we have bought with him for nothing, whereas otherwise one has to pay double the freight for it. He sent us word today that as soon as the wagons expected from the Cape had arrived [19], he would leave with them for the Cape at once; but he would let us know the day of his departure more exactly.

<!-- lines 2770-2822 -->
Because of the undertaking of this journey to the Cape in company with Baas Teunis, we resolved to baptise beforehand the candidate indicated to us on 24 August, so that all three of us could be present. We fixed 28 August for it, and for that reason on 27 August had the woman to be baptised come to us, to put to her the same questions we had put to those before her: namely whether her whole heart was so set as to renounce all heathen works and traditions once for all, to give herself wholly to Jesus soul and body, and to live for him alone in this world. She answered with tears in her eyes: "Yes, that I will, trusting in God's grace and help." In the evening we made this known in the general meeting, and on 28 August at 10 o'clock holy baptism was administered to her, and the name Elisabeth given her [20]. The presence and nearness of God filled the meeting with a blessed and deep emotion. Because of the heavy rain the baptism had to be performed indoors, so that many for want of room had to stay standing outside the house, to see and hear this solemn act too.

In the evening we 3 poor missionaries closed our choir year with a humble confession of guilt for everything with which we have grieved his compassionate heart in this past year, and with an abashed thanksgiving for all the benefits enjoyed from him for soul and body, above all with regard to the poor Hottentots whom he has been pleased to entrust to our care and to our serving them with the saving Gospel.

On 29 August, to celebrate this day of remembrance so important to us [21], we had our morning blessing early in the morning. At 10 o'clock we read together the address that Brother Reichel gave us at our sending out at Herrnhut, and in the evening we partook of Holy Communion. The Saviour was feelingly near us the whole day and blessed our little meetings together.

<!-- lines 2823-2869 -->
On 30 August Brother Schwinn left for Baas Teunis's, to go with him to the Cape. [There he heard that a parcel from] the fatherland had come [22]. This moved him to come back the same day, supposing that there would be letters from the congregation in it, which we were looking out for with the greatest longing. But when we had opened the parcel we found only one letter in it, from Brother Treschow in Copenhagen, which was nevertheless very dear and welcome to us.

Brother Schwinn then went back to Baas Teunis on 31 August, who intends to leave for the Cape on 2 September, if he is not prevented by continuing heavy rain. The letter from Brother Treschow mentioned above, in which he takes it for granted that we had received letters from the fatherland, was brought to us by a ship that had an extraordinarily quick voyage, and was on the way from Copenhagen to the Cape roads no more than 2 months and 9 days. With the letter mentioned we also received a letter from Mr Heusser at the Cape, in which he tells us that if we had anything to write or send to the fatherland, we should just let him have it, as he expected that an opportunity to send it there would soon arise. This offer from Mr Heusser was very welcome to us, and made us no longer put off giving our dear brethren news of ourselves and of the work of God among the Hottentots through the enclosed diary, trusting that our kind Lord will let it come safely into your hands, so that in the congregations too the due offering of praise may be brought to him for his great mercies shown to us and the Hottentots.

<!-- lines 2870-2898 -->
We must add this: that we hold the singing meeting, or singing school, with the children twice a week at about 8 o'clock in the evening, and it is so often attended by the women too that there is often not room enough. Brother Kühnel plays the flute for it, which the children follow carefully, and their voices truly sound fine. In this way they learn many verses, and one even hears little children of 3 or 4 years singing them.

With this we close our diary, and commend ourselves and the Hottentots to the remembrance and intercession of all our congregations, that they may beg for us grace and wisdom from the Lord to serve this work he has begun according to his heart, and to let ourselves be led in all circumstances by his Spirit, which we need in the utmost degree. Help us to beg this from our kind Lord, being and remaining your brethren bound together in Jesus' death,
Hendrik Marsveld
Daniel Schwinn
Joh. Christian Kühnel.

Enclosed also is a letter from Juffrouw Smith at the Cape, whom we have often mentioned in the diary we have sent, and who will perhaps be mentioned still more in future. She is a member of the pious society at the Cape, from whom, as from others of one mind with her, we have several letters in hand. It will probably not be unwelcome to you to read such a letter, written in her own hand.
