10 August was a joyful day for us; since we received letters from Zeist from Brother Richter, and from Copenhagen from Brothers Treschow and Satterup [1]. From them we learned that our brothers had taken all possible trouble to send us letters and goods; but to our sorrow we must report that we have received nothing of all this. Brother Richter writes that the goods sent first were taken by a French ship; but that they had turned to Copenhagen, and supposed that everything would now have come into our hands. Brother Satterup mentions 2 letters, and other packets and goods besides, that have been sent to us; but we have received nothing in [...] heartfelt sharing of our dear brothers and sisters with us and the work of God here; over which we were much ashamed: and that the brothers in London had commended us to the government in the best way [2]. The reports from Zeist and Neuwied were very important to us; and we commend these two places, Neuwied especially, to God's mercy and care [3].

<!-- lines 16367-16372 -->
At this time our garden gave us much work, since the right planting time is now drawing near. The peach trees have already given us some supply, and now promise much. This fruit, when dried, makes good food for the winter.

<!-- lines 16373-16377 -->
On 13 August we first held a morning blessing with our baptised; then a general meeting, in which a child was baptised. In the evening we enjoyed Holy Communion [4].

<!-- lines 16378-16396 -->
On the 14th Baas Teunis told us that a fleet had put into one of the bays, which was taken for a Dutch one. This rumour spread quickly far and wide, and the farmers at once began to ill-treat everywhere the Hottentots whom the English General had taken under his special protection. Of ours a whole family was dragged away, with threats of beating them to death as soon as they showed themselves with us again [5]. One of the farmers said: "Now we will settle with you and the Herrnhuters; until now you have been master; now it will be our turn again." Now since the General has ordered Baas Teunis to report to him at once as soon as the farmers made so bold as to be their own judges, we informed him of this by letter [6].

<!-- lines 16397-16419 -->
On the 18th a baptised Hottentot woman came to us with her little son, 4 years old, and declared that this child of hers gave her no rest until she went to us with him. So we asked him what he had to tell us? Upon which the child burst into tears and said: I want to become a child of the Saviour, and to go to him with my little sister. This little sister of his, 1½ years old, had been buried yesterday. On the whole a special work of grace is now to be seen among the children. So a girl of 9 years said lately, weeping: "My heart hurts when I think of the Saviour, and how much he has suffered for me. I want to know nothing more now of the things of this world: I want to live only for the dear Saviour." One often hears such expressions, and many of the grown women, too, now come to disclose the unrest of their hearts and their longing to be saved.

<!-- lines 16420-16437 -->
Many rumours were going about at this time over the newly arrived fleet. To learn something certain of it Brother Kühnel travelled to Mr Nelson from the Cape, who we had heard had come to his country place [7]. This gentleman told us that it was all over already; that 9 Dutch warships, having put into Saldanha Bay, had surrendered without firing a shot to the English admiral, who had come to seek them out with 14 warships; while the English General had come to occupy the said bay from the land side with 4000 men, partly Hottentots; so that this event had passed off without bloodshed [8]. But now the talk among the farmers was that a French fleet would come; that would teach the English something other than to surrender to them without a blow struck.

<!-- lines 16438-16453 -->
On the 28th Brother Schwinn was called to a sick woman, and found her in a [...] said, among other things, "on the Saviour, what he has suffered for me out of love, and I pray him for the forgiveness of my sins" [9]. To the question how she felt when she thought of dying, she answered: "I go as a poor sinner to our dear Father." On the 30th Brother Marsveld visited a sick man, and found him too in a blessed state of soul. "If I die," he said, "then I go to the Saviour: for I pray him for the forgiveness of all my sins, and he will not cast me out."

## Notes

1. *van Zeist van Br. Richter, en uit Coppenhagen van Br: Treſchow, en Satterup*: Brother Richter at Zeist, not identified; Brother Treschow of Copenhagen (see the glossary); Brother Satterup, not identified.
2. Line 16358 is missing. The transcription runs *doch niets hebben wij in* / *lijk deelneemen van onze gel: Brrs en Zrs met ons*. The lost line probably finished *in handen gekregen* ("received into our hands") and began a new report of what the letters said, ending in *hart-lijk*, "heartfelt". The words in the translation are only those that survive.
3. Neuwied lay on the Rhine, in the path of the French and Austrian armies in these years.
4. 13 August is the memorial day of the renewal of the Brethren's Church (13 August 1727), kept with Holy Communion; in 1796 it fell on a Saturday.
5. *op beeren en dood te zullen ſlaan*: *beeren* is not clear, perhaps a misreading of a word such as *lam*, "lame". The German column has *zu Tode zu prügeln*, "beat to death", and the English follows it. To be checked against the image.
6. *denzelven*: "him", which may be Baas Teunis or the General. The sentence is not clear about who was told.
7. *den Heer Nelſon van de Kaap*: a gentleman of Cape Town with a country place near the mission. Not identified.
8. The surrender of the Dutch squadron under Rear-Admiral Engelbertus Lucas to the British fleet of Vice-Admiral Sir George Keith Elphinstone in Saldanha Bay on 17 August 1796. The figures are the diarists'.
9. Line 16440 is missing. The transcription runs *vond dezelve in eene* / *zeide zij onder anderen, "aan den Heiland, wat*. The lost line probably said that she was in a blessed state, and began her words ("I think ..."). The words in the translation are only those that survive.
