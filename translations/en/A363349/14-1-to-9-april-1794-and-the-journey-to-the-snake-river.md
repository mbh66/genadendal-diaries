Brother Schwinn writes further:

<!-- lines 4784-4785 -->
"On 1 April another child was buried.

<!-- lines 4785-4793 -->
"On the 2nd a woman said to me: 'My dear teacher, may it please God to give me the great grace of being baptised in Jesus' blood and washed from sins! I have grieved the Saviour with my sins for so many years; now it is my whole mind to live only for him in this world.' With that she burst into tears.

<!-- lines 4793-4797 -->
"Hanna revealed to me that when she recently had something to do at a farmer's she had watched the dancing, and [had become uneasy] about it. [She was told what was need]ful about this [1].

<!-- lines 4797-4812 -->
"On the 3rd I received by a Hottentot a small chest from the Cape, in which I thought I would find letters, news and Daily Texts. I opened it at once, but found myself disappointed: it was a small chest of snuff. However, I also received a couple of letters that the minister Vos had brought with him. This gentleman wrote with them that he had wished to visit us and see the work of God among the Hottentots in person, but his circumstances had not allowed it. He is going to Roodezand, a day's journey from the Cape, and the minister there is going to the Cape [2].

<!-- lines 4812-4819 -->
"On the 4th the catechising was very moving. Afterwards a woman came to me and lamented that she was so troubled, for at one time she could believe, and then again not. Another said she had believed that she was not as bad as others, because she had not done this and that; 'but now I must believe that if anyone goes to hell, it will be I.'

<!-- lines 4819-4820 -->
"On the 5th there was again the burial of a child.

<!-- lines 4820-4834 -->
"From time to time it becomes more and more plain that a storm is gathering over our heads, namely for this reason: since the Hottentots have been hearing and accepting the word of God, they will no longer let themselves be used for sinning, and from this bitter enmity arises. A neighbour of ours, who was otherwise truly our friend, is now angry [3] with us. Several told us that we had been very harshly accused before the government, and that we would have to move away from here to the Kaffers. 'If this happens,' they said, 'we will move with you.'

<!-- lines 4835-4842 -->
"The cause is the Saviour's. If we must go away from here, he will go before us; he has called us, he will help us through. Meanwhile his work goes on here with blessing, and the more people take pains to draw the Hottentots away from us, the more of them come to us. Just today 3 large families came from the Salt River, two days' journey from here, to live with us.

<!-- lines 4842-4846 -->
"On Sunday the 6th more came to the meeting than have ever been here. I prayed to the Saviour that all these people might become his own. Afterwards some came to me, at whose declarations I could rejoice.

<!-- lines 4847-4852 -->
"On the 7th 10 women and 7 children of those who came here the day before yesterday came to ask leave to come to school. These are not yet all who want to move here; their number is not yet known for certain, but the talk is that there are more than thirty."

<!-- lines 4852-4854 -->
On the 8th towards evening Brothers Marsveld and Kühnel came home safe and well.

<!-- lines 4854-4923 -->
They report the following of their journey: "On 31 March we left with our Jacob, and on 1 April at 5 o'clock in the afternoon, without having suffered any harm, we arrived at the Landdrost's at Swellendam, by whom we were received in the friendliest manner.

<!-- lines 4923-4930 -->
"His wife was not at home, but at another place 2 hours from here. He had the horses harnessed at once to fetch her, and she came home the same evening. She was very glad of our visit, asked at once how things went among the Hottentots, rejoiced at what we told her, and declared that she took a great share in this work.

<!-- lines 4930-4953 -->
"On the 2nd this gentleman gave us the chance to see some more places in this district, and after our return we disclosed to him our plan to travel to the Snake River and visit the Hottentots there. On the 3rd in the afternoon we rode off towards the Karremelks River [5], and on the 4th came to some kraals, where among others we visited the Hottentot who was recently with us and would like to move to us. He and his family were all eager to hear God's word. We told them that we had come in order to make the Hottentots acquainted with their Saviour, Creator and Redeemer, and then told them the chief content of the Gospel. They listened very attentively and said that their concern was to be saved; and also that those whom we had seen on the way were of the same mind and would like to move to us.

<!-- lines 4953-4963 -->
"As regards the land, we found that from here to Swellendam the best land is occupied by the farmers, and the Hottentots must make do with the driest and most barren land and with brackish water too. By another road, which had been pointed out to us as being shorter, we set off back to [Swellendam; and it would have cost us] dear [because of its] danger and pathlessness, had we not been kept by a higher hand [6].

<!-- lines 4963-4972 -->
"On the 5th we came back to the Landdrost's and stayed with him overnight, until the 6th towards midday. We must testify that he as well as his wife, and others who were in his house, showed us as much love and friendship as we could hardly have expected. They wished us every blessing of God on our journey and on our work among the Hottentots, so that many more might be converted.

<!-- lines 4973-4979 -->
"On the 6th we spent the night at the farmer Horz's [7], from whom we heard that some 50 Hottentots meant to move to Baviaanskloof.

<!-- lines 4979-4986 -->
"On the 7th we stayed overnight with Mrs Lind [8]; with this family one could hold pleasant conversations about salvation, which one does not otherwise find among the farmers. On the 8th we came home to our dear Brother Schwinn, heartily thankful to the Saviour for his keeping and support."

<!-- lines 4986-4993 -->
A gentleman named Freister [9], whom we had spoken with at the post, visited us on the 9th with his wife, son and daughter, and likewise Mrs Teunis with another woman from the Cape. This company ate with us at midday, and afterwards we had to let some children come together so that they could hear their singing, at which they were full of wonder.

<!-- lines 4994-4997 -->
With this we now close this diary, and commend ourselves with our Hottentots to the remembrance and prayers of all our dear brothers and sisters.

<!-- lines 4998-5000 -->
Henrich Marsveld, Daniel Schwinn and Johan Christian Kühnel

## Notes

1. Line 4796 is missing. The transcription runs *en nu daarover* / *dige hier omtrent gezegd*. The German diary has "and now she had become uneasy about it. She was told what was needful". The words in square brackets are supplied from there; *dige* is the end of *nodige* or *noodige*, "needful".
2. See Michiel Christiaan Vos in the glossary. The German diary tells more about the chest: it had been wetted when the Khoekhoe man who carried it drove through a deep river, and the snuff was from Captain Olhof; the letters were from Brother Marsveld's brother in Gouda and from a Brother Heg.
3. *bos op ons*: probably *boos*, "angry".
4. The first copy keeps line 4818 (*daan had: maar nu moet ik gelooven, dat zoo iemand*); in the repeat, line 4878 runs on *wijl zij het een en ander niet ge-* / *in de Hel komt* without it. Page 73b ends with the catchword *het* (line 4860), and page 75a begins *het vriendelijkſt ontfangen werden* (line 4923), so no text is lost between them.
5. *Karnmelks Rivier*; see Karringmelks River in the glossary. The German diary gives much more of the first days of the journey (the post, snakes and ostriches, a night at the farmer Kempe's, a steep mountain, a meal at the farmer Herz's), the Landdrost's secretary and led horse, and a hard night ride to the farmer Beukes's, whose water was salty. The German diary breaks off on 4 April in the middle of the visit to the kraals (note 6 of entry 20 of A363595, where seven page images are missing); the Dutch gives the rest of the journey, from "They listened very attentively" to the return on the 8th.
6. Line 4960 is missing. The transcription runs *vertrokken wij weder naar* / *vaarlijkheid en ongebaandheid des Zelfs duur te ſtaan gekomen zijn*. *vaarlijkheid* is the end of *gevaarlijkheid*, "danger". The words in square brackets are supplied from the sense.
7. *den Boer Horz*: perhaps the farmer Herz, "a baptised Jew", with whom the German diary has the brethren dine on the way out on 1 April. Not identified.
8. *Vrouw Lint*: perhaps Juffrouw Lind; see Lind in the glossary.
9. *een Heer met Naam Freiſter*: not identified. The Dutch leaves the brethren's report without a closing mark; the visit on the 9th is told by all three.
10. An archive shelf mark, left untranslated; compare *PAIRSE 12* in A363597.
