<!-- lines 00002-00004 -->
Diary of the Hottentot congregation in Baviaanskloof. From May 1803 to February 1804.

<!-- lines 00005-00024 -->
Several of our Hottentots, especially the older ones, find it very hard to commit anything from God's Word to memory, because they know little Dutch. Yet we wish that those who want to advance in the graces of the congregation and grow in the knowledge of Jesus Christ should be able to do so. It was therefore thought good to call the candidates for Holy Communion together on their own from time to time in future, and to talk with them about Holy Communion, so that in this way too they might gain a truer understanding of it. Brother Rose made a start on 2 May, and the Hottentots showed themselves very grateful for this instruction.

<!-- lines 00025-00031 -->
On the 16th a young man of the Kaffer nation [1] came to us and earnestly asked permission to live with us. We granted it, at least for a time, and wished that he might be won for the Saviour.

<!-- lines 00032-00045 -->
At the speaking before Holy Communion [2] this month, several brothers and sisters confessed that they were in the habit of looking too much at others, and that this kept them from making greater progress in grace themselves. One sister said: "My heart is still full of what I enjoyed at the last Communion. I often go off by myself, so that my heart stays open to the Saviour and I do not lose him."

<!-- lines 00045-00058 -->
On 24 May several Hottentot families, 24 persons in all, arrived here on their way back from the camp [3]. They asked permission to stay with us. We therefore spoke with them at length in the church and set before them all the rules in force among us, and they promised with a handshake to keep them.

<!-- lines 00058-00099 -->
On 30 May, the second day of Pentecost, 6 adults and 1 child were baptised into the death of Jesus, and 13 persons became candidates for baptism. When we told the former a few days before that the Saviour had granted them the grace of holy baptism, they were deeply humbled and glad. In the days that followed they told us plainly of their hearts' longing to be cleansed from all their sins by the blood of Jesus, and we took the opportunity to put it to them that coming to the Saviour was not enough: they must now also stay faithful to him. The baptism itself stirred all our Hottentots, and one could see clearly that the Holy Spirit had been especially at work in hearts during these days of Pentecost. On the day after the baptism the Hottentots came to us in great numbers and told us of their wish to advance in the graces of the congregation. Among them were some who have lived here a long time; we would be very glad if their hour had also come to wake from the sleep of sin. The children, too, came to us one after another, asking us to remember them, because they would gladly become children of the Saviour.

<!-- lines 00100-00109 -->
About this time our Hottentots learned that runaway slaves were hiding in our district and had already stolen an ox in the veld, so several of our young men went out to pursue these robbers. They came home again, however, without having found them.

<!-- lines 00109-132 -->
On 20 May [4] another 37 Hottentots, men, women and children, came to us from the camp, several of whom had lived here before. Since they very much wanted to stay with us, we spoke with them thoroughly. We explained our arrangements to them fully and told them that anyone who did not wish to live by them had better leave again at once; but whoever was willing, with God's help, to keep them should give us his hand on it. At once they all stood up to give us their hands, and one could feel that it came from their hearts.

<!-- lines 132-136 -->
For the rest, we had few visitors from outside in these months, either from Cape Town or from places nearer by, so that we mostly lived quietly by ourselves.

## Notes

1. *Kaffern-Nation*: the diarists' term for the Xhosa. See the glossary for how the repository handles this word.
2. *Sprechen*, "speaking": the Moravian practice of individual conversations with each member before Holy Communion. The later passages of this kind report what people said at these conversations.
3. The transcription reads *von der Waſen am Camp*; the word *Wasen* is unclear and may be a misreading, so it is left out of the English. The camp is probably the military camp near Cape Town: the 1803 figures in entry 07 record 45 men who came to live here "after discharge from military service". To be checked against the manuscript image.
4. The date 20 May comes after 30 May. The transcription reads *20ten May*. It may be a slip in the manuscript or a misreading (for example of 30, or of June). To be checked against the image. Pentecost Monday fell on 30 May in 1803, so that date is consistent.
