On 11 and 12 July we had bitter cold here, so that there was ice in the mornings. On the 13th, however, rain came, to the great joy of the Hottentots, who could now plough their land; it is high time for that now.

<!-- lines 145-163 -->
At the speaking of the baptised and the candidates for baptism there were several lovely declarations, full of the sense of their sin [1]. One of the candidates for baptism said: "Until now things have looked very bad in my heart. This is already my third year as a candidate for baptism, and I must confess that I had lost from my heart the grace that had come to me. When I heard that we were to be spoken with, I was very frightened and thought: 'Oh God, how do things stand with you?' Then I fell at the Saviour's feet and begged him to have mercy on me anew."

<!-- lines 163-178 -->
A woman candidate for baptism said: "The Holy Spirit set my lost condition, and all the sins I have committed since my youth, so plainly before my eyes that I could think nothing else than that I must be lost for ever. But Jesus revealed himself to me as my Saviour and gave the comfort of his death to my poor heart, so that now I can believe that he let his hands and feet be pierced on the tree of the cross for my sins too."

<!-- lines 178-193 -->
A girl said: "There must be great corruption in my heart, for at one moment I feel disobedience towards my parents, at another I am plagued by pride, because I often want to be better than others. I grieve the Saviour so often that I am sometimes afraid he will cast me off in the end. But when I ask his forgiveness for all the bad I find in myself, I feel his love in my heart again."

<!-- lines 193-211 -->
One of the baptised said: "Every day I ask the Saviour to keep me close to him, so that I may always feel his peace in my heart. I am old and do not know when the thread of my life will be cut. With that thought I have only the one wish, that the Saviour would show me grace and bring me one step nearer to him." (By these words he meant to make known his wish to become a candidate for Communion.) He added: "I am not worthy of it, for I am a great sinner, but all the more do I need grace."

<!-- lines 211-225 -->
Another said: "I am no longer as simple and childlike towards the Saviour as I was for a long time. That is why I no longer feel the nearness of Jesus in my heart as I did, and notice more clearly that I keep getting worse. So I have asked the Saviour to have mercy on me anew and give me his peace in my heart, because I do mean to live for him alone in this world."

<!-- lines 225-241 -->
An excluded baptised woman [2] said: "I have broken the covenant I made with the Saviour in baptism, and by my sins I have crucified Jesus anew. The Spirit of God often knocked at my door, but for a long time I went about as if dead and gave him no hearing. Now I see what harm I did myself by leaving the Saviour and giving myself over again to the service of sin, and I ask the Saviour to forgive me my strayings."

<!-- lines 242-275 -->
Another woman, who had stayed for a time with her mother on a farm, said: "By going away from here I did myself much harm [3]. When I came back and went to the meetings again, I noticed that there was no feeling left in my heart and that I had grown cold and indifferent towards the Saviour, though before I had lived in the enjoyment of his love. In the past, when a bad thought came to me, I confessed it at once to the Saviour and to my teachers, and then my heart was light and glad again. But now that I had lost my simplicity and the peace of God, I was anxious and afraid to admit to my teachers the bad things springing up in my heart. Yet the Holy Spirit gave me no rest until I turned to the Saviour in all my distress and threw myself at his pierced feet. There I found forgiveness, and now I know again what I have in the Saviour."

<!-- lines 275-301 -->
A girl of 10, who had been baptised as a small child, also came to Sister Rose [4] and wished to be spoken with. When the sister said that she was now speaking with the adult baptised and not with the children, the girl said with many tears: "Oh, I poor lost child must come too and say how things are with me. I do not really feel the Saviour's love in my heart, and that is why I am crying. I am afraid my poor soul may be lost, even though I am baptised." She asked whether the Saviour would have mercy on her again. She was assured that if she turned to him as a poor child he would certainly hear her, and then she said: "I will ask him with my whole heart to let me feel his love again."

<!-- lines 302-310 -->
Towards the end of July we had very cold weather, and our mountains were covered with snow. This cold is very hard on us European Brethren and Sisters in this country every time, and it always brings various ailments with it.

<!-- lines 310-316 -->
On the 29th, to our great joy, a Hottentot returning from Cape Town brought us some congregation news [5] and several letters from our dear Brethren and Sisters in Zeist.

## Notes

1. *sündengefaßte Erklärungen*: an unusual compound, rendered here by sense. The transcription then has *PAIRSE 12* at the foot of manuscript page 5a (line 149). This is probably an archive shelf mark, like the *P.A.I. R.5.E. 6.* at the head of A363596, and not part of the diary text. It is left untranslated. To be checked against the image.
2. "Excluded": a baptised member barred from Communion and some meetings for a time as church discipline.
3. The transcription has *thau* at the top of page 8a (line 249). It is the end of the word *gethan* ("done"), split across the page break, and not a separate word.
4. *Schwester Rosin*: the wife of Brother Rose. The ending *-in* makes a woman's form of the surname.
5. *Gemeinnachrichten*: the handwritten newsletter of the Moravian Church, copied and sent to its congregations and missions.
